    Find the right part of the material

    Search broadly. Then narrow the question.¶

    A title, a course or a teaching unit¶

    The header search accepts concept names, aliases, course codes and lecture tags. For example, m1c identifies Math I: Calculus; m1c-lecture02 finds its Lecture 2 material.

    Keep the search inside your course¶

    Use Search in course when a global search is too broad. The Math I–IV hubs now organise 37 lecture or week entries, with shortcuts into their concepts.

    Build a focused pool in Finder¶

    Combine keywords, course codes or teaching-unit tags with AND, OR and parentheses. Select useful results, open pages directly, or use the selection for random revision.

    Personal evidence, practical next steps

    Notice what needs another attempt.¶

    Course diagnostics brings together visited and unvisited concepts, ratings, lower-readiness topics and available AI-check evidence by teaching unit. Open it from a course page and choose a manageable review target.

    Your mastery rating¶

    Rate a concept after trying to explain or use it. Ratings support diagnostics, revision and Learning Path.

    Mastered
    I can use it confidently.
    Clear
    I mostly understand it.
    Unclear
    Some steps still need work.
    Unknown
    I am not ready to use it yet.

    Practise before you reveal¶

    Random revision moves through a chosen pool of concepts.

    Self-test opens content folded so that you can attempt recall before reading the explanation.

    AI-test uses an available AI mastery check as the next step. Availability depends on the concept.

    Choose your revision pool →

    A check is a prompt to think¶

    Some concept pages offer a short AI-generated multiple-choice check. Feedback can suggest a mastery level; your own rating remains under your control. Compare the explanation with your reasoning and course sources. Quiz history can be included in a connected account's synced record.

    Readiness is a personal study indicator. It is not a course grade, a predicted exam mark, or a report about a class. A visit or a completed quiz alone does not establish understanding.

    When the missing step is elsewhere

    Follow the connection back.¶

    Open Concept connections or Concept maps on a concept page to inspect prerequisites, later uses and nearby ideas. Learning Path helps you work towards a target through those relationships.

    The maps include 2D and 3D views. Optional map enhancements may require an unlock or an active trial; the interface shows their availability. The underlying page links remain useful when you prefer to read directly.

    A small amount of time, used deliberately

    Give your next session a shape.¶

    The homepage planner turns 15, 30 or 45 minutes into an ordered checklist for understanding, recall, extending ideas or lesson preparation. Add an optional topic, tick the steps as you work, and download the checklist to keep it.

    Ticks belong to that page session. They do not change mastery, XP or account records, and they do not create a saved revision session.

    For teachers & tutors

    Review the assumptions behind an explanation.¶

    The Teaching workbench shows a published concept's direct foundations, earlier prerequisites and downstream connections. Add an intended learning outcome and preparation notes, then download or print a relationship review.

    It reads the Wiki's published prerequisite links. It does not edit the curriculum, evaluate students or produce class analytics. Check the linked definitions and sources against your own course materials; a missing link does not prove that no prerequisite exists.

    Your tools, your learning record

    Make it your workspace.¶

    You can read the notes and use basic search without an account. My is the personal area for ratings, saved pages, history, profile settings, study connections and sync. A connected account can sync supported learning records across devices; inspect its sync status to see whether the transfer succeeded.

    In this browser

    Local ratings and study history depend on this browser's storage. Clearing site data can remove local records.

    With a connected account

    Supported learning events, quiz history and personal settings can travel between connected devices.

    Keep a downloaded copy

    Homepage checklists and workbench notes are page-session tools. Download their exports to keep them.

    Choose what others can see¶

    In My → Privacy, choose Private, Connections only or Public for supported profile sections. “Connections only” means accepted study connections. These settings control visibility; they are not a switch that turns cloud sync into local-only storage.

    New accounts currently make User rankings public by default, while the other profile sections default to private. Review your own settings. Public rankings can include your username, XP, quiz results, lifetime Store spending and mastered-concept count; showing an avatar and intro also requires a public profile.

    Profile comment summaries follow their visibility setting. Comments posted on concept pages remain visible there. Removing an account from this browser signs out here; it does not delete cloud data or public comments.

    Personalise the experience¶

    My → Store uses EORbits earned through study activity. It offers interface and profile themes, fonts, effects and optional study enhancements. Many items offer previews or trials; check the item's description and access label before unlocking it.

    Activity is not academic performance¶

    Rankings show activity and engagement across the Wiki. XP, EORbits, visits and public quiz signals are not course grades or an academic ranking. Choose a study target from your understanding and course needs.

    Keep a useful route forward

    When something gets in the way¶

    Too many search results?
    Search inside the course, or add its code and a specific term in Finder.
    A formula is showing raw LaTeX?
    Refresh once. If it persists, report the page and formula that failed to render.
    Two devices show different progress?
    Check that both use the intended account, open My → Account → Sync & learning data, and inspect the result. If history needs recovery, follow the offered review and backup steps.
    Not sure what to study next?
    Choose one uncertain concept from Course diagnostics, revisit its prerequisites and try an example. Rankings are not a study plan.

    Help improve a page¶

    Use an edit request for an error, unclear step, missing prerequisite, broken link or mismatched AI check. Include the page, the problem and a suggested correction or source.

    Questions or suggestions?¶

    Contact W. Zhu, aka Rock at w.zhu@rug.nl. For a technical issue, include the page and steps that led to it; keep login links and private learning records out of public reports.
