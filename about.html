    Open Concept Finder →

    Keep a set for the next tutorial.¶

    Save selected Finder concepts as a named study set for tutorial preparation, revision or teaching. Reopen it, add a note, reorder the concepts or remove items. Each set keeps the selection and order you saved.

    Try one small review. Find one lecture in Finder, select three to five concepts, then choose Save as set → Review this set. Try explaining each idea before revealing the notes. Record one point to revisit in your set notes, and return to the same set for your next session.

    Study sets stay in this browser, in the current local account workspace. Account cloud sync and Account backups do not include them. Download a dedicated JSON backup to keep a recoverable copy; preview a backup before importing it as new sets. Use Markdown for a shareable reading list.

    If local account storage is unavailable, you can still preview and download a draft. Named study sets are unavailable in Exam Mode.

    With Account connected, Share with a connection sends a saved set to one of your study connections. It reaches them as a notification; Copy to my study sets opens it in their Finder as an unsaved draft, leaving out any concept that is no longer published, and they choose whether to save it.

    Choose Review this set to start a 5- or 10-concept review session. The queue follows your set order and shows the actual available count. Read a concept, try Self-test or an existing AI check, then choose Done & next or Skip & next. Pause and resume at your next concept, or end the session and download its summary.

    Review sessions are saved in this browser and are unavailable in Exam Mode. Resume restores the concept queue and your position; it does not restore an unfinished AI question set. Session completion does not change mastery or award extra XP. AI checks and mastery ratings keep their existing records.

    Personal evidence, practical next steps

    Notice what needs another attempt.¶

    Course diagnostics brings together visited and unvisited concepts, ratings, lower-readiness topics and available AI-check evidence by teaching unit. Open it from a course page and choose a manageable review target.

    AI concept checks need a connection to check your answers. Questions and options load first; the correct answer and explanation arrive after you submit. If checking fails, your selected answer stays in the open quiz for Retry check answer. A question updated since you loaded it requires a fresh page before starting another check. These repeatable learning questions support practice and friendly rankings; they are not a confidential examination bank.

    A check is a prompt to think¶

    Some concept pages offer a short AI-generated multiple-choice check. Feedback can suggest a mastery level; your own rating remains under your control. Compare the explanation with your reasoning and course sources. Quiz history can be included in a connected account's synced record. Before starting, choose whether to use a timer; this choice is remembered on this browser. You can check a concept again as often as you like, for example to consolidate a week's work. With a connected account and server-confirmed answers, repeat checks earn XP up to the daily AI-check limit, pay EORbits for one check per concept each UTC week (Monday to Sunday), and count once per concept per UTC day towards quiz achievements and Most tested. Guest or offline practice still provides feedback and mastery, but does not earn new cloud rewards when imported later. Once you have shown you know a concept, its answers move your EOR Rating only a little.

    No prerequisite ratings yet? Readiness says Not assessed, rather than implying a failed 0% result. Rate a prerequisite after trying to explain it to begin your own assessment.

    Readiness is a personal study indicator. It is not a course grade, a predicted exam mark, or a report about a class. A visit or a completed quiz alone does not establish understanding.

    Discover treasure as you explore.¶

    In the ordinary site's Nearby map, click or tap a concept to keep its connections. If this adds at least one new concept, a connected account gets a chance to discover a chest in either 2D or 3D. Each concept gives that account one chance per UTC day, shared across devices. Hovering, opening or refreshing the map, moving it, switching views and following a learning path do not give extra chances. Guest browsing and the Exam site have no treasure rewards.

    Chest Chance per eligible expansion Daily limit per account XP + EORbits Extra reward
    Crown 1 in 1,000 1 40 XP + 60 EORbits A package of three permanent shop items
    Gold 1 in 100 1 15 XP + 25 EORbits One permanent shop item
    Silver 1 in 20 2 5 XP + 8 EORbits 30% chance of one permanent shop item
    Bronze 1 in 5 5 2 XP + 2 EORbits 10% chance of one permanent shop item

    At most one chest appears per eligible expansion. Found but unopened chests reserve a place in the daily limit together with opened chests. Once a tier is full, drawing that tier gives no chest; its chance is not transferred to another tier. Limits reset at midnight UTC, when unopened chests expire. Reopening the map before then restores your pending chests.

    Click the chest to open it. It appears in a free part of the map, or in a compact tray when the map is crowded. Rewards can include map features, colours, fonts and other permanent shop customisations. Crown packages contain either 3D maps, map animations and a teal avatar initial, or the Nordic map style, Clear Silver mastery effect and a serif avatar initial. Items are unlocked for you to choose later; your current appearance is not changed automatically.

    Already own a rewarded item? It is converted into EORbits at its current shop price when you open the chest, including any current discount. If opening fails, use Retry to claim the same chest; retrying does not reroll or pay twice. Chest XP is a bonus: it does not increase EOR Rating, Most Active totals, learning achievements or study-day counts, and does not generate additional XP-to-EORbits income.

In a comment, type @ and the first letters of a name to mention a learner. On any other learner's profile, Pin as rival keeps up to five learners beside you on the user rankings; Rivals then shows just you and them, with the gap. Rival pins stay in this browser and are never sent to anyone.

    Choose what others can see¶

    In My → Account → Privacy, choose Private, Connections only or Public for supported profile sections. “Connections only” means accepted study connections. These settings control visibility; they are not a switch that turns cloud sync into local-only storage.

    New accounts currently make User rankings public by default, while the other profile sections default to private. Review your own settings. Public rankings can include your username, level, XP, quiz results, Store spending and EOR Rating; your public profile card shows your EOR Rating beside your level and XP; showing an avatar and intro also requires a public profile. Anyone who finds you in public rankings can open a basic profile card with that username, level and XP.

    Arena has separate switches for receiving challenges and sharing your competition record, inside My → Arena & weekend events → Your record. Both Arena switches start on. While invitations are on, learners who find you in public rankings or on your public profile can challenge you. Your competition record is shared only when your profile and rankings are both public, so making a private profile public can also reveal an existing record. Review these switches before changing profile visibility. You can participate without publishing your record. Hiding a record removes it from public display without promoting someone else into a previously awarded title.

    Profile comment summaries follow their visibility setting. Comments posted on concept pages remain visible there. A comment can include formulas: choose ∑ beside the comment box to type LaTeX with a live preview, or write $…$ inline and $$…$$ on its own line. Removing an account from this browser signs out here; it does not delete cloud data or public comments.

    Measure yourself with the EOR Rating¶

    Your EOR Rating is one Elo-style score that starts at 1200. Every rated result counts as a game against an opponent rating, so a harder success earns more and an easier miss costs more.

    • AI quizzes: a basic, standard or challenge question counts as a 1000, 1200 or 1400-rated opponent. Only your first server-reserved attempt at each question counts, up to 40 reserved questions per UTC day. Leaving a question uses its reserved slot; later practice neither adds nor subtracts Rating. Connect your account before starting to receive rated questions. At 1200, a correct challenge answer adds about 6 points and a wrong basic answer removes about 6. Answers on a concept you have recently shown you know count for less: if your best AI check on that concept in the last 30 days was 3 of 3, a correct basic, standard or challenge answer adds only about 1, 2 or 3, and a best of 2 of 3 counts three-quarters as much. Only your best check counts, so a deliberately poor check never makes a concept worth more again. Older score-only sessions already held by the server keep their one-time standard-tier history; newly imported summaries do not earn Rating.
    • Duels and ghost races: the other player's rating is the opponent. Beating an equal player adds 20. The same two players are rated at most three times in a rolling seven-day window, counting both kinds together.
    • Weekend events: after an event closes, a ten-question Quiz Sprint weighs the same as five games against a 1500-rated opponent. Each question gives credit for its share of the ten points it can earn. Historical puzzle events keep their original rating rules and results.

    Current ranks by the standing rating; Today, Last 7 days and Last 30 days rank by how far the rating moved inside that window, and a losing window is listed rather than hidden. One rated result puts you on Rankings → EOR Rating, following your ranking privacy setting, so practising alone is enough to be ranked; under ten results the rating is marked provisional. Rated answers are checked against the server's question bank. The study questions and answers are public, so Rating is a friendly learning signal, not a grade.

    Revisit a month's best¶

    On each user ranking, Monthly honours sits between Last 30 days and All time. Browse past champions for XP, EOR Rating gained and Store spending; each row shows the month's trophy, the learner as on the other boards, and the winning score. Honours use completed UTC calendar months; winning scores are frozen and every tied winner shares the title. A month with no eligible positive score has no champion for that metric; it keeps its row on each board with the reason, such as nobody earning XP, no rated results or nobody spending EORbits.

    The live Last 30 days tab currently covers the most recent 30 days. It can therefore differ from a completed calendar month's frozen honour score.

    Monthly trophies appear in your collection and public profile when shared. Choose up to three personal achievements or monthly honours for your showcase. Repeated wins of the same monthly title share one slot and appear as stacked medals with ×2, ×3, …; open a medal to see every month you won.

    Invite someone to a friendly duel¶

    Open another learner's public profile → Challenge. Choose an available course, 10–50 questions, and now or an agreed time within the next four weeks. Times show their time zone. The recipient can accept, decline or counter with a different course, question count or time. Invitations appear as a small notice when the reader is free. Choose Review invitation to open one on this device; Later keeps it in Arena. Signing in elsewhere does not open a table automatically.

    Both players press Ready and receive the same question and option order from the existing AI quiz bank. Each question has no answer timer. The first correct answer received by the server wins one point; a wrong option is visibly ruled out for both players.

    Agree an optional EORbits stake. Live duels and Ghost races support a whole-number stake of 0–10,000 EORbits per player; choose 0 to play without one. Either player can propose a different amount before acceptance, and the other player must accept the latest proposal. If an invitation asks for more than your available balance, suggest a smaller amount or 0, or decline. Acceptance checks both current balances and holds the same stake from each account together.

    The winner receives both stakes. A draw, cancellation, expiry or other no-result ending returns each player's stake. Leaving a started match follows the score rules below: it can still produce a winner, so leaving does not guarantee a refund. Stakes and their returns give no additional XP or EOR Rating and do not count as shop spending or monthly shopping honours.

    Mistakes reset on every question. For each player, the first wrong answer on that question gives a 5-second cooldown, the second gives 10 seconds, and the third awards that question's point to the opponent. Both review the explanation and press Next to continue. The match uses total question points, with equal totals recorded as a draw.

    If a player does not arrive within ten minutes of the agreed time, the match never started: nobody wins or loses. Rejoin a disconnected live match within 90 seconds. Leaving a started match — choosing Leave match, not reconnecting in time, or not pressing Next within two minutes after your opponent — is judged on the score at that moment. Leave while behind and your opponent wins. Leave with a lead your opponent could not even tie by answering every remaining question and you keep the win. Leave at level scores, or with a lead that could still be caught, and the match ends with no result: no win, loss or rating change. Ten minutes without any answer or Next also ends a match with no result. Answers are ordered by their arrival at the server, so connection quality can affect a close race.

    My → Arena & weekend events → Your record shows your EOR Rating, wins, draws, losses, opponents and earned honours. Complete a full duel, win your first one, beat three different opponents or complete duels with ten different opponents to earn the four duel keepsakes. Results decided by someone leaving do not advance these full-duel honours. These are friendly learning games, with public study material, rather than course assessments.

    Race a ghost instead. When you challenge someone, choose Ghost race to play at different times. Nothing is played until they accept, within three days. Then each of you has a week to play the same 10, 15 or 20 questions, in either order and whenever suits you; the server times each question from the moment it appears, and wrong answers pause you for 5 and then 10 seconds, as in a live duel. Each question's point goes where a shared table would have sent it: the earlier correct answer wins it, and using up three attempts first hands it to the other player. Whoever plays second races the first player's recorded run, their ghost, and sees its time for a question after finishing that question. Nobody wins because the other player did not turn up: an invitation that is not accepted, a week that runs out before both runs are played, or backing out before starting your run all end with no result. A run that was started and then left, with Leave race or by not answering for a day, is judged like leaving a live duel. A finished ghost race counts like a duel for your record, honours, EOR Rating and completion rewards.

    Watch a connection's match. During a live duel, each player can choose Let study connections watch. Watching opens only while both players allow it, and only to accepted study connections of either player, who find it under Connections playing now in Duels. Watchers see the score, and each question only after its point is decided, so nobody can be helped from outside. Players see how many are watching, and either can close watching at any time.

    Quiz the courses you are learning¶

    Open My → Arena & weekend events → Weekend events. Activities run from Saturday 00:00 to Monday 00:00, Europe/Amsterdam, with the following two weekends previewed.

    From 26 September 2026, scheduled competitions use a ten-question Quiz Sprint linked to the current Year 1 block. The programme currently covers 2026–2027 semester 1 (blocks 1a and 1b). Semester 2 will be added after its course timetable is published; Year 2 courses are not included.

    • During teaching, questions come only from that week's taught topics, using the Wiki's lecture or week labels and the course timetable. A course with no scheduled lecture that week contributes no new topics. Topics spanning several lecture weeks enter the pool in their final labelled week.
    • After the block finishes teaching, weekends draw ten random questions from the full available quiz pool of that block until the next block starts.
    • Once a new block begins, its courses become the scope. Courses without quizzes are listed but contribute no questions. If weekly topics or enough questions are unavailable, no competition opens for that weekend.

    The event shows the included courses and lecture or week scope. Everyone receives the same ten selected questions that weekend, worth 100 points in total. “Full block revision” means ten questions sampled from the block's available pool, not answering every question in that pool.

    Which courses contribute questions now? Block 1a has quizzes for Math I: Calculus; OR Modelling and Introduction to Data Analytics do not yet contribute questions. Block 1b has quizzes for Math II: Linear Algebra and Probability Theory for EOR; Programming does not yet contribute questions. Probability follows six lecture weeks; its former Week 7 material is currently hidden and excluded from quizzes.

    For example, 26–27 September has no competition because the available quiz course has no new lecture topics that week. 3–4 October covers Calculus Lectures 7–8. From 17 October, weekends revise the available block 1a pool; 7–8 November switches to the first week's topics in block 1b. A No competition scheduled card explains a gap: it does not substitute unrelated questions or offer an official run.

    All growth from earlier weekends is kept: scores, XP, EORbits, EOR Rating and honours. Events that have already started keep their original format, including the weekend of 19–20 September 2026. Historical Prerequisite Relay, Route Builder and Concept Match events keep their original rules and remain available for practice; they do not rotate into future weekends. Historical puzzle runs have five puzzles worth 20, 12 or 6 points for a first, second or third correct attempt, with hints after misses and zero for a skip or third miss. Their cards and controls still work with keyboard and touch.

    A Quiz Sprint run has ten questions, opened one at a time; only one can be open, and its clock starts when it appears. A correct answer earns 6, 3 or 1 points on the first, second or third attempt, plus 4, 3, 2 or 1 more for answering within 10, 20, 30 or 45 seconds, so a quick first answer is worth 10. A wrong answer is crossed out and pauses you for 5 seconds, then 10; a third miss or a skip earns zero. After the run, or once the weekend closes, every question can be reviewed and practised.

    The board shows provisional leaders during play. Confirmed weekend champions appear only after closure and settlement, when at least two publicly eligible entrants have answered all ten questions with at least six correct. Historical puzzle events require all five puzzles completed and at least three solved. Highest scores share the title when tied. Weekend honours join your collection and shared competition record.

    Choose Mute this weekend to silence that event's wiki reminder, or use Add to my calendar (.ics) to download a file for your own calendar. Muting the wiki reminder does not remove a calendar event you already imported. A completed official puzzle or sprint question with a submitted answer also earns up to 8 XP and 8 EORbits, once each, up to five per UTC day. A normally completed duel or ghost race in which you submitted answers earns the same, up to five per UTC day. Correctness or winning is not required for participation credit. Skipping, practice, matches ended by leaving and matches with no result do not earn these completion rewards. The existing daily XP limits apply; these activities also count towards the existing once-per-day active-day credit. Rewards are confirmed and synced by the server.

    Keep a useful route forward

    When something gets in the way¶

    Too many search results?
    Search inside the course, or add its code and a specific term in Finder.
    A formula is showing raw LaTeX?
    Refresh once. If it persists, report the page and formula that failed to render.
    Two devices show different progress?
    Check that both use the intended account, open My → Account → Sync & learning data, and inspect the result. If history needs recovery, follow the offered review and backup steps.
    Not sure what to study next?
    Choose one uncertain concept from Course diagnostics, revisit its prerequisites and try an example. Rankings are not a study plan.
    Where is my public profile?
    Open My → My public profile. Use Edit profile to update your account details; check My → Account → Privacy and Arena's public-record setting to control sharing.
    Cannot find an invitation or weekend activity?
    Open My → Arena & weekend events. Pending invitations stay in Duels. You can preview the weekend calendar before connecting an account; connect to join an official run. Scheduled competitions open at their displayed time. If My is unavailable, enable Account in the startup settings and follow the offered Reload action.
    Why is there no competition, or only one course this weekend?
    Teaching-week quizzes use only that week's taught topics in courses with available quizzes. A course without new lectures contributes no new topics. If the combined pool has fewer than ten eligible questions, no competition opens. Check the course scope on the weekend card. Only semester 1 of 2026–2027 is scheduled so far.
    Will the new weekend format reset my progress?
    No. Earlier scores, XP, EORbits, EOR Rating and honours stay with you. Events that have already started keep their rules, and historical events remain available for practice. The new format changes future competitions.
