    A function with three local extrema¶

    Math I: Calculus · Mock exam, Question 3(b) · 14 points · Lectures 6–7

    Problem¶

    Let

    g(x)=e−x+ax,x∈[0,∞),g(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-x}+a\sqrt{x},\quad x\in[0,\infty),g(x)=e−x+ax​,x∈[0,∞),

    • (b1) Find g′(x)g'(x)g′(x) for x>0.x>0\text{.}x>0. Determine for which values of aaa a finite right derivative exists at 0.0\text{.}0. [4 points]
    • (b2) Determine all possible values of aaa under the stated assumption of three local extrema. [6 points]
    • (b3) Prove that x2+g(x1)>3/2.x_2+g(x_1)>3/2\text{.}x2​+g(x1​)>3/2. [4 points]

    What you need¶

    • One-sided derivative: the right difference quotient at an endpoint.
    • Exponential function: (e−h−1)/h→−1(\mathrm{e}^{-h}-1)/h\to-1(e−h−1)/h→−1 as h→0.h\to0\text{.}h→0.
    • Stationary point and local extremum, including extrema at an endpoint of the domain.
    • Monotone function: the sign of a derivative gives where a function increases or decreases.
