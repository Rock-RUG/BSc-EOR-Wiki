Interpretation¶

The limit superior and limit inferior describe where a sequence ends up in the long run, also when it does not converge: they are the eventual upper and lower bounds of its terms. Only the tail counts, so changing finitely many terms changes neither of them.

Key Results¶

  • The limit superior and limit inferior of a real sequence always exist in the extended real numbers [−∞,+∞][-\infty,+\infty][−∞,+∞].
  • A sequence converges if and only if its lim sup⁡\limsuplimsup and lim inf⁡\liminfliminf are equal and finite; otherwise, it's divergent.
