Topologically Equivalent Metrics¶

Definition¶

Two metrics dpd_pdp​ and dqd_qdq​ on a space XXX are termed (topologically) equivalent if they produce identical topology (the set of open subsets of XXX) on XXX, i.e., a subset E⊆XE \subseteq XE⊆X is open with respect to dpd_pdp​ if and only if it's open under dqd_qdq​. This relationship is expressed as dp∼dq.d_p \sim d_q.dp​∼dq​.

Interpretation¶

Two metrics are topologically equivalent if they determine the same collection of open sets. They may measure distances differently, but they agree on which sets are open, and therefore on every notion that is defined through open sets.

In the left panel, the round dpd_pdp​-ball Bdp(x,r)B_{d_p}(\boldsymbol{x},r)Bdp​​(x,r) is dpd_pdp​-open, and around its point y\boldsymbol{y}y fits a diamond-shaped dqd_qdq​-ball Bdq(y,δ)B_{d_q}(\boldsymbol{y},\delta)Bdq​​(y,δ), so y\boldsymbol{y}y is an interior point of that ball for dqd_qdq​ as well. The right panel does the same with the roles swapped. If this works around every point of every ball, a set is dpd_pdp​-open if and only if it is dqd_qdq​-open: the two metrics give the same topology.

The radius δ\deltaδ may depend on the point and on rrr; no fixed ratio between the metrics is required. This is the difference from strongly equivalent metrics: there dq≤b dpd_q\le b\,d_pdq​≤bdp​ gives Bdp(x,r/b)⊆Bdq(x,r)B_{d_p}(x,r/b)\subseteq B_{d_q}(x,r)Bdp​​(x,r/b)⊆Bdq​​(x,r) with the same factor everywhere, which is why strong equivalence implies topological equivalence. On Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn all ppp-metrics are strongly equivalent, so "open" in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn does not depend on which of them is used.

Topologically equivalent metrics have the same convergent sequences. A common slip is to assume that they also agree on how large distances are. Key Results¶

  • If two metrics are strongly equivalent, then they are topologically equivalent.

Examples¶

  • On the interval (−π/2,π/2),(-\pi/2,\pi/2)\text{,}(−π/2,π/2), the metric dtan⁡(x,y)=∣tan⁡x−tan⁡y∣d_{\tan}(x,y)=|\tan x-\tan y|dtan​(x,y)=∣tanx−tany∣ is topologically equivalent to the absolute value metric, but they are not strongly equivalent: the ratio ∣tan⁡x−tan⁡y∣/∣x−y∣|\tan x-\tan y|/|x-y|∣tanx−tany∣/∣x−y∣ is unbounded near ±π/2.\pm\pi/2\text{.}±π/2.
  • On Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, the discrete metric is not topologically equivalent to any ppp-metric: every singleton is open for the discrete metric, but not for a ppp-metric.
