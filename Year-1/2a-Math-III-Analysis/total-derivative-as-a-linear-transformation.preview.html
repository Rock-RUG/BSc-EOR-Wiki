Total Derivative as a Linear Transformation¶

Definition¶

Let E⊆Rn\boldsymbol{E}\subseteq \mathbb{R}^nE⊆Rn, let f ⁣:E→Rk\boldsymbol{f}\colon \boldsymbol{E}\to\mathbb{R}^kf:E→Rk be a function, let x0∈intE\boldsymbol{x}_0\in\mathrm{int}\boldsymbol{E}x0​∈intE, and let L ⁣:Rn→Rk\boldsymbol{L}\colon \mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^kL:Rn→Rk be a linear transformation. We say that f\boldsymbol{f}f is (totally) differentiable at x=x0\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_{\boldsymbol{0}}x=x0​ with (total) derivative L\boldsymbol{L}L if:

lim⁡x→x0∥f(x)−(f(x0)+L(x−x0))∥∥x−x0∥=0.\lim_{\substack{\boldsymbol{x}\to\boldsymbol{x}_0}}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x})-(\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_0)+\boldsymbol{L}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_0))\|}{\|\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{x}_0\|}=0.x→x0​​lim​∥x−x0​∥∥f(x)−(f(x0​)+L(x−x0​))∥​=0.

Interpretation¶

Total differentiability means the function can be approximated near the point by a linear transformation, with the error shrinking to zero faster than the distance to the point.

It is the multidimensional version of the Newton's approximation, which is an equivalent formulation of differentiability.

Key Results¶

  • For the function f ⁣:Rn→Rk\boldsymbol{f}\colon \mathbb{R}^n\to\mathbb{R}^kf:Rn→Rk at x=x0\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_0x=x0​, its derivative, Dfx0\mathrm{D} \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{x}_0}Dfx0​​, is a k×nk\times nk×n matrix or an equivalent linear transformation from Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn to Rk\mathbb{R}^kRk.
  • Dfx0(h)\mathrm{D} \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{x}_0}(\boldsymbol{h})Dfx0​​(h), where h\boldsymbol{h}h is an infinitesimal vector, provides an approximation of the shift in f\boldsymbol{f}f (a vector in Rk\mathbb{R}^kRk) arising from the change h\boldsymbol{h}h in x\boldsymbol{x}x around x=x0\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_0x=x0​ (a vector in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn).
