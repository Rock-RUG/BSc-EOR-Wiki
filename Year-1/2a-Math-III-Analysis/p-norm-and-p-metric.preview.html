Interpretation¶

The ppp-norm is a way of measuring the size of a vector by raising each coordinate's absolute value to the ppp-th power, summing them, and taking the ppp-th root. The exponent decides how much weight the large coordinates get: p=1p=1p=1 adds all coordinates equally, p=2p=2p=2 gives the Euclidean length, and as p→+∞p\to+\inftyp→+∞ only the largest coordinate counts.

As ppp increases, these balls expand from the diamond towards the square, because ∥x∥q≤∥x∥p\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_q\le\|\boldsymbol{x}\|_p∥x∥q​≤∥x∥p​ whenever p≤qp\le qp≤q. All four boundaries pass through (±1,0)(\pm1,0)(±1,0) and (0,±1)(0,\pm1)(0,±1): a vector with a single nonzero coordinate has the same ppp-norm for every ppp.
