Directional Derivative¶

Definition¶

Consider a subset E⊆Rn\boldsymbol{E} \subseteq \mathbb{R}^nE⊆Rn and a function f ⁣:E→Rk\boldsymbol{f}\colon \boldsymbol{E}\to\mathbb{R}^kf:E→Rk. Interpretation¶

The directional derivative measures the instantaneous rate of change of a function as you move from a point in a specific direction.

It generalizes the one-sided derivative to functions of several variables by looking at how the function changes along a straight line in that direction.

Key Results¶

  • The directional derivative, Dvf(x0)\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{v}}\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{x}_0)Dv​f(x0​), is equal to the total derivative at v\boldsymbol vv, Dfx0(v)\mathrm{D} \boldsymbol{f}_{\boldsymbol{x}_0}(\boldsymbol{v})Dfx0​​(v).
  • A function is totally differentiable at a point implies that this function is directionally differentiable in any direction. The converse is incorrect.
