    Complex Plane (Cartesian Coordinate System)¶

    Definition¶

    The complex plane or z\boldsymbol{z}z-plane is the plane formed by the complex numbers, with a Cartesian coordinate system such that the horizontal xxx-axis, called the real axis, is formed by the real numbers, and the vertical yyy-axis, called the imaginary axis, is formed by the purely imaginary numbers. The form a+iba+\mathrm i ba+ib is called the Cartesian form of a complex number.

    Interpretation¶

    The complex plane can be seen as the Cartesian plane R2\mathbb R^2R2 equipped with a number of additional structures (e.g., complex multiplication).

    Key Results¶

    • Each complex number a+iba+\mathrm i ba+ib corresponds to unique coordinates (a,b).(a,b).(a,b).
    • In the Cartesian coordinate system, the addition of complex numbers can be visualized as vector addition.

    Examples¶

    • 1+i1+i1+i and 2−i2-i2−i are elements in the complex plane C\mathbb CC.
