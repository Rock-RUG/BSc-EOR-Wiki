Root Test¶

Proposition¶

Consider the series ∑n=1∞an\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_nn=1∑∞​an​ where an∈R.a_n \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}an​∈R. Let L=lim sup⁡n→∞∣an∣n\displaystyle L = \limsup_{n \to \infty} \sqrt[n]{|a_n|}L=n→∞limsup​n∣an​∣​, then:

  • If L<1,L < 1\text{,}L<1, the series converges. Moreover, ∑n=1∞∣an∣\displaystyle \sum_{n=1}^\infty |a_n|n=1∑∞​∣an​∣ also converges.
  • If L>1,L > 1\text{,}L>1, the series diverges.
  • If L=1,L = 1\text{,}L=1, the test is inconclusive.

Interpretation¶

The root test determines convergence by comparing the limit of the nnn-th root of the terms' absolute values to 1.

Key Results¶

  • One of the differences between the ratio test and the root test is that the limit in the ratio test may not exist, while the limit in the root test always exists.
  • The radius of convergence of a power series ∑n=0∞cn(x−a)n\displaystyle \sum_{n=0}^\infty c_n (x-a)^nn=0∑∞​cn​(x−a)n is given by
R=1lim sup⁡n→∞∣cn∣1/n.R=\frac{1}{\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty} |c_n|^{1/n}}.R=n→∞limsup​∣cn​∣1/n1​.
