Gradient¶

Definition¶

Let E⊆Rn\boldsymbol{E}\subseteq \mathbb{R}^nE⊆Rn, and let f ⁣:E→Rf \colon \boldsymbol{E} \to \mathbb{R}f:E→R be a real-valued function. If x0\boldsymbol{x}_0x0​ is an interior point of E\boldsymbol{E}E, the gradient ∇f(x0)\nabla f (\boldsymbol{x}_0)∇f(x0​) of fff at x=x0\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_0x=x0​ is given as the nnn-dimensional column vector:

∇f(x0)=(∂f∂x1(x0), ∂f∂x2(x0), …, ∂f∂xn(x0))⊺.\nabla f (\boldsymbol{x}_0) = \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_1}(\boldsymbol{x}_0),\, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_2}(\boldsymbol{x}_0),\, \ldots,\, \frac{\partial f}{\partial x_n}(\boldsymbol{x}_0)\right)^\intercal.∇f(x0​)=(∂x1​∂f​(x0​),∂x2​∂f​(x0​),…,∂xn​∂f​(x0​))⊺.

Interpretation¶

When ∇f(x0)=0\nabla f (\boldsymbol{x}_0)= \boldsymbol{0}∇f(x0​)=0, we say that x0\boldsymbol{x}_0x0​ is a stationary point of fff (possibly a local extremum point).

When ∇f(x0)≠0\nabla f (\boldsymbol{x}_0)\neq \boldsymbol{0}∇f(x0​)=0, the gradient offers two insights: the maximum value of Duf(x0)\mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{u}}f(\boldsymbol{x}_0)Du​f(x0​) and the direction u\boldsymbol{u}u where this maximum occurs.

Key Results¶

  • The gradient can be perceived as a vector whose dot product with any unit vector v\boldsymbol{v}v equals the directional derivative of fff at x=x0\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{x}_0x=x0​ along v\boldsymbol{v}v, i.e.,
∇f(x0)⋅v=Dvf(x0).\nabla f (\boldsymbol{x}_0) \cdot \boldsymbol{v} = \mathrm{D}_{\boldsymbol{v}}f(\boldsymbol{x}_0).∇f(x0​)⋅v=Dv​f(x0​).
