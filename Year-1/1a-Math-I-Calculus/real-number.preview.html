Real Number¶

Definition¶

The set of all real numbers, denoted by R,\mathbb{R}\text{,}R, is the complete ordered number system represented by the real number line. It contains the rational numbers, obeys the usual arithmetic and order rules, and has no gaps.

Completeness distinguishes R\mathbb{R}R from Q.\mathbb{Q}\text{.}Q. In this course it appears through the existence of suprema and through convergence theorems such as the monotone convergence theorem.

Each enlargement of the number systems removes a limitation of the previous one:

N⊂Z⊂Q⊂R.\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{Z}\subset\mathbb{Q}\subset\mathbb{R}.N⊂Z⊂Q⊂R.

Natural numbers support counting, integers support unrestricted subtraction, rationals support division by nonzero numbers, and real numbers fill the gaps on the number line.

Interpretation¶

Math I: Calculus uses R\mathbb{R}R and its arithmetic and order directly. Real numbers fill the gaps left by the rationals, allowing limits and geometric quantities to be represented exactly.

They allow limits and convergence to be treated as exact numerical objects.

Key Results¶

  • Every nonempty subset of R\mathbb{R}R that is bounded above has exactly one least upper bound; this existence statement is the defining advantage of R\mathbb{R}R over Q.\mathbb{Q}\text{.}Q.
  • Every real number is either rational or irrational.
  • The order on R\mathbb{R}R satisfies trichotomy and is preserved by addition and by multiplication with a positive number.
  • Between any two distinct real numbers lies a rational number; this is the density of Q\mathbb{Q}Q in R.\mathbb{R}\text{.}R.
  • The Archimedean property says that for positive real numbers xxx and ε,\varepsilon\text{,}ε, there is a positive integer MMM with Mε>x.M\varepsilon>x\text{.}Mε>x.

Examples¶

  • 2\sqrt{2}2​ is a real number but not rational: no rational number squares to 2,2\text{,}2, yet a real number does.
  • π,\pi\text{,}π, the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter, is real but not rational.
  • The approximations 1.4, 1.41, 1.414, 1.4142,…1.4,\,1.41,\,1.414,\,1.4142,\ldots1.4,1.41,1.414,1.4142,… have squares approaching 2,2\text{,}2, but the number they approach is missing from Q.\mathbb{Q}\text{.}Q.
