Indefinite Integral¶

Definition¶

The set of all antiderivatives of a function fff is the indefinite integral of fff with respect to xxx and denoted by ∫f(x) dx.\displaystyle \int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\text{.}∫f(x)dx. If FFF is an antiderivative of f,f\text{,}f, then we have ∫f(x) dx=F(x)+C\displaystyle \int f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=F(x)+C∫f(x)dx=F(x)+C where CCC is any constant.

Interpretation¶

The indefinite integral collects all antiderivatives of fff in one expression. It is not a number but a family of functions, and on an interval the family is F+CF+CF+C: one antiderivative shifted by every constant CCC.

The notation deliberately resembles the definite integral. By the fundamental theorem of calculus, any member of the family evaluates definite integrals, ∫abf(x) dx=F(b)−F(a)\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=F(b)-F(a)∫ab​f(x)dx=F(b)−F(a), and the constant CCC cancels in the difference. An equation such as ∫2x dx=x2+C\int 2x\,\mathrm{d}x=x^2+C∫2xdx=x2+C is an equality of families, and it is checked by differentiating the right-hand side.

Because the answer is a family, correct answers can look different. Both sin⁡2x+C\sin^2x+Csin2x+C and −cos⁡2x+C-\cos^2x+C−cos2x+C are correct for ∫2sin⁡xcos⁡x dx\int 2\sin x\cos x\,\mathrm{d}x∫2sinxcosxdx: differentiating either gives 2sin⁡xcos⁡x2\sin x\cos x2sinxcosx, and sin⁡2x−(−cos⁡2x)=1\sin^2x-(-\cos^2x)=1sin2x−(−cos2x)=1 is a constant.

The single constant CCC relies on the domain being an interval. For ∫dxx=ln⁡∣x∣+C\int\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{x}=\ln|x|+C∫xdx​=ln∣x∣+C the formula holds on (−∞,0)(-\infty,0)(−∞,0) and on (0,∞)(0,\infty)(0,∞) separately: the function equal to ln⁡∣x∣\ln|x|ln∣x∣ for x<0x<0x<0 and to ln⁡∣x∣+1\ln|x|+1ln∣x∣+1 for x>0x>0x>0 also has derivative 1/x1/x1/x at every x≠0x\ne0x=0.

Later, the rules of substitution and integration by parts help to find antiderivatives that cannot be read off directly.

Key Results¶

  • Indefinite integrals are defined up to an additive constant.
  • Differentiation recovers the original integrand from any antiderivative.

Examples¶

  • ∫2x dx=x2+C.\displaystyle\int 2x\,\mathrm{d}x = x^2 + C\text{.}∫2xdx=x2+C.
  • ∫cos⁡x dx=sin⁡x+C.\displaystyle \int \cos x\,\mathrm{d}x = \sin x + C\text{.}∫cosxdx=sinx+C.
