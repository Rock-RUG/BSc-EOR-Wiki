Reciprocal¶

Definition¶

The reciprocal of a nonzero rational number x,x\text{,}x, written x−1,x^{-1}\text{,}x−1, is the unique rational number satisfying

x x−1=1.x\,x^{-1}=1.xx−1=1.

If x=a/bx=a/bx=a/b with a,b∈Z∖{0},a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}\text{,}a,b∈Z∖{0}, then

(ab)−1=ba.\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{-1}=\frac{b}{a}.(ba​)−1=ab​.

The reciprocal of 000 is undefined. The same definition applies to nonzero real numbers.

Interpretation¶

The reciprocal of a number represents multiplicative inversion.

It formalises the idea of undoing multiplication and plays a central role in division and rational expressions.

Key Results¶

  • The reciprocal of a nonzero number is unique.
  • (x−1)−1=x(x^{-1})^{-1}=x(x−1)−1=x for every x≠0.x\neq0\text{.}x=0.
  • (xy)−1=x−1y−1(xy)^{-1}=x^{-1}y^{-1}(xy)−1=x−1y−1 when xxx and yyy are nonzero.
  • For positive numbers, taking reciprocals reverses order: if 0<x<y,0<x<y\text{,}0<x<y, then x−1>y−1.x^{-1}>y^{-1}\text{.}x−1>y−1.
  • Zero has no reciprocal.
