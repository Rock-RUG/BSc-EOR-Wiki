Subsequence¶

Definition¶

Where this appears in the course

Subsequences are not defined in the 2026-27 lectures. The definition is given inside the tutorial question that uses it (Tutorial 2), and this page records it in the same form.

Interpretation¶

A subsequence is what you get when you “thin out” a sequence while keeping the original order.

You are not allowed to reorder terms. You only choose some of them, further and further along the sequence.

Key Results¶

  • Every subsequence of a sequence uses terms from the original sequence, but with a new index set n1<n2<⋯.n_1<n_2<\cdots\text{.}n1​<n2​<⋯.
  • If an→L,a_n \to L\text{,}an​→L, then every subsequence anka_{n_k}ank​​ also converges to L.L\text{.}L.
  • Subsequence limits help detect non-convergence: if two subsequences converge to different limits, then the original sequence does not converge.
