Subsequence¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

A subsequence is what you get when you thin out a sequence while keeping the original order. You choose infinitely many of its terms, further and further along, and you may not reorder or repeat them.

Subsequences are the usual tool for proving divergence: two subsequences with different limits, as in the figure, show that (an)(a_n)(an​) has no limit. They also return in Math III, where the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem states that every bounded sequence has a convergent subsequence.

A common slip is to run the implication backwards. One convergent subsequence says nothing about the whole sequence: (−1)n(-1)^n(−1)n has the constant subsequence 1,1,1,…1,1,1,\dots1,1,1,… but diverges.

Key Results¶

  • Every subsequence of a sequence uses terms from the original sequence, but with a new index set n1<n2<⋯.n_1<n_2<\cdots\text{.}n1​<n2​<⋯.
  • If an→L,a_n \to L\text{,}an​→L, then every subsequence anka_{n_k}ank​​ also converges to L.L\text{.}L.
  • Subsequence limits help detect non-convergence: if two subsequences converge to different limits, then the original sequence does not converge.
