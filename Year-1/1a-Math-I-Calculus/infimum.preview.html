Infimum¶

Definition¶

Let E⊆RE\subseteq\mathbb{R}E⊆R be nonempty and bounded below. The infimum of EEE is its greatest lower bound, denoted by inf⁡E.\inf E\text{.}infE.

Interpretation¶

The infimum captures the greatest value that does not exceed any element of a set.

It provides a precise way to describe lower bounds, even when no minimum exists.

Key Results¶

  • By the greatest-lower-bound property of R,\mathbb{R}\text{,}R, every nonempty subset of R\mathbb{R}R that is bounded below has a unique infimum.
  • An infimum need not belong to the set; it is a minimum exactly when it is attained.
  • In the extended real numbers, one uses the conventions inf⁡E=−∞\inf E=-\inftyinfE=−∞ for a nonempty set with no lower bound and inf⁡∅=+∞;\inf\varnothing=+\infty\text{;}inf∅=+∞; neither +∞+\infty+∞ nor −∞-\infty−∞ is a real number.

Examples¶

  • The infimum of (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) is 0.0\text{.}0.
  • The infimum of {x∈R ⁣:x>2}\{x\in\mathbb{R}\colon x>2\}{x∈R:x>2} is 2.2\text{.}2.
