Periodic Function¶

Definition¶

Supplementary page

Periodicity is not a separate topic of Math I: Calculus. This page makes precise the word periodic used for the trigonometric functions and elsewhere on the wiki.

Let D⊆RD\subseteq\mathbb{R}D⊆R and let f ⁣:D→Rf\colon D\to\mathbb{R}f:D→R be a function. A number T>0T>0T>0 is a period of fff if, for every x∈Dx\in Dx∈D, also x+T∈Dx+T\in Dx+T∈D and x−T∈Dx-T\in Dx−T∈D, and

f(x+T)=f(x).f(x+T)=f(x).f(x+T)=f(x).

The function fff is periodic if it has a period. The smallest period, if one exists, is the fundamental period. The same definition is used for functions of a complex variable, where the period may be a nonzero complex number.

Interpretation¶

A periodic function repeats itself: its graph on any interval of length TTT determines the whole graph, by shifting copies TTT to the left and right. Periodic functions model cycles such as rotations, oscillations and seasonal patterns.

The shaded window [0,T][0,T][0,T] holds one period; every other part of the graph is a copy of it shifted by a multiple of TTT. Key Results¶

  • If TTT is a period of fff, then so is kTkTkT for every positive integer kkk. So "the period" usually means the fundamental period.
  • If TTT is a period of fff and a>0a>0a>0, then g(x)=f(ax)g(x)=f(ax)g(x)=f(ax) has period T/aT/aT/a, since g(x+T/a)=f(ax+T)=f(ax)g(x+T/a)=f(ax+T)=f(ax)g(x+T/a)=f(ax+T)=f(ax).
  • Sums and products of functions with a common period TTT also have period TTT, though their fundamental period can be smaller: sin⁡2x=12(1−cos⁡2x)\sin^2x=\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\cos2x)sin2x=21​(1−cos2x) has fundamental period π\piπ, not 2π2\pi2π.
  • A continuous periodic function on R\mathbb{R}R is bounded and attains its maximum and minimum: by the extreme value theorem it does so on [0,T][0,T][0,T], and every value is repeated there.
  • A periodic function need not have a fundamental period: a constant function has every T>0T>0T>0 as a period, and the Dirichlet function has every positive rational number as a period, but no smallest one.
