Rational Number¶

Definition¶

A rational number is a number of the form

ab,a,b∈Z,b≠0.\frac{a}{b}, \qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z},\quad b\neq0.ba​,a,b∈Z,b=0.

Two such fractions represent the same rational number exactly when

ab=cd⟺ad=bc,\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ad=bc,ba​=dc​⟺ad=bc,

where b,d≠0.b,d\neq0\text{.}b,d=0. The set of all rational numbers is denoted by Q.\mathbb{Q}\text{.}Q. Math I: Calculus takes Q\mathbb{Q}Q and its usual arithmetic as given.

Interpretation¶

Rational numbers represent ratios of integers and enlarge Z\mathbb{Z}Z so that division by a nonzero number is possible.

Every integer is rational because a=a/1,a=a/1\text{,}a=a/1, so Z⊊Q.\mathbb{Z}\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}\text{.}Z⊊Q.

Key Results¶

  • Every rational number has infinitely many representations.

  • For b,d≠0,b,d\neq0\text{,}b,d=0,

    ab+cd=ad+bcbd,abcd=acbd,−ab=−ab.\frac{a}{b}+\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ad+bc}{bd}, \qquad \frac{a}{b}\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ac}{bd}, \qquad -\frac{a}{b}=\frac{-a}{b}.ba​+dc​=bdad+bc​,ba​dc​=bdac​,−ba​=b−a​.

  • Rational numbers are closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication, and under division by a nonzero rational number.

  • Between any two distinct rational numbers lies another rational number; this is the density of Q\mathbb{Q}Q in itself.
