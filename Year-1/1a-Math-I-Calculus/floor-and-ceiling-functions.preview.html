Floor and Ceiling Functions¶

Definition¶

For a real number xxx, the floor of xxx is the largest integer less than or equal to xxx, and the ceiling of xxx is the smallest integer greater than or equal to xxx:

⌊x⌋=max⁡{n∈Z ⁣:n≤x},⌈x⌉=min⁡{n∈Z ⁣:n≥x}.\begin{aligned} \lfloor x\rfloor&=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon n\le x\},\\ \lceil x\rceil&=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon n\ge x\}. \end{aligned}⌊x⌋⌈x⌉​=max{n∈Z:n≤x},=min{n∈Z:n≥x}.​

Equivalently, for an integer nnn, ⌊x⌋=n\lfloor x\rfloor=n⌊x⌋=n exactly when n≤x<n+1n\le x<n+1n≤x<n+1, and ⌈x⌉=n\lceil x\rceil=n⌈x⌉=n exactly when n−1<x≤nn-1<x\le nn−1<x≤n. Both are functions from R\mathbb{R}R to Z\mathbb{Z}Z.

Interpretation¶

The floor rounds down and the ceiling rounds up to the nearest integer. On the number line, ⌊x⌋\lfloor x\rfloor⌊x⌋ is the integer at or immediately to the left of xxx and ⌈x⌉\lceil x\rceil⌈x⌉ the integer at or immediately to the right.

"Down" means towards −∞-\infty−∞, not towards 000. The name integer part usually means ⌊x⌋\lfloor x\rfloor⌊x⌋, but for negative numbers some texts instead mean truncation towards zero, which differs: ⌊−2.5⌋=−3\lfloor-2.5\rfloor=-3⌊−2.5⌋=−3, while truncation gives −2-2−2. For the same reason ⌊−x⌋\lfloor-x\rfloor⌊−x⌋ is not −⌊x⌋-\lfloor x\rfloor−⌊x⌋ unless xxx is an integer.

The graphs are staircases. On each interval [n,n+1)[n,n+1)[n,n+1) the floor is constant, so it is continuous at every non-integer; at each integer it jumps up by 111. This makes the floor a standard example for one-sided limits and one-sided continuity: the filled dot of each step sits at its left end, so the function agrees with its limit from the right.

The floor (solid blue) has its filled dots at the left ends of the steps; the ceiling (dashed red) is constant on each (n−1,n](n-1,n](n−1,n] and has its filled dots at the right ends, so it is left-continuous instead. At an integer nnn both take the value nnn. The thin grey line y=xy=xy=x runs between the two staircases, which is the inequality ⌊x⌋≤x≤⌈x⌉\lfloor x\rfloor\le x\le\lceil x\rceil⌊x⌋≤x≤⌈x⌉, and the vertical line at x=2.7x=2.7x=2.7 meets the floor at 222 and the ceiling at 333, as in the first example.

  • ⌈x⌉−⌊x⌋\lceil x\rceil-\lfloor x\rfloor⌈x⌉−⌊x⌋ is 000 if xxx is an integer and 111 otherwise.
  • ⌈x⌉=−⌊−x⌋\lceil x\rceil=-\lfloor-x\rfloor⌈x⌉=−⌊−x⌋, and ⌊x+n⌋=⌊x⌋+n\lfloor x+n\rfloor=\lfloor x\rfloor+n⌊x+n⌋=⌊x⌋+n for every integer nnn.
