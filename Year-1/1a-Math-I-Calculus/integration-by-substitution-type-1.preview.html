Integration by Substitution Type 1¶

Proposition¶

Let g ⁣:[α,β]→Rg\colon[\alpha,\beta]\to\mathbb{R}g:[α,β]→R be continuously differentiable, and let fff be continuous on an interval containing the range of g.g\text{.}g. Then

∫αβf(g(x))g′(x) dx=t=g(x)∫g(α)g(β)f(t) dt.\int_\alpha^\beta {\color{red}f(g(x))}{\color{blue}g'(x)\,\mathrm{d}x} \overset{t=g(x)}{=} \int_{g(\alpha)}^{g(\beta)}{\color{red}f(t)}\,{\color{blue}\mathrm{d}t}.∫αβ​f(g(x))g′(x)dx=t=g(x)∫g(α)g(β)​f(t)dt.

Interpretation¶

This version of substitution starts from proposing a new variable of the form t=g(x).t=g(x)\text{.}t=g(x).

It is particularly effective when a derivative component g′(x)g'(x)g′(x) already appears in the integrand, allowing a direct change of variables.

Key Results¶

  • The function ggg need not be invertible; the formula follows from the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus.
  • The limits of integration must be transformed together with the integrand, from α,β\alpha,\betaα,β to g(α),g(β).g(\alpha),g(\beta)\text{.}g(α),g(β).
  • If F′=f,F'=f\text{,}F′=f, then the corresponding indefinite identity is ∫f(g(x))g′(x) dx=F(g(x))+C.\displaystyle\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=F(g(x))+C\text{.}∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx=F(g(x))+C.
