Integral Test for Convergence¶

Proposition¶

Let f ⁣:[1,∞)→Rf\colon[1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}f:[1,∞)→R be continuous, nonnegative, and nonincreasing. Then

∑n=1∞f(n)and∫1∞f(x) dx\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(n) \quad\text{and}\quad \int_1^{\infty}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}xn=1∑∞​f(n)and∫1∞​f(x)dx

converge or diverge together. Moreover, the sequence

(∑i=1nf(i)−∫1nf(x) dx)n=1∞\left(\sum_{i=1}^n f(i)-\int_1^n f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}(i=1∑n​f(i)−∫1n​f(x)dx)n=1∞​

converges.

Interpretation¶

The integral test links the convergence of an infinite series to the convergence of a related improper integral.

It exploits the fact that a decreasing function can be compared with the areas of rectangles under its graph.

Key Results¶

  • The test applies only to continuous, nonnegative, nonincreasing functions.
  • Convergence of the improper integral implies convergence of the series.
  • Divergence of the improper integral implies divergence of the series.
  • The test provides a comparison, not the value of the sum.
