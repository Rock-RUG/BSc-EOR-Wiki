Interpretation¶

The monotone convergence theorem gives convergence from two facts that are often easy to check: the sequence never goes down, and it never rises above a fixed number MMM. It also names the limit, the supremum of the terms, so the limit need not be guessed in advance as in the definition of a limit.

The proof shows why the limit is the supremum. Given ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, the number sup⁡nan−ε\sup_n a_n-\varepsilonsupn​an​−ε is not an upper bound, so some aNa_NaN​ lies above it, and since the sequence is increasing, every later term lies between aNa_NaN​ and sup⁡nan\sup_n a_nsupn​an​. A decreasing sequence that is bounded below converges to its infimum.

A common slip is to take the bound MMM for the limit. The theorem only gives lim⁡n→∞an≤M\lim_{n\to\infty}a_n\le Mlimn→∞​an​≤M; the limit is the least upper bound.

Key Results¶

  • Every bounded monotone sequence is convergent.
  • The limit is given by the supremum or infimum (given a decreasing sequence with lower bound) of the sequence.
