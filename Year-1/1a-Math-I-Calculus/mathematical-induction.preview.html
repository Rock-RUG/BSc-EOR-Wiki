Mathematical Induction¶

Definition¶

Let P(n)P(n)P(n) be a statement about a natural number nnn. The principle of mathematical induction says: if

  1. base case: P(0)P(0)P(0) is true, and
  2. inductive step: for every natural number kkk, P(k)P(k)P(k) implies P(k+1)P(k+1)P(k+1),

then P(n)P(n)P(n) is true for every n∈Nn\in\mathbb{N}n∈N.

This is the fifth Peano axiom, where the successor S(k)S(k)S(k) is written k+1k+1k+1 once addition is available. A proof by induction checks the base case and proves the inductive step. In the step, kkk is an arbitrary but fixed natural number, and the assumption that P(k)P(k)P(k) holds is called the inductive hypothesis.

Interpretation¶

Induction works like a row of dominoes. The base case knocks over the first domino. The inductive step says that whichever domino falls, it knocks over the next one. Together they make every domino fall: P(0)P(0)P(0) gives P(1)P(1)P(1), P(1)P(1)P(1) gives P(2)P(2)P(2), and so on through all of N\mathbb{N}N.

The green arrow is the base case: it pushes P(0)P(0)P(0). Each blue arc is one instance of the inductive step, and the arc from P(k)P(k)P(k) to P(k+1)P(k+1)P(k+1) stands for all of them at once, because kkk is arbitrary. The green line underneath is the conclusion: P(n)P(n)P(n) holds for every natural number nnn.

Neither part works alone. A step without a base case is a chain of dominoes that nobody pushes, and a base case without a step knocks over only the first domino. Checking P(0),P(1),…,P(40)P(0),P(1),\ldots,P(40)P(0),P(1),…,P(40) one by one is still not a proof for every nnn.

The inductive step proves an implication. It does not claim that P(k)P(k)P(k) is true; it shows that P(k+1)P(k+1)P(k+1) would follow if P(k)P(k)P(k) were true. Assuming "P(n)P(n)P(n) for every nnn" in the step would assume the very statement to be proved.

Key Results¶

  • Set form. If A⊆NA\subseteq\mathbb{N}A⊆N contains 000, and k+1∈Ak+1\in Ak+1∈A whenever k∈Ak\in Ak∈A, then A=NA=\mathbb{N}A=N. Apply the principle to the statement "n∈An\in An∈A".
  • Starting at n0n_0n0​. If P(n0)P(n_0)P(n0​) is true and P(k)P(k)P(k) implies P(k+1)P(k+1)P(k+1) for every integer k≥n0k\ge n_0k≥n0​, then P(n)P(n)P(n) is true for every integer n≥n0n\ge n_0n≥n0​. Apply the principle to Q(m)Q(m)Q(m): "P(n0+m)P(n_0+m)P(n0​+m)". Statements about positive integers start at n0=1n_0=1n0​=1.
  • Both parts are needed. For P(n)P(n)P(n): "n2+n+1n^2+n+1n2+n+1 is even", the step holds, since (k+1)2+(k+1)+1=(k2+k+1)+2(k+1)(k+1)^2+(k+1)+1=(k^2+k+1)+2(k+1)(k+1)2+(k+1)+1=(k2+k+1)+2(k+1). But P(0)P(0)P(0) fails, and in fact n2+n+1n^2+n+1n2+n+1 is odd for every nnn. For P(n)P(n)P(n): "n2−n+41n^2-n+41n2−n+41 is prime", the cases n=0,1,…,40n=0,1,\ldots,40n=0,1,…,40 are all true, but n=41n=41n=41 gives 41241^2412.
  • Recursion and induction go together. The factorial is defined recursively: 0!=10!=10!=1, and (k+1)!=(k+1)×k!(k+1)!=(k+1)\times k!(k+1)!=(k+1)×k! for every k∈Nk\in\mathbb{N}k∈N. Statements about a recursively defined quantity are proved by induction: the value at 000 settles the base case, and the rule from kkk to k+1k+1k+1 drives the step. Powers (m0=1m^0=1m0=1, mk+1=mkmm^{k+1}=m^k mmk+1=mkm) and multiplication on N\mathbb{N}N (0⋅m=00\cdot m=00⋅m=0, S(n)⋅m=n⋅m+mS(n)\cdot m=n\cdot m+mS(n)⋅m=n⋅m+m) can be built the same way.
  • Several variables. For a statement about nnn and mmm, such as n⋅S(m)=n⋅m+nn\cdot S(m)=n\cdot m+nn⋅S(m)=n⋅m+n, fix mmm and induct on nnn: the base case is n=0n=0n=0 for that mmm, and the step goes from nnn to S(n)S(n)S(n) with the same mmm.
