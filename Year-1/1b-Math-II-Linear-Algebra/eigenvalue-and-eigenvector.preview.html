Eigenvalue and Eigenvector¶

Definition¶

For a linear operator or square matrix A\boldsymbol{A}A, a nonzero vector p\boldsymbol{p}p and scalar λ\lambdaλ satisfying Ap=λp\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}=\lambda \boldsymbol{p}Ap=λp give an eigenvector p\boldsymbol{p}p and corresponding eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ.

Interpretation¶

For a real eigenvector with a real eigenvalue, the line it spans is invariant under the transformation. A positive eigenvalue preserves its direction, a negative eigenvalue reverses it, and a zero eigenvalue maps it to the zero vector. The equation Ap=λp\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}=\lambda \boldsymbol{p}Ap=λp also defines eigenvectors and eigenvalues over the complex numbers.

Ap=λp\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}=\lambda \boldsymbol{p}Ap=λp with p≠0\boldsymbol{p}\neq\boldsymbol{0}p=0: A\boldsymbol{A}A only rescales p\boldsymbol{p}p, by the eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ. Here A=(2112)\boldsymbol{A}=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}A=(21​12​) has λ1=3\lambda_1=3λ1​=3 on p1=(11)\boldsymbol{p}_1=\binom{1}{1}p1​=(11​) and λ2=1\lambda_2=1λ2​=1 on p2=(1−1)\boldsymbol{p}_2=\binom{1}{-1}p2​=(−11​); a vector v\boldsymbol{v}v off these lines is turned.

The vector space supplies both the vectors and the scalar multiplication in Ap=λp\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{p}=\lambda\boldsymbol{p}Ap=λp; this equation compares two vectors in that same space.

Key Results¶

  • The zero vector is excluded from being an eigenvector.
  • Eigenvalues of triangular or diagonal matrices are their diagonal entries.
  • For a real-valued matrix, nonreal complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors occur in complex conjugate pairs.

  • For A=(2003)\boldsymbol{A}=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&3\end{pmatrix}A=(20​03​), (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 222.
  • For the same matrix, (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) is an eigenvector with eigenvalue 333.
