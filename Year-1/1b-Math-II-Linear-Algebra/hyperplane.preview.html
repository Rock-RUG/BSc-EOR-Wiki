Hyperplane¶

Definition¶

Let VVV be a vector space of dimension n≥1n\ge1n≥1.

  • A linear hyperplane of VVV is a subspace HHH of dimension n−1n-1n−1.
  • An affine hyperplane of VVV is a translate p+H={p+h ⁣:h∈H}p+H=\{p+h\colon h\in H\}p+H={p+h:h∈H} of a linear hyperplane HHH, where p∈Vp\in Vp∈V.

In Rn\mathbb R^nRn the affine hyperplanes are exactly the sets

{x∈Rn ⁣:a1x1+⋯+anxn=d}with a=(a1,…,an)≠0,\begin{gathered} \{x\in\mathbb R^n\colon a_1x_1+\cdots+a_nx_n=d\}\\ \text{with }a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\neq0, \end{gathered}{x∈Rn:a1​x1​+⋯+an​xn​=d}with a=(a1​,…,an​)=0,​

and such a hyperplane is linear exactly when d=0d=0d=0, that is, when it passes through the origin. Used without a qualifier in Rn\mathbb R^nRn, "hyperplane" usually means an affine hyperplane; some linear algebra texts reserve it for linear hyperplanes, so the qualifier is worth stating.

Interpretation¶

Hyperplanes generalize lines in R2\mathbb R^2R2 and planes in R3\mathbb R^3R3.

Key Results¶

  • The vector a=(a1,…,an)a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)a=(a1​,…,an​) is a normal vector of the hyperplane a1x1+⋯+anxn=da_1x_1+\cdots+a_nx_n=da1​x1​+⋯+an​xn​=d: it is orthogonal to every difference x−yx-yx−y of two of its points.
  • An affine hyperplane a⋅x=da\cdot x=da⋅x=d divides Rn\mathbb R^nRn into the two closed half-spaces {x ⁣:a⋅x≤d}\{x\colon a\cdot x\le d\}{x:a⋅x≤d} and {x ⁣:a⋅x≥d}\{x\colon a\cdot x\ge d\}{x:a⋅x≥d}, which meet exactly in the hyperplane.
  • The hyperplanes of R2\mathbb R^2R2 are the lines and those of R3\mathbb R^3R3 are the planes.

Examples¶

  • x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1 is an affine hyperplane of R2\mathbb R^2R2 that is not linear, since it misses the origin.
  • x+y+z=0x+y+z=0x+y+z=0 is a linear hyperplane of R3\mathbb R^3R3.
  • The xyxyxy-plane {(x,y,z) ⁣:z=0}\{(x,y,z)\colon z=0\}{(x,y,z):z=0} is a linear hyperplane of R3\mathbb R^3R3.
