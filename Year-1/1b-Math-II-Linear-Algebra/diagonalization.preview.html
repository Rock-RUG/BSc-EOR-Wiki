Diagonalization¶

Definition¶

Diagonalization is the process of finding an invertible matrix PPP and a diagonal matrix DDD such that A=PDP−1A=PDP^{-1}A=PDP−1, equivalently P−1AP=DP^{-1}AP=DP−1AP=D.

Interpretation¶

Diagonalization rewrites a linear transformation in an eigenbasis, where its action is simple coordinate-wise scaling.

P−1P^{-1}P−1 changes to the eigenvector basis, D=diag⁡(λ1,…,λn)D=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n)D=diag(λ1​,…,λn​) only scales, PPP changes back: A=PDP−1A=PDP^{-1}A=PDP−1 with the eigenvectors as columns of PPP and the eigenvalues on the diagonal of DDD. Hence Ak=PDkP−1A^k=PD^kP^{-1}Ak=PDkP−1, and AAA is diagonalizable   ⟺  \iff⟺ it has a basis of eigenvectors.

Key Results¶

  • If A=PDP−1A=PDP^{-1}A=PDP−1, then Ak=PDkP−1A^k=PD^kP^{-1}Ak=PDkP−1.
  • The columns of PPP are eigenvectors of AAA, and the diagonal entries of DDD are the corresponding eigenvalues.

Examples¶

  • A diagonal matrix is already diagonalized with P=IP=IP=I.
  • If AAA has a basis of eigenvectors, then it is diagonalizable.
