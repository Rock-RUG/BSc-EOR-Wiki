Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality¶

Theorem¶

For vectors x\boldsymbol{x}x and y\boldsymbol{y}y in an inner product space, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality states ∣⟨x,y⟩∣≤∥x∥∥y∥|\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\rangle|\leq\|\boldsymbol{x}\|\|\boldsymbol{y}\|∣⟨x,y⟩∣≤∥x∥∥y∥; in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn with the dot product this reads ∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥|\boldsymbol{x}\cdot \boldsymbol{y}|\leq\|\boldsymbol{x}\|\|\boldsymbol{y}\|∣x⋅y∣≤∥x∥∥y∥.

Interpretation¶

The inner product of two vectors can never exceed the product of their lengths in absolute value. Equivalently, for nonzero vectors the quotient x⋅y∥x∥ ∥y∥\dfrac{\boldsymbol{x}\cdot \boldsymbol{y}}{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|\,\|\boldsymbol{y}\|}∥x∥∥y∥x⋅y​ always lies between −1-1−1 and 111.

The figure drops a perpendicular from the tip of y\boldsymbol{y}y onto the line of the nonzero vector x\boldsymbol{x}x. The green segment from the origin to its foot has length ∣x⋅y∣∥x∥\dfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}\cdot \boldsymbol{y}|}{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|}∥x∥∣x⋅y∣​, and as a side of a right triangle with hypotenuse y\boldsymbol{y}y it ends inside the arc of radius ∥y∥\|\boldsymbol{y}\|∥y∥. Multiplying ∣x⋅y∣∥x∥≤∥y∥\dfrac{|\boldsymbol{x}\cdot \boldsymbol{y}|}{\|\boldsymbol{x}\|}\le\|\boldsymbol{y}\|∥x∥∣x⋅y∣​≤∥y∥ by ∥x∥\|\boldsymbol{x}\|∥x∥ gives the inequality; equality holds when y\boldsymbol{y}y already lies on the line of x\boldsymbol{x}x, so that the segment reaches the arc.

The inequality holds for all vectors, including 0\boldsymbol{0}0, where both sides are 000. Equality holds if and only if one vector is a multiple of the other, that is, when both lie on one line through the origin. The absolute value matters: x⋅(−x)=−∥x∥2\boldsymbol{x}\cdot(-\boldsymbol{x})=-\|\boldsymbol{x}\|^2x⋅(−x)=−∥x∥2 is very negative for long x\boldsymbol{x}x; only the size of the inner product is bounded.

The inequality is what makes the angle between vectors well defined in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, since a cosine must lie in [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1], and it is the key step in proving the triangle inequality ∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥\|\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{y}\|\le\|\boldsymbol{x}\|+\|\boldsymbol{y}\|∥x+y∥≤∥x∥+∥y∥. Its proof uses only the defining properties of an inner product, so it holds for every inner product with ∥x∥=⟨x,x⟩\|\boldsymbol{x}\|=\sqrt{\langle\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{x}\rangle}∥x∥=⟨x,x⟩​. It returns later in a version for random variables.

Key Results¶

  • Cauchy-Schwarz justifies the formula for the angle between vectors.
  • It can be used to prove the triangle inequality.

Examples¶

  • For orthogonal vectors, the left side is 000.
  • For x=y\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{y}x=y, equality holds.
