Euler's Formula¶

Theorem¶

For any z∈Cz\in\mathbb{C}z∈C, we have eiz=cos⁡z+isin⁡z.\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}z}=\cos z+\mathrm{i}\sin z.eiz=cosz+isinz.

Interpretation¶

Euler's formula connects complex exponentials to trigonometry.

Key Results¶

  • We have ∣ez∣=eRe⁡(z)|\mathrm e^z| = \mathrm e^{\operatorname{Re}(z)}∣ez∣=eRe(z) and arg⁡(ez)=Im⁡(z)+2πk.\arg(\mathrm e^z) = \operatorname{Im}(z) + 2\pi k .arg(ez)=Im(z)+2πk.
  • We have cos⁡θ=eiθ+e−iθ2\cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta} + \mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\theta}}{2}cosθ=2eiθ+e−iθ​ and sin⁡θ=eiθ−e−iθ2i.\sin\theta = \dfrac{\mathrm e^{\mathrm i\theta} - \mathrm e^{-\mathrm i\theta}}{2\mathrm i}.sinθ=2ieiθ−e−iθ​.

Examples¶

  • When z=πz=\piz=π, we get the well-known Euler's identity.
