Eigenvalue-Root Theorem¶

Theorem¶

For any square matrix AAA, the eigenvalues of AAA are exactly the roots of its characteristic polynomial det⁡(A−λI)\det(A-\lambda I)det(A−λI).

Interpretation¶

The eigenvalue equation Ap=λpAp=\lambda pAp=λp is equivalent to the singularity of A−λIA-\lambda IA−λI.

Key Results¶

  • λ\lambdaλ is an eigenvalue iff det⁡(A−λI)=0\det(A-\lambda I)=0det(A−λI)=0.
  • Every eigenvalue has at least one corresponding eigenvector.

Examples¶

  • For a diagonal matrix, the roots are precisely the diagonal entries.
  • For A=(1−221)A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}A=(12​−21​), the eigenvalues are 1±2i1\pm2\mathrm i1±2i.
