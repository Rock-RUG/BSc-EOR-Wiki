Distributivity¶

Definition¶

Let ⋆\star⋆ and ∘\circ∘ be binary operations on a set AAA. The operation ⋆\star⋆ is left distributive over ∘\circ∘ if

a⋆(b∘c)=(a⋆b)∘(a⋆c)a\star(b\circ c)=(a\star b)\circ(a\star c)a⋆(b∘c)=(a⋆b)∘(a⋆c)

for all a,b,c∈Aa,b,c\in Aa,b,c∈A, and right distributive over ∘\circ∘ if

(a∘b)⋆c=(a⋆c)∘(b⋆c)(a\circ b)\star c=(a\star c)\circ(b\star c)(a∘b)⋆c=(a⋆c)∘(b⋆c)

for all a,b,c∈Aa,b,c\in Aa,b,c∈A. It is distributive over ∘\circ∘ if it is both left and right distributive.

Interpretation¶

Distributivity is the rule for multiplying out brackets, and read backwards, for factoring out a common factor. It is the law that connects two operations, whereas associativity and commutativity each concern a single operation.

The figure reads the first example, 3(4+5)=3⋅4+3⋅5=273(4+5)=3\cdot4+3\cdot5=273(4+5)=3⋅4+3⋅5=27, as areas. A rectangle of height 333 and width 4+5=94+5=94+5=9 has area 3(4+5)3(4+5)3(4+5); cutting it where the width 444 ends splits it into rectangles of areas 3⋅4=123\cdot4=123⋅4=12 and 3⋅5=153\cdot5=153⋅5=15. Multiplying out the brackets is this cut, and factoring out the common factor 333 is gluing the two rectangles back together.

If ⋆\star⋆ is commutative, left and right distributivity are equivalent. For a non-commutative operation such as the matrix product of Week 3, both laws must be stated.

The order of the two operations matters: "⋆\star⋆ distributes over ∘\circ∘" is a different statement from "∘\circ∘ distributes over ⋆\star⋆". Multiplication of real numbers distributes over addition, but addition does not distribute over multiplication. Many algebra slips come from applying a distributive law that does not hold, such as splitting c/(a+b)c/(a+b)c/(a+b) into c/a+c/bc/a+c/bc/a+c/b.
