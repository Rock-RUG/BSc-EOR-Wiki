Fundamental Theorem of Linear Maps¶

Theorem¶

Let VVV be a finite-dimensional vector space and let A∈L(V,W)A \in \mathcal{L}(V,W)A∈L(V,W) be a linear map. Then

dim⁡(ker⁡A)+dim⁡(range⁡A)=dim⁡V.\dim(\ker A) + \dim(\operatorname{range} A) = \dim V.dim(kerA)+dim(rangeA)=dimV.

Interpretation¶

A linear map splits the dimension of its domain into directions lost to the kernel and directions visible in the range.

Key Results¶

  • The theorem is also called the rank-nullity theorem.
  • It connects injectivity, surjectivity, and dimension for finite-dimensional vector spaces.

Examples¶

  • If A:R3→R2A:\mathbb R^3\to\mathbb R^2A:R3→R2 has a one-dimensional kernel, then its range has dimension 222.
  • If A∈L(V)A\in\mathcal L(V)A∈L(V) and ker⁡A={0}\ker A=\{0\}kerA={0}, then AAA has full range and is bijective.
