Definite Matrix¶

Definition¶

Let A\boldsymbol{A}A be a real symmetric matrix. The definiteness of A\boldsymbol{A}A classifies the sign of the quadratic form x⊤Ax\boldsymbol{x}^{\top} \boldsymbol{A} \boldsymbol{x}x⊤Ax:

  • Positive definite: x⊤Ax>0\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}>0x⊤Ax>0 for every nonzero vector x\boldsymbol{x}x.
  • Positive semidefinite: x⊤Ax≥0\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\ge 0x⊤Ax≥0 for every vector x\boldsymbol{x}x.
  • Negative definite: x⊤Ax<0\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}<0x⊤Ax<0 for every nonzero vector x\boldsymbol{x}x.
  • Negative semidefinite: x⊤Ax≤0\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\le 0x⊤Ax≤0 for every vector x\boldsymbol{x}x.
  • Indefinite: x⊤Ax\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}x⊤Ax takes both positive and negative values.

Interpretation¶

Definiteness tells whether the quadratic form determined by a symmetric matrix is always positive, always nonnegative, always negative, always nonpositive, or changes sign. It is the matrix version of the sign of aaa in ax2ax^2ax2.

The definition quantifies over all nonzero x\boldsymbol{x}x, not only the axes. Testing the standard basis vectors gives ei⊤Aei=aii\boldsymbol{e}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{e}_i=a_{ii}ei⊤​Aei​=aii​, so a positive definite matrix has positive diagonal entries. The converse fails: (1221)\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{pmatrix}(12​21​) has a positive diagonal, but the value 1−4+1=−21-4+1=-21−4+1=−2 at x=(1,−1)\boldsymbol{x}=(1,-1)x=(1,−1), so it is indefinite. The categories overlap: every positive definite matrix is also positive semidefinite, and the zero matrix is both positive and negative semidefinite.

In one variable, the second-order condition reads the sign of f′′(x0)f''(x_0)f′′(x0​) at a stationary point. For functions of several variables, a symmetric matrix of second derivatives takes the place of f′′(x0)f''(x_0)f′′(x0​), and its definiteness separates local minima, maxima and saddle points.

Key Results¶

  • A symmetric matrix is positive definite if and only if all its eigenvalues are positive.
  • A symmetric matrix is positive semidefinite if and only if all its eigenvalues are nonnegative.
  • A symmetric matrix is negative definite if and only if all its eigenvalues are negative.
  • A symmetric matrix is negative semidefinite if and only if all its eigenvalues are nonpositive.
  • A symmetric matrix is indefinite if and only if it has both positive and negative eigenvalues.
