Algebraic and Geometric Multiplicity¶

Definition¶

If the characteristic polynomial factors as c(λ−λ1)k1⋯(λ−λl)klc(\lambda-\lambda_1)^{k_1}\cdots(\lambda-\lambda_l)^{k_l}c(λ−λ1​)k1​⋯(λ−λl​)kl​ with distinct λ1,…,λl\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_lλ1​,…,λl​, then kik_iki​ is the algebraic multiplicity of the eigenvalue λi\lambda_iλi​, written am(λi)am(\lambda_i)am(λi​).

The geometric multiplicity of an eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ is the maximal number of linearly independent eigenvectors associated with λ\lambdaλ, written gm(λ)gm(\lambda)gm(λ).

Interpretation¶

Algebraic multiplicity counts how often an eigenvalue appears as a root of the characteristic polynomial. Geometric multiplicity counts how many independent eigendirections it actually has.

Key Results¶

  • For every eigenvalue of an n×nn\times nn×n matrix, 1≤gm(λ)≤am(λ)≤n1\leq gm(\lambda)\leq am(\lambda)\leq n1≤gm(λ)≤am(λ)≤n.
  • A matrix is diagonalizable iff gm(λ)=am(λ)gm(\lambda)=am(\lambda)gm(λ)=am(λ) for every eigenvalue, the eigenvalues being taken in C\mathbb{C}C so that the characteristic polynomial factors completely. A real matrix is diagonalizable over R\mathbb{R}R only if, in addition, all its eigenvalues are real.
