De Moivre's Formula¶

Theorem¶

For any integer nnn and nonzero complex number z=r(cos⁡φ+isin⁡φ)z = r(\cos\varphi + \mathrm{i}\sin\varphi)z=r(cosφ+isinφ), with r>0r>0r>0, it holds that:

zn=rn(cos⁡(nφ)+isin⁡(nφ)).z^n = r^n(\cos(n\varphi) + \mathrm{i}\sin(n\varphi)).zn=rn(cos(nφ)+isin(nφ)).

For positive integers nnn, the identity also holds at z=0z=0z=0. Negative powers require z≠0z\ne0z=0.

Interpretation¶

To raise a complex number in polar form to an integer power nnn, raise its modulus to the power nnn and multiply its angle by nnn.

The figure shows the case r=1r=1r=1: the powers of cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ\cos\theta+\mathrm{i}\sin\thetacosθ+isinθ stay on the unit circle, and each multiplication adds the same angle θ\thetaθ, so the first, second and third powers lie at the angles θ\thetaθ, 2θ2\theta2θ and 3θ3\theta3θ. For r>1r>1r>1 the powers also move outwards and for r<1r<1r<1 inwards, which gives the spiral in the interactive version.

The formula follows from complex multiplication, which multiplies moduli and adds angles: multiplying zzz by itself nnn times gives modulus rnr^nrn and angle nφn\varphinφ. The formula is stated for integers nnn only. Key Results¶

  • The formula converts integer powers of a complex number into powers of its modulus and multiples of its argument.
  • It is closely related to Euler's formula.
