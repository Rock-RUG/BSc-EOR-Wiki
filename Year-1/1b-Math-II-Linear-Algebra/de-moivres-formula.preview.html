De Moivre's Formula¶

Theorem¶

For any integer nnn, and any complex number z=r(cos⁡φ+isin⁡φ)z = r(\cos\varphi + \mathrm{i}\sin\varphi)z=r(cosφ+isinφ), it holds that:

zn=rn(cos⁡(nφ)+isin⁡(nφ)).z^n = r^n(\cos(n\varphi) + \mathrm{i}\sin(n\varphi)).zn=rn(cos(nφ)+isin(nφ)).

Interpretation¶

To raise a complex number in polar form to an integer power nnn, raise its modulus to the power nnn and multiply its angle by nnn.

Key Results¶

  • The formula converts integer powers of a complex number into powers of its modulus and multiples of its argument.
  • It is closely related to Euler's formula.

Examples¶

  • (1+3i)8=28(cos⁡(8π/3)+isin⁡(8π/3))(1+\sqrt{3}\mathrm{i})^8=2^8(\cos(8\pi/3)+\mathrm{i}\sin(8\pi/3))(1+3​i)8=28(cos(8π/3)+isin(8π/3)).
  • (−1+i)4=2(cos⁡(3π)+isin⁡(3π))(-1+\mathrm{i})^4=2 (\cos(3\pi)+\mathrm{i}\sin(3\pi))(−1+i)4=2(cos(3π)+isin(3π)).
