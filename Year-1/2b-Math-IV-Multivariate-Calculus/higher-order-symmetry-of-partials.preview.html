Higher-Order Symmetry of Partials¶

Theorem¶

Let fff have continuous partial derivatives up to order kkk on an open neighbourhood of aaa. Then any two kkkth-order partial derivatives that differentiate with respect to the same variables the same number of times are equal at aaa.

For third-order derivatives this means, for example,

fxyz(a)=fxzy(a)=fyxz(a)=fyzx(a)=fzxy(a)=fzyx(a)f_{xyz}(a)=f_{xzy}(a)=f_{yxz}(a)=f_{yzx}(a)=f_{zxy}(a)=f_{zyx}(a)fxyz​(a)=fxzy​(a)=fyxz​(a)=fyzx​(a)=fzxy​(a)=fzyx​(a)

whenever the required third-order partial derivatives are continuous near aaa.

Interpretation¶

When the function is smooth enough, only the number of times each direction is used matters, not the order in which the differentiations are performed.

Key Results¶

  • The second-order case is the usual symmetry of mixed partial derivatives.
  • For a sufficiently smooth scalar function, the third derivative tensor is symmetric under permutations of its indices.
  • Without the needed continuity assumptions, mixed partials can fail to agree.

Examples¶

  • For f(x,y,z)=x2yz3f(x,y,z)=x^2yz^3f(x,y,z)=x2yz3, the derivatives fxyzf_{xyz}fxyz​, fxzyf_{xzy}fxzy​, and fzyxf_{zyx}fzyx​ agree because fff is a polynomial.
  • For f(x,y)=exyf(x,y)=e^{xy}f(x,y)=exy, fxxy=fxyx=fyxxf_{xxy}=f_{xyx}=f_{yxx}fxxy​=fxyx​=fyxx​ at every point because all partial derivatives are continuous.
