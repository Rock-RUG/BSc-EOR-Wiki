Method of Lagrange for Equality Constraints¶

Theorem¶

Let A⊆RnA\subseteq\mathbb R^nA⊆Rn be open. Consider

max⁡x∈Af(x)subject tohj(x)=cj, j=1,…,m,\max_{x\in A} f(x)\quad\text{subject to}\quad h_j(x)=c_j,\ j=1,\ldots,m,x∈Amax​f(x)subject tohj​(x)=cj​, j=1,…,m,

with C1C^1C1 data and m<nm<nm<n. Define

L(x,μ)=f(x)+∑j=1mμj(cj−hj(x)).L(x,\mu)=f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^m\mu_j(c_j-h_j(x)).L(x,μ)=f(x)+j=1∑m​μj​(cj​−hj​(x)).

If f(x∗)f(x^\ast)f(x∗) is a local maximum or minimum subject to the constraints, then either NDCQ fails at x∗x^\astx∗ or there exists μ∗∈Rm\mu^\ast\in\mathbb{R}^mμ∗∈Rm such that

∇L(x∗,μ∗)=0.\nabla L(x^\ast,\mu^\ast)=0.∇L(x∗,μ∗)=0.

Interpretation¶

A regular equality-constrained optimum must be a critical point of the Lagrangian. Nonregular feasible points are kept as candidates rather than thrown away.

Key Results¶

  • The theorem gives necessary, not sufficient, conditions.
  • Stationarity in the multiplier variables recovers the equality constraints.
  • Equality constraints can be written on either side because they have no direction.

Examples¶

  • Maximizing xyxyxy subject to x+y=10x+y=10x+y=10 gives L=xy+μ(10−x−y)L=xy+\mu(10-x-y)L=xy+μ(10−x−y).
  • Minimizing x2+y2x^2+y^2x2+y2 subject to x+y=1x+y=1x+y=1 uses the same equality-constrained stationarity method.
