Shadow Price¶

This page presents the Math IV: Multivariate Calculus treatment. See also the treatment in OR Modelling. Mastery is recorded separately for each course context.

Definition¶

In a constrained optimization problem, a shadow price is the marginal change in the optimal value when a constraint is relaxed slightly. Under the usual regularity assumptions and sign conventions, this marginal change is represented by the associated Lagrange multiplier.

For a constraint written as g(x)=bg(x)=bg(x)=b, one commonly has

dVdb=λ\frac{dV}{db}=\lambdadbdV​=λ

or the corresponding sign-adjusted version determined by the Lagrangian convention.

Interpretation¶

A shadow price measures how valuable a little extra room in a constraint would be at the optimum.

Key Results¶

  • A positive shadow price means relaxing the constraint raises the optimal value under the chosen convention.
  • A zero shadow price often corresponds to a nonbinding inequality constraint.
  • The sign depends on whether the constraint is written as a resource, residual, or equality condition.

Examples¶

  • If one more unit of a resource increases maximum profit by 333, the shadow price of that resource is 333.
  • If a budget constraint is slack, its shadow price is typically 000 because a small increase in the budget does not improve the optimum.
