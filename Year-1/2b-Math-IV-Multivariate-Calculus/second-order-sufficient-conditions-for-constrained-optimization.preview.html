Second-order Sufficient Conditions for Constrained Optimization¶

Theorem¶

Let L(x,λ,μ)L(x,\lambda,\mu)L(x,λ,μ) be the Lagrangian of a C2C^2C2 constrained problem. At a first-order candidate, collect the binding inequality constraints and all equality constraints in C(x)C(x)C(x), and let rrr be the number of active constraints. The relevant bordered Hessian is

H∗=(0rDC(x∗)DC(x∗)⊤Dxx2L(x∗,λ∗,μ∗)).H^\ast= \begin{pmatrix} 0_r & DC(x^\ast)\\ DC(x^\ast)^\top & D^2_{xx}L(x^\ast,\lambda^\ast,\mu^\ast) \end{pmatrix}.H∗=(0r​DC(x∗)⊤​DC(x∗)Dxx2​L(x∗,λ∗,μ∗)​).

Inspect the last n−rn-rn−r leading principal minors after ignoring the first 2r2r2r leading minors. The candidate is a strict local constrained maximizer if these minors alternate in sign and the last one has sign (−1)n(-1)^n(−1)n. It is a strict local constrained minimizer if these minors all have sign (−1)r(-1)^r(−1)r.

Interpretation¶

The bordered Hessian checks curvature only along feasible directions. It is the constrained counterpart of using the Hessian in an unconstrained second-order test.

Key Results¶

  • Only binding inequality constraints and equality constraints enter the relevant bordered Hessian.
  • The theorem gives sufficient conditions for strict local constrained optima.
  • Zero or semidefinite cases can be inconclusive.

Examples¶

  • With one equality constraint in two variables, the relevant bordered Hessian is a three-by-three matrix.
  • For a maximization problem with three variables and one active constraint, the final relevant determinant must have sign (−1)3(-1)^3(−1)3.
