Bordered Matrix Test¶

Theorem¶

Let Q(x)=x⊤AxQ(\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{x}^{\top} \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}Q(x)=x⊤Ax with A∈Rn×n\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}A∈Rn×n symmetric, and let the independent linear constraint set be Bx=0\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{x}=0Bx=0 with B∈Rm×n\boldsymbol{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}B∈Rm×n and rank⁡(B)=m\operatorname{rank}(\boldsymbol{B})=mrank(B)=m. The bordered matrix is

H=(0mBB⊤A).\boldsymbol{H}= \begin{pmatrix} \boldsymbol{0}_m & \boldsymbol{B}\\ \boldsymbol{B}^{\top} & \boldsymbol{A} \end{pmatrix}.H=(0m​B⊤​BA​).

Ignore the first 2m2m2m leading principal minors. For k=2m+1,…,m+nk=2m+1,\ldots,m+nk=2m+1,…,m+n:

  • If (−1)mdet⁡(Hk)>0(-1)^m\det(\boldsymbol{H}_k)>0(−1)mdet(Hk​)>0 for all relevant kkk, then QQQ is positive definite on the constraint set.
  • If (−1)k−mdet⁡(Hk)>0(-1)^{k-m}\det(\boldsymbol{H}_k)>0(−1)k−mdet(Hk​)>0 for all relevant kkk, then QQQ is negative definite on the constraint set.

Interpretation¶

The bordered matrix test checks the sign of a quadratic form only along the directions that linear constraints leave free (definiteness on the constraint set). It is the constrained version of Sylvester's criterion, again read from determinant signs.

The figure shows the blocks of H\boldsymbol{H}H: an m×mm\times mm×m zero block (grey), the constraint matrix B\boldsymbol{B}B and its transpose as a border (red), and the matrix A\boldsymbol{A}A of the form (blue). Hk\boldsymbol{H}_kHk​ is the top-left k×kk\times kk×k submatrix. The first 2m2m2m leading minors do not depend on A\boldsymbol{A}A, which is why they are ignored; the remaining n−mn-mn−m are as many as the dimension of the constraint set {x ⁣:Bx=0}\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{B}\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{0}\}{x:Bx=0}.

For x12−x22x_1^2-x_2^2x12​−x22​ on the line x1=2x2x_1=2x_2x1​=2x2​ from the Examples, B=(1−2)\boldsymbol{B}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\end{pmatrix}B=(1​−2​) and A=diag⁡(1,−1)\boldsymbol{A}=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)A=diag(1,−1), so det⁡H=−3\det\boldsymbol{H}=-3detH=−3 and (−1)1det⁡H>0(-1)^1\det\boldsymbol{H}>0(−1)1detH>0; indeed Q(2t,t)=3t2>0Q(2t,t)=3t^2>0Q(2t,t)=3t2>0 for t≠0t\neq0t=0.

The rank condition says that the mmm constraints are independent, so they remove exactly mmm directions. Without it the test breaks down: if the rows of B\boldsymbol{B}B are dependent, some w≠0\boldsymbol{w}\neq\boldsymbol{0}w=0 has B⊤w=0\boldsymbol{B}^{\top}\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{0}B⊤w=0, then H\boldsymbol{H}H sends (w,0)(\boldsymbol{w},\boldsymbol{0})(w,0) to 0\boldsymbol{0}0, and det⁡H=0\det\boldsymbol{H}=0detH=0 whatever A\boldsymbol{A}A is.

A common slip is to expect positive determinants for a positive definite form. With one constraint, positive definiteness on the constraint set shows up as negative minors, as in the example; the factor (−1)m(-1)^m(−1)m tells which sign to look for. The test returns later in the course, with a Lagrangian's Hessian as A\boldsymbol{A}A, as the bordered Hessian.

Key Results¶

  • Definiteness on the whole space can change after restricting to a constraint set.
  • For positive definiteness, all relevant signs match (−1)m(-1)^m(−1)m.
  • For negative definiteness, relevant signs alternate and the full determinant has sign (−1)n(-1)^n(−1)n.
