Lagrange Multiplier¶

Definition¶

A Lagrange multiplier is an auxiliary variable attached to a constraint in a Lagrangian. For equality constraints hj(x)=cjh_j(x)=c_jhj​(x)=cj​, a typical course convention writes

L(x,μ)=f(x)+∑j=1mμj(cj−hj(x)).L(x,\mu)=f(x)+\sum_{j=1}^m\mu_j(c_j-h_j(x)).L(x,μ)=f(x)+j=1∑m​μj​(cj​−hj​(x)).

At a regular constrained optimum, the multipliers make the gradient of the objective a linear combination of the gradients of the active constraints.

Interpretation¶

A multiplier records how a constraint enters the first-order trade-off at a candidate optimum. In parameterized problems, it can also measure the marginal value of relaxing a constraint.

Key Results¶

  • Equality multipliers may be positive, negative, or zero.
  • Inequality multipliers have sign restrictions under the course convention.
  • In the envelope theorem, some multipliers can be interpreted as shadow prices.

Examples¶

  • For maximizing xyxyxy subject to x+y=10x+y=10x+y=10, the multiplier is attached to the budget equation.
  • For L(x,y,μ)=x2+y2+μ(1−x−y)L(x,y,\mu)=x^2+y^2+\mu(1-x-y)L(x,y,μ)=x2+y2+μ(1−x−y), stationarity gives 2x−μ=02x-\mu=02x−μ=0 and 2y−μ=02y-\mu=02y−μ=0.
