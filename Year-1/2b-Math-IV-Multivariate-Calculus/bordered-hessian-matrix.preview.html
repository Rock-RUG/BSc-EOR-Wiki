Bordered Hessian Matrix¶

Definition¶

For an equality-constrained problem with Lagrangian L(x,λ)L(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{\lambda})L(x,λ) and constraint Jacobian Dg(x∗)\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast)Dg(x∗), the bordered Hessian matrix is the block matrix

(0Dg(x∗)Dg(x∗)⊤HxL(x∗,λ∗)),\begin{pmatrix} \boldsymbol{0} & \boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast)\\ \boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast)^{\top} & \boldsymbol{H}_xL(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast,\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\ast) \end{pmatrix},(0Dg(x∗)⊤​Dg(x∗)Hx​L(x∗,λ∗)​),

with the exact block orientation depending on whether gradients are written as rows or columns.

Interpretation¶

The bordered Hessian packages the constraint linearization and the curvature of the Lagrangian into one matrix. Its leading principal minors tell whether LLL bends down or up along the directions that keep the constraints satisfied to first order.

The figure shows the block layout: an m×mm\times mm×m zero block, the constraint Jacobian Dg(x∗)\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast)Dg(x∗) (m×m×nm\times nn) and its transpose as the border, and the n×nn\times nn×n Hessian of LLL with respect to x\boldsymbol{x}x in the corner, (m+n)×(m+n)(m+n)\times(m+n)(m+n)×(m+n) in total. This is the matrix of the bordered matrix test, with B=Dg(x∗)\boldsymbol{B}=\boldsymbol{D}\boldsymbol{g}(\boldsymbol{x}^\ast)B=Dg(x∗) and A\boldsymbol{A}A the Hessian of LLL.

The Hessian of LLL, not of the objective, is needed because the bending of the constraint matters too. When x+yx+yx+y is maximized on the circle x2+y2=1x^2+y^2=1x2+y2=1, the objective has zero Hessian and says nothing. With L=x+y+λ(1−x2−y2)L=x+y+\lambda(1-x^2-y^2)L=x+y+λ(1−x2−y2), the candidate (1/2,1/2)(1/\sqrt{2},1/\sqrt{2})(1/2​,1/2​) has λ∗=1/2\lambda^\ast=1/\sqrt{2}λ∗=1/2​, the Hessian of LLL is −2I-\sqrt{2}\boldsymbol{I}−2​I, and the bordered Hessian has determinant 42>04\sqrt{2}>042​>0, the sign (−1)m+1=+1(-1)^{m+1}=+1(−1)m+1=+1 that the Key Results require for a strict local maximum. Writing a constraint as −g-g−g instead of ggg flips the sign of a border row and the matching column together, which leaves every leading minor unchanged.

A common slip is to inspect all leading minors. The first 2m2m2m do not depend on the Hessian block, so they say nothing about curvature; only the last n−mn-mn−m count, as in the second-order sufficient conditions.

Key Results¶

  • The m×mm\times mm×m zero block corresponds to the multiplier variables; the m×nm\times nm×n border contains the constraint gradients.
  • The n×nn\times nn×n Hessian block is taken with respect to the choice variables, so the full matrix has size (m+n)×(m+n)(m+n)\times(m+n)(m+n)×(m+n).
  • Determinants of selected bordered submatrices are used in bordered matrix tests.
  • For m<nm<nm<n equality constraints with independent gradients at a first-order candidate, the second-order sufficient conditions inspect the last n−mn-mn−m leading principal minors. Alternating signs starting with (−1)m+1(-1)^{m+1}(−1)m+1 give a strict local maximum; signs all equal to (−1)m(-1)^m(−1)m give a strict local minimum. These tests check the curvature of LLL along directions tangent to the constraints.

Examples¶

  • For one constraint g(x,y)=0g(x,y)=0g(x,y)=0, a common bordered Hessian has first row and column built from gxg_xgx​ and gyg_ygy​.
  • For two equality constraints in three variables, the bordered Hessian has a 222 by 222 zero block and a 333 by 333 Hessian block.
