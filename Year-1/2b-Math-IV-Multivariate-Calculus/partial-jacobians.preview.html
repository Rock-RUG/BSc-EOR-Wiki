Partial Jacobians¶

Definition¶

Let F ⁣:A⊆Rp+q→RmF\colon A\subseteq\mathbb R^{p+q}\to\mathbb R^mF:A⊆Rp+q→Rm be differentiable, and write the variables as (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) with x∈Rpx\in\mathbb R^px∈Rp and y∈Rqy\in\mathbb R^qy∈Rq. The partial Jacobians of FFF are the blocks

DxF(x,y)∈Rm×p,DyF(x,y)∈Rm×q.D_xF(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{m\times p}, \qquad D_yF(x,y)\in\mathbb R^{m\times q}.Dx​F(x,y)∈Rm×p,Dy​F(x,y)∈Rm×q.

Together they form the full Jacobian:

DF(x,y)=(DxF(x,y)DyF(x,y)).DF(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}D_xF(x,y)&D_yF(x,y)\end{pmatrix}.DF(x,y)=(Dx​F(x,y)​Dy​F(x,y)​).

Interpretation¶

A partial Jacobian isolates how an equation system responds to one chosen block of variables while the other block is held fixed.

Key Results¶

  • Partial Jacobians are the matrix version of holding some variables constant in partial differentiation.
  • The implicit function theorem uses the partial Jacobian with respect to the variables being solved for.
  • Block notation makes comparative statics formulas easier to write and remember.
