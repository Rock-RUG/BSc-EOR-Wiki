Convergence in Probability¶

Definition¶

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… and XXX be random variables on a common probability space. The sequence converges in probability to XXX, written Xn→PXX_n\xrightarrow{P}XXn​P​X, if for every ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0,

Interpretation¶

Convergence in probability asks, for each fixed tolerance ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0, whether the probability of an error larger than ε\varepsilonε tends to zero. It makes a statement about each sample size nnn separately, not about how a single sample path behaves as nnn grows.

Two steps are involved: first ε\varepsilonε is fixed, then the limit of the numbers P(∣Xn−X∣>ε)\mathbb{P}(|X_n-X|>\varepsilon)P(∣Xn​−X∣>ε) is taken. Small errors are allowed at every nnn; only the probability of errors above the tolerance must vanish. Most uses have a constant limit, as in the weak law of large numbers. A quick proof for a constant limit uses the Chebyshev inequality: if EXn=c\mathbb{E}X_n=cEXn​=c and Var⁡(Xn)→0\operatorname{Var}(X_n)\to0Var(Xn​)→0, then P(∣Xn−c∣≥ε)≤Var⁡(Xn)/ε2→0\mathbb{P}(|X_n-c|\ge\varepsilon)\le\operatorname{Var}(X_n)/\varepsilon^2\to0P(∣Xn​−c∣≥ε)≤Var(Xn​)/ε2→0.

A common slip is to conclude that expectations converge as well. If Xn=nX_n=nXn​=n with probability 1/n1/n1/n and Xn=0X_n=0Xn​=0 otherwise, then Xn→P0X_n\xrightarrow{P}0Xn​P​0, yet EXn=1\mathbb{E}X_n=1EXn​=1 for every nnn: a rare but large error does not count in the definition. The stronger almost-sure convergence additionally requires almost every path to converge.

Key Results¶

  • Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in probability.
  • Convergence in probability implies convergence in distribution; the converses generally fail.
