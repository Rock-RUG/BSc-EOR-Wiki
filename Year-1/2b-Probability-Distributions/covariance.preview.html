Covariance¶

Definition¶

For square-integrable X,YX,YX,Y, covariance is

Cov⁡(X,Y)=E[(X−EX)(Y−EY)]=E[XY]−EX EY.\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)&=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}X)(Y-\mathbb{E}Y)]\\ &=\mathbb{E}[XY]-\mathbb{E}X\,\mathbb{E}Y. \end{aligned}Cov(X,Y)​=E[(X−EX)(Y−EY)]=E[XY]−EXEY.​

Independence implies zero covariance; the converse generally fails.

Interpretation¶

Covariance measures whether two variables tend to deviate from their means in the same direction. It also accounts for dependence in the variance of a sum.

Points in the green quadrants contribute positive products of deviations, while points in the red quadrants contribute negative products. Covariance averages these products, taking both their magnitudes and probabilities into account.

Key Results¶

  • Cov⁡(a+bX,c+dY)=bdCov⁡(X,Y)\operatorname{Cov}(a+bX,c+dY)=bd\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)Cov(a+bX,c+dY)=bdCov(X,Y).
  • Var⁡(X+Y)=Var⁡(X)+Var⁡(Y)+2Cov⁡(X,Y)\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y)+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)Var(X+Y)=Var(X)+Var(Y)+2Cov(X,Y).

Examples¶

  • If Y=2XY=2XY=2X and Var⁡(X)=3\operatorname{Var}(X)=3Var(X)=3, then Cov⁡(X,Y)=6\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=6Cov(X,Y)=6 and Var⁡(X+Y)=27\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=27Var(X+Y)=27.
  • For independent fair Bernoulli variables, E[XY]=1/4\mathbb{E}[XY]=1/4E[XY]=1/4 and EXEY=1/4\mathbb{E}X\mathbb{E}Y=1/4EXEY=1/4, so covariance is zero.
