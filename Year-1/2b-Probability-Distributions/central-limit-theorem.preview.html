Central Limit Theorem¶

Theorem¶

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be iid with mean μ\muμ and finite positive variance 0<σ2<∞0<\sigma^2<\infty0<σ2<∞. Then the sample mean Xˉn=1n∑i=1nXi\bar X_n=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_iXˉn​=n1​∑i=1n​Xi​ satisfies

Xˉn−μσ/n→dN(0,1).\begin{aligned} \frac{\bar X_n-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1). \end{aligned}σ/n​Xˉn​−μ​d​N(0,1).​

Equivalently, for the sum Sn=∑i=1nXiS_n=\sum_{i=1}^n X_iSn​=∑i=1n​Xi​,

Sn−nμσn→dN(0,1).\begin{aligned} \frac{S_n-n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(0,1). \end{aligned}σn​Sn​−nμ​d​N(0,1).​

Here N(μ,v)\mathcal{N}(\mu,v)N(μ,v) denotes a normal distribution with mean μ\muμ and variance vvv, and Φ\PhiΦ is the standard normal CDF.

Interpretation¶

The properly centered and scaled fluctuations of an iid sum approach a standard normal distribution. This gives approximate probabilities for sums and averages.

Key Results¶

  • For large nnn, SnS_nSn​ is approximately N(nμ,nσ2)\mathcal{N}(n\mu,n\sigma^2)N(nμ,nσ2) and Xˉn\bar X_nXˉn​ approximately N(μ,σ2/n)\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2/n)N(μ,σ2/n).
  • The finite-sample approximation is not an exact normality claim; iid normal observations are a special case where the standardized sum is exactly normal.
