Almost-Sure Convergence¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

Almost-sure convergence looks at one outcome at a time. For a fixed outcome, X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… is an ordinary sequence of numbers, the sample path of that outcome, and the question is whether it tends to the value of XXX. Outside a set of outcomes of probability zero, the answer must be yes: this is pointwise convergence on the sample space, with a negligible exceptional set allowed.

The word "almost" allows that exceptional set; see almost surely. The probability in the definition is taken once, for the set of outcomes whose whole path converges, not separately for each nnn. Almost-sure convergence is the stronger of the two, and it is the mode of convergence in the strong law of large numbers.

Key Results¶

  • Almost-sure convergence implies convergence in probability.
  • Changing any of the variables on a common event of probability zero does not change almost-sure convergence.
