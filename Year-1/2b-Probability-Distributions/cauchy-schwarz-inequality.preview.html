Cauchy–Schwarz Inequality for Random Variables¶

Theorem¶

For random variables XXX and YYY with E[X2]<∞\mathbb{E}[X^2]<\inftyE[X2]<∞ and E[Y2]<∞\mathbb{E}[Y^2]<\inftyE[Y2]<∞ (which also makes XYXYXY integrable),

(E[XY])2≤E[X2]⋅E[Y2].\begin{aligned} (\mathbb{E}[X Y])^2\leq \mathbb{E}[X^2]\cdot \mathbb{E}[Y^2]. \end{aligned}(E[XY])2≤E[X2]⋅E[Y2].​

If either second moment is zero, equality holds trivially.

Interpretation¶

The average product of two square-integrable variables is bounded by their root-mean-square sizes. Applied to centered variables, this bounds covariance and correlation.

For nonzero root-mean-square lengths, the angle in the diagram satisfies E[XY]=E[X2]E[Y2]cos⁡θ\mathbb{E}[XY]=\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[X^2]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[Y^2]}\cos\thetaE[XY]=E[X2]​E[Y2]​cosθ. The bound follows from ∣cos⁡θ∣≤1|\cos\theta|\le1∣cosθ∣≤1. The theorem above also covers zero second moments.

Key Results¶

  • ∣Cov⁡(X,Y)∣≤Var⁡(X)Var⁡(Y)|\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)|\le\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)\operatorname{Var}(Y)}∣Cov(X,Y)∣≤Var(X)Var(Y)​.
  • If both second moments are positive, equality holds exactly when Y=cXY=cXY=cX almost surely for some real ccc.

Examples¶

  • If E[X2]=4\mathbb{E}[X^2]=4E[X2]=4 and E[Y2]=9\mathbb{E}[Y^2]=9E[Y2]=9, then ∣E[XY]∣≤6|\mathbb{E}[XY]|\le6∣E[XY]∣≤6.
  • Taking Y=1Y=1Y=1 gives (EX)2≤E[X2](\mathbb{E}X)^2\le\mathbb{E}[X^2](EX)2≤E[X2]; for a fair die this is 12.25≤91/612.25\le91/612.25≤91/6.
