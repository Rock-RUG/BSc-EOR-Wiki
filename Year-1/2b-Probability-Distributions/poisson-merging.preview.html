Poisson Merging¶

Theorem¶

If X∼Pois⁡(λ1)X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_1)X∼Pois(λ1​) and Y∼Pois⁡(λ2)Y\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_2)Y∼Pois(λ2​) are independent, then X+Y∼Pois⁡(λ1+λ2)X+Y\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)X+Y∼Pois(λ1​+λ2​). Independence is needed: Poisson marginals alone do not make the sum Poisson.

Interpretation¶

Independent Poisson sources combine into a Poisson total whose parameter is the sum of the source parameters.

For independent X∼Pois⁡(λ1)X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_1)X∼Pois(λ1​) and Y∼Pois⁡(λ2)Y\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_2)Y∼Pois(λ2​), the sum has distribution Pois⁡(λ1+λ2)\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_1+\lambda_2)Pois(λ1​+λ2​). Independence is essential.
