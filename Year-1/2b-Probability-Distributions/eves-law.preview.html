Eve’s Law: Total Variance¶

Theorem¶

For random variables XXX and YYY with E[Y2]<∞\mathbb{E}[Y^2]<\inftyE[Y2]<∞,

Var⁡(Y)=E[Var⁡(Y∣X)]+Var⁡(E[Y∣X]).\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Var}(Y\mid X)]+\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]). \end{aligned}Var(Y)=E[Var(Y∣X)]+Var(E[Y∣X]).​

Consequence: the residual Y−E[Y∣X]Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]Y−E[Y∣X] is uncorrelated with every function h(X)h(X)h(X) that has E[h(X)2]<∞\mathbb{E}[h(X)^2]<\inftyE[h(X)2]<∞ (or satisfies another assumption that makes the products integrable):

Cov⁡(Y−E[Y∣X], h(X))=0.\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Cov}(Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X],\,h(X))=0. \end{aligned}Cov(Y−E[Y∣X],h(X))=0.​

Interpretation¶

Total spread consists of average spread within conditional groups plus spread between their conditional means.

The first term in the total-variance identity measures the average spread within groups around the red group means. The second measures the spread of those means around EY\mathbb{E}YEY.

Key Results¶

  • Var⁡(E[Y∣X])≤Var⁡(Y)\operatorname{Var}(\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X])\le\operatorname{Var}(Y)Var(E[Y∣X])≤Var(Y).
  • The residual R=Y−E[Y∣X]R=Y-\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]R=Y−E[Y∣X] has mean zero and Cov⁡(R,h(X))=0\operatorname{Cov}(R,h(X))=0Cov(R,h(X))=0 for every square-integrable h(X)h(X)h(X).

  • If X∼Exp⁡(2)X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(2)X∼Exp(2) and Y∣X∼Pois⁡(X)Y\mid X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(X)Y∣X∼Pois(X), then Var⁡(Y)=EX+Var⁡(X)=1/2+1/4=3/4\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\mathbb{E}X+\operatorname{Var}(X)=1/2+1/4=3/4Var(Y)=EX+Var(X)=1/2+1/4=3/4.
  • If X∼Unif⁡(0,1)X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)X∼Unif(0,1) and Y∣X∼Bin⁡(n,X)Y\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,X)Y∣X∼Bin(n,X), then Var⁡(Y)=n/6+n2/12=n(n+2)/12\operatorname{Var}(Y)=n/6+n^2/12=n(n+2)/12Var(Y)=n/6+n2/12=n(n+2)/12.
