Adam’s Law: Total Expectation¶

Theorem¶

For random variables XXX and YYY with E∣Y∣<∞\mathbb{E}|Y|<\inftyE∣Y∣<∞,

E[E[Y∣X]]=E[Y].\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]]=\mathbb{E}[Y]. \end{aligned}E[E[Y∣X]]=E[Y].​

Interpretation¶

Averaging the conditional average recovers the original average. Conditioning simplifies a calculation while keeping its final unconditional answer.

For X∼Unif⁡(0,1)X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)X∼Unif(0,1) and Y∣X∼Bin⁡(10,X)Y\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(10,X)Y∣X∼Bin(10,X), the conditional mean is 10X10X10X. Averaging over XXX gives E[Y]=E[E[Y∣X]]=∫0110x dx=5\mathbb{E}[Y]=\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X]]=\int_0^1 10x\,\mathrm{d}x=5E[Y]=E[E[Y∣X]]=∫01​10xdx=5.

Key Results¶

  • For discrete XXX, EY=∑xE[Y∣X=x]pX(x)\mathbb{E}Y=\sum_x\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X=x]p_X(x)EY=∑x​E[Y∣X=x]pX​(x); for density XXX, use the corresponding integral.
  • Conditioning on more information first and then on less gives the conditional expectation given the smaller information: E[E[Y∣X,Z]∣Z]=E[Y∣Z]\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}[Y\mid X,Z]\mid Z]=\mathbb{E}[Y\mid Z]E[E[Y∣X,Z]∣Z]=E[Y∣Z] (Adam's law with extra conditioning).

Examples¶

  • If X∼Unif⁡(0,1)X\sim\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)X∼Unif(0,1) and Y∣X∼Bin⁡(10,X)Y\mid X\sim\operatorname{Bin}(10,X)Y∣X∼Bin(10,X), then EY=E[10X]=5\mathbb{E}Y=\mathbb{E}[10X]=5EY=E[10X]=5.
  • If X∼Exp⁡(2)X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(2)X∼Exp(2) and Y∣X∼Pois⁡(X)Y\mid X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(X)Y∣X∼Pois(X), then EY=EX=1/2\mathbb{E}Y=\mathbb{E}X=1/2EY=EX=1/2.
