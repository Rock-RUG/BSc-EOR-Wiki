Moment¶

Definition¶

Let XXX be a random variable and let r≥1r\ge1r≥1 be a natural number. If E∣X∣r<∞\mathbb{E}|X|^r<\inftyE∣X∣r<∞, the rrrth moment (or raw moment, moment about the origin) of XXX is

E[Xr].\mathbb{E}[X^r].E[Xr].

Writing μ=EX\mu=\mathbb{E}Xμ=EX for the first moment, the rrrth central moment (moment about the mean) is

E[(X−μ)r].\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^r].E[(X−μ)r].

If E∣X∣r=∞\mathbb{E}|X|^r=\inftyE∣X∣r=∞, the rrrth moment does not exist. Both moments are expectations of a function of XXX, so by LOTUS they are computed from the PMF as ∑xxrpX(x)\displaystyle \sum_x x^r p_X(x)x∑​xrpX​(x) or from the PDF as ∫xrfX(x) dx\int x^r f_X(x)\,\mathrm{d}x∫xrfX​(x)dx, and similarly with (x−μ)r(x-\mu)^r(x−μ)r.

Interpretation¶

Moments summarize a distribution through averages of powers. Raw moments are measured from 000; central moments are measured from the mean, so they describe the shape of the distribution around its centre and do not change when XXX is shifted. Higher powers give large deviations more weight, so higher moments are increasingly sensitive to the tails.

For XXX taking the values 1,2,31,2,31,2,3 with probabilities 0.5,0.3,0.20.5,0.3,0.20.5,0.3,0.2:

  • raw moments: EX=1.7\mathbb{E}X=1.7EX=1.7, E[X2]=0.5+1.2+1.8=3.5\mathbb{E}[X^2]=0.5+1.2+1.8=3.5E[X2]=0.5+1.2+1.8=3.5 and E[X3]=0.5+2.4+5.4=8.3\mathbb{E}[X^3]=0.5+2.4+5.4=8.3E[X3]=0.5+2.4+5.4=8.3;
  • central moments: the deviations from μ=1.7\mu=1.7μ=1.7 are −0.7,0.3,1.3-0.7,0.3,1.3−0.7,0.3,1.3, so the second central moment is 0.5(0.49)+0.3(0.09)+0.2(1.69)=0.610.5(0.49)+0.3(0.09)+0.2(1.69)=0.610.5(0.49)+0.3(0.09)+0.2(1.69)=0.61 and the third is 0.5(−0.343)+0.3(0.027)+0.2(2.197)=0.2760.5(-0.343)+0.3(0.027)+0.2(2.197)=0.2760.5(−0.343)+0.3(0.027)+0.2(2.197)=0.276.

Key Results¶

The first moment is the expectation, and the first central moment is always 000. The second central moment is the variance; expanding the power and using linearity of expectation expresses central moments through raw moments:

Var⁡(X)=E[(X−μ)2]=E[X2]−μ2,E[(X−μ)3]=E[X3]−3μ E[X2]+2μ3.\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Var}(X)&=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2]\\ &=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mu^2,\\ \mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^3]&=\mathbb{E}[X^3]-3\mu\,\mathbb{E}[X^2] +2\mu^3. \end{aligned}Var(X)E[(X−μ)3]​=E[(X−μ)2]=E[X2]−μ2,=E[X3]−3μE[X2]+2μ3.​

The cross terms do not cancel: the third central moment is not E[X3]−μ3\mathbb{E}[X^3]-\mu^3E[X3]−μ3. The third central moment divided by σ3\sigma^3σ3, where σ\sigmaσ is the standard deviation, is the skewness, a measure of asymmetry.

  • Since a variance is never negative, E[X2]≥(EX)2\mathbb{E}[X^2]\ge(\mathbb{E}X)^2E[X2]≥(EX)2, with equality exactly when XXX is constant almost surely.
  • For constants a,ba,ba,b, the rrrth central moment of a+bXa+bXa+bX is brb^rbr (not ∣b∣r|b|^r∣b∣r) times that of XXX. In particular, shifting by aaa leaves all central moments unchanged, and the odd central moments change sign when b=−1b=-1b=−1. Raw moments are not shift-invariant: E[(a+bX)2]=a2+2ab EX+b2 E[X2]\mathbb{E}[(a+bX)^2]=a^2+2ab\,\mathbb{E}X+b^2\,\mathbb{E}[X^2]E[(a+bX)2]=a2+2abEX+b2E[X2].
  • If the sssth moment exists, so does every rrrth moment with r≤sr\le sr≤s, because ∣x∣r≤1+∣x∣s|x|^r\le1+|x|^s∣x∣r≤1+∣x∣s. The converse fails: moments can fail to exist for heavy-tailed distributions even when lower moments are finite.
  • If XXX is bounded, say ∣X∣≤c|X|\le c∣X∣≤c, every moment exists and ∣E[Xr]∣≤cr|\mathbb{E}[X^r]|\le c^r∣E[Xr]∣≤cr.
  • For an indicator random variable IAI_AIA​, IAr=IAI_A^r=I_AIAr​=IA​, so every moment equals P(A)\mathbb{P}(A)P(A).
  • If the moment generating function is finite on an open interval around 000, all moments exist and E[Xn]=MX(n)(0)\mathbb{E}[X^n]=M_X^{(n)}(0)E[Xn]=MX(n)​(0) (moments from the MGF).
