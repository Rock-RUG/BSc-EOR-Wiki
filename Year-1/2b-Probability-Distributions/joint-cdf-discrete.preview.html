Joint CDF of Discrete Random Variables¶

Definition¶

The joint CDF of a random pair (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) is

FX,Y(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y).\begin{aligned} F_{X,Y}(x,y)=\mathbb{P}(X\leq x,Y\leq y). \end{aligned}FX,Y​(x,y)=P(X≤x,Y≤y).​

The definition applies to any random pair; this page develops its discrete form, and Joint CDF of Continuous Random Variables develops the form with a joint density.

Interpretation¶

The joint CDF measures the probability that two variables simultaneously lie below specified thresholds: FX,Y(x,y)F_{X,Y}(x,y)FX,Y​(x,y) is the probability that XXX is at most xxx and YYY is at most yyy. For a discrete pair it is the sum of the joint masses in the lower-left corner below (x,y)(x,y)(x,y).

For two fair dice the highlighted corner {X≤2, Y≤3}\{X\le2,\,Y\le3\}{X≤2,Y≤3} contains 2⋅3=62\cdot3=62⋅3=6 of the 363636 equally likely outcomes, so FX,Y(2,3)=6/36=16F_{X,Y}(2,3)=6/36=\tfrac{1}{6}FX,Y​(2,3)=6/36=61​. Because the dice are independent, this also equals FX(2)FY(3)=26⋅36F_X(2)F_Y(3)=\tfrac{2}{6}\cdot\tfrac{3}{6}FX​(2)FY​(3)=62​⋅63​.

The joint CDF is defined at every point (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) of the plane, not only at possible values. Between possible values it is constant, so for the dice FX,Y(2.5,3.7)=FX,Y(2,3)F_{X,Y}(2.5,3.7)=F_{X,Y}(2,3)FX,Y​(2.5,3.7)=FX,Y​(2,3). It is nondecreasing in each argument, and letting y→∞y\to\inftyy→∞ removes the condition on YYY and leaves the CDF of XXX. In general the joint CDF is not the product of the two marginal CDFs; it is for independent variables.
