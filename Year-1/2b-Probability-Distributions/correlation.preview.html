Correlation¶

Definition¶

For random variables XXX and YYY with 0<Var⁡(X)<∞0<\operatorname{Var}(X)<\infty0<Var(X)<∞ and 0<Var⁡(Y)<∞0<\operatorname{Var}(Y)<\infty0<Var(Y)<∞, the correlation of XXX and YYY is

Corr⁡(X,Y)=Cov⁡(X,Y)Var⁡(X)Var⁡(Y)∈[−1,1].\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Corr}(X,Y)=\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)\operatorname{Var}(Y)}}\in[-1,1]. \end{aligned}Corr(X,Y)=Var(X)Var(Y)​Cov(X,Y)​∈[−1,1].​

Correlation is undefined if either variance is zero.

Interpretation¶

Correlation standardizes covariance into a unit-free measure of linear association. Zero correlation rules out linear association but can coexist with dependence.

The left panel shows the exact decreasing relation Y=5−2XY=5-2XY=5−2X. The middle panel shows a positive but imperfect linear association.
