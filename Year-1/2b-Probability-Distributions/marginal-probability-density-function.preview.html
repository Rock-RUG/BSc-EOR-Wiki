Marginal Probability Density Function¶

Definition¶

For a jointly continuous pair (X,Y)(X,Y)(X,Y) with joint density fX,Yf_{X,Y}fX,Y​, the marginal PDF of XXX is

fX(x)=∫fX,Y(x,y) dy,\begin{aligned} f_X(x)=\int f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d} y, \end{aligned}fX​(x)=∫fX,Y​(x,y)dy,​

where the integral runs over the values allowed for the other coordinate; the density is zero outside the joint support.

Interpretation¶

Integrating out one coordinate of a joint density leaves the density of the other coordinate.

For the illustrated triangular density, integrating over the other coordinate gives fX(x)=∫x12 dy=2(1−x)f_X(x)=\int_x^1 2\,\mathrm{d}y=2(1-x)fX​(x)=∫x1​2dy=2(1−x) for 0<x<10<x<10<x<1 and fY(y)=∫0y2 dx=2yf_Y(y)=\int_0^y2\,\mathrm{d}x=2yfY​(y)=∫0y​2dx=2y for 0<y<10<y<10<y<1. The red and green slices represent these two integrals.

Key Results¶

  • fY(y)=∫RfX,Y(x,y) dxf_Y(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{X,Y}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}xfY​(y)=∫R​fX,Y​(x,y)dx.
  • Each marginal integrates to one, since the joint density does.
