Weak Law of Large Numbers¶

Theorem¶

Let X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… be independent and identically distributed random variables with finite expectation μ\muμ, and let Xˉn\bar X_nXˉn​ be their sample mean. The weak law of large numbers states that Xˉn\bar X_nXˉn​ converges in probability to μ\muμ.

Interpretation¶

The weak law says that averages stabilize as the sample size grows. Individual observations may be random, but their average becomes predictable.

Key Results¶

  • The sample mean converges in probability to the common mean under standard finite-variance assumptions.
  • Chebyshev's inequality gives a common proof when the variance is finite.
  • The law justifies using large-sample averages to estimate expectations.

Examples¶

  • The proportion of heads in many independent fair coin tosses becomes close to one half with high probability.
  • The average demand over many similar days becomes close to expected demand.
  • The average of repeated independent measurement errors becomes close to the true mean error.
