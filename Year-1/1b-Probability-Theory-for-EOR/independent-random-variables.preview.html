Independent Random Variables¶

Definition¶

Real-valued random variables XXX and YYY are independent if

P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B)\mathbb{P}(X\in A,Y\in B)=\mathbb{P}(X\in A)\mathbb{P}(Y\in B)P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B)

for every pair of intervals A,BA,BA,B. For a finite family, mutual independence requires the analogous product formula for every selection of sets and every subfamily, rather than merely for pairs.

For discrete variables, this is equivalent to pX,Y(x,y)=pX(x)pY(y)p_{X,Y}(x,y)=p_X(x)p_Y(y)pX,Y​(x,y)=pX​(x)pY​(y) for every x,yx,yx,y. For a pair with a joint density, it is equivalent to fX,Y(x,y)=fX(x)fY(y)f_{X,Y}(x,y)=f_X(x)f_Y(y)fX,Y​(x,y)=fX​(x)fY​(y) for all (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) except possibly on a set of zero area, for example along finitely many lines. In either case, conditional laws agree with the corresponding marginal laws wherever the conditioning denominator is positive (with density identities allowed to fail on a set of zero length or area).

Interpretation¶

Independent variables supply separate sources of randomness: learning one variable does not change the distribution of the other. PMF and PDF factorization are two ways to check the same property.

Every cell is the product of its row and column marginal, e.g. P(X=1,Y=1)=0.5⋅0.6=0.30\mathbb{P}(X=1,Y=1)=0.5\cdot0.6=0.30P(X=1,Y=1)=0.5⋅0.6=0.30: XXX and YYY are independent, P(X∈A, Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B)\mathbb{P}(X\in A,\,Y\in B)=\mathbb{P}(X\in A)\mathbb{P}(Y\in B)P(X∈A,Y∈B)=P(X∈A)P(Y∈B) for all A,BA,BA,B; knowing YYY does not change the distribution of XXX.

Key Results¶

  • For arbitrary real-valued X,YX,YX,Y, independence is equivalent to FX,Y(x,y)=FX(x)FY(y)F_{X,Y}(x,y)=F_X(x)F_Y(y)FX,Y​(x,y)=FX​(x)FY​(y) for every x,yx,yx,y.
  • If X,YX,YX,Y are independent and integrable, then XYXYXY is integrable and E[XY]=EX EY\mathbb{E}[XY]=\mathbb{E}X\,\mathbb{E}YE[XY]=EXEY.
