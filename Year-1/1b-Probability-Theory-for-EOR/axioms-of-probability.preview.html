Axioms of Probability¶

Axiom¶

Let SSS be a sample space, let F\mathcal FF be a collection of events, and let P:F→RP:\mathcal F\to\mathbb RP:F→R. The function PPP satisfies the axioms of probability if:

  1. P(A)≥0P(A)\ge 0P(A)≥0 for every event AAA.
  2. P(S)=1P(S)=1P(S)=1.
  3. If A1,A2,…A_1,A_2,\ldotsA1​,A2​,… are pairwise disjoint events, then
P(⋃i=1∞Ai)=∑i=1∞P(Ai).P\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_i\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}P(A_i).P(i=1⋃∞​Ai​)=i=1∑∞​P(Ai​).

Interpretation¶

These axioms set the basic rules that every probability model must obey. They say probabilities cannot be negative, certainty has probability one, and mutually exclusive possibilities can be added.

Key Results¶

  • From the axioms, P(Ac)=1−P(A)P(A^c)=1-P(A)P(Ac)=1−P(A) for every event AAA.
  • If A⊆BA\subseteq BA⊆B, then P(A)≤P(B)P(A)\le P(B)P(A)≤P(B).
  • Finite additivity follows from countable additivity when only finitely many disjoint events have positive probability.

Examples¶

  • In a fair die model, nonnegativity assigns no negative chance to any face, and the sample space has probability one.
  • If the events 'roll a one' and 'roll a two' are disjoint, their union has probability equal to the sum of their probabilities.
  • For nested events such as 'roll at most two' and 'roll at most four', monotonicity gives the smaller event no larger probability.
