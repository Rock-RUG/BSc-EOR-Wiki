Fundamental Bridge¶

Proposition¶

For any event AAA, let 1A\mathbf{1}_A1A​ equal one on AAA and zero outside AAA. The fundamental bridge is

E[1A]=P(A).\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_A]=\mathbb{P}(A).E[1A​]=P(A).

The expectation always exists because the indicator is bounded.

Interpretation¶

An event probability is the expectation of its indicator. This connects event calculations to expectation.

Key Results¶

  • E[1A]=1P(A)+0P(Ac)=P(A)\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_A]=1\mathbb{P}(A)+0\mathbb{P}(A^c)=\mathbb{P}(A)E[1A​]=1P(A)+0P(Ac)=P(A).
  • E[∑i1Ai]=∑iP(Ai)\mathbb{E}[\sum_i\mathbf{1}_{A_i}]=\sum_i\mathbb{P}(A_i)E[∑i​1Ai​​]=∑i​P(Ai​) for finitely many events, without independence.

Examples¶

  • For an even result on a fair die, E[1A]=1/2\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{1}_A]=1/2E[1A​]=1/2.
  • For 101010 components each failing with probability 0.020.020.02, the expected failure count is 0.20.20.2, even if failures are dependent.
