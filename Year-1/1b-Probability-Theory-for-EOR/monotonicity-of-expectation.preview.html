Monotonicity of Expectation¶

Theorem¶

Let XXX and YYY be random variables with finite expectations. If X≤YX\le YX≤Y almost surely (that is, P(X≤Y)=1\mathbb{P}(X\le Y)=1P(X≤Y)=1), then monotonicity of expectation states that

E[X]≤E[Y].\mathbb{E}[X]\le \mathbb{E}[Y].E[X]≤E[Y].

Interpretation¶

Monotonicity says that if one random variable is never larger than another, its average is not larger either: taking expectations preserves the inequality X≤YX\le YX≤Y.

Write D=Y−XD=Y-XD=Y−X. The hypothesis says that D≥0D\ge0D≥0 with probability one, and an average of nonnegative values cannot be negative; by linearity of expectation, E[Y]−E[X]=E[D]≥0\mathbb{E}[Y]-\mathbb{E}[X]=\mathbb{E}[D]\ge0E[Y]−E[X]=E[D]≥0. An event of probability zero cannot affect any average, which is why the comparison need only hold with probability one. The converse fails: for a fair die roll XXX and Y=7−XY=7-XY=7−X the means are equal, yet X>YX>YX>Y whenever X≥4X\ge4X≥4.

The theorem is mostly used to bound an expectation by a simpler one. If X≤cX\le cX≤c for a constant ccc, then E[X]≤c\mathbb{E}[X]\le cE[X]≤c. For X≥0X\ge0X≥0 and c>0c>0c>0, the inequality c 𝟙1{X≥c}≤Xc\,\indicator_{\{X\ge c\}}\le Xc𝟙1{X≥c}​≤X together with the fundamental bridge gives c P(X≥c)≤E[X]c\,\mathbb{P}(X\ge c)\le\mathbb{E}[X]cP(X≥c)≤E[X]: a nonnegative variable with a small mean rarely takes large values.

Key Results¶

  • If X≤YX\le YX≤Y almost surely, then E[X]≤E[Y]\mathbb{E}[X]\le \mathbb{E}[Y]E[X]≤E[Y] when expectations exist.
  • Strict inequality is not guaranteed unless the ordering is strict with positive probability.
  • Monotonicity is often used to bound expectations.

Examples¶

  • If a premium service always costs at least as much as a basic service, its expected cost is at least as large.
  • If one policy always pays no more than another, its expected payout is no larger.
  • Truncating losses from above produces a random variable with no larger expectation.
