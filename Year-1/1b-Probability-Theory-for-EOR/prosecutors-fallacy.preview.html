Prosecutors Fallacy¶

Description¶

The prosecutor's fallacy is the error of confusing the probability of observing evidence under a hypothesis with the probability that the hypothesis is true given the evidence.

Interpretation¶

This fallacy reverses a conditional probability. It can make evidence sound much stronger than it actually is.

Key Results¶

  • The fallacy confuses P(E∣H)P(E\mid H)P(E∣H) with P(H∣E)P(H\mid E)P(H∣E).
  • Bayes' rule shows that posterior probabilities require prior probabilities and alternative explanations.
  • Rare evidence is not automatically decisive if it can also occur under competing hypotheses.

Examples¶

  • A rare DNA match probability is not the same as the probability that a defendant is innocent.
  • A rare disease test result is not the same as the probability of disease without considering prevalence.
  • A suspicious transaction pattern must be compared against both fraud and non-fraud explanations.
