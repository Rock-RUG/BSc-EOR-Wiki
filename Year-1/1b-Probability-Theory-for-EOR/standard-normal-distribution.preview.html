Standard Normal Distribution¶

Definition¶

A random variable ZZZ has the standard normal distribution, written Z∼N(0,1)Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)Z∼N(0,1), if its density is ϕ\phiϕ; its CDF is Φ\PhiΦ:

ϕ(z)=12πe−z2/2,Φ(z)=∫−∞zϕ(t) dt.\phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\mathrm{e}^{-z^2/2},\qquad \Phi(z)=\int_{-\infty}^z\phi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.ϕ(z)=2π​1​e−z2/2,Φ(z)=∫−∞z​ϕ(t)dt.

In N(μ,σ2)\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)N(μ,σ2) the second parameter is the variance.

Interpretation¶

The standard normal law provides the reference scale for normal approximations: a centered value is measured in standard deviations.
