LOTUS¶

Theorem¶

Let XXX be a random variable and let ggg be a function such that the relevant expectation exists. The law of the unconscious statistician states that the expectation of g(X)g(X)g(X) can be computed directly from the distribution of XXX without first finding the distribution of g(X)g(X)g(X).

Interpretation¶

This result lets transformed averages be computed directly. It avoids the extra work of deriving a new distribution when only an expectation is needed.

Key Results¶

  • LOTUS computes E[g(X)]E[g(X)]E[g(X)] from the distribution of XXX directly.
  • For discrete variables, it uses a sum over values of XXX.
  • For continuous variables, it uses an integral against the density of XXX.

Examples¶

  • If XXX is a die roll, LOTUS computes E[X2]E[X^2]E[X2] without creating a separate distribution table for X2X^2X2.
  • If XXX is an exponential waiting time, LOTUS can compute the expected cost g(X)g(X)g(X) of waiting.
  • If XXX is a demand level, LOTUS can compute expected revenue after applying a pricing function.
