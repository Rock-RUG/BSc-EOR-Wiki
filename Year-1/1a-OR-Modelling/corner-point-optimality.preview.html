Corner Point Optimality¶

Proposition¶

For the LPs in the course with a nonempty feasible region in nonnegative variables, if a finite optimum exists, at least one feasible corner point attains it. Consequently, a unique optimal solution must be a feasible corner point. If a bounded feasible region has multiple optimal solutions, at least two of its corner points are optimal.

Interpretation¶

This result allows an LP to be solved without testing every point in its feasible region. Simplex uses the algebraic counterparts of these corners.

Key Results¶

  • The existence of a finite optimum is essential.
  • The bounded-region qualification in the multiple-corner statement matters when the feasible region has rays.
  • Every convex combination of optimal corners is optimal.

Examples¶

  • The manufacturing optimum (2,5)(2,5)(2,5) is a feasible corner.
  • For the course LP with objective 4x1+2x24x_1+2x_24x1​+2x2​ and boundary 2x1+x2≤112x_1+x_2\le112x1​+x2​≤11, an entire feasible boundary segment attains 22.
