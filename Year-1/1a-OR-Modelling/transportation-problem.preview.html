Transportation Problem¶

Definition¶

The transportation problem chooses shipments xij≥0x_{ij}\ge0xij​≥0 from plants iii to distribution centres jjj to minimize total transport cost. With unit costs cijc_{ij}cij​, plant capacities sis_isi​, and required deliveries djd_jdj​, a formulation is

min⁡∑i∑jcijxij,∑jxij≤si for every i,∑ixij=dj for every j.\begin{gathered} \min\sum_i\sum_j c_{ij}x_{ij},\\ \sum_jx_{ij}\le s_i\ \text{for every }i,\\ \sum_ix_{ij}=d_j\ \text{for every }j. \end{gathered}mini∑​j∑​cij​xij​,j∑​xij​≤si​ for every i,i∑​xij​=dj​ for every j.​

The demand constraints can also be written as ∑ixij≥dj\displaystyle \sum_ix_{ij}\ge d_ji∑​xij​≥dj​: when every unit cost cijc_{ij}cij​ is positive, an optimal plan never delivers more than required, so both versions have the same optimal solutions.

Interpretation¶

The transportation problem decides how much to send along each route from origins with limited supply to destinations with given demand, so that the total transport cost is as small as possible. Each decision variable xijx_{ij}xij​ is the amount on one route, and every unit on that route costs cijc_{ij}cij​.

The plants P1P_1P1​ and P2P_2P2​ have supplies 303030 and 202020, and the customers need 151515, 202020 and 151515. The thick edges are the routes in use, labelled amount @ unit cost: P1P_1P1​ sends 151515 to C1C_1C1​ and 151515 to C3C_3C3​, and P2P_2P2​ sends 202020 to C2C_2C2​, for a total cost of 125125125. Each customer receives exactly its demand, and the thin routes carry nothing.

Every variable appears in exactly two constraints, the supply constraint ∑jxij≤si\displaystyle \sum_jx_{ij}\le s_ij∑​xij​≤si​ of its origin and the demand constraint of its destination. Supplies use ≤\le≤ because a plant need not use all of its capacity. Adding the demand constraints shows that a feasible plan needs total supply at least total demand, and since every route may be used, this is also enough. When capacity exceeds demand, as in the week 2 data in the Examples, some capacity stays unused.

The model is an ordinary linear program, solvable with the simplex method or Excel Solver. The same bookkeeping returns later in the assignment problem, where every supply and every demand equals 111.

Key Results¶

  • The decision variables are shipment quantities on routes.
  • Plant capacities are upper bounds rather than mandatory production amounts.
  • With positive unit costs, allowing deliveries above demand (≥dj\ge d_j≥dj​ instead of =dj=d_j=dj​) cannot improve the optimum.
