    Objective Line¶

    Definition¶

    For a two-variable linear objective z=c1x1+c2x2z=c_1x_1+c_2x_2z=c1​x1​+c2​x2​ with at least one nonzero coefficient, an objective line is the set of points giving a fixed value z=zˉz=\bar zz=zˉ. If c2≠0c_2\ne0c2​=0, its slope-intercept form is

    x2=−c1c2x1+zˉc2.x_2=-\frac{c_1}{c_2}x_1+\frac{\bar z}{c_2}.x2​=−c2​c1​​x1​+c2​zˉ​.

    Interpretation¶

    Changing the objective value translates the line without changing its slope. This is the geometric basis of the graphical solution method.

    Key Results¶

    • All constant-objective lines for a fixed objective are parallel.
    • If c2<0c_2<0c2​<0, a larger objective value lowers the vertical intercept.
    • When c2=0c_2=0c2​=0, the objective lines are vertical.

    • z=x1+2x2z=x_1+2x_2z=x1​+2x2​ gives x2=−x1/2+z/2x_2=-x_1/2+z/2x2​=−x1​/2+z/2.
