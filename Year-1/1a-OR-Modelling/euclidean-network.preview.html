Euclidean Network¶

Definition¶

In the course's Euclidean network, cities have plane coordinates (xi,yi)(x_i,y_i)(xi​,yi​), an edge may join every pair of cities, and its length is

dij=(xi−xj)2+(yi−yj)2.d_{ij}=\sqrt{(x_i-x_j)^2+(y_i-y_j)^2}.dij​=(xi​−xj​)2+(yi​−yj​)2​.

The resulting distances provide the weights for the minimum spanning tree problem.

Interpretation¶

Coordinates turn a location problem into a complete weighted network. Distances are straight-line lengths rather than road travel times.

Key Results¶

  • Distances are symmetric: dij=djid_{ij}=d_{ji}dij​=dji​.
  • Use sufficient precision when comparing close distances.
  • The usual spanning-tree algorithms apply once the distance matrix is computed.

Examples¶

  • Between (13,6)(13,6)(13,6) and (15,9)(15,9)(15,9) the distance is 13≈3.61\sqrt{13}\approx3.6113​≈3.61.
  • The seven-city example has tree length about 27.29 using the displayed rounded distances.
