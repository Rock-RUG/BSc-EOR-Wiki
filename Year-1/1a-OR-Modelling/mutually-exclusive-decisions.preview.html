Mutually Exclusive Decisions¶

Definition¶

For binary variables y1,…,yky_1,\ldots,y_ky1​,…,yk​, mutually exclusive decisions allow at most one variable to equal 1:

∑j=1kyj≤1.\sum_{j=1}^ky_j\le1.j=1∑k​yj​≤1.

If one alternative must be chosen, use ∑j=1kyj=1\sum_{j=1}^ky_j=1∑j=1k​yj​=1 instead.

Interpretation¶

The inequality permits choosing none, while the equality requires one choice. This distinction matters when translating phrases such as “at most one” and “exactly one.”

Key Results¶

  • Binarity is essential to interpret the sum as the number of yes decisions.
  • Exactly one implies at most one, but the reverse is false.
  • This restriction concerns decisions, not an either-or condition on continuous inequalities.

Examples¶

  • WLA+WSF≤1W_{LA}+W_{SF}\le1WLA​+WSF​≤1 permits at most one warehouse.
  • y1+y2+y3=1y_1+y_2+y_3=1y1​+y2​+y3​=1 selects exactly one allowed quantity value.
