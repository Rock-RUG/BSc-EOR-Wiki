Interpretation¶

The theorem (Nash, 1950–1951) guarantees that the Nash equilibrium concept never makes an empty prediction in a finite game, provided mixed strategies are allowed. It says nothing about how many equilibria there are, which one is played, or whether any equilibrium is in pure strategies.

Beyond the course

Textbooks prove the theorem with a fixed-point theorem (Kakutani's or Brouwer's) applied to the players' best responses. The course uses the statement and its consequences; the proof is not part of the course.

Finiteness is a sufficient condition, not a necessary one. Many games with infinite strategy sets, such as the Cournot duopoly, have equilibria, but some do not (see the last example).

Key Results¶

  • Existence holds for every finite game; uniqueness does not (coordination games have several equilibria).
  • Existence of a pure-strategy equilibrium is not guaranteed: Matching Pennies has none.
  • Both finiteness assumptions (players and strategies) appear in the statement; dropping finiteness of SiS_iSi​ without further conditions can destroy existence.
  • The result extends to existence of subgame-perfect equilibria in finite extensive-form games with perfect recall and of Bayesian Nash equilibria in finite Bayesian games, in mixed strategies.
