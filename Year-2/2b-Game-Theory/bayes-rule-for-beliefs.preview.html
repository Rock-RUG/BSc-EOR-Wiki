Bayes Rule for Beliefs¶

Method¶

Let (σ,μ)(\sigma,\mu)(σ,μ) be an assessment: a strategy profile σ\sigmaσ and a system of beliefs μ\muμ. For an information set hhh and a node x∈hx\in hx∈h, write Pσ(x)\mathbb{P}_\sigma(x)Pσ​(x) for the probability that play reaches xxx when Nature moves according to the common prior and players follow σ\sigmaσ. Interpretation¶

This is Bayes' rule applied to the nodes of an information set: the prior over types is updated with the probability that each type takes the observed action. The denominator is the total probability of the observed action, by the law of total probability.

The figure draws the mixed-strategy example below. The widths of the two strips are the prior probabilities p=0.2p=0.2p=0.2 and 1−p=0.81-p=0.81−p=0.8, and the shaded height in each strip is that type's entry probability, qC=1q_C=1qC​=1 and qW=12q_W=\tfrac{1}{2}qW​=21​. Observing entry means play is somewhere in the shaded area, of total size 0.2+0.4=0.60.2+0.4=0.60.2+0.4=0.6; the posterior μ\muμ is the share of that area belonging to type CCC, namely 13\tfrac{1}{3}31​.

The new element compared with Year-1 probability is that the "likelihoods" qC,qWq_C,q_WqC​,qW​ are not given by nature but chosen in equilibrium: they come from the players' strategies, possibly a mixed strategy. Beliefs and strategies are therefore determined jointly, and a change in one type's behaviour changes what the other side infers.

A common mistake is to apply the formula at an information set that is reached with probability zero, or to conclude that off-path beliefs must equal the prior. Neither is implied: Bayes' rule simply has nothing to say there.

Key Results¶

  • If all types take the observed action with the same probability (qC​=qC=qW>0q_C=q_W>0qW​>0), the posterior equals the prior: μ=p\mu=pμ=p. This is the pooling case.
  • If only CCC takes the action, with qW=0q_W=0qW​=0 and qCp>0q_Cp>0qC​p>0, then μ=1\mu=1μ=1; if only WWW takes it, with qC=0q_C=0qC​=0 and qW(1−p)>0q_W(1-p)>0qW​(1−p)>0, then μ=0\mu=0μ=0. The observed action then identifies its taker's type. This alone need not make the whole strategy profile separating: separation requires the types' equilibrium actions to distinguish them.
  • If the action is taken with probability 000 by all types, μ\muμ is unrestricted by Bayes' rule.
  • For 0<p<10<p<10<p<1 and a positive denominator, the posterior is nondecreasing in qCq_CqC​ and nonincreasing in qWq_WqW​; it is strictly monotone in both when qC>0q_C>0qC​>0 and qW>0q_W>0qW​>0.
  • In a perfect Bayesian equilibrium, Bayes' rule is required at every information set reached with positive probability.
