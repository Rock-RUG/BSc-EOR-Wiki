Stage Game¶

Definition¶

A stage game is the simultaneous-move normal-form game

G=⟨N, {Ai}i∈N, {vi}i∈N⟩G=\big\langle N,\ \{A_i\}_{i\in N},\ \{v_i\}_{i\in N}\big\rangleG=⟨N, {Ai​}i∈N​, {vi​}i∈N​⟩

that is played once in every period of a repeated game, or in one stage of a multistage game. Here N={1,…,n}N=\{1,\dots,n\}N={1,…,n} is the set of players, AiA_iAi​ is player iii's set of stage actions, A=A1×⋯×AnA=A_1\times\cdots\times A_nA=A1​×⋯×An​ is the set of action profiles and vi ⁣:A→Rv_i\colon A\to\mathbb{R}vi​:A→R is player iii's stage payoff. A Nash equilibrium of GGG (pure or mixed) is called a stage-game Nash equilibrium.

Interpretation¶

The stage game is the building block; the repeated game adds time, observation of past play and a way of adding up payoffs. A strategy in the stage game is a single action, whereas a strategy in the repeated game is a whole plan that may condition on past play. The two must not be confused: an action profile that is not a stage-game equilibrium (cooperation in the Prisoner's Dilemma) can still be played on the equilibrium path of the repeated game.

Stage-game equilibria are always available as credible continuation play, so they serve as rewards or punishments in the repeated game.

Key Results¶

  • Playing a fixed stage-game Nash equilibrium in every period, whatever happened before, is a subgame-perfect equilibrium of the repeated game, finite or infinite, for every discount factor δ\deltaδ.
  • If GGG has a unique Nash equilibrium and is repeated finitely often, that history-independent repetition is the unique subgame-perfect equilibrium.
  • The set of feasible payoffs used in the folk theorem is the set of convex combinations of the stage payoff vectors v(a)v(a)v(a), a∈Aa\in Aa∈A.
