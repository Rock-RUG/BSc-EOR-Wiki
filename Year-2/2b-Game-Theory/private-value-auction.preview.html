Private-Value Auction¶

Definition¶

In a private-value auction, one indivisible object is sold to one of nnn bidders. Bidder iii has a valuation θi\theta_iθi​ for the object that is known only to iii and that does not depend on the information of the other bidders. Interpretation¶

An auction with private values is a Bayesian game: bidder iii's type is θi\theta_iθi​, the distribution FFF plays the role of the common prior, and a strategy is a bidding function bi(θi)b_i(\theta_i)bi​(θi​) giving a bid for every possible valuation. The solution concept is Bayesian Nash equilibrium: every type's bid maximizes its expected payoff given the rivals' bidding functions.

A strategy is the whole line, one bid for every type, not a single number: in the second-price auction each type bids its value, in the first-price auction each type shades its bid to half its value. The type θ1=0.7\theta_1=0.7θ1​=0.7 therefore bids 0.70.70.7 or 0.350.350.35, yet both bids give the same interim expected payoff 0.2450.2450.245.

"Private" refers to two things: each bidder knows its own value, and learning the rivals' information would not change that value. This is what distinguishes it from a common-value auction, where the rivals' signals are informative about one's own value. A frequent confusion is to think that private values must differ across bidders; they may coincide, the point is only that each value is a matter of personal taste rather than an estimate of a shared quantity.

The format matters for strategy but, under the conditions of the revenue equivalence theorem, not for expected revenue.
