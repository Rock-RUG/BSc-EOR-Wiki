Dutch Auction¶

Definition¶

In a Dutch auction the price starts high and falls continuously (for example on a clock). The first bidder to stop the clock wins the object and pays the current price; the auction then ends. A strategy specifies, for each valuation θi\theta_iθi​, the price at which the bidder will stop the clock if nobody has stopped it before.

The Dutch auction is strategically equivalent to the first-price auction: the two games have the same strategy sets (a planned stopping price is a bid) and the same mapping from strategy profiles to outcomes (the highest planned price wins and is paid).

Interpretation¶

The equivalence is easiest to see by writing the Dutch auction as a normal-form game. A strategy in an extensive-form game must say what to do at every point where the bidder could act. But the only information a bidder ever receives before the auction ends is that no one has stopped yet; once someone stops, the auction is over. So a complete plan reduces to one number: the price at which to stop. Bidder iii planning to stop at bib_ibi​ wins if and only if bib_ibi​ is the highest planned stopping price, and then pays bib_ibi​. That is exactly the first-price sealed-bid rule.

The bidder faces the same trade-off as in a sealed first-price bid: waiting lowers the price paid but raises the risk that a rival stops first.

Unlike the English–second-price equivalence, the Dutch–first-price equivalence does not require private values. Because no rival's action is observed during a Dutch auction until it ends, the equivalence also holds with common values or risk-averse bidders.

Key Results¶

  • The Dutch and first-price sealed-bid auctions are strategically equivalent under any assumptions on values and risk attitudes.
  • With i.i.d. Unif⁡(0,1)\operatorname{Unif}(0,1)Unif(0,1) private values and nnn risk-neutral bidders, the symmetric equilibrium stopping price is n−1nθ\frac{n-1}{n}\thetann−1​θ and expected revenue is n−1n+1\frac{n-1}{n+1}n+1n−1​.
  • There is no weakly dominant stopping rule: the best stopping price depends on the rivals' stopping prices.
  • Under the conditions of the revenue equivalence theorem, the Dutch auction gives the same expected revenue as the English and second-price auctions.
