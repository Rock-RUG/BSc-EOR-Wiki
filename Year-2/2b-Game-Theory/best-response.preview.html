Best Response¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

A best response is what a rational agent does once a belief about the others has been formed: the opponents' play is treated as given and the player solves an ordinary maximization problem. With a probabilistic belief this is expected payoff maximization.

A best response is defined relative to s−is_{-i}s−i​; it changes when s−is_{-i}s−i​ changes. If the belief is wrong, the chosen strategy may not be optimal against what is actually played. Nash equilibrium is the situation in which every player best responds to the strategies the others actually use.

Best responses need not be unique: with ties, all maximizers are best responses, collected in the best-response correspondence BRi(s−i)BR_i(s_{-i})BRi​(s−i​).

Key Results¶

  • If SiS_iSi​ is finite, a best response exists for every s−is_{-i}s−i​. If SiS_iSi​ is compact and vi(⋅,s−i)v_i(\cdot,s_{-i})vi​(⋅,s−i​) is continuous, existence follows from the extreme value theorem on compact sets; otherwise a best response may fail to exist (undercutting in a Bertrand duopoly).
  • A strictly dominated strategy is never a best response, to any s−is_{-i}s−i​ or any belief.
  • A strictly dominant strategy is the unique best response to every s−is_{-i}s−i​.
  • If SiS_iSi​ is an interval and vi(⋅,s−i)v_i(\cdot,s_{-i})vi​(⋅,s−i​) is differentiable and concave, every interior point satisfying the first-order condition ∂vi/∂si=0\partial v_i/\partial s_i=0∂vi​/∂si​=0 is a best response; if no interior point satisfies it, check the endpoints.
