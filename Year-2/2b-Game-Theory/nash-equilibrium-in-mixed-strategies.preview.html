Nash Equilibrium in Mixed Strategies¶

Definition¶

In a finite normal-form game with players i∈Ni\in Ni∈N, a profile of mixed strategies σ∗=(σ1∗,…,σn∗)\sigma^*=(\sigma_1^*,\ldots,\sigma_n^*)σ∗=(σ1∗​,…,σn∗​) with σi∗∈ΔSi\sigma_i^*\in\Delta S_iσi∗​∈ΔSi​ is a Nash equilibrium if, for every player iii and every σi∈ΔSi\sigma_i\in\Delta S_iσi​∈ΔSi​,

vi(σi∗,σ−i∗) ≥ vi(σi,σ−i∗).v_i(\sigma_i^*,\sigma_{-i}^*)\ \ge\ v_i(\sigma_i,\sigma_{-i}^*).vi​(σi∗​,σ−i∗​) ≥ vi​(σi​,σ−i∗​).

Equivalently, σi∗∈BRi(σ−i∗)\sigma_i^*\in BR_i(\sigma_{-i}^*)σi∗​∈BRi​(σ−i∗​) for every iii, where BRiBR_iBRi​ is the best-response correspondence extended to mixed strategies of the opponents.

Interpretation¶

The definition is the Nash equilibrium condition applied to the mixed extension of the game: no player can raise her expected payoff by a unilateral change of mixture. In the belief interpretation, σ−i∗\sigma_{-i}^*σ−i∗​ describes player iii's correct conjecture about her opponents, and each player best responds to that conjecture.

The jump of BR1BR_1BR1​ sits at a value set by player 2's mixture, and the jump of BR2BR_2BR2​ at one set by player 1's, which is why each player's equilibrium probability is fixed by the opponent's indifference.

Each player chooses a mixture that maximizes her own expected payoff against the opponents' mixtures. To compute a fully mixed equilibrium of a 2×22\times22×2 game, however, player 1's indifference equation constrains player 2's probability, and player 2's equation constrains player 1's. When these equations are nontrivial, they determine the probabilities. They need not have unique solutions in a degenerate game: if every payoff is zero, both equations are identities and every pair of mixtures is an equilibrium.

Key Results¶

  • Every pure-strategy Nash equilibrium is a mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium with degenerate mixtures.
  • It suffices to check pure deviations: σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ is a Nash equilibrium if and only if vi(σ∗)≥vi(si,σ−i∗)v_i(\sigma^*)\ge v_i(s_i,\sigma_{-i}^*)vi​(σ∗)≥vi​(si​,σ−i∗​) for all iii and all si∈Sis_i\in S_isi​∈Si​.
  • Every pure strategy in the support of σi∗\sigma_i^*σi∗​ earns exactly vi(σ∗)v_i(\sigma^*)vi​(σ∗) against σ−i∗\sigma_{-i}^*σ−i∗​, and no pure strategy earns more (the indifference principle).
  • A pure strategy that is strictly dominated (by a pure or a mixed strategy) has probability 000 in every mixed-strategy Nash equilibrium.
  • Every finite game has at least one Nash equilibrium in mixed strategies (Nash existence theorem).

Examples¶

Matching Pennies. Player 1 wins 111 on a match and loses 111 otherwise; player 2 gets the negative. There is no pure equilibrium: at every cell one player wants to switch. If player 2 plays HHH with probability qqq, player 1 is indifferent when 2q−1=1−2q2q-1=1-2q2q−1=1−2q, so q=12q=\tfrac{1}{2}q=21​; symmetrically p=12p=\tfrac{1}{2}p=21​.   • Player 2 indifferent: 1⋅p=2(1−p)1\cdot p=2(1-p)1⋅p=2(1−p), so p=23p=\tfrac{2}{3}p=32​.

The game has three Nash equilibria: (O,O)(O,O)(O,O), (F,F)(F,F)(F,F) and the mixed profile p=23p=\tfrac{2}{3}p=32​, q=13q=\tfrac{1}{3}q=31​.
