Subgame-Perfect Nash Equilibrium¶

Definition¶

A profile σ∗=(σ1∗,…,σn∗)\sigma^*=(\sigma_1^*,\ldots,\sigma_n^*)σ∗=(σ1∗​,…,σn∗​) of strategies in an extensive-form game Γ\GammaΓ is a subgame-perfect Nash equilibrium (SPNE) if, for every subgame Γ′\Gamma'Γ′ of Γ\GammaΓ, the restriction of σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ to Γ′\Gamma'Γ′ is a Nash equilibrium of Γ′\Gamma'Γ′.

Since Γ\GammaΓ is a subgame of itself, every SPNE is a Nash equilibrium of Γ\GammaΓ.

Interpretation¶

SPNE (Selten, 1965) refines Nash equilibrium by removing non-credible threats. In a Nash equilibrium, a player's prescribed behaviour at an unreached information set does not affect her payoff, so it may be irrational there. Subgame perfection requires optimal behaviour in every subgame, on or off the equilibrium path, which is a form of sequential rationality.

In the figure (the entry game below), the incumbent's decision after In is a subgame of its own. There Accommodate gives the incumbent 111 and Fight gives −1-1−1, so the only Nash equilibrium of the subgame is AAA, and the threat to play FFF is not credible. The profile (Out, FFF) is a Nash equilibrium of the whole game but prescribes FFF in this subgame, so it is not subgame perfect; the green path is the unique SPNE (In, AAA).

An SPNE is a profile of complete strategies, not a path of play. In the plain entry game each player has a single decision node, so (In, AAA) is already a complete strategy profile and the SPNE. In larger games, where a player moves at several information sets (as player 1 does in the outside-option example below), writing only the path of play does not specify an SPNE; the answer must state what every player does at every information set, including those off the equilibrium path.

The refinement has bite only when the game has subgames other than itself. When information sets prevent this (for example, an uninformed player moving after a private move), Nash equilibria with implausible behaviour can survive; perfect Bayesian equilibrium addresses this with beliefs.

Key Results¶

  • SPNE ⊆\subseteq⊆ Nash equilibria; the inclusion can be strict.
  • In a finite game of perfect information, the pure-strategy SPNE are exactly the profiles found by backward induction; a pure-strategy SPNE exists. Without Nature, distinct payoffs for each player at different terminal nodes ensure uniqueness. With Nature, first average at chance nodes; a unique maximizing action at every player node in the backward calculation ensures uniqueness.
  • Every finite extensive-form game with perfect recall has an SPNE, possibly in mixed or behavioural strategies: apply the Nash existence theorem to the smallest subgames, replace each by its equilibrium payoffs, and repeat (generalized backward induction).
  • Different equilibria of a subgame can lead to different SPNE of the whole game.
  • If the only subgame is Γ\GammaΓ itself, SPNE and Nash equilibrium coincide.
