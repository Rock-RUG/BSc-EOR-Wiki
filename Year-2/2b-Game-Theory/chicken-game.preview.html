Chicken Game¶

Definition¶

The chicken game is a symmetric two-player normal-form game in which each player chooses SSS (swerve, yield) or TTT (go straight, stand firm), and each player's payoffs satisfy

vi(T,S)>vi(S,S)>vi(S,T)>vi(T,T).\begin{aligned} v_i(T,S)&>v_i(S,S)\\ &>v_i(S,T)>v_i(T,T). \end{aligned}vi​(T,S)​>vi​(S,S)>vi​(S,T)>vi​(T,T).​

Standing firm is best if the other yields, yielding is best if the other stands firm, and mutual standing firm is the worst outcome. A standard numerical version:

Interpretation¶

Chicken is an anti-coordination game: each player wants to do the opposite of the other. It models standoffs such as two drivers heading for each other, price wars, or a confrontation in which backing down is costly but collision is disastrous. In biology the same structure is called the hawk–dove game.

Compare it with the Prisoner's Dilemma. There, defecting is best whatever the other does, so there is a single equilibrium. In chicken, no action is dominant, (T,T)(T,T)(T,T) is worse than being the one who yields, and the game has three equilibria. The theory alone does not say which one is played; the two pure equilibria require the players to know who yields.

Chicken is a standard example for purification: if each player's toughness, that is the payoff from standing firm, is slightly private, the mixed equilibrium can be reinterpreted as pure behaviour by different types, and the game becomes a Bayesian game.

Key Results¶

  • The pure Nash equilibria are (S,T)(S,T)(S,T) and (T,S)(T,S)(T,S); (S,S)(S,S)(S,S) and (T,T)(T,T)(T,T) are not equilibria.
  • There is exactly one further Nash equilibrium in mixed strategies, symmetric, in which each player chooses TTT with the probability that makes the other indifferent.
  • No action is strictly dominated, so iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies removes nothing.
  • The mixed equilibrium is Pareto-dominated by (S,S)(S,S)(S,S), which is not an equilibrium: it is not Pareto efficient.
