Iterated Elimination of Strictly Dominated Strategies¶

Method¶

  2. Delete these strategies, giving Sik⊆Sik−1S_i^{k}\subseteq S_i^{k-1}Sik​⊆Sik−1​.
  3. Repeat until no player has a strictly dominated strategy in the reduced game.

The profiles that remain form the IESDS outcome. If exactly one profile remains, the game is dominance solvable and that profile is the IESDS prediction.

Interpretation¶

Round 1 removes what no rational player would play. Later rounds use reasoning about others: once player 1 knows player 2 will not use a deleted strategy, some strategy of player 1 may become dominated in the smaller game even though it was not dominated in the original game. Round kkk needs rationality plus k−1k-1k−1 levels of "everyone knows that ..." about rationality, so common knowledge of rationality justifies running the procedure to the end.

The figure shows Example 1 after the procedure has finished. Each grey strategy carries the round in which it was deleted: RRR first (dominated by MMM for player 2), then BBB (dominated by TTT only once RRR is gone), then LLL. The order of the labels is the chain of reasoning: deleting BBB needs player 1 to know that player 2 is rational, and deleting LLL needs player 2 to know that too. The surviving cell (T,M)(T,M)(T,M) with payoffs (3,2)(3,2)(3,2) is the IESDS prediction.

The procedure checks dominance in the current reduced game; checking against the original game misses later deletions. It also never uses beliefs, so when nothing is dominated it makes no prediction at all.

Key Results¶

  • In a finite game the IESDS outcome does not depend on the order of deletion or on how many dominated strategies are removed in each round.
  • Every Nash equilibrium survives IESDS.
  • In a finite game, if IESDS leaves a single profile, that profile is the unique Nash equilibrium.
  • If every player has a strictly dominant strategy, one round leaves exactly the strictly dominant strategy equilibrium.
  • IESDS can delete nothing: in Battle of the Sexes and Matching Pennies no strategy is strictly dominated.
  • For weak dominance (see weakly dominant strategy) the result can depend on the order of deletion, which is one reason the procedure is stated with strict dominance.
