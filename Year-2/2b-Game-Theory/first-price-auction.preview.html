First-Price Auction¶

Definition¶

In a first-price sealed-bid auction, each of nnn bidders submits a bid bi≥0b_i\ge0bi​≥0 simultaneously and without seeing the others' bids. The highest bid wins (ties are broken at random) and the winner pays her own bid; losers pay nothing. Interpretation¶

Raising the bid has two opposing effects: it raises the probability of winning and lowers the surplus θi−bi\theta_i-b_iθi​−bi​ obtained when winning. Bidding one's value guarantees a payoff of 000, so in equilibrium every bidder shades her bid below her value. The amount of shading, θ/n\theta/nθ/n in the uniform case, falls as competition increases.

Unlike the second-price auction, the first-price auction has no dominant strategy: the best bid depends on what the rivals bid, so a bidder needs beliefs about the rivals' values and strategies. This is why the equilibrium is computed as a BNE rather than by a dominance argument.

A useful reading of the equilibrium bid: a bidder bids the expected highest rival value, conditional on that value being below her own. In the uniform case the highest of n−1n-1n−1 rival values, given that all are below θ\thetaθ, has expectation n−1nθ\frac{n-1}{n}\thetann−1​θ, which is exactly b(θ)b(\theta)b(θ).
