Harsanyi Purification Theorem¶

Theorem¶

Informal statement (Harsanyi, 1973). Let GGG be a finite normal-form game with payoffs vi(a)v_i(a)vi​(a), and let σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ be a Nash equilibrium in mixed strategies of GGG. For ε>0\varepsilon>0ε>0 consider the perturbed Bayesian game G(ε)G(\varepsilon)G(ε) in which player iii's payoff is

vi(a)+ε θia,v_i(a)+\varepsilon\,\theta_i^{a},vi​(a)+εθia​,

where the perturbation vector θi=(θia)a∈A\theta_i=(\theta_i^a)_{a\in A}θi​=(θia​)a∈A​ is player iii's privately observed type, drawn from a distribution with a continuous density, independently across players. Then, for generic GGG (more precisely, for every regular equilibrium σ∗\sigma^*σ∗), there are equilibria of G(ε)G(\varepsilon)G(ε) such that

  • almost every type of every player has a unique best action, so the equilibrium is in pure strategies, and
  • the probability with which each player's equilibrium strategy, viewed from outside, chooses each action converges to σi∗\sigma_i^*σi∗​ as ε→0\varepsilon\to0ε→0.

So every such mixed equilibrium is the limit of pure-strategy Bayesian Nash equilibria of slightly perturbed games of incomplete information.

Interpretation¶

In a mixed equilibrium each player is indifferent between the actions she mixes over, so it is unclear why she would randomize with exactly the equilibrium probabilities. Purification answers: she need not randomize at all. Small private payoff differences break her indifference one way or the other; an opponent who does not observe them sees a player whose action is random, with probabilities that approach the mixed equilibrium.

The limiting probabilities are pinned down by the opponents' incentives, not by the player's own. In the perturbed game, player jjj's probability of an action must leave player iii's cutoff type exactly indifferent; as ε→0\varepsilon\to0ε→0 this forces it towards the value that makes iii indifferent in GGG, as in the indifference principle for mixed strategies.

The theorem is about limits of equilibria of nearby games, not about every perturbation of every game; nongeneric games can have mixed equilibria that cannot be purified.

Key Results¶

  • Pure strategies in G(ε)G(\varepsilon)G(ε) are type-contingent: typically a cutoff rule "choose action xxx if and only if θi\theta_iθi​ is above a threshold".
  • The induced action distribution of player iii converges to σi∗\sigma_i^*σi∗​ as ε→0\varepsilon\to0ε→0.
  • Pure Nash equilibria of GGG that are strict are also approximated by equilibria of G(ε)G(\varepsilon)G(ε), in which almost all types play the pure action.
  • In the theorem the perturbations are private (each player observes only her own θi\theta_iθi​) and independent across players; these are hypotheses, and the conclusion is not claimed without them.
