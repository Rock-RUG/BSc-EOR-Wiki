Indifference Principle for Mixed Strategies¶

Proposition¶

Let σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ be a profile of mixed strategies in a finite normal-form game, and let Si+={si∈Si ⁣:σi∗(si)>0}S_i^+=\{s_i\in S_i\colon\sigma_i^*(s_i)>0\}Si+​={si​∈Si​:σi∗​(si​)>0} be the support of σi∗\sigma_i^*σi∗​. Then σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ is a Nash equilibrium in mixed strategies if and only if, for every player iii, there is a number vˉi\bar v_ivˉi​ such that

vi(si,σ−i∗)=vˉifor all si∈Si+,vi(si′,σ−i∗)≤vˉifor all si′∉Si+.\begin{aligned} v_i(s_i,\sigma_{-i}^*)&=\bar v_i &&\text{for all } s_i\in S_i^+,\\ v_i(s_i',\sigma_{-i}^*)&\le \bar v_i &&\text{for all } s_i'\notin S_i^+. \end{aligned}vi​(si​,σ−i∗​)vi​(si′​,σ−i∗​)​=vˉi​≤vˉi​​​for all si​∈Si+​,for all si′​∈/Si+​.​

In that case vˉi=vi(σ∗)\bar v_i=v_i(\sigma^*)vˉi​=vi​(σ∗) is player iii's equilibrium payoff.

Interpretation¶

The reason is that vi(σi∗,σ−i∗)=∑siσi∗(si) vi(si,σ−i∗)\displaystyle v_i(\sigma_i^*,\sigma_{-i}^*)=\sum_{s_i}\sigma_i^*(s_i)\,v_i(s_i,\sigma_{-i}^*)vi​(σi∗​,σ−i∗​)=si​∑​σi∗​(si​)vi​(si​,σ−i∗​) is a weighted average. If one strategy in the support earned less than another, an expected payoff maximizer could move its probability to the better one and gain. So all strategies used must tie, and none unused may do better.

Player 1 is willing to mix only where the two lines cross, at q=12q=\tfrac{1}{2}q=21​ with value vˉ1=32\bar v_1=\tfrac{3}{2}vˉ1​=23​; this is how player 1's indifference pins down player 2's probability. The flat lines are the extra row of the second example: BBB paying 111 stays below 32\tfrac{3}{2}23​, so the equilibrium survives, while a BBB paying 222 lies above it and violates the second condition.

In practice the conditions are used in reverse: player jjj's mixing probabilities are found by solving player iii's indifference equations. The usual error is to solve a player's own indifference for her own probabilities; the equations for σj\sigma_jσj​ come from player iii's payoffs.

Indifference is not the whole test. The second line (no profitable strategy outside the support) must also be checked, and a strategy outside the support may earn exactly vˉi\bar v_ivˉi​. Indifference also means the player has no strict reason to use the equilibrium weights; the Harsanyi purification theorem gives one justification for them.

Key Results¶

  • The two conditions are necessary and sufficient for σ∗\sigma^*σ∗ to be a Nash equilibrium.
  • The equilibrium payoff vi(σ∗)v_i(\sigma^*)vi​(σ∗) can be computed from any single pure strategy in the support.
  • A strictly dominated strategy never lies in the support of an equilibrium strategy.
  • In a 2×22\times 22×2 game with no pure-strategy equilibrium, there is a unique equilibrium; it is fully mixed, and each player's probability solves the other player's indifference equation.
  • Finding equilibria by support enumeration: guess the supports, solve the indifference equations, then check that probabilities lie in [0,1][0,1][0,1] and that no strategy outside the support does better.

Examples¶

A 2×22\times 22×2 game. Payoffs (player 1, player 2):
