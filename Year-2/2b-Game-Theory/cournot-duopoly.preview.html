Cournot Duopoly¶

Definition¶

In a Cournot duopoly two firms produce a homogeneous good and simultaneously choose quantities q1,q2≥0q_1,q_2\ge0q1​,q2​≥0. In the standard linear version:

Interpretation¶

Each firm's profit depends on the rival's quantity through the common price: more output by the rival lowers the price and makes one's own output less valuable. A firm's best response therefore falls as the rival's quantity rises.

Firm 1's reaction line q1=(90−q2)/2q_1=(90-q_2)/2q1​=(90−q2​)/2 gives its profit-maximizing output for each q2q_2q2​, and firm 2's line q2=(90−q1)/2q_2=(90-q_1)/2q2​=(90−q1​)/2 does the same with the roles swapped; both slope downwards because a larger rival output lowers one's own best output. The only point on both lines is (30,30)(30,30)(30,30), where each quantity is a best response to the other, so it is the Nash equilibrium.

The equilibrium is found by computing both best responses and solving them together, which is the definition of a Nash equilibrium applied to continuous strategies. Taking the partial derivative of viv_ivi​ with respect to the firm's own quantity, holding the rival's fixed, is the key step; differentiating the sum or the rival's quantity is a common error.

The equilibrium lies between monopoly and perfect competition: the firms together produce more than a monopolist, but less than the competitive output at which price equals marginal cost.
