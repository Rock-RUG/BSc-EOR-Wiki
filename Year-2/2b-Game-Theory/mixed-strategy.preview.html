Mixed Strategy¶

Definition¶

Let player iii in a normal-form game have a finite set of pure strategies Si={si1,…,sik}S_i=\{s_{i1},\ldots,s_{ik}\}Si​={si1​,…,sik​}. A mixed strategy for player iii is a probability distribution σi\sigma_iσi​ on SiS_iSi​: a function σi ⁣:Si→[0,1]\sigma_i\colon S_i\to[0,1]σi​:Si​→[0,1] with

∑si∈Siσi(si)=1.\sum_{s_i\in S_i}\sigma_i(s_i)=1.si​∈Si​∑​σi​(si​)=1.

The set of all mixed strategies of player iii is written ΔSi\Delta S_iΔSi​. The support of σi\sigma_iσi​ is the set of pure strategies with σi(si)>0\sigma_i(s_i)>0σi​(si​)>0. A pure strategy sis_isi​ is the degenerate mixed strategy that puts probability 111 on sis_isi​. When SiS_iSi​ is an interval (for example, prices or bids), a mixed strategy is given by a cumulative distribution function on SiS_iSi​.

A mixed-strategy profile σ=(σ1,…,σn)\sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_n)σ=(σ1​,…,σn​) assumes that players randomize independently. Player iii's payoff is the expectation

Interpretation¶

A mixed strategy can be read literally, as a player who uses a randomizing device, or as a description of the opponents' uncertainty about what player iii will do. Either reading leads to the same calculations; it relies on players being expected payoff maximizers, so that only expected payoffs matter.

Mixing never lets a player beat her own best pure strategy against fixed opponents. Because the expected payoff is a weighted average of the payoffs of the pure strategies in the support, it is at most the largest of them. Randomization matters for a different reason: it changes what the opponents face, which is why games such as Matching Pennies have an equilibrium only in mixed strategies.

The independence assumption matters: a mixed-strategy profile cannot describe correlated play such as "both players follow the same coin toss".
