M/M/1/K Queue¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

A finite waiting room turns excess demand into lost jobs instead of an ever longer queue. Because the state space is finite, a stationary distribution exists for every ρ\rhoρ, also when λ>μ\lambda>\muλ>μ; the price is a gap between the offered arrival rate λ\lambdaλ and the rate λ+\lambda^+λ+ at which jobs actually enter.

The bars are the barber of Example 1, with ρ=1.2\rho=1.2ρ=1.2. Because ρ>1\rho>1ρ>1, the probabilities increase with nnn and the largest mass sits at the capacity: the orange bar p(K)p(K)p(K) is the fraction of time the shop is full, 0.3220.3220.322 for K=3K=3K=3 and 0.2310.2310.231 for K=6K=6K=6. Doubling the capacity lowers the blocking probability by less than a third.

The fraction of arrivals that is blocked equals p(K)p(K)p(K) by PASTA: blocked customers still arrive at rate λ(K)=λ\lambda(K)=\lambdaλ(K)=λ, they only fail to enter, so π(K)=p(K)\pi(K)=p(K)π(K)=p(K). The level-crossing equation must use λ+(K)=0\lambda^+(K)=0λ+(K)=0, however, because blocked arrivals do not move the state up. For the same reason Little's law needs the admitted rate: E[W]=E[Q]/λ+\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[Q]/\lambda^+E[W]=E[Q]/λ+.

The normalizing sum is a finite geometric series. If ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 and K→∞K\to\inftyK→∞, the distribution tends to that of the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue. A common slip is to equate the utilization with ρ\rhoρ: the server works only for admitted jobs, so the utilization is λ(1−p(K))/μ<ρ\lambda(1-p(K))/\mu<\rhoλ(1−p(K))/μ<ρ.
