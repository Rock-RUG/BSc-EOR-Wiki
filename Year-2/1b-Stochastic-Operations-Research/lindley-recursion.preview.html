Lindley Recursion¶

Proposition¶

Consider a single server that serves jobs in order of arrival (FIFO) and works whenever a job is present. Let Xk=Ak−Ak−1X_k=A_k-A_{k-1}Xk​=Ak​−Ak−1​ be the inter-arrival time between jobs k−1k-1k−1 and kkk, and SkS_kSk​ the service time of job kkk. Then the waiting times satisfy

Wk=[Wk−1+Sk−1−Xk]+,k≥2,W_k=[W_{k-1}+S_{k-1}-X_k]^+,\qquad k\geq2,Wk​=[Wk−1​+Sk−1​−Xk​]+,k≥2,

with W1=0W_1=0W1​=0 when job 1 finds the system empty (the notes start from W0=0W_0=0W0​=0, which has no physical meaning), and the sojourn times are Jk=Wk+SkJ_k=W_k+S_kJk​=Wk​+Sk​. Equivalently, Jk=[Jk−1−Xk]++SkJ_k=[J_{k-1}-X_k]^+ +S_kJk​=[Jk−1​−Xk​]++Sk​ with J0=0J_0=0J0​=0.

Why. Job k−1k-1k−1 arrives at Ak−1A_{k-1}Ak−1​ and leaves at Dk−1=Ak−1+Wk−1+Sk−1D_{k-1}=A_{k-1}+W_{k-1}+S_{k-1}Dk−1​=Ak−1​+Wk−1​+Sk−1​. Job kkk arrives XkX_kXk​ later and, by the single-server service recursion, waits [Dk−1−Ak]+=[Wk−1+Sk−1−Xk]+[D_{k-1}-A_k]^+=[W_{k-1}+S_{k-1}-X_k]^+[Dk−1​−Ak​]+=[Wk−1​+Sk−1​−Xk​]+.

Interpretation¶

Job k−1k-1k−1 finds Wk−1W_{k-1}Wk−1​ of work ahead of it and adds its own service Sk−1S_{k-1}Sk−1​. During the next inter-arrival time XkX_kXk​ the server works this amount off at rate one. Whatever is left is the wait of job kkk; if the server finishes earlier, job kkk finds it idle and does not wait.

Both panels follow the workload of the five-job path in Example 1. On the left, job 2 arrives at time 2 and finds W2=2W_2=2W2​=2, which its own service lifts to J2=4J_2=4J2​=4. The work then falls with slope −1-1−1 for X3=3X_3=3X3​=3 time units, so job 3 finds W3=[2+2−3]+=1W_3=[2+2-3]^+=1W3​=[2+2−3]+=1. On the right, job 3 brings the work to J3=1+1=2J_3=1+1=2J3​=1+1=2, the work reaches zero after 2 units, and the server is idle for the rest of X4=4X_4=4X4​=4, so W4=[1+1−4]+=0W_4=[1+1-4]^+=0W4​=[1+1−4]+=0. The positive part cuts off the negative value 1+1−4=−21+1-4=-21+1−4=−2, whose size is exactly this idle time.

The derivation uses one server, FIFO order and service at rate one; with ccc servers each server needs its own workload (TF 3.3.5). When the XkX_kXk​ are i.i.d., the SkS_kSk​ are i.i.d. and both sequences are independent, Wk−1W_{k-1}Wk−1​ depends only on X2,…,Xk−1X_2,\ldots,X_{k-1}X2​,…,Xk−1​ and S1,…,Sk−2S_1,\ldots,S_{k-2}S1​,…,Sk−2​. Hence it is independent of Sk−1S_{k-1}Sk−1​ and XkX_kXk​, and the distribution of WkW_kWk​ can be computed step by step with the probabilistic arithmetic of Week 1.

The JJJ-form has the same shape as the discrete-time queue recursion Lk=[Lk−1−ck]++akL_k=[L_{k-1}-c_k]^+ +a_kLk​=[Lk−1​−ck​]++ak​: capacity is subtracted, the result is cut at zero, and new work is added. Optional Section 2.4 of the notes views such recursions as random walks kept from going below zero.

Key Results¶

  • Wk=[Wk−1+Sk−1−Xk]+=[Jk−1−Xk]+W_k=[W_{k-1}+S_{k-1}-X_k]^+=[J_{k-1}-X_k]^+Wk​=[Wk−1​+Sk−1​−Xk​]+=[Jk−1​−Xk​]+ and Jk=Wk+SkJ_k=W_k+S_kJk​=Wk​+Sk​, so either sequence gives the other (TF 3.3.2).
  • Distribution of WkW_kWk​. Under the independence assumptions above, the pmf of WkW_kWk​ follows from that of Wk−1W_{k-1}Wk−1​ by adding SSS, subtracting XXX and taking the positive part, starting from P(W1=0)=1\mathbb{P}(W_1=0)=1P(W1​=0)=1 (Algorithm 3.3.5).
  • No such shortcut for DkD_kDk​. AkA_kAk​ and WkW_kWk​ are negatively correlated, since a long XkX_kXk​ makes AkA_kAk​ large and WkW_kWk​ small. The distribution of Dk=Ak+Wk+SkD_k=A_k+W_k+S_kDk​=Ak​+Wk​+Sk​ therefore cannot be obtained by treating the three terms as independent.
  • Admission control. If job k−1k-1k−1 is rejected it brings no work, which gives Wk=[Wk−1+Ik−1Sk−1−Xk]+W_k=[W_{k-1}+I_{k-1}S_{k-1}-X_k]^+Wk​=[Wk−1​+Ik−1​Sk−1​−Xk​]+ with Ik−1∈{0,1}I_{k-1}\in\{0,1\}Ik−1​∈{0,1} the acceptance indicator (Algorithm 3.3.2); see waiting-time admission control.
  • Several servers. Track a vector of per-server workloads: subtract XkX_kXk​ from each, cut at zero, and add the service of job kkk to the server it joins (Algorithm 3.3.6).
  • Instability. If E[S]>E[X]\mathbb{E}[S]>\mathbb{E}[X]E[S]>E[X], work arrives faster than it can be served on average, and the distribution of WkW_kWk​ drifts to the right as kkk grows (Fig. 3.3.1 of the notes); compare rate stability.

3. Lindley in Python. In the style of Algorithm 3.3.2 without rejections:

def waiting_times(x, s):
    W = [0]                  # job 1 finds the system empty
    for k in range(1, len(x)):
        W.append(max(W[-1] + s[k - 1] - x[k], 0))
    return W

waiting_times([1, 1, 3, 4, 1], [3, 2, 1, 2, 2])   # [0, 2, 1, 0, 1]

With 0-based lists, x[k] is Xk+1X_{k+1}Xk+1​ and s[k - 1] is SkS_kSk​, so each pass computes Wk+1=[Wk+Sk−Xk+1]+W_{k+1}=[W_k+S_k-X_{k+1}]^+Wk+1​=[Wk​+Sk​−Xk+1​]+; the first inter-arrival time only fixes A1A_1A1​ and does not affect any wait.

This gives E[D30]≈164.18\mathbb{E}[D_{30}]\approx164.18E[D30​]≈164.18 with standard deviation 9.229.229.22.
