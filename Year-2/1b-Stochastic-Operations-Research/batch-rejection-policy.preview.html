Batch Rejection Policy¶

Definition¶

In a batch-arrival queue with a capacity level KKK, the MB/M/1/KM^B/M/1/KMB/M/1/K queue, a batch rejection policy decides which jobs of an arriving batch enter, given the number mmm of jobs the batch finds in the system. The notes consider three policies:

  1. Complete acceptance. Accept the whole batch if m≤K−1m\le K-1m≤K−1 and reject the whole batch if m≥Km\ge Km≥K. With batch sizes bounded by KBK_BKB​, the number in the system then satisfies L(t)≤K−1+KBL(t)\le K-1+K_BL(t)≤K−1+KB​.
  2. Partial acceptance. Accept what fits, min⁡{B,K−m}\min\{B,K-m\}min{B,K−m} jobs, and reject the rest, so that L(t)≤KL(t)\le KL(t)≤K.
  3. Complete rejection. Accept the batch if it fits entirely, m+B≤Km+B\le Km+B≤K, and reject it completely otherwise, so that L(t)≤KL(t)\le KL(t)≤K.

As before, batches arrive as a Poisson process with rate λB\lambda_BλB​, batch sizes are i.i.d. copies of BBB with survivor function G(i)=P(B>i)G(i)=\mathbb{P}(B>i)G(i)=P(B>i), and jobs are served one by one at rate μ\muμ.

Interpretation¶

With single arrivals there is only one sensible way to block: refuse a job when the system is full. A batch can overshoot the capacity, so one must decide whether to let it overshoot, cut it off, or refuse it altogether. Each choice bounds the state space, which also removes the need to truncate the infinite recursion of the MB/M/1M^B/M/1MB/M/1 queue.

The figure is Example 1: a batch of 555 jobs finds m=8m=8m=8 jobs and K=10K=10K=10; blue squares are admitted, orange crossed squares rejected. Complete acceptance admits all 555 since 8≤K−18\le K-18≤K−1, ending above the capacity line at 131313 jobs; partial acceptance fills up to KKK and rejects 333; complete rejection refuses all 555 since 8+5>108+5>108+5>10.

The policy enters the level-crossing equation through the up-crossings. By PASTA, batches that see mmm jobs arrive at rate λBπ(m)\lambda_B\pi(m)λB​π(m); such a batch up-crosses level n≥mn\ge mn≥m only if it contains more than n−mn-mn−m jobs and is admitted far enough. Under partial acceptance the levels below KKK are crossed exactly as without rejection. Under complete rejection a batch that does not fit causes no up-crossing, so batches with B>K−mB>K-mB>K−m are removed: hence G(n−m)−G(K−m)G(n-m)-G(K-m)G(n−m)−G(K−m). Down-crossings stay μπ(n+1)\mu\pi(n+1)μπ(n+1), since jobs leave one at a time.

For B≡1B\equiv1B≡1 the three policies coincide with blocking in the M/M/1/K queue. The decision is made by the system, unlike balking, where customers decide for themselves. A common slip, the subject of TF 5.3.4, is to let the sum in the recursion stop at m=n−1m=n-1m=n−1: a batch that finds exactly nnn jobs also up-crosses level nnn.
