Interpretation¶

Once we know how many arrivals a Poisson process produced in [0,t][0,t][0,t], the process tells us nothing more about where they fell. The arrivals then behave like nnn points thrown independently and uniformly onto [0,t][0,t][0,t] and sorted afterwards, and the rate λ\lambdaλ has disappeared from the conditional law.

Key Results¶

  • Given N(t)=nN(t)=nN(t)=n, the arrival times have joint density n!/tnn!/t^nn!/tn on {0<t1<⋯<tn<t}\{0<t_1<\dots<t_n<t\}{0<t1​<⋯<tn​<t} (Theorem 1.3.3), whatever the rate λ\lambdaλ.
  • The unsorted points are i.i.d. Unif⁡([0,t])\operatorname{Unif}([0,t])Unif([0,t]); sorting them, for example with quicksort, gives the arrivals in time order.
  • For 0≤s≤t0\le s\le t0≤s≤t, each of the nnn uniform points lands in [0,s][0,s][0,s] independently with probability s/ts/ts/t, so given N(t)=nN(t)=nN(t)=n the count N(s)N(s)N(s) has the binomial distribution Bin⁡(n,s/t)\operatorname{Bin}(n,s/t)Bin(n,s/t).
