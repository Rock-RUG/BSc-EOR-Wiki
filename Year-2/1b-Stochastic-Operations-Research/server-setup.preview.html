Server Setup¶

Definition¶

A setup (or change-over) is an interval during which a server does not serve but prepares for jobs of another type, for example cleaning a paint machine before a change of colour, bringing an oven to a new temperature, or staff walking to another part of a building.

The notes' setup model has one machine and two job families, red and blue, arriving at rates λr\lambda_rλr​ and λb\lambda_bλb​, λ=λr+λb\lambda=\lambda_r+\lambda_bλ=λr​+λb​, with inter-arrival SCV Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ for both. Net service times are i.i.d. copies of S0S_0S0​, and setup times RkR_kRk​ are i.i.d. copies of RRR, independent of the service times. Jobs are collected per colour into batches of fixed size BBB. A complete batch joins a queue of batches; when it reaches the server, the machine performs one setup and then serves the BBB jobs one by one, and each job leaves as soon as its own service ends. Interpretation¶

Setups consume capacity without serving anyone. Producing in batches spreads one setup over BBB jobs, so larger batches lower the load, but every job must then wait for its batch to fill and for the jobs ahead of it in the batch. The model makes this trade-off computable: each component is either a simple average or an application of Sakasegawa's formula to the queue of batches.

The figure plots Example 1 against BBB. Just above the minimal batch size Bm=24B_m=24Bm​=24 the load is close to 111 and the batch queue time (red) explodes, because E[WB]\mathbb{E}[W_B]E[WB​] grows like (1−ρ)−1(1-\rho)^{-1}(1−ρ)−1. For large BBB the queue time is small but the formation time B−1B-1B−1 hours (blue) and the time at the server grow linearly, so the total (green) rises again. The minimum lies near B=29B=29B=29, and the curve is far from symmetric: a BBB slightly too small is much worse than one too large.

Stability needs ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1, that is,

B>Bm=λE[R]1−λE[S0],B>B_m=\frac{\lambda\mathbb{E}[R]}{1-\lambda\mathbb{E}[S_0]},B>Bm​=1−λE[S0​]λE[R]​,

which requires λE[S0]<1\lambda\mathbb{E}[S_0]<1λE[S0​]<1 in the first place: no batch size can absorb a server that is already overloaded by net work. The model is the queueing counterpart of the setup cost KKK of OR Modelling: there a fixed charge per order favours large lots, here a fixed time per batch does. Turning the setup into an extra service time per job leads to the setup effective service time. A common slip is to forget the formation and in-batch waiting.

Key Results¶

  • Batch load. ρ=λBE[SB]=λE[S0]+λE[R]/B\rho=\lambda_B\mathbb{E}[S_B]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S_0]+\lambda\mathbb{E}[R]/Bρ=λB​E[SB​]=λE[S0​]+λE[R]/B, which decreases in BBB when E[R]>0\mathbb{E}[R]>0E[R]>0 (TF 6.2.3).
  • Minimal batch size. ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 if and only if B>Bm=λE[R]/(1−λE[S0])B>B_m=\lambda\mathbb{E}[R]/(1-\lambda\mathbb{E}[S_0])B>Bm​=λE[R]/(1−λE[S0​]), provided λE[S0]<1\lambda\mathbb{E}[S_0]<1λE[S0​]<1.
  • Batch SCVs (6.2.3). Ca,B2=Ca2/BC_{a,B}^2=C_a^2/BCa,B2​=Ca2​/B, since BBB inter-arrival times are added (TF 6.2.4); Var⁡(SB)=Var⁡(R)+BVar⁡(S0)\operatorname{Var}(S_B)=\operatorname{Var}(R)+B\operatorname{Var}(S_0)Var(SB​)=Var(R)+BVar(S0​) by independence (Exercise 6.2.9).
  • Time at the server (6.2.5). E[R]+B+12E[S0]\mathbb{E}[R]+\frac{B+1}{2}\mathbb{E}[S_0]E[R]+2B+1​E[S0​] if a job leaves after its own service, and E[R]+BE[S0]\mathbb{E}[R]+B\mathbb{E}[S_0]E[R]+BE[S0​] if it waits for the whole batch.
  • No monotonicity. Larger batches lower the queue time but raise the formation time, so increasing BBB does not always reduce E[Ji]\mathbb{E}[J_i]E[Ji​] (TF 6.2.2, 6.2.5).
  • Family-specific batches. With very different arrival rates, BBB should in general depend on the family, because the formation time B−12λi\frac{B-1}{2\lambda_i}2λi​B−1​ is largest for the rarest family.
