Availability¶

Definition¶

For a server that fails at rate λf\lambda_fλf​, so with mean time to fail (MTTF) mf=1/λfm_f=1/\lambda_fmf​=1/λf​, and whose repairs take E[R]\mathbb{E}[R]E[R] on average, the availability is

A:=mfmf+E[R]=11+λf E[R].A:=\frac{m_f}{m_f+\mathbb{E}[R]}=\frac{1}{1+\lambda_f\,\mathbb{E}[R]}.A:=mf​+E[R]mf​​=1+λf​E[R]1​.

The second form (6.4.2) follows by dividing numerator and denominator by mfm_fmf​. Both mfm_fmf​ and E[R]\mathbb{E}[R]E[R] must be in the same time unit; λfE[R]\lambda_f\mathbb{E}[R]λf​E[R] is then the dimensionless expected repair time per unit of up time.

Interpretation¶

Availability is the fraction of time the server is up: of every mf+E[R]m_f+\mathbb{E}[R]mf​+E[R] time units in an average up-and-repair cycle, mfm_fmf​ are productive.

The figure shows the up periods ZkZ_kZk​ and repairs RkR_kRk​ of a server. Each repair ends at a time TkT_kTk​, and the interval (Tk−1,Tk](T_{k-1},T_k](Tk−1​,Tk​] is one cycle: an up period followed by a repair. Taking the up time in a cycle as the reward, the renewal reward theorem says that the long-run fraction of up time equals the expected reward per cycle divided by the expected cycle length, E[Z]/(E[Z]+E[R])\mathbb{E}[Z]/(\mathbb{E}[Z]+\mathbb{E}[R])E[Z]/(E[Z]+E[R]), with E[Z]=mf\mathbb{E}[Z]=m_fE[Z]=mf​ (Exercise 6.4.11).

In the queueing model of Server Failure the failure clock runs only while a job is being served, so the up periods are pieces of net service. There AAA is best read as the fraction of the server's occupied time that is spent processing, A=E[S0]/E[S]A=\mathbb{E}[S_0]/\mathbb{E}[S]A=E[S0​]/E[S]; an idle server cannot fail. Failures thus inflate every unit of work by 1/A1/A1/A, raising the load to ρ=λ E[S0]/A\rho=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S_0]/Aρ=λE[S0​]/A.

AAA depends on λf\lambda_fλf​ and E[R]\mathbb{E}[R]E[R] only through the product λfE[R]\lambda_f\mathbb{E}[R]λf​E[R]. Frequent short repairs and rare long repairs can therefore have the same availability but very different service-time variability, so AAA alone does not determine the waiting time. A frequent slip is mixing units, for instance a failure rate per hour with a repair time in minutes (Example 3).
