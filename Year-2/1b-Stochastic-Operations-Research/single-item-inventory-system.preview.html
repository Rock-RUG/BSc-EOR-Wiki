Single-Item Inventory System¶

Definition¶

Demand DkD_kDk​ in period kkk is discrete and random, unsold items carry over to the next period, and demand that finds no stock is backlogged. Interpretation¶

An inventory system is a queue seen from the other side: items wait on the shelf for demand, and when the shelf is empty, demand waits for items. "Single item" means that each product is controlled on its own, with its own demand, orders and costs, like the macaroni boxes on a supermarket shelf in the notes.

With lead time l=1l=1l=1 this order is in transit during period 4, while the 333 items on the shelf meet the demand D4=2D_4=2D4​=2. The inventory level counts only the shelf, I4=3I_4=3I4​=3; the position adds the order in transit, P4=3+5=8P_4=3+5=8P4​=3+5=8.

The lead time is why two state variables are needed. A rule that looked only at the level would ignore the orders already under way, and with a positive lead time its replenishments would come too late; so all policies in the course use the position. With l=0l=0l=0 an order arrives at once and the two coincide. The model assumes a fixed lead time, items that do not perish (yogurt, not fresh fish) and backlogging; lost sales need a different recursion (Example 3).

The EOQ model of OR Modelling had constant demand; here demand is random and the same recursions that build a queue are used to simulate any inventory policy. Without a closed-form cost, the notes compare parameters by simulating each candidate on one demand list.
