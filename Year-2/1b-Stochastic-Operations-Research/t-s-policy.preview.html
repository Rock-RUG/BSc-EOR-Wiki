(T, S) Policy¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system, a (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) policy with review period T∈{1,2,… }T\in\{1,2,\dots\}T∈{1,2,…} and order-up-to level SSS orders up to SSS once every TTT periods:

Qk=(S−Pk−1′) 1{mod⁡(k,T)=0},Q_k=(S-P_{k-1}')\,\mathbf{1}\{\operatorname{mod}(k,T)=0\},Qk​=(S−Pk−1′​)1{mod(k,T)=0},

where Pk−1′P_{k-1}'Pk−1′​ is the end-of-period inventory position of period k−1k-1k−1 and mod⁡(k,T)\operatorname{mod}(k,T)mod(k,T) is the remainder of k/Tk/Tk/T. Orders are placed only at the starts of periods T,2T,3T,…T,2T,3T,\dotsT,2T,3T,…; each brings the position back to SSS, whatever it has fallen to.

Interpretation¶

The (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) policy fixes when to order and lets demand decide how much. This suits replenishment on a timetable, such as a gas station whose tanks a truck refills every three days.

This is the price of a fixed timetable. A rule triggered by the position, such as the (s,S)(s,S)(s,S) policy, reacts in the period in which the position becomes low; the (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) policy has to carry the risk of TTT periods of demand, plus the lead time, with one level SSS. A larger TTT means fewer orders but a higher SSS for the same protection, and with T=1T=1T=1 the policy orders every period and becomes the base-stock policy.

The review period plays the role of the ordering cycle t0t_0t0​ of the EOQ model: with constant demand ddd per period, every order after the first equals TdTdTd. A common slip is to treat SSS as the order size; the order is S−Pk−1′S-P_{k-1}'S−Pk−1′​ and varies from review to review.
