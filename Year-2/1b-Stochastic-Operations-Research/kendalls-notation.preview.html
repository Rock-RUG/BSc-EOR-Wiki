Kendall's Notation¶

Definition¶

Suppose that the inter-arrival times XkX_kXk​ are i.i.d., the service times SkS_kSk​ are i.i.d., and the two sequences are independent. Kendall's notation

AB/YC/c/KA^B/Y^C/c/KAB/YC/c/K

describes such a single queueing station by six entries:

  • AAA: the distribution of the inter-arrival times;
  • BBB: the distribution of the number of jobs that arrive together as a batch;
  • YYY: the distribution of the service times;
  • CCC: the distribution of the number of jobs the server processes together as a batch;
  • ccc: the number of servers;
  • KKK: the system size, the largest number of jobs that can be present at the same time, waiting or in service.

Entries at their default value are omitted: BBB or CCC when jobs arrive or are served one at a time (B≡B≡1B\equiv11, C≡1C\equiv1C≡1), and KKK when the system is unbounded. The letter MMM (Markov, or memoryless) stands for the exponential distribution, GGG for a general distribution and DDD for a deterministic one. Service is assumed first in, first out (FIFO) and work-conserving: a server is never idle while a job is present.

Interpretation¶

A Kendall label is a compact summary of the model behind a queue. Reading it tells you what has been assumed about arrivals, service, servers and space, and therefore which results may be used.

The figure maps each entry to a part of the station. Arrivals (blue) come with inter-arrival law AAA in batches of size BBB, the servers (red) have service-time law YYY and serve batches of size CCC, there are ccc servers, and the brace KKK covers the queue and the servers. Hence the queue holds at most K−cK-cK−c jobs.

The label is meaningful only under the independence assumptions above, and it does not record the service discipline: a system with priorities, like the business desk in Example 4, is not described by the label alone. MMM for inter-arrival times means Poisson arrivals, by the link between the memoryless exponential distribution and the Poisson process from Week 1. A common slip is to read KKK as the size of the waiting room; some authors do use it that way, but in this course KKK counts the whole system.

Examples¶

1. Hospital beds. A ward is modelled as an M/M/c/cM/M/c/cM/M/c/c queue. Patients arrive as a Poisson stream (many independent potential patients), recovery times are roughly exponential, each of the ccc beds serves one patient, and a patient who finds all beds occupied cannot wait in a bed queue, so K=cK=cK=c. The hospital chooses ccc so that the blocking probability stays below a target such as 1%1\%1%.

2. Reading M2/G/3/10M^2/G/3/10M2/G/3/10. Jobs arrive in pairs (B≡B≡2B\equiv22) at Poisson epochs; service times are general, so deterministic service times are allowed as a special case; there are 3 servers; and at most 10 jobs are present, so at most 10−3=710-3=710−3=7 wait. These are the three statements of TF 3.2.1, and all three are true.

4. Labels read wrongly. In D/M/1D/M/1D/M/1 the inter-arrival times are deterministic and the service times exponential, not the other way round (TF 3.2.2). In M/M/c/KM/M/c/KM/M/c/K the number of servers is ccc, not KKK (TF 3.2.5). An airport with ccc economy desks and one business desk that serves economy customers only when no business customer is present is not M/M/(c+1)M/M/(c+1)M/M/(c+1) for economy customers: the business desk gives priority to business customers, so it behaves differently from the other ccc desks (TF 3.2.3).
