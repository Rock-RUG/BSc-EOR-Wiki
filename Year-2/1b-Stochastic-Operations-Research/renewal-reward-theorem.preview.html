Interpretation¶

The theorem turns an average per event into an average per unit time: reward per time equals events per time times reward per event. A one-dollar shop whose customers come on average once a minute and spend on average 10 dollars earns 10 dollars per minute, whether or not arrivals and purchases vary.

Key Results¶

  • R=αZR=\alpha ZR=αZ: the long-run reward rate is the rate of epochs times the average reward per cycle (Theorem 4.3.1). The epochs need not be arrivals, so α\alphaα need not equal the arrival rate λ\lambdaλ (TF 4.3.4).
  • Cost of failures: if repairs take no time, failures occur with mean inter-failure time E[X]\mathbb{E}[X]E[X] and each repair costs ccc, the cost rate is c/E[X]c/\mathbb{E}[X]c/E[X] (TF 4.3.1).
  • Busy fraction: with R(t)=∫0t1{L(s)>0} dsR(t)=\int_0^t\mathbf{1}\{L(s)>0\}\,\mathrm{d}sR(t)=∫0t​1{L(s)>0}ds and Tk=DkT_k=D_kTk​=Dk​, the long-run fraction of time a single server is busy is δ E[S]\delta\,\mathbb{E}[S]δE[S], which equals the load ρ=λ E[S]\rho=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]ρ=λE[S] when the queue is rate-stable (Ex 4.3.5).
  • Busy and idle periods: sampling at the ends of busy periods gives ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I])\rho=\mathbb{E}[U]/(\mathbb{E}[U]+\mathbb{E}[I])ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I]), with UUU the busy time and III the idle time (Ex 4.3.7).
  • Inventory cycles: the long-run average cost of an (s,S)(s,S)(s,S)-controlled production system is the cycle cost divided by the cycle length, which gives the economic production quantity formulas.
