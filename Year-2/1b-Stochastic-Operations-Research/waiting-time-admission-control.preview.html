Waiting-Time Admission Control¶

Method¶

Interpretation¶

A rejected job removes its whole service time from the work that every later job must wait behind. Because the Lindley recursion carries work forward from job to job, blocking a few jobs that would join a long queue shortens the waits of many jobs after them.

The figure is Example 1, where a job arrives every time unit. Without control (grey) the waits climb 0,2,3,4,4,6,6,70,2,3,4,4,6,6,70,2,3,4,4,6,6,7. Under complete acceptance with T=2T=2T=2 (blue), jobs 3, 6 and 7 find a wait above the orange line and are rejected (orange rings); their service times are not added, so the next job's wait falls by one time unit and the path stays near TTT.

The factor Ik−1I_{k-1}Ik−1​ implements blocking without changing the input list s1,…,sns_1,\dots,s_ns1​,…,sn​. All rules can therefore be compared on the same simulated sample path. The rules guarantee different things. Complete acceptance bounds the wait Wk≤TW_k\leq TWk​≤T of accepted jobs, but an accepted job can still stay up to T+skT+s_kT+sk​. Complete rejection bounds the sojourn time Jk≤TJ_k\leq TJk​≤T, so it rejects every job with sk>Ts_k>Tsk​>T. Partial acceptance bounds Jk≤TJ_k\leq TJk​≤T for every job, at the price of serving only part of some jobs.

In the Week 2 psychiatrists case, capacity was adapted to the queue; here the inflow is controlled instead.

A common slip is to forget the factor Ik−1I_{k-1}Ik−1​, so that rejected jobs still add work (Example 3). Another is to compare the mean wait of accepted jobs under one rule with the mean wait of all jobs under another. Week 4 returns to blocking with the M/M/1/KM/M/1/KM/M/1/K queue.

Key Results¶

  • A rejected job adds no work: Ik−1sk−1=0I_{k-1}s_{k-1}=0Ik−1​sk−1​=0 when Ik−1=0I_{k-1}=0Ik−1​=0. The original service times are never overwritten.
  • Complete acceptance: every accepted job has Wk≤TW_k\leq TWk​≤T. Complete rejection: every accepted job has Jk≤TJ_k\leq TJk​≤T, so in particular every job with sk>Ts_k>Tsk​>T is rejected. Partial acceptance: Jk≤TJ_k\leq TJk​≤T for every job.
  • Under partial acceptance Wk=[Jk−1−xk]+≤Jk−1≤TW_k=[J_{k-1}-x_k]^+\leq J_{k-1}\leq TWk​=[Jk−1​−xk​]+≤Jk−1​≤T, so Bk=min⁡{T−Wk,sk}≥0B_k=\min\{T-W_k,s_k\}\geq0Bk​=min{T−Wk​,sk​}≥0 is a valid accepted amount.
  • For the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 sample path of the notes (λ=λ=1\lambda=11, μ=1.1\mu=1.1μ=1.1, 10 00010\,00010000 jobs) and T=5T=5T=5, complete acceptance keeps about 90%90\%90% of the jobs while the average wait over all jobs drops from 9.209.209.20 to 2.232.232.23 and the maximum wait of accepted jobs from 52.1252.1252.12 to 5.005.005.00.
  • Complete rejection accepts more jobs (0.970.970.97) than complete acceptance (0.900.900.90) on the same input, although its test is stricter: it turns jobs away before their work enters the system.
  • Rejection lowers the rate at which work enters the server, so the server utilization falls below the offered load; compare rate stability.
