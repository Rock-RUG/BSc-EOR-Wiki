Definition¶

Interpretation¶

The sojourn time is the end-to-end experience of a job: queueing plus service. A patient who waits two weeks for an intake and then spends one hour with the psychiatrist has a sojourn time of two weeks and one hour.

The figure draws the arrival and departure counting processes of the five-job path in Example 1. Under first-in-first-out service the kkk-th departure is job kkk, so at height kkk the horizontal distance from the curve A(t)A(t)A(t) to the curve D(t)D(t)D(t) is JkJ_kJk​. The vertical distance between the curves at time ttt is the number in the system L(t)L(t)L(t). Reading the shaded area row by row gives ∑kJk\displaystyle \sum_k J_kk∑​Jk​, reading it column by column gives ∫L(s) ds\int L(s)\,\mathrm{d}s∫L(s)ds, and both equal 141414.

This double reading is the identity (3.1.8) of the notes: summing Jk=∫Ik(s) dsJ_k=\int I_k(s)\,\mathrm{d}sJk​=∫Ik​(s)ds over jobs and using ∑kIk(s)=L(s)\displaystyle \sum_k I_k(s)=L(s)k∑​Ik​(s)=L(s) gives ∑kJk=∫L(s) ds\displaystyle \sum_k J_k=\int L(s)\,\mathrm{d}sk∑​Jk​=∫L(s)ds.

Unlike the waiting time, the sojourn time always contains the job's own service, so Jk≥SkJ_k\geq S_kJk​≥Sk​, with equality exactly when the job does not wait. The row-by-row reading needs FIFO: with another service order the kkk-th departure need not be job kkk, and the horizontal distances at the heights are no longer the sojourn times, although the area identity still holds.

The same identity is the sample-path starting point of Little's law in Week 4.
