Traffic Equations¶

Proposition¶

Consider an open network of MMM stations. New jobs arrive from outside at station iii with rate γi≥0\gamma_i\geq0γi​≥0. After service at station iii a job moves to station jjj with probability PijP_{ij}Pij​, independently of everything else (Markov routing), and it leaves the network with probability Pi0=1−∑j=1MPij\displaystyle P_{i0}=1-\sum_{j=1}^MP_{ij}Pi0​=1−j=1∑M​Pij​. Let λi\lambda_iλi​ be the total arrival rate at station iii, external and internal together. If every station is rate stable, so that its departure rate equals its arrival rate, δi=λi\delta_i=\lambda_iδi​=λi​, then the traffic equations hold:

λi=γi+∑j=1MλjPji,i=1,…,M.\lambda_i=\gamma_i+\sum_{j=1}^M\lambda_jP_{ji},\qquad i=1,\ldots,M.λi​=γi​+j=1∑M​λj​Pji​,i=1,…,M.

With the row vectors λ=(λ1,…,λM)\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_M)λ=(λ1​,…,λM​) and γ=(γ1,…,γM)\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_M)γ=(γ1​,…,γM​) and the routing matrix P=(Pij)\boldsymbol{P}=(P_{ij})P=(Pij​), this is the linear system λ=γ+λP\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}\boldsymbol{P}λ=γ+λP. When I−P\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{P}I−P is invertible, its unique solution is λ=γ(I−P)−1\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{P})^{-1}λ=γ(I−P)−1.

Interpretation¶

The traffic equations are bookkeeping for flows. Jobs reach station iii from outside at rate γi\gamma_iγi​ and from every station jjj, including iii itself. A stable station passes on everything it receives, so station jjj releases jobs at rate λj\lambda_jλj​, and the share PjiP_{ji}Pji​ of them goes to iii.

The figure shows the network of Example 1, each arrow labelled with its flow rate λjPji\lambda_jP_{ji}λj​Pji​. At station 1 the inflows 1+1.5+0.51+1.5+0.51+1.5+0.5 add up to λ1=3\lambda_1=3λ1​=3, at station 2 the inflows 1.5+0.51.5+0.51.5+0.5 add up to λ2=2\lambda_2=2λ2​=2, and the flow λ2P20=1\lambda_2P_{20}=1λ2​P20​=1 out of the network equals the external input.

Rate stability turns the output of station jjj into λj\lambda_jλj​; an overloaded station releases jobs at most at its capacity, and then the equations no longer describe the real flows. Markov routing makes the long-run fraction of departures from jjj that go to iii equal to PjiP_{ji}Pji​. No exponential or Poisson assumption is needed, but the equations do not prove stability either: one still checks that every load satisfies ρi=λiE[Si]<1\rho_i=\lambda_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]<1ρi​=λi​E[Si​]<1. The notes reach the same equations by following Poisson streams through M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 stations, split by the routing and merged at the next station.

The most common slip is the index order: the flow into iii uses the column entries PjiP_{ji}Pji​, not the row PijP_{ij}Pij​. Another is to expect λi\lambda_iλi​ to be at most the total external rate; with feedback a job visits a station several times, so λ1=3>γ=1\lambda_1=3>\gamma=1λ1​=3>γ=1 in the figure. Dividing λi\lambda_iλi​ by the total external rate gives the visit ratio.

Key Results¶

  • Network output. Summing the traffic equations over iii and using ∑jPij=1−Pi0\displaystyle \sum_jP_{ij}=1-P_{i0}j∑​Pij​=1−Pi0​ gives ∑i=1MλiPi0=∣γ∣\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^M\lambda_iP_{i0}=|\boldsymbol{\gamma}|i=1∑M​λi​Pi0​=∣γ∣, where ∣γ∣=∑i=1Mγi\displaystyle |\boldsymbol{\gamma}|=\sum_{i=1}^M\gamma_i∣γ∣=i=1∑M​γi​. The network as a whole releases jobs at the total external rate, whereas a single station has δi=λi\delta_i=\lambda_iδi​=λi​.
  • Solution by iteration (Section 7.4, optional reading). Starting from λ0=0\boldsymbol{\lambda}^0=\boldsymbol{0}λ0=0, the iteration λn=γ+λn−1P\boldsymbol{\lambda}^n=\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{n-1}\boldsymbol{P}λn=γ+λn−1P gives λn=γ∑m=0n−1Pm\displaystyle \boldsymbol{\lambda}^n=\boldsymbol{\gamma}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\boldsymbol{P}^mλn=γm=0∑n−1​Pm. The sum converges when P\boldsymbol{P}P is transient, that is, when a job can leave the network from every station within MMM steps; the limit is γ(I−P)−1\boldsymbol{\gamma}(\boldsymbol{I}-\boldsymbol{P})^{-1}γ(I−P)−1.
  • Stability check. The network can only be stable if ρi=λiE[Si]<1\rho_i=\lambda_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]<1ρi​=λi​E[Si​]<1 at every single-server station, or λiE[Si]/ci<1\lambda_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]/c_i<1λi​E[Si​]/ci​<1 at a station with cic_ici​ servers.
  • Tandem line. With Pi,i+1=1P_{i,i+1}=1Pi,i+1​=1 for i<Mi<Mi<M, PM0=1P_{M0}=1PM0​=1 and all external jobs entering at station 1, every station has λi=γ1\lambda_i=\gamma_1λi​=γ1​; here PM=0\boldsymbol{P}^M=\boldsymbol{0}PM=0 (Exercise 7.4.1). See tandem queue.
  • Two stations with feedback (Exercise 7.3.3). With external rate γ\gammaγ into station 1, P11=αP_{11}=\alphaP11​=α, P12=1−αP_{12}=1-\alphaP12​=1−α, P21=β1P_{21}=\beta_1P21​=β1​ and P22=β2P_{22}=\beta_2P22​=β2​, the solution is λ1=γ(1−β2)/((1−α)(1−β1−β2))\lambda_1=\gamma(1-\beta_2)/\bigl((1-\alpha)(1-\beta_1-\beta_2)\bigr)λ1​=γ(1−β2​)/((1−α)(1−β1​−β2​)) and λ2=γ/(1−β1−β2)\lambda_2=\gamma/(1-\beta_1-\beta_2)λ2​=γ/(1−β1​−β2​), valid for α<1\alpha<1α<1 and β1+β2<1\beta_1+\beta_2<1β1​+β2​<1. As α↑1\alpha\uparrow1α↑1 the rate at station 1 explodes.
