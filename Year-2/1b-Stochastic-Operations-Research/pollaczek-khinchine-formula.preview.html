Pollaczek-Khinchine Formula¶

Theorem¶

Consider the M/G/1 queue: jobs arrive as a Poisson process with rate λ\lambdaλ, service times are i.i.d. copies of SSS with E[S2]<∞\mathbb{E}[S^2]<\inftyE[S2]<∞, there is one server that serves in order of arrival, and the load ρ=λE[S]\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]ρ=λE[S] satisfies ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1. Then the mean waiting time in queue is (Theorem 5.4.2)

E[W]=12 λ E[S2]1−ρ=1+Cs22 ρ1−ρ E[S],\mathbb{E}[W]=\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S^2]}{1-\rho}=\frac{1+C_s^2}{2}\,\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\,\mathbb{E}[S],E[W]=21​1−ρλE[S2]​=21+Cs2​​1−ρρ​E[S],

where Cs2=Var⁡(S)/(E[S])2C_s^2=\operatorname{Var}(S)/(\mathbb{E}[S])^2Cs2​=Var(S)/(E[S])2 is the squared coefficient of variation of the service time.

Interpretation¶

The formula splits the mean waiting time into three factors: the variability of the service times, (1+Cs2)/2(1+C_s^2)/2(1+Cs2​)/2; a load factor, ρ/(1−ρ)\rho/(1-\rho)ρ/(1−ρ); and the time scale, E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S]. Each factor points to a different way to reduce waiting: make services more regular, reduce the load, or make services shorter.

The figure plots E[W]/E[S]=12(1+Cs2)ρ/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[W]/\mathbb{E}[S]=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+C_s^2)\rho/(1-\rho)E[W]/E[S]=21​(1+Cs2​)ρ/(1−ρ) for the M/D/1 queue (Cs2​=Cs2=0C_s^2=00), for Cs2=1/2C_s^2=1/2Cs2​=1/2 and for the M/M/1 queue (Cs2​=Cs2=1C_s^2=11). All three curves are convex and explode as ρ↑1\rho\uparrow1ρ↑1, so E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] is not linear in ρ\rhoρ (TF 5.4.3). The marked point is the workstation of Example 1: ρ=3/4\rho=3/4ρ=3/4 and Cs2=1/2C_s^2=1/2Cs2​=1/2 give E[W]=2.25 E[S]=4.5\mathbb{E}[W]=2.25\,\mathbb{E}[S]=4.5E[W]=2.25E[S]=4.5 hours. At a fixed load, the M/D/1 curve is exactly half the M/M/1 curve.

The derivation shows why each condition is there. An arriving job first waits for the remaining service time of the job in service and then for the jobs in queue. Poisson arrivals give, by PASTA, E[W]=E[Sr]+E[Q]E[S]\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[S_r]+\mathbb{E}[Q]\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]=E[Sr​]+E[Q]E[S]; Little's law replaces E[Q]\mathbb{E}[Q]E[Q] by λE[W]\lambda\mathbb{E}[W]λE[W], so E[W]=E[Sr]/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[S_r]/(1-\rho)E[W]=E[Sr​]/(1−ρ), which needs ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1; and E[Sr]=λE[S2]/2\mathbb{E}[S_r]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S^2]/2E[Sr​]=λE[S2]/2 needs the finite second moment. For non-Poisson arrivals PASTA fails, and the formula need not hold.

The M/M/1 waiting time ρ E[S]/(1−ρ)\rho\,\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)ρE[S]/(1−ρ) from Week 4 is the case Cs2=1C_s^2=1Cs2​=1. The batch-arrival waiting time has the same structure, with the variability of the batch size in place of Cs2C_s^2Cs2​. A common slip is to think that only E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] matters: two machines with the same mean service time and load can have very different queues. In Week 6, Sakasegawa's formula extends this structure to queues with general arrivals and several servers.

Key Results¶

  • Derivation (5.4.1). E[W]=E[Sr]+λE[W]E[S]\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[S_r]+\lambda\mathbb{E}[W]\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]=E[Sr​]+λE[W]E[S], so E[W]=E[Sr]/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[S_r]/(1-\rho)E[W]=E[Sr​]/(1−ρ), and Theorem 5.4.1 gives E[Sr]=λE[S2]/2\mathbb{E}[S_r]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S^2]/2E[Sr​]=λE[S2]/2. Both PASTA and Little's law are used (TF 5.4.5).
  • Second form. E[S2]/(E[S])2=1+Cs2\mathbb{E}[S^2]/(\mathbb{E}[S])^2=1+C_s^2E[S2]/(E[S])2=1+Cs2​ turns the first expression into the second.
  • Deterministic service (TF 5.4.2, Exercise 5.4.7). For M/D/1, Cs2=0C_s^2=0Cs2​=0 and E[W]=12ρ E[S]/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[W]=\tfrac{1}{2}\rho\,\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)E[W]=21​ρE[S]/(1−ρ), half the M/M/1 value at the same load.
  • Other measures. E[J]=E[W]+E[S]\mathbb{E}[J]=\mathbb{E}[W]+\mathbb{E}[S]E[J]=E[W]+E[S] for the sojourn time, E[Q]=λE[W]\mathbb{E}[Q]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[W]E[Q]=λE[W] and E[L]=E[Q]+ρ\mathbb{E}[L]=\mathbb{E}[Q]+\rhoE[L]=E[Q]+ρ.
