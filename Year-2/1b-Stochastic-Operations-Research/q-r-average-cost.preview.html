(Q, r) Average Cost¶

Proposition¶

Consider a periodic-review single-item inventory system with iid integer period demands DDD, a constant lead time of lll periods, backlogging, and a (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) policy. Let KKK be the cost per order, and hhh and bbb the holding and backlog costs per unit per period, charged on the inventory level at the start of a period. Under the assumptions of Uniform Inventory Position under a (Q, r) Policy, the long-run average cost per period is

c(Q,r)=KE[D]Q+h E[I+]+b E[I−],c(Q,r)=K\frac{\mathbb{E}[D]}{Q}+h\,\mathbb{E}[I^+]+b\,\mathbb{E}[I^-],c(Q,r)=KQE[D]​+hE[I+]+bE[I−],

where E[I±]=1Q∑i=r+1r+QE[I±(i)]\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[I^{\pm}]=\frac{1}{Q}\sum_{i=r+1}^{r+Q}\mathbb{E}[I^{\pm}(i)]E[I±]=Q1​i=r+1∑r+Q​E[I±(i)] averages the base-stock on-hand stock and backlog E[I+(i)]=i−l E[D]+E[I−(i)]\mathbb{E}[I^+(i)]=i-l\,\mathbb{E}[D]+\mathbb{E}[I^-(i)]E[I+(i)]=i−lE[D]+E[I−(i)] and E[I−(i)]=E[(X−i)+]\mathbb{E}[I^-(i)]=\mathbb{E}[(X-i)^+]E[I−(i)]=E[(X−i)+] over the QQQ possible positions, with XXX the lead-time demand. The factor E[D]/Q\mathbb{E}[D]/QE[D]/Q is the order frequency: the long-run number of batches of size QQQ ordered per period.

Interpretation¶

The cost has three parts that pull in different directions. A large QQQ means few orders, so the ordering term KE[D]/QK\mathbb{E}[D]/QKE[D]/Q falls, but the position spreads over a wider window and the average stock grows by about Q/2Q/2Q/2. The reorder level rrr shifts that window: raising it trades backlog for holding cost, just as raising SSS does under base stock.

The figure plots the notes' Lighthouse table: for each QQQ the cost with the best rrr, which is r=5r=5r=5 except at Q=24Q=24Q=24, where r=4r=4r=4. The curve is flat near the optimum (Q⋆,r⋆)=(20,5)(Q^\star,r^\star)=(20,5)(Q⋆,r⋆)=(20,5) with c⋆≈13.73c^\star\approx13.73c⋆≈13.73, so neighbouring values of QQQ cost little extra, and the EOQ value 2E[D]K/h≈18.47\sqrt{2\mathbb{E}[D]K/h}\approx18.472E[D]K/h​≈18.47 (dashed) lands close to it.

The order frequency is E[D]/Q\mathbb{E}[D]/QE[D]/Q because, in the long run, everything that is demanded is reordered, in batches of QQQ. It counts orders exactly when one batch always suffices, for instance when D≤QD\leq QD≤Q, as in the Lighthouse case; otherwise one order may contain several batches. The holding and backlog terms need the uniform position and I=P−XI=P-XI=P−X, which is why the formula holds only under those assumptions. Since there is no closed-form optimum, the notes search a grid of pairs; the EOQ value gives a good starting QQQ, after which only rrr has to be searched. A common slip is to write the ordering term as KQ/E[D]KQ/\mathbb{E}[D]KQ/E[D] (TF 5.8.6), which charges more for fewer orders.
