Arrival-Average Performance¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

An arrival average answers the question a customer asks: how long did I wait, and how many people did I find in front of me? Every job contributes one observation, so the measure describes the experience of the typical customer.

The figure shows the five-job path of Example 1. The blue step function is L(t)L(t)L(t); each green dot sits at the arrival time AkA_kAk​ on the level L(Ak−)L(A_k-)L(Ak​−) before the upward jump, because an arriving job does not count itself. The arrivals see 0,1,1,0,00,1,1,0,00,1,1,0,0, so three of the five see an empty system, although the system is empty for only 1.51.51.5 of the 999 minutes.

The definitions are counts divided by the number of jobs, using indicators 1{Wk≤x}\mathbf{1}\{W_k\le x\}1{Wk​≤x}. The limits are assumed to exist; the notes state them for rate-stable systems without proof. When jobs arrive faster than the server can handle them, the queue, and with it WkW_kWk​, grows without bound, and E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] is infinite.

Arrival averages are what a simulation produces directly: the recursions for WkW_kWk​ and JkJ_kJk​ give one number per job, and averaging them estimates E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] or the fraction qTq_TqT​ of jobs that wait at least TTT, as in queue sample-path simulation. The common slip is to assume that what arrivals see equals what holds over time. A job that always arrives just after the previous one has left sees an idle server even when the server is almost always busy. In Week 4, PASTA gives a condition under which the two perspectives agree.

Key Results¶

  • Arrival averages of WWW, JJJ and L(Ak−)L(A_k-)L(Ak​−) are the customer-perspective measures (4.2.1)–(4.2.2); they sample the system only at arrival epochs (TF 4.2.5).
  • Since Jk=Wk+SkJ_k=W_k+S_kJk​=Wk​+Sk​ for every job, averaging gives E[J]=E[W]+E[S]\mathbb{E}[J]=\mathbb{E}[W]+\mathbb{E}[S]E[J]=E[W]+E[S] whenever two of the three averages exist.
  • Counting per unit time gives a rate: if λ=lim⁡t→∞A(t)/t\lambda=\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)/tλ=limt→∞​A(t)/t exists, then t−1∑k=1A(t)1{Wk≤x}→λ P(W≤x)\displaystyle t^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{A(t)}\mathbf{1}\{W_k\le x\}\to\lambda\,\mathbb{P}(W\le x)t−1k=1∑A(t)​1{Wk​≤x}→λP(W≤x) (TF 4.2.1).
  • In general, what arrivals see differs from the time-average performance: with constant inter-arrival times 101010 and service times 999, every arrival sees an empty system, but the time-average number in system is 9/109/109/10 (TF 4.2.3, Ex 4.2.6).
  • For Poisson arrivals the arrival and time averages of the state coincide; this is PASTA (Week 4).
