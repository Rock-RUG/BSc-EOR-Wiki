Batch-Arrival Waiting Time¶

Proposition¶

Consider the MB/M/1M^B/M/1MB/M/1 queue: batches arrive as a Poisson process with rate λB\lambda_BλB​, batch sizes are i.i.d. copies of BBB, job service times are i.i.d. Exp⁡(μ)\operatorname{Exp}(\mu)Exp(μ) with mean E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S], batches are served in order of arrival, and each batch moves as a whole to the server. Assume ρ=λBE[B]E[S]<1\rho=\lambda_B\mathbb{E}[B]\mathbb{E}[S]<1ρ=λB​E[B]E[S]<1 and E[B2]<∞\mathbb{E}[B^2]<\inftyE[B2]<∞. Then the mean time E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] a job spends in queue, that is, until its batch reaches the server, is (5.3.4)

E[W]=1+CB22 ρ1−ρ E[B] E[S]+12 ρ1−ρ E[S],\mathbb{E}[W]=\frac{1+C_B^2}{2}\,\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\,\mathbb{E}[B]\,\mathbb{E}[S]+\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\,\mathbb{E}[S],E[W]=21+CB2​​1−ρρ​E[B]E[S]+21​1−ρρ​E[S],

where CB2=Var⁡(B)/(E[B])2C_B^2=\operatorname{Var}(B)/(\mathbb{E}[B])^2CB2​=Var(B)/(E[B])2 is the squared coefficient of variation of the batch size. Moreover E[W]=E[L] E[S]\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[L]\,\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]=E[L]E[S], so E[L]=E[W]/E[S]\mathbb{E}[L]=\mathbb{E}[W]/\mathbb{E}[S]E[L]=E[W]/E[S].

Interpretation¶

A job arriving with a batch must wait until all work ahead of its batch is done. The formula shows what this waiting depends on: the load ρ\rhoρ, the mean batch size E[B]\mathbb{E}[B]E[B], the variability CB2C_B^2CB2​ of the batch size and the mean job service time E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S]. Increasing any of them increases E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W], even when ρ\rhoρ stays the same.

During the first service three jobs are at the server, then two, then one, so the area under LsBL_s^BLsB​ is Zk=3⋅20+2⋅30+1⋅25=145Z_k=3\cdot20+2\cdot30+1\cdot25=145Zk​=3⋅20+2⋅30+1⋅25=145 job-minutes. The renewal reward theorem turns the mean area per batch into the time average E[LsB]\mathbb{E}[L_s^B]E[LsB​].

The derivation needs exponential service times: by the memoryless property the job in service still needs E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] on average, so an arriving batch waits (E[LsB]+E[QB]E[B])E[S]=E[L]E[S](\mathbb{E}[L_s^B]+\mathbb{E}[Q^B]\mathbb{E}[B])\mathbb{E}[S]=\mathbb{E}[L]\mathbb{E}[S](E[LsB​]+E[QB]E[B])E[S]=E[L]E[S]. The condition E[B2]<∞\mathbb{E}[B^2]<\inftyE[B2]<∞ is needed because E[LsB]\mathbb{E}[L_s^B]E[LsB​] involves E[B2]\mathbb{E}[B^2]E[B2].

For B≡1B\equiv1B≡1 the two terms are equal and add up to the M/M/1 value ρ E[S]/(1−ρ)\rho\,\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)ρE[S]/(1−ρ). A common slip is to drop the factor E[B]\mathbb{E}[B]E[B] when BBB is deterministic: then CB2=0C_B^2=0CB2​=0, but E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] reduces to the M/M/1 result only when B≡1B\equiv1B≡1 (TF 5.3.5). The structure, a load factor ρ/(1−ρ)\rho/(1-\rho)ρ/(1−ρ) times a variability factor times a mean, returns in the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula for the M/G/1 queue.
