Balking¶

Definition¶

A customer balks when, on arrival, he or she finds the queue too long and decides not to join. Customers still arrive at rate λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) when the system contains nnn jobs, but only some of them enter; the entry rate λ+(n)≤λ(n)\lambda^{+}(n)\le\lambda(n)λ+(n)≤λ(n) of the level-crossing equation λ+(n)p(n)=μ(n+1)p(n+1)\lambda^{+}(n)p(n)=\mu(n+1)p(n+1)λ+(n)p(n)=μ(n+1)p(n+1) records only those who join.

Balking differs from blocking. Under blocking, as in the M/M/1/K queue, λ+(n)=λ 1{n<K}\lambda^{+}(n)=\lambda\,\mathbf{1}\{n<K\}λ+(n)=λ1{n<K}: everybody enters until the system is full. Under balking, part of the customers already decline at levels below KKK. A simple model for balking at a single server is

λ+(n)=λ[K−n]+,μ(n)=μ,\lambda^{+}(n)=\lambda[K-n]^{+},\qquad \mu(n)=\mu,λ+(n)=λ[K−n]+,μ(n)=μ,

so the entry rate falls linearly from KλK\lambdaKλ in an empty system to 000 at level KKK. One way to obtain it: potential customers arrive as a Poisson process with rate KλK\lambdaKλ, and a customer who finds n<Kn<Kn<K jobs joins with probability (K−n)/K(K-n)/K(K−n)/K.

Interpretation¶

Balking is a decision of the customer, blocking a limit of the system. A long queue discourages new customers, so the input slows down gradually as congestion builds instead of being cut off at a hard capacity.
