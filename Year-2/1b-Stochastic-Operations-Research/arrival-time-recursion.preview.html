Arrival-Time Recursion¶

Method¶

Given non-negative inter-arrival times X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,…, where XkX_kXk​ is the time between the arrivals of jobs k−1k-1k−1 and kkk, the arrival-time recursion constructs the arrival times

A0=0,Ak=Ak−1+Xk,k≥1.A_0=0,\qquad A_k=A_{k-1}+X_k,\quad k\geq1.A0​=0,Ak​=Ak−1​+Xk​,k≥1.

Unrolling it gives Ak=∑i=1kXi\displaystyle A_k=\sum_{i=1}^kX_iAk​=i=1∑k​Xi​, and conversely Xk=Ak−Ak−1X_k=A_k-A_{k-1}Xk​=Ak​−Ak−1​. The number of arrivals in [0,t][0,t][0,t] follows from the arrival times:

A(t)=k  ⟺  Ak≤t<Ak+1.A(t)=k\iff A_k\leq t<A_{k+1}.A(t)=k⟺Ak​≤t<Ak+1​.

When X1,X2,…X_1,X_2,\ldotsX1​,X2​,… are iid Exp⁡(λ)\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)Exp(λ), the counting process is a Poisson process with rate λ\lambdaλ, written N(t)N(t)N(t) in the notes.

Interpretation¶

Durations between events are easy to model, but a simulation needs the calendar times at which the events happen. The recursion turns the first into the second by adding each new gap to the previous arrival time.

The figure draws Example 1. The braces on the time axis are the inter-arrival times X1,…,X4X_1,\ldots,X_4X1​,…,X4​; the arrival times are their running totals. Above them the staircase A(t)A(t)A(t) jumps by one at each AkA_kAk​, so at t=6t=6t=6 it reads A(6)=2A(6)=2A(6)=2 because A2=5≤6<A3=8A_2=5\leq6<A_3=8A2​=5≤6<A3​=8.

The recursion needs only a starting point and an order: A0=0A_0=0A0​=0 fixes the origin, and Xk≥0X_k\geq0Xk​≥0 keeps the arrival times in non-decreasing order. Nothing else is assumed, so any model for the gaps can be plugged in. Distributional results do need assumptions: Ak=∑i≤kXi\displaystyle A_k=\sum_{i\leq k}X_iAk​=i≤k∑​Xi​ is a sum of independent random variables only when the gaps are independent, and Ak∼Gamma⁡(k,λ)A_k\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(k,\lambda)Ak​∼Gamma(k,λ) only when they are iid exponential. This is the convolution argument of Probability Distributions, and the event identity above links it to the Poisson counts of Probability Theory.

The common slip is to confuse the two sequences: AkA_kAk​ is a time on the clock, XkX_kXk​ a length of time. Sorting or averaging the wrong one changes the meaning. In Week 3 the same pattern, "previous time plus a new duration", builds departure times in the single-server service recursion.
