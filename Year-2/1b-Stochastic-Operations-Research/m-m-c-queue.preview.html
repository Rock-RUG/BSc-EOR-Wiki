M/M/c Queue¶

Definition¶

The M/M/cM/M/cM/M/c queue has Poisson arrivals with rate λ\lambdaλ, ccc identical servers whose service times are independent and exponential with rate μ\muμ, one shared queue and unlimited waiting room. It is the birth-death queue with

λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=μmin⁡{n,c},\lambda(n)=\lambda,\qquad\mu(n)=\mu\min\{n,c\},λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=μmin{n,c},

and its load is ρ=λ/(cμ)\rho=\lambda/(c\mu)ρ=λ/(cμ). If ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1, the stationary probabilities are

so that p(n)=p(0)(cρ)n/n!p(n)=p(0)(c\rho)^n/n!p(n)=p(0)(cρ)n/n! for n≤cn\leq cn≤c and p(n)=p(0) ccρn/c!p(n)=p(0)\,c^c\rho^n/c!p(n)=p(0)ccρn/c! for n≥cn\geq cn≥c.

Interpretation¶

Several servers share one queue. The service capacity grows with the number of jobs present until all ccc servers are busy; from then on the queue empties at the constant rate cμc\mucμ, just like an M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue with a fast server.

The figure compares Riley's ovens of Example 1, each with rate μ=2\mu=2μ=2 per hour. With one oven E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] explodes as λ\lambdaλ approaches 222; with two it stays small until λ\lambdaλ comes close to 444. At λ=1.9\lambda=1.9λ=1.9 the mean wait drops from 9.59.59.5 hours to 0.150.150.15 hours, far more than the factor two a naive guess would give.

The rate μmin⁡{n,c}\mu\min\{n,c\}μmin{n,c} comes from the minimum of independent exponentials: with n≤cn\leq cn≤c jobs in service the next completion occurs at rate nμn\munμ, and at most ccc servers can work. The condition ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1, that is, λ<cμ\lambda<c\muλ<cμ, makes the tail of p(n)p(n)p(n) geometric with ratio ρ\rhoρ and the normalizing sum finite; ccc must exceed λ/μ\lambda/\muλ/μ, not merely equal it.

Two slips are frequent. The mean number of busy servers is E[Ls]=λ/μ=cρ\mathbb{E}[L_s]=\lambda/\mu=c\rhoE[Ls​]=λ/μ=cρ, not ρ\rhoρ; ρ\rhoρ is the fraction of time a single server is busy. And the Poisson-like term (λ/μ)n/n!(\lambda/\mu)^n/n!(λ/μ)n/n! holds only for n≤cn\leq cn≤c; beyond ccc each step multiplies by ρ\rhoρ.
