Interpretation¶

Throw nnn points at random onto [0,1][0,1][0,1]. The gaps between neighbours have mean 1/(n+1)1/(n+1)1/(n+1), and measured in units of 1/n1/n1/n each gap behaves, for large nnn, like an exponential random variable. The first gap shows why: it exceeds x/nx/nx/n exactly when all nnn points miss [0,x/n][0,x/n][0,x/n], which has probability (1−x/n)n→e−x(1-x/n)^n\to\mathrm{e}^{-x}(1−x/n)n→e−x by the exponential limit.

The notes use this to explain the hospital case of Section 1.4. Each patient returns about every half year, and after many visits the patient's phase within the half-year becomes uniformly distributed (Theorem 1.4.3), and a uniform phase stays uniform when further gaps are added (Theorem 1.4.4 shows this for uniform gaps). The planner merges and sorts the visits of all patients, so the gaps the hospital sees are uniform spacings, and with nnn patients per period they are approximately exponential with mean 1/n1/n1/n: the gaps of a Poisson process with rate nnn. The conditional uniform arrival times of Week 1 run the same link in the other direction.

Two conditions matter. The phases must be independent and uniform; if all patients start together, the early visits bunch up (Example 3). And only the scaled gap has a limit: XknX_k^nXkn​ itself tends to 000. The theorem does not say that a finite merged stream is exactly Poisson, and the spacings remain dependent for every nnn.
