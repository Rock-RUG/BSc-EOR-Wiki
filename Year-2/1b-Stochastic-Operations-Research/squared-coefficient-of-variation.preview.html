Squared Coefficient of Variation¶

Definition¶

For a random variable XXX with finite variance and E[X]≠0\mathbb{E}[X]\ne0E[X]=0, the squared coefficient of variation (scv) is

CX2=Var⁡(X)(E[X])2.C_X^2=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{(\mathbb{E}[X])^2}.CX2​=(E[X])2Var(X)​.

In the course XXX is non-negative with positive mean: an inter-arrival time, a service time or a capacity per period. The notes write Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ for the scv of the inter-arrival times and Cs2C_s^2Cs2​ for the scv of the service times.

Interpretation¶

The scv compares the spread of XXX with its size. A standard deviation of 111 minute is small for a service that takes an hour on average and large for one that takes a minute; dividing the variance by the squared mean turns this judgement into a number without units.

The figure shows the capacity distributions that the notes use for Fig. 2.3.2: X=bBX=bBX=bB with B∼Bern⁡(p)B\sim\operatorname{Bern}(p)B∼Bern(p), p=1/(1+c2)p=1/(1+c^2)p=1/(1+c2) and b=μ(1+c2)b=\mu(1+c^2)b=μ(1+c2), so that E[X]=6.5\mathbb{E}[X]=6.5E[X]=6.5 and CX2=c2C_X^2=c^2CX2​=c2. For c2=0.5c^2=0.5c2=0.5 the server delivers 9.759.759.75 with probability 2/32/32/3; for c2=1c^2=1c2=1 it delivers 131313 with probability 1/21/21/2; for c2=2c^2=2c2=2 it delivers 19.519.519.5 with probability 1/31/31/3 and nothing otherwise. The mean, and hence the drift, is the same in all rows; only the lumpiness grows, and with it the queue fluctuations in the notes' simulation.

Reading the formula as CX2=E[X2]/(E[X])2−1C_X^2=\mathbb{E}[X^2]/(\mathbb{E}[X])^2-1CX2​=E[X2]/(E[X])2−1 shows that CX2≥0C_X^2\ge0CX2​≥0, with equality exactly when XXX is constant. Multiplying XXX by a positive constant, for example changing hours into minutes, leaves CX2C_X^2CX2​ unchanged, but adding a constant does not. Useful reference values are 000 for deterministic times, 1/31/31/3 for a uniform time on [0,A][0,A][0,A] and 111 for an exponential time; an scv above 111 means more variable than exponential.

A common slip is to mix up counts and times. Poisson arrivals with 11.811.811.8 jobs per week give weekly counts with scv 1/11.81/11.81/11.8, yet the inter-arrival times are exponential with scv 111, and Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ refers to the times. Later the scv returns in waiting-time formulas: Cs2C_s^2Cs2​ in the M/G/1 queue of Week 5, and Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ and Cs2C_s^2Cs2​ together in the G/G/c approximation of Week 6.

  • CαX2=CX2C_{\alpha X}^2=C_X^2CαX2​=CX2​ for every constant α>0\alpha>0α>0 (TF 2.3.4): the scv has no unit.
  • Reference values: X∼Exp⁡(λ)X\sim\operatorname{Exp}(\lambda)X∼Exp(λ) gives CX2=1C_X^2=1CX2​=1 (so TF 2.3.6, "exceeds 1", is false); X∼Unif⁡([0,A])X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,A])X∼Unif([0,A]) gives 1/31/31/3 (TF 2.3.7); X∼Pois⁡(λ)X\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)X∼Pois(λ) gives 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ (Solution 2.3.3); a sum of kkk i.i.d.   • Two-point construction: for μ>0\mu>0μ>0 and c2≥0c^2\ge0c2≥0, X=bBX=bBX=bB with B∼Bern⁡(p)B\sim\operatorname{Bern}(p)B∼Bern(p), p=1/(1+c2)p=1/(1+c^2)p=1/(1+c2) and b=μ(1+c2)b=\mu(1+c^2)b=μ(1+c2) has mean μ\muμ and scv c2c^2c2, because E[X2]=pb2=μ2(1+c2)\mathbb{E}[X^2]=pb^2=\mu^2(1+c^2)E[X2]=pb2=μ2(1+c2).
  • In the queue simulations of Section 2.3, greater relative variability of arrivals or capacity gives larger queue-length fluctuations; reducing the scv of the arrivals or the services is one of four ways to shorten queues, next to adding capacity and reducing load.
