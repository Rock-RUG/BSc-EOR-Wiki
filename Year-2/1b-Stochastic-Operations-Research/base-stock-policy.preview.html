Base-Stock Policy¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system, a base-stock policy with order-up-to level (base-stock level) S∈ZS\in\mathbb{Z}S∈Z places at the start of period kkk the order

Qk=S−Pk−1′,Q_k=S-P_{k-1}',Qk​=S−Pk−1′​,

where Pk−1′P_{k-1}'Pk−1′​ is the end-of-period inventory position of period k−1k-1k−1. Starting from P1=SP_1=SP1​=S, the position update Pk=Pk−1′+QkP_k=P_{k-1}'+Q_kPk​=Pk−1′​+Qk​ gives Pk=SP_k=SPk​=S for every kkk. Since Pk−1′=S−Dk−1P_{k-1}'=S-D_{k-1}Pk−1′​=S−Dk−1​, the order equals the previous period's demand, Qk=Dk−1Q_k=D_{k-1}Qk​=Dk−1​: every unit demanded is reordered one for one.

Interpretation¶

A base-stock policy replaces exactly what was sold. The parameter SSS fixes the inventory position: what the shop holds plus what it has ordered always adds up to SSS, and demand only decides how this total is split between shelf and pipeline.

As an inventory policy, "when" is every period with demand and "how much" is that demand. A common slip is to read SSS as the stock on the shelf: the shelf holds SSS minus what is on order.
