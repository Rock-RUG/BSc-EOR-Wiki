Tandem Queue¶

Definition¶

A tandem queue (stations "in tandem", that is, in line) is a network of stations 1,2,…,M1,2,\ldots,M1,2,…,M in which every job enters at station 1, moves after service at station iii to station i+1i+1i+1, and leaves the network after station MMM. Station iii has cic_ici​ servers, service times SiS_iSi​ with scv Cs,i2C_{s,i}^2Cs,i2​, and its own queue; Ca,i2C_{a,i}^2Ca,i2​ is the scv of the inter-arrival times at station iii, and Cd,i2C_{d,i}^2Cd,i2​ that of its inter-departure times. Jobs arrive externally at station 1 only, at rate λ\lambdaλ.

The defining link between the stations is that the departure process of station iii is the arrival process of station i+1i+1i+1; in particular Ca,i+12=Cd,i2C_{a,i+1}^2=C_{d,i}^2Ca,i+12​=Cd,i2​.

Interpretation¶

A tandem queue is a production line: each job needs several processing steps in a fixed order, and what one station sends out is exactly what the next one receives. Its total time in the line is the sum of the times at the stations.

The figure shows Example 1. External jobs reach station 1 with Ca,12=2C_{a,1}^2=2Ca,12​=2. Station 1 smooths this stream a little, so station 2 receives arrivals with Ca,22=Cd,12≈4/3C_{a,2}^2=C_{d,1}^2\approx4/3Ca,22​=Cd,12​≈4/3, computed with the departure scv approximation. Each station is then treated as a single G/G/1G/G/1G/G/1 queue with Sakasegawa's formula, and the expected sojourn times are added.

Two facts make this work. First, if every station is rate stable, jobs leave each station at the rate they enter, so all stations face the same arrival rate λ\lambdaλ and have utilization ρi=λ E[Si]/ci\rho_i=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S_i]/c_iρi​=λE[Si​]/ci​; the whole line is stable only if every ρi<1\rho_i<1ρi​<1. Second, by linearity of expectation E[J]=∑iE[Ji]\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[J]=\sum_i\mathbb{E}[J_i]E[J]=i∑​E[Ji​], even though the station sojourn times are dependent. What does not carry over is the arrival variability: downstream stations see the departures of upstream stations, which need not look like the original input. A machine with nearly constant service times sends nearly regular jobs downstream, so a Poisson model for the second station's arrivals would be poor (Exercise 7.1.5).

Common slips are to locate the bottleneck by the longest service time rather than the highest utilization (Example 2), and to add the dimensionless numbers ρi/(1−ρi)\rho_i/(1-\rho_i)ρi​/(1−ρi​) as if they were times (Example 3).

Key Results¶

  • Throughput: in a rate-stable tandem line every station has arrival and departure rate λ\lambdaλ, and ρi=λ E[Si]/ci<1\rho_i=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S_i]/c_i<1ρi​=λE[Si​]/ci​<1 is required at every station; the station with the largest ρi\rho_iρi​ is the bottleneck.
  • Sojourn time: E[J]=∑i=1ME[Ji]\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[J]=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbb{E}[J_i]E[J]=i=1∑M​E[Ji​] with E[Ji]=E[Wi]+E[Si]\mathbb{E}[J_i]=\mathbb{E}[W_i]+\mathbb{E}[S_i]E[Ji​]=E[Wi​]+E[Si​].
  • G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c stations (Section 7.1): apply Sakasegawa's formula station by station, with Ca,i+12=Cd,i2C_{a,i+1}^2=C_{d,i}^2Ca,i+12​=Cd,i2​ from the Departure SCV Approximation.
  • Variability propagates: lowering Cs,12C_{s,1}^2Cs,12​ lowers E[W1]\mathbb{E}[W_1]E[W1​] and Cd,12C_{d,1}^2Cd,12​, hence E[W2]\mathbb{E}[W_2]E[W2​] and Cd,22C_{d,2}^2Cd,22​, and so on down the line; the notes therefore advise improving from the start of the chain.
  • M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 stations (Section 7.3): the departures of a stable M/M/1 queue form a Poisson process of rate λ\lambdaλ, so every station is again M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 and E[J]=∑i=1ME[Si]/(1−ρi)\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[J]=\sum_{i=1}^M\mathbb{E}[S_i]/(1-\rho_i)E[J]=i=1∑M​E[Si​]/(1−ρi​) exactly.
  • In terms of a routing matrix, a tandem line has Pi,i+1=1P_{i,i+1}=1Pi,i+1​=1 for i<Mi<Mi<M and exit probability PM0=1P_{M0}=1PM0​=1.
