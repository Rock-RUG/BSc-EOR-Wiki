M/M/1 Queue¶

Definition¶

In Kendall's notation, the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue has Poisson arrivals with rate λ\lambdaλ, independent exponential service times SSS with rate μ=1/E[S]\mu=1/\mathbb{E}[S]μ=1/E[S], one server and unlimited waiting room; waiting-time results assume first-come first-served (FCFS). It is the birth-death queue with

λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=μ,\lambda(n)=\lambda,\qquad\mu(n)=\mu,λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=μ,

and its load is ρ=λ/μ\rho=\lambda/\muρ=λ/μ. If ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1, the stationary-probability recursion p(n+1)=ρ p(n)p(n+1)=\rho\,p(n)p(n+1)=ρp(n) gives the stationary distribution

p(n)=(1−ρ)ρn,n≥0.p(n)=(1-\rho)\rho^n,\qquad n\geq0.p(n)=(1−ρ)ρn,n≥0.

Interpretation¶

The M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue is the simplest queue with randomness in both arrivals and service, and the one in which every performance measure has a short formula: the number in the system is geometric with parameter ρ\rhoρ. It serves as the benchmark against which the effect of extra servers, blocking or less variable service is measured.

The curve is E[L]=ρ/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[L]=\rho/(1-\rho)E[L]=ρ/(1−ρ). The marked points are E[L]=23\mathbb{E}[L]=\tfrac{2}{3}E[L]=32​ at ρ=0.4\rho=0.4ρ=0.4, 444 at ρ=0.8\rho=0.8ρ=0.8 and 191919 at ρ=0.95\rho=0.95ρ=0.95, Riley's single oven. Doubling the load from 0.40.40.4 to 0.80.80.8 multiplies the mean number in the system by six, and the curve has no bound as ρ↑1\rho\uparrow1ρ↑1.

The condition ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 makes the normalizing sum ∑nρn\displaystyle \sum_n\rho^nn∑​ρn, a geometric series, finite; for ρ≥1\rho\geq1ρ≥1 there is no stationary distribution. Exponential service makes μ(n)=μ\mu(n)=\muμ(n)=μ by the memoryless property, and Poisson arrivals give PASTA, so an arriving job finds nnn jobs with probability p(n)p(n)p(n) and waits not at all with probability p(0)=1−ρp(0)=1-\rhop(0)=1−ρ. In the zero-based convention of Probability Theory, L∼Geo⁡(1−ρ)L\sim\operatorname{Geo}(1-\rho)L∼Geo(1−ρ) (the geometric distribution counts failures before the first success).

Two slips recur. E[L]\mathbb{E}[L]E[L] is not ρ\rhoρ; ρ\rhoρ is the mean number in service. And E[Q]=E[L]−ρ\mathbb{E}[Q]=\mathbb{E}[L]-\rhoE[Q]=E[L]−ρ, not E[L]−1\mathbb{E}[L]-1E[L]−1, because an empty system has an empty queue. The Pollaczek-Khinchine formula of Week 5 extends the waiting-time result to general service times.
