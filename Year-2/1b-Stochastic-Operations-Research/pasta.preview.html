Poisson Arrivals See Time Averages¶

Theorem¶

Let p(n)p(n)p(n) be the long-run fraction of time that the system contains nnn jobs, π(n)\pi(n)π(n) the long-run fraction of arrivals that find nnn jobs just before they arrive, λ=lim⁡t→∞A(t)/t\lambda=\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)/tλ=limt→∞​A(t)/t the arrival rate, and λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) the state-dependent arrival rate, the rate of arrivals while the system is in state nnn. If all these limits exist, then for every state nnn

λ π(n)=λ(n) p(n).\lambda\,\pi(n)=\lambda(n)\,p(n).λπ(n)=λ(n)p(n).

Hence, if the arrival rate does not depend on the state, λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ, the arrival-average and time-average state probabilities coincide:

π(n)=p(n).\pi(n)=p(n).π(n)=p(n).

If the arrival process is a Poisson process, then λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ for all nnn, so Poisson arrivals see time averages (PASTA). The notes state this corollary without proof, since a rigorous proof is difficult.

Interpretation¶

What arrivals see generally differs from what holds over time; PASTA says that the two agree for Poisson arrivals. Poisson arrivals come at moments that are independent of the past, so they cannot favour quiet or busy periods: they sample the system as if an inspector dropped by at random times.

The right panel is Example 2 with regular arrivals: the system is empty only 10%10\%10% of the time, yet every arrival finds it empty.

The discrete-time picture in the notes explains the condition. With ak=1a_k=1ak​=1 if a job arrives in period kkk, the arrival sees Lk−1L_{k-1}Lk−1​, which depends only on a1,…,ak−1a_1,\dots,a_{k-1}a1​,…,ak−1​. If aka_kak​ is independent of these, the conditional probability P(Lk−1=m∣ak=1)\mathbb{P}(L_{k-1}=m\mid a_k=1)P(Lk−1​=m∣ak​=1) equals P(Lk−1=m)\mathbb{P}(L_{k-1}=m)P(Lk−1​=m), which is the fraction of time with mmm jobs. Regular arrivals, or customers who come only when the queue is short, break this independence.

Two slips are common. PASTA concerns the arrival process, so it applies to the M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1 queue but not to the G/M/1G/M/1G/M/1 queue, whose service times are exponential but whose arrivals are not. And PASTA gives the distribution seen at arrival; it does not by itself give the waiting time, which also needs the remaining service of the job in service. The memoryless property supplies this for exponential service.

3. The inspection paradox. Mr Johnson walks to a railway crossing at random times and notes the direction of the first train to pass; trains alternate east and west, yet about 75%75\%75% of his first trains go east. One timetable that explains this: every hour an eastbound train passes at minute 000 and a westbound train at minute 151515. He meets a westbound train first only if he arrives during the 151515 minutes after an eastbound train, a fraction 15/60=25%15/60=25\%15/60=25% of the time; otherwise, a fraction 45/60=75%45/60=75\%45/60=75%, he meets an eastbound train. His random visits sample time, so they see time averages, while counting trains is an average over trains.
