Interpretation¶

Lead-time demand is the demand that hits the system while an order is on its way. Whatever is ordered now cannot help for lll periods, so the stock that is on hand or on order must cover XXX; the randomness that matters for the inventory level is therefore that of XXX, not of a single period.

Identical distributions make every window D[k−l,k)D[k-l,k)D[k−l,k) look like XXX, which is what lets the inventory pages replace a whole sample path by one random variable. A constant lead time matters too, because it fixes the number of terms.

With constant demand ddd the lead-time demand is the number dldldl, which is the reorder level of the EOQ model with lead times when the lead time is shorter than one order cycle. A frequent slip is to model XXX as lDl DlD, one period's demand scaled up: it has the right mean but too much spread, because it assumes that the lll days are all equally high or low.
