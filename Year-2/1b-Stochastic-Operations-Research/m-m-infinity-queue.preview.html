M/M/Infinity Queue¶

Definition¶

The M/M/∞M/M/\inftyM/M/∞ queue has Poisson arrivals with rate λ\lambdaλ and independent exponential service times with rate μ\muμ, and it assigns a free server to every arrival, so no job ever waits. It is the birth-death queue with

λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=nμ.\lambda(n)=\lambda,\qquad\mu(n)=n\mu.λ(n)=λ,μ(n)=nμ.

For every λ,μ>0\lambda,\mu>0λ,μ>0 the stationary number of jobs in the system is Poisson with mean λ/μ=λE[S]\lambda/\mu=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]λ/μ=λE[S]:

p(n)=e−λ/μ (λ/μ)nn!,n≥0.p(n)=\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda/\mu}\,\frac{(\lambda/\mu)^n}{n!},\qquad n\geq0.p(n)=e−λ/μn!(λ/μ)n​,n≥0.

Interpretation¶

With ample servers nobody waits, so the number of jobs present is simply the number of service periods that overlap at a given moment. That number is Poisson, and its mean is the arrival rate times the mean service time.

The bars are the self-service shop of Example 1, with λ=12\lambda=12λ=12 customers per hour and a mean stay of 10 minutes, so λ/μ=2\lambda/\mu=2λ/μ=2. One and two customers are equally likely, p(1)=p(2)≈0.271p(1)=p(2)\approx0.271p(1)=p(2)≈0.271, and the shop is empty with probability e−2≈0.135\mathrm{e}^{-2}\approx0.135e−2≈0.135. The dashed line marks the mean E[L]=2\mathbb{E}[L]=2E[L]=2.

The recursion p(n+1)=λp(n)/((n+1)μ)p(n+1)=\lambda p(n)/((n+1)\mu)p(n+1)=λp(n)/((n+1)μ) produces the terms of the exponential series ∑nan/n!=ea\displaystyle \sum_n a^n/n!=\mathrm{e}^{a}n∑​an/n!=ea, known from Taylor series, and this sum is finite for every a=λ/μa=\lambda/\mua=λ/μ. Hence there is no stability condition: the departure rate nμn\munμ keeps pace with any load. The model is the limit c→∞c\to\inftyc→∞ of the Erlang B model, whose distribution is this Poisson distribution cut off at ccc. Little's law confirms the mean: each job stays exactly its service time, so E[L]=λE[S]\mathbb{E}[L]=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]E[L]=λE[S].

A common slip is to read "no waiting" as "no congestion measure". The queue QQQ is always empty, but L=LsL=L_sL=Ls​ still fluctuates, with variance λ/μ\lambda/\muλ/μ, and its tail tells how many servers would almost always suffice.
