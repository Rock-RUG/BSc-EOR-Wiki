Workload¶

Definition¶

The workload, or work in the system, W(t)W(t)W(t) is the time the server needs to finish the remaining service of all jobs present at time ttt, if no further jobs arrived. For a single server that serves jobs in order of arrival (FIFO), the job that arrived last, job A(t)A(t)A(t), also leaves last, at DA(t)D_{A(t)}DA(t)​, so

W(t)=[DA(t)−t]+,W(t)=[D_{A(t)}-t]^+,W(t)=[DA(t)​−t]+,

The workload is also called the virtual waiting time: a job arriving at time ttt would wait W(t)W(t)W(t) before its service starts. The notes write W(t)W(t)W(t) for the workload process and WkW_kWk​ for the waiting time of job kkk; some other texts write V(t)V(t)V(t) for the workload.

Interpretation¶

The workload measures congestion in time instead of in jobs: not how many jobs are present, but how long the server would need to clear them all. Each arrival adds its service time, and the server removes one hour of work per hour.

The formula [DA(t)−t]+[D_{A(t)}-t]^+[DA(t)​−t]+ relies on two facts: under FIFO the last job to arrive is the last to leave, and a single server works at rate one, so the slope is −1-1−1. With ccc servers each server has its own workload, and the arriving job's wait depends on the server it joins.

Sampling the workload just before arrivals gives the waiting times, W(Ak−)=WkW(A_k-)=W_kW(Ak​−)=Wk​, and the Lindley recursion is exactly this sampling. A common slip is to equate the workload with the number of jobs present: two jobs can mean very different amounts of work (Example 3).
