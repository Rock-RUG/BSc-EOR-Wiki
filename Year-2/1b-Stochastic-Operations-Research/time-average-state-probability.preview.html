Time-Average State Probability¶

Definition¶

Let L(t)L(t)L(t) be the number of jobs in the system at time ttt, and let

Y(n,t)=∫0t1{L(s)=n} dsY(n,t)=\int_0^t\mathbf{1}\{L(s)=n\}\,\mathrm{d}sY(n,t)=∫0t​1{L(s)=n}ds

be the total time during [0,t][0,t][0,t] that the system spends in state nnn, where 1{⋅}\mathbf{1}\{\cdot\}1{⋅} is the indicator function. The time-average state probability of state nnn is

p(n)=lim⁡t→∞Y(n,t)t=lim⁡t→∞1t∫0t1{L(s)=n} ds,p(n)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{Y(n,t)}{t}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t\mathbf{1}\{L(s)=n\}\,\mathrm{d}s,p(n)=t→∞lim​tY(n,t)​=t→∞lim​t1​∫0t​1{L(s)=n}ds,

provided the limit exists. It is the long-run fraction of time during which exactly nnn jobs are present. The ratio Y(n,t)/tY(n,t)/tY(n,t)/t for a finite ttt is its empirical estimate from one sample path.

Interpretation¶

p(n)p(n)p(n) answers the question "if I watch the system for a very long time, what fraction of the time does it contain nnn jobs?" It is the view of a server or a manager who observes the system continuously, not the view of a customer.

The left panel is the sample path of Example 1. Adding the lengths of the horizontal pieces at height nnn gives Y(n,10)Y(n,10)Y(n,10): two time units at 000, five at 111 and three at 222. Dividing by 101010 gives the bars on the right, which sum to one because at every moment the system is in exactly one state.

The definition weights every moment equally, which is why it is a time average. Two conditions hide in it. First, the limit must exist; for a single finite path we only have the estimate Y(n,t)/tY(n,t)/tY(n,t)/t. Second, the fractions p(n)p(n)p(n) need not add up to one in the limit: if jobs keep arriving faster than they can be served, L(t)L(t)L(t) tends to infinity, every fixed state is visited for only a finite total time, and every p(n)p(n)p(n) is zero.

With the indicator the time in a state becomes an integral, just as the fundamental bridge of Probability Theory turns a probability into an expectation. A common slip is to treat p(n)p(n)p(n) as the fraction of customers who see nnn jobs; that is the arrival-average state probability π(n)\pi(n)π(n), and Example 2 shows the two can differ completely. In Week 4 PASTA gives conditions under which they coincide.
