SCV Estimation from Counts¶

Proposition¶

Interpretation¶

In practice the arrival moments of jobs are often not recorded, only the day or hour in which each customer arrived. The proposition says that such counts still contain the variability of the inter-arrival times: the ratio of the variance to the mean of the number of arrivals per period estimates Ca2C_a^2Ca2​, which is the input Sakasegawa's formula needs.

The figure shows the ten counts of Example 1. They fluctuate around their mean λ~=10\tilde{\lambda}=10λ~=10 with σ~2=3\tilde{\sigma}^2=3σ~2=3, much less than the variance 101010 that Poisson arrivals would produce, so the estimate Ca2≈0.3C_a^2\approx0.3Ca2​≈0.3 says the arrivals are more regular than Poisson.

The derivation in the notes uses P(A(t)<k)=P(Ak>t)\mathbb{P}(A(t)<k)=\mathbb{P}(A_k>t)P(A(t)<k)=P(Ak​>t), which links the count A(t)A(t)A(t) to the arrival time Ak=X1+⋯+XkA_k=X_1+\dots+X_kAk​=X1​+⋯+Xk​. The central limit theorem makes (Ak−k/λ)/(σk)(A_k-k/\lambda)/(\sigma\sqrt{k})(Ak​−k/λ)/(σk​) approximately standard normal. Assuming that (A(t)−λt)/(αt)(A(t)-\lambda t)/(\alpha\sqrt{t})(A(t)−λt)/(αt​) is also approximately standard normal, matching the two statements forces α=λ3/2σ\alpha=\lambda^{3/2}\sigmaα=λ3/2σ, so Var⁡(A(t))≈α2t=λ3σ2t\operatorname{Var}(A(t))\approx\alpha^2t=\lambda^3\sigma^2tVar(A(t))≈α2t=λ3σ2t.

Each assumption matters. The inter-arrival times must be i.i.d. with a finite second moment, and the process must be stationary over the logging horizon, otherwise λ~\tilde{\lambda}λ~ and σ~2\tilde{\sigma}^2σ~2 mix different regimes. The result is a limit for long periods: each period must contain many arrivals, as Example 3 shows. Finally, λ~\tilde{\lambda}λ~ is the mean count per period, not a rate per hour; the ratio is dimensionless, so no conversion is needed. A common slip is to divide by λ~2\tilde{\lambda}^2λ~2, which gives the SCV of the counts, not of the inter-arrival times.

Key Results¶

  • Asymptotic variance of counts. Var⁡(A(t))/t→λ3σ2\operatorname{Var}(A(t))/t\to\lambda^3\sigma^2Var(A(t))/t→λ3σ2 and E[A(t)]/t→λ\mathbb{E}[A(t)]/t\to\lambdaE[A(t)]/t→λ, so Var⁡(A(t))/E[A(t)]→λ2σ2=Ca2\operatorname{Var}(A(t))/\mathbb{E}[A(t)]\to\lambda^2\sigma^2=C_a^2Var(A(t))/E[A(t)]→λ2σ2=Ca2​.
  • Estimator. Ca2≈σ~2/λ~C_a^2\approx\tilde{\sigma}^2/\tilde{\lambda}Ca2​≈σ~2/λ~ from the mean and variance of the counts per period; with finitely many periods the limits are replaced by averages.
  • Poisson benchmark. For a Poisson process, A(t)∼Pois⁡(λt)A(t)\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda t)A(t)∼Pois(λt) has variance equal to its mean for every ttt, matching Ca2=1C_a^2=1Ca2​=1 for exponential inter-arrival times.
  • Service times. The notes remark that the same idea can be used to estimate Cs2C_s^2Cs2​.
  • Use. The estimate feeds the variability factor (Ca2+Cs2)/2(C_a^2+C_s^2)/2(Ca2​+Cs2​)/2 of Sakasegawa's formula.
