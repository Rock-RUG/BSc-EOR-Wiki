Queue-Length Conservation¶

Proposition¶

Let A(t)A(t)A(t) be the number of jobs that enter a system during [0,t][0,t][0,t], D(t)D(t)D(t) the number that leave it during [0,t][0,t][0,t], and L(0)L(0)L(0) the number present at time 000. Then the number of jobs in the system at time ttt is

Interpretation¶

The number in the system is bookkeeping: every job that enters adds one, every job that leaves subtracts one, and the jobs present at the start are carried along. Nothing about distributions, service order or the number of servers is needed; the identity holds on every sample path.

The counts must match the system: A(t)A(t)A(t) counts only the jobs that actually enter, so rejected arrivals are left out, and every job leaves at most once. Because the counting processes are right-continuous, an arrival at ttt is included in L(t)L(t)L(t); what job kkk sees on arrival is the left limit L(Ak−)L(A_k-)L(Ak​−).

The same balance appeared in the discrete-time queue recursion as Lk=Lk−1−dk+akL_k=L_{k-1}-d_k+a_kLk​=Lk−1​−dk​+ak​; here it holds at every instant of continuous time.
