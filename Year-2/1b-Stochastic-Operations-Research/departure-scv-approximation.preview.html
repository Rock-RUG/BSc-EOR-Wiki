Departure SCV Approximation¶

Proposition¶

Consider a stable G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c station with utilization ρ=λ E[S]/c<1\rho=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]/c<1ρ=λE[S]/c<1, inter-arrival scv Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ and service-time scv Cs2C_s^2Cs2​. The course approximates the squared coefficient of variation Cd2C_d^2Cd2​ of the inter-departure times by

Cd2≈1+(1−ρ2)(Ca2−1)+ρ2c(Cs2−1).C_d^2\approx1+(1-\rho^2)(C_a^2-1)+\frac{\rho^2}{\sqrt{c}}(C_s^2-1).Cd2​≈1+(1−ρ2)(Ca2​−1)+c​ρ2​(Cs2​−1).

For a single server, c=1c=1c=1, this reduces to (7.1.1)

Cd2≈(1−ρ2) Ca2+ρ2 Cs2.C_d^2\approx(1-\rho^2)\,C_a^2+\rho^2\,C_s^2.Cd2​≈(1−ρ2)Ca2​+ρ2Cs2​.

This is an interpolation, not an exact result. In a tandem queue it is used as the link between stations, Ca,i+12=Cd,i2C_{a,i+1}^2=C_{d,i}^2Ca,i+12​=Cd,i2​.

Interpretation¶

How variable is the output of a queue? It depends on how often the server idles. A busy server releases jobs at the pace of its service times; an idle server simply passes on the gaps between arrivals.

The figure plots the c=1c=1c=1 formula for the first station of Example 1, Ca2=2C_a^2=2Ca2​=2 and Cs2=1/2C_s^2=1/2Cs2​=1/2, which gives Cd2=2−1.5ρ2C_d^2=2-1.5\rho^2Cd2​=2−1.5ρ2. At ρ=0\rho=0ρ=0 the curve starts at the arrival level and at ρ=1\rho=1ρ=1 it ends at the service level; in between it weights the two by 1−ρ21-\rho^21−ρ2 and ρ2\rho^2ρ2. The marked point is the actual load ρ=2/3\rho=2/3ρ=2/3, where Cd2≈4/3C_d^2\approx4/3Cd2​≈4/3.

The two end points carry the argument. When ρ\rhoρ is close to 111 the server is seldom idle, so most inter-departure times are service times and Cd2≈Cs2C_d^2\approx C_s^2Cd2​≈Cs2​. When ρ\rhoρ is small the server is usually idle at an arrival, so a job leaves about one service time after it came and the inter-departure times are close to the inter-arrival times, Cd2≈Ca2C_d^2\approx C_a^2Cd2​≈Ca2​. The weights 1−ρ21-\rho^21−ρ2 and ρ2\rho^2ρ2 are a choice that respects both limits; the exactness of any value in between is not claimed. With ccc servers the service term is damped by 1/c1/\sqrt{c}1/c​, since several servers interleave their completions.

A useful check: for Ca2=Cs2=1C_a^2=C_s^2=1Ca2​=Cs2​=1 the formula gives Cd2=1C_d^2=1Cd2​=1 for every ρ\rhoρ and ccc, which matches the exact fact that a stable M/M/1 queue sends out a Poisson process. A common slip is to feed Ca,12C_{a,1}^2Ca,12​ to every station of a line: the second station sees the first station's departures, not the original arrivals.

Key Results¶

  • G/G/1G/G/1G/G/1 (7.1.1): Cd2≈(1−ρ2)Ca2+ρ2Cs2C_d^2\approx(1-\rho^2)C_a^2+\rho^2C_s^2Cd2​≈(1−ρ2)Ca2​+ρ2Cs2​, a weighted average of Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ and Cs2C_s^2Cs2​, so it always lies between them.
  • G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c: Cd2≈1+(1−ρ2)(Ca2−1)+ρ2c(Cs2−1)C_d^2\approx1+(1-\rho^2)(C_a^2-1)+\frac{\rho^2}{\sqrt{c}}(C_s^2-1)Cd2​≈1+(1−ρ2)(Ca2​−1)+c​ρ2​(Cs2​−1), with ρ=λ E[S]/c\rho=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]/cρ=λE[S]/c.
  • Limits: Cd2→Ca2C_d^2\to C_a^2Cd2​→Ca2​ as ρ→0\rho\to0ρ→0; for c=1c=1c=1, Cd2→Cs2C_d^2\to C_s^2Cd2​→Cs2​ as ρ→1\rho\to1ρ→1.
  • Tandem link: Ca,i+12=Cd,i2C_{a,i+1}^2=C_{d,i}^2Ca,i+12​=Cd,i2​, combined with Sakasegawa's formula at every station.
  • Propagation: for c=1c=1c=1 a reduction Δ\DeltaΔ in Cs,12C_{s,1}^2Cs,12​ lowers Cd,12C_{d,1}^2Cd,12​ by ρ12Δ\rho_1^2\Deltaρ12​Δ, so every downstream station also waits less; the notes advise improving variability from the start of the chain (TF 7.1.1).
