Birth-Death Queue¶

Definition¶

A birth-death queue, called the M(n)/M(n)/1M(n)/M(n)/1M(n)/M(n)/1 queue in the course notes, is a model for the number of jobs L(t)L(t)L(t) in which jobs arrive and leave one at a time and, given L=nL=nL=n, the time to the next arrival is exponential with rate λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) and the time to the next departure is exponential with rate μ(n)\mu(n)μ(n). As Δt↓0\Delta t\downarrow0Δt↓0,

P(L(t+Δt)=n+1∣L(t)=n)=λ+(n)Δt+o(Δt),n≥0,\mathbb{P}\bigl(L(t+\Delta t)=n+1\mid L(t)=n\bigr)=\lambda^+(n)\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\qquad n\geq0,P(L(t+Δt)=n+1∣L(t)=n)=λ+(n)Δt+o(Δt),n≥0,
P(L(t+Δt)=n−1∣L(t)=n)=μ(n)Δt+o(Δt),n≥1,\mathbb{P}\bigl(L(t+\Delta t)=n-1\mid L(t)=n\bigr)=\mu(n)\Delta t+o(\Delta t),\qquad n\geq1,P(L(t+Δt)=n−1∣L(t)=n)=μ(n)Δt+o(Δt),n≥1,

where λ+(n)≤λ(n)\lambda^+(n)\leq\lambda(n)λ+(n)≤λ(n) is the rate at which jobs enter in state nnn; without blocking, λ+(n)=λ(n)\lambda^+(n)=\lambda(n)λ+(n)=λ(n). Arrivals are "births" and departures "deaths". The exponential models of Kendall's notation, and others such as a finite calling population, differ only in the choice of the two rate functions:

Interpretation¶

One template covers many queues: the state is the number of jobs, it moves up or down by one, and all the modelling goes into two rate functions. Choosing λ+(n)\lambda^+(n)λ+(n) says who may enter and how eagerly; choosing μ(n)\mu(n)μ(n) says how much service capacity is working when nnn jobs are present.

The figure shows the controlled two-oven policy of Example 1. Jobs arrive at rate λ=1.9\lambda=1.9λ=1.9 per hour in every state. Up to four jobs one oven works and μ(n)=2\mu(n)=2μ(n)=2; from five jobs on, in the orange region, the second oven is switched on and μ(n)=4\mu(n)=4μ(n)=4. The other three policies change only these rates.

Memoryless times are what make the rates enough. Given L=nL=nL=n, the remaining times to the next arrival and departure are exponential whatever happened before, so the future depends on the past only through nnn. With nnn busy servers, each finishing at rate μ\muμ, the minimum of independent exponentials gives the total departure rate nμn\munμ; this is where μmin⁡{n,c}\mu\min\{n,c\}μmin{n,c} comes from. Inter-arrival times need not be identically distributed: λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) may change with the state (True/False 5.1.1). The stationary probabilities follow from the stationary-probability recursion with λ+(n)\lambda^+(n)λ+(n), provided the normalizing sum is finite; a system that blocks at a finite level always satisfies this.

Two slips are common. Arrivals that are blocked still arrive, so they count in λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) but not in λ+(n)\lambda^+(n)λ+(n). And arrivals see time averages only when λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ, which PASTA guarantees for Poisson arrivals; when the arrival rate depends on the state, as with a finite calling population, π(n)≠p(n)\pi(n)\neq p(n)π(n)=p(n) in general.

Key Results¶

  • Stationary probabilities: λ+(n)p(n)=μ(n+1)p(n+1)\lambda^+(n)p(n)=\mu(n+1)p(n+1)λ+(n)p(n)=μ(n+1)p(n+1), solved by the stationary-probability recursion; for a birth-death queue these level-crossing equations are equivalent to the balance equations.
  • Mean acceptance rate (equation 5.1.4): λ+=∑n≥0λ+(n)p(n)\displaystyle \lambda^+=\sum_{n\geq0}\lambda^+(n)p(n)λ+=n≥0∑​λ+(n)p(n); this is the throughput, and Little's law uses it: E[J]=E[L]/λ+\mathbb{E}[J]=\mathbb{E}[L]/\lambda^+E[J]=E[L]/λ+ and E[W]=E[Q]/λ+\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[Q]/\lambda^+E[W]=E[Q]/λ+.
  • Single server with μ(n)=μ\mu(n)=\muμ(n)=μ: utilization is λ+/μ=E[Ls]=1−p(0)\lambda^+/\mu=\mathbb{E}[L_s]=1-p(0)λ+/μ=E[Ls​]=1−p(0) (equation 5.1.5), which is below the load ρ=λ/μ\rho=\lambda/\muρ=λ/μ when jobs are blocked.
  • When arrivals form a Poisson process, λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ also in blocking states, so by PASTA π(n)=p(n)\pi(n)=p(n)π(n)=p(n) and the fraction of arrivals blocked at capacity KKK is p(K)p(K)p(K).   • Without blocking, a stationary distribution requires a finite normalizing sum; for constant arrival rate and ccc servers this is λ<cμ\lambda<c\muλ<cμ.
