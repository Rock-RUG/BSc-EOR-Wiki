Remaining Service Time¶

Theorem¶

In a single-server queue, let Sr(t)S_r(t)Sr​(t) be the time the job in service at time ttt still needs, with Sr(t)=0S_r(t)=0Sr​(t)=0 when the server is idle. Consider the M/G/1 queue: jobs arrive as a Poisson process with rate λ\lambdaλ, service times are i.i.d. copies of SSS, and the queue is rate stable with load ρ=λE[S]<1\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]<1ρ=λE[S]<1. If E[S2]<∞\mathbb{E}[S^2]<\inftyE[S2]<∞, then the time-average remaining service time, which by PASTA is also the expected remaining service time seen by an arriving job, is (Theorem 5.4.1)

E[Sr]=lim⁡t→∞1t∫0tSr(s) ds=λ2 E[S2].\mathbb{E}[S_r]=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t S_r(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\mathbb{E}[S^2].E[Sr​]=t→∞lim​t1​∫0t​Sr​(s)ds=2λ​E[S2].

Interpretation¶

An arriving job that finds the server busy is more likely to land in a long service than in a short one, because long services cover more of the time axis. This is the inspection paradox of Section 4.5, the trains seen by Mr Johnson, in a queueing setting. That is why E[Sr]\mathbb{E}[S_r]E[Sr​] depends on the second moment E[S2]\mathbb{E}[S^2]E[S2] and not only on E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S].

In Example 1, shown in the figure, jobs with service times 111 and 333 start at times 111 and 333. During each service SrS_rSr​ falls linearly from SkS_kSk​ to 000, adding a triangle of area Sk2/2S_k^2/2Sk2​/2; the long job contributes nine times as much. The proof of the theorem is this picture in general: the renewal reward theorem with reward Sk2/2S_k^2/2Sk2​/2 per departure and departure rate δ\deltaδ gives E[Sr]=δ E[S2]/2\mathbb{E}[S_r]=\delta\,\mathbb{E}[S^2]/2E[Sr​]=δE[S2]/2.

The conditions each play a role. Rate stability gives δ=λ\delta=\lambdaδ=λ. The finite second moment makes the mean triangle area finite. Poisson arrivals are only needed in the last step, where PASTA turns the time average into what arriving jobs see. Since idle periods count as Sr=0S_r=0Sr​=0, E[Sr]=ρ E[Sr∣Sr>0]\mathbb{E}[S_r]=\rho\,\mathbb{E}[S_r\mid S_r>0]E[Sr​]=ρE[Sr​∣Sr​>0].

For exponential service times the memoryless property gives E[Sr∣Sr>0]=E[S]\mathbb{E}[S_r\mid S_r>0]=\mathbb{E}[S]E[Sr​∣Sr​>0]=E[S], which is why E[W]=E[L]E[S]\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[L]\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]=E[L]E[S] holds in the M/M/1 queue but fails for general service times. The remaining service time is the part of the workload that belongs to the job in service.

A common slip is to set the mean remaining service time of a busy server equal to E[S]/2\mathbb{E}[S]/2E[S]/2; this holds only for deterministic services. In general it equals E[S2]/(2E[S])\mathbb{E}[S^2]/(2\mathbb{E}[S])E[S2]/(2E[S]).
