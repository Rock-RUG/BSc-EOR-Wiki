Sakasegawa's Formula¶

Proposition¶

Consider a G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c queue (Kendall's notation): one queue in front of ccc identical servers, i.i.d. inter-arrival times XXX with mean 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ, and i.i.d. service times SSS, independent of the arrivals. Let

ρ=λ E[S]c∈[0,1),Ca2=Var⁡(X)(E[X])2,Cs2=Var⁡(S)(E[S])2\rho=\frac{\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]}{c}\in[0,1),\qquad C_a^2=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(X)}{(\mathbb{E}[X])^2},\qquad C_s^2=\frac{\operatorname{Var}(S)}{(\mathbb{E}[S])^2}ρ=cλE[S]​∈[0,1),Ca2​=(E[X])2Var(X)​,Cs2​=(E[S])2Var(S)​

be the load of the station and the squared coefficients of variation of the inter-arrival and service times. Sakasegawa's formula approximates the expected waiting time in queue by

E[W]≈Ca2+Cs22⋅ρ2(c+1)−11−ρ⋅E[S]c.\mathbb{E}[W]\approx\frac{C_a^2+C_s^2}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho^{\sqrt{2(c+1)}-1}}{1-\rho}\cdot\frac{\mathbb{E}[S]}{c}.E[W]≈2Ca2​+Cs2​​⋅1−ρρ2(c+1)​−1​⋅cE[S]​.

No exact expression is known for the G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c queue. For c=1c=1c=1 and Poisson arrivals (so Ca2=1C_a^2=1Ca2​=1) the formula is exact: it is the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula of the M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1 queue. Other renewal arrivals with Ca2=1C_a^2=1Ca2​=1 do not make it exact.

Interpretation¶

The formula is a product of three factors: variability (Ca2+Cs2)/2(C_a^2+C_s^2)/2(Ca2​+Cs2​)/2, a load factor that explodes as ρ→1\rho\to1ρ→1, and a time scale E[S]/c\mathbb{E}[S]/cE[S]/c. It turns λ\lambdaλ, E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S], ccc and the two SCVs into a waiting-time estimate and shows which lever matters most.

The figure plots the formula for Example 1, one microscope with E[S]=5\mathbb{E}[S]=5E[S]=5 minutes and Cs2=425C_s^2=\tfrac{4}{25}Cs2​=254​, as the arrival rate and hence ρ\rhoρ varies while the SCVs are kept fixed. At ρ=56\rho=\tfrac{5}{6}ρ=65​ the uniform inter-arrival times (Ca2​=Ca2=112C_a^2=\tfrac{1}{12}121​) give 3.043.043.04 minutes and Poisson arrivals (Ca2​=Ca2=1C_a^2=11) give 14.514.514.5 minutes. Both curves rise steeply as ρ→1\rho\to1ρ→1, like (1−ρ)−1(1-\rho)^{-1}(1−ρ)−1.

The notes draw four lessons. First, near ρ=1\rho=1ρ=1 waiting is long and very sensitive to ρ\rhoρ, so reduce the load first. Second, ρ=λE[S]/c\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]/cρ=λE[S]/c leaves only three ways to do so: fewer arrivals, shorter services, more servers. Third, E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] is proportional to Ca2+Cs2C_a^2+C_s^2Ca2​+Cs2​, so once ρ\rhoρ is under control, reduce variability. Fourth, E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] scales with E[S]/c\mathbb{E}[S]/cE[S]/c: seen from the queue, ccc servers finish jobs ccc times as fast as one.

The route to the formula is to start from the exact M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 result, replace (1+1)/2(1+1)/2(1+1)/2 by (1+Cs2)/2(1+C_s^2)/2(1+Cs2​)/2 (still exact, M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1), then by (Ca2+Cs2)/2(C_a^2+C_s^2)/2(Ca2​+Cs2​)/2 (no longer exact, but good in practice). The power of ρ\rhoρ handles ccc servers and equals 111 for c=1c=1c=1. The condition ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 is essential: as ρ↑1\rho\uparrow1ρ↑1 the estimate grows without bound, and for ρ≥1\rho\geq1ρ≥1 the formula is meaningless (see rate stability). With c>1c>1c>1, ρ\rhoρ is the utilization of each server only when the servers are equally fast and share the work equally.

Key Results¶

  • Single server. For c=1c=1c=1 the exponent 2(c+1)−1=1\sqrt{2(c+1)}-1=12(c+1)​−1=1, so E[W]≈Ca2+Cs22ρ1−ρE[S]\mathbb{E}[W]\approx\frac{C_a^2+C_s^2}{2}\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]≈2Ca2​+Cs2​​1−ρρ​E[S]; for Poisson arrivals (M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1, Ca2=1C_a^2=1Ca2​=1) this is exact (TF 6.1.1), and for the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue (Ca2​=Ca2=Cs2=1C_a^2=C_s^2=1Cs2​=1) it is ρ1−ρE[S]\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\mathbb{E}[S]1−ρρ​E[S].
  • Deterministic system. A stable D/D/1D/D/1D/D/1 queue has Ca2=Cs2=0C_a^2=C_s^2=0Ca2​=Cs2​=0, hence E[W]=0\mathbb{E}[W]=0E[W]=0, which is exact (TF 6.1.3).
  • Load sensitivity. With E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] and the SCVs fixed, E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] is proportional to ρ/(1−ρ)\rho/(1-\rho)ρ/(1−ρ) when c=1c=1c=1, so near ρ=1\rho=1ρ=1 a small reduction of the load removes a large part of the waiting (TF 6.1.2).
  • Variability. Mixing job types with different mean service times raises Cs2C_s^2Cs2​: Var⁡(S)=pVar⁡(S1)+qVar⁡(S2)+pq(E[S1]−E[S2])2\operatorname{Var}(S)=p\operatorname{Var}(S_1)+q\operatorname{Var}(S_2)+pq(\mathbb{E}[S_1]-\mathbb{E}[S_2])^2Var(S)=pVar(S1​)+qVar(S2​)+pq(E[S1​]−E[S2​])2 (Exercise 6.1.7).
  • Estimating the SCVs. When only counts per period are logged, Ca2C_a^2Ca2​ can be estimated by SCV estimation from counts.
  • Sojourn time. The expected sojourn time follows as E[J]≈E[W]+E[S]\mathbb{E}[J]\approx\mathbb{E}[W]+\mathbb{E}[S]E[J]≈E[W]+E[S].
