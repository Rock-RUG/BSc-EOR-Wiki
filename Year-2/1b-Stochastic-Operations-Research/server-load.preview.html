Server Load¶

Definition¶

For a single-server queue with arrival rate λ\lambdaλ, mean inter-arrival time E[X]=1/λ\mathbb{E}[X]=1/\lambdaE[X]=1/λ, mean service time E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] and service rate μ=1/E[S]\mu=1/\mathbb{E}[S]μ=1/E[S], the server load is

ρ=λE[S]=λμ=E[S]E[X].\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}=\frac{\mathbb{E}[S]}{\mathbb{E}[X]}.ρ=λE[S]=μλ​=E[X]E[S]​.

For a GB/GC/cG^B/G^C/cGB/GC/c queue, in which batches arrive at rate λ\lambdaλ and contain on average E[B]\mathbb{E}[B]E[B] jobs, each job needs on average E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] of service, and each of the ccc identical servers serves on average E[C]\mathbb{E}[C]E[C] jobs per service time, the load is

ρ=λ E[S]c E[B]E[C].\rho=\frac{\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]}{c}\,\frac{\mathbb{E}[B]}{\mathbb{E}[C]}.ρ=cλE[S]​E[C]E[B]​.

Interpretation¶

The load compares the work that arrives with the work the station can do. Each time unit brings on average λ\lambdaλ jobs of E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] work each, while one server clears one unit of work per unit time, so ρ\rhoρ is the offered work per unit time and per unit of capacity.

The figure is Example 2. Each arrival raises the blue staircase by its service time. The red line ttt is the work one server can clear by time ttt. With service time 1.51.51.5 the staircase grows at average slope 0.750.750.75 and falls further and further below the line; at t=12t=12t=12 it stands at 999 against 121212. With service time 2.52.52.5 it grows at average slope 1.251.251.25 and pulls away above the line, reaching 151515: work then accumulates faster than the server can remove it.

The load is computed from the offered arrivals, including jobs that are later blocked, so it can exceed 111. It is a long-run average: a week in which capacity drops below demand does not by itself make ρ>1\rho>1ρ>1. In the general formula the batch size E[B]\mathbb{E}[B]E[B] multiplies the work per arrival, while ccc and E[C]\mathbb{E}[C]E[C] multiply the capacity.

In the discrete-time models of Week 2, ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 means negative queue drift, λ<μ\lambda<\muλ<μ.

Common slips: the load is not always at most 111 (TF 4.1.5); it is the utilization that is. Equality of the arrival and departure rates does not mean ρ=1\rho=1ρ=1 (TF 4.1.3); it means rate stability, which needs ρ≤1\rho\leq1ρ≤1.

  • Small excess capacity makes queues long and variable. With λ=6\lambda=6λ=6 arrivals per period, raising the mean capacity from 6.16.16.1 to 6.56.56.5 per period, that is, lowering ρ\rhoρ from 0.980.980.98 to 0.920.920.92, cut the simulated mean queue length from 4.364.364.36 to 0.450.450.45 and its variance from 25.3025.3025.30 to 0.640.640.64 (Section 2.3).
