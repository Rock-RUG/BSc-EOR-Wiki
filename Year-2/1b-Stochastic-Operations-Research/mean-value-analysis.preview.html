Mean Value Analysis¶

Method¶

Mean value analysis (MVA) computes the mean performance of a closed network of mmm single-server first-come-first-served stations with exponential service times, for populations n=1,2,…,Nn=1,2,\ldots,Nn=1,2,…,N. Its input is the mean service times E[Si]>0\mathbb{E}[S_i]>0E[Si​]>0 and the visit ratios Vi≥0V_i\geq0Vi​≥0 with V1=1V_1=1V1​=1. Start with E[Li(0)]=0\mathbb{E}[L_i(0)]=0E[Li​(0)]=0 for all iii and, for n=1,2,…n=1,2,\ldotsn=1,2,…, compute

E[Ji(n)]=(1+E[Li(n−1)])E[Si],E[J(n)]=∑i=1mViE[Ji(n)],\mathbb{E}[J_i(n)]=\bigl(1+\mathbb{E}[L_i(n-1)]\bigr)\mathbb{E}[S_i],\qquad \mathbb{E}[J(n)]=\sum_{i=1}^mV_i\mathbb{E}[J_i(n)],E[Ji​(n)]=(1+E[Li​(n−1)])E[Si​],E[J(n)]=i=1∑m​Vi​E[Ji​(n)],
TH1(n)=nE[J(n)],THi(n)=ViTH1(n),E[Li(n)]=THi(n)E[Ji(n)].\mathrm{TH}_1(n)=\frac{n}{\mathbb{E}[J(n)]},\qquad \mathrm{TH}_i(n)=V_i\mathrm{TH}_1(n),\qquad \mathbb{E}[L_i(n)]=\mathrm{TH}_i(n)\mathbb{E}[J_i(n)].TH1​(n)=E[J(n)]n​,THi​(n)=Vi​TH1​(n),E[Li​(n)]=THi​(n)E[Ji​(n)].

The first line is the arrival theorem and the cycle time; the second is Little's law for the whole network and for each station. Under the assumptions above the results are exact.

Interpretation¶

MVA climbs the population one job at a time. The arrival theorem says what a job meets with nnn jobs in the network, using only means from the network with n−1n-1n−1 jobs; Little's law then turns times into throughputs and mean populations. No state probabilities are ever needed.

The figure shows the five-station example of Section 7.5: mean service times 2,1.5,1,0.75,0.52,1.5,1,0.75,0.52,1.5,1,0.75,0.5 at stations 1 to 5, each visited once per cycle (Example 2). The curves of the four faster stations flatten out (at n=12n=12n=12 they stand at 2.502.502.50, 0.950.950.95, 0.580.580.58 and 0.320.320.32), while almost every additional job joins station 1, the bottleneck, whose mean reaches 7.657.657.65.

The assumptions enter through the arrival theorem: exponential service makes the remaining service of the job in service as long as a fresh one, and the single-server first-come-first-served stations make the network one for which the theorem holds. The recursion must go up from n=0n=0n=0; population nnn cannot be computed without n−1n-1n−1. A useful check is ∑iE[Li(n)]=n\displaystyle \sum_i\mathbb{E}[L_i(n)]=ni∑​E[Li​(n)]=n, which holds automatically when the visit ratios are used consistently (Example 4).

Enumerating states instead would mean handling all ways to place nnn jobs at mmm stations, (n+m−1m−1)\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}(m−1n+m−1​) by stars and bars, which is 1820 for the figure at n=12n=12n=12; MVA needs mmm steps per population. Algorithm 7.5.1 wraps the recursion in a closure with a cache (memoization), so later requests reuse earlier populations. The notes apply it to CONWIP lines.

Key Results¶

  • Exactness. For closed networks of single-server FCFS stations with exponential service times and fixed or Markov routing, the recursion gives the exact means (Section 7.5).
  • Population check. ∑i=1mE[Li(n)]=TH1(n)∑iViE[Ji(n)]=n\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb{E}[L_i(n)]=\mathrm{TH}_1(n)\sum_iV_i\mathbb{E}[J_i(n)]=ni=1∑m​E[Li​(n)]=TH1​(n)i∑​Vi​E[Ji​(n)]=n.
  • Throughput bounds. TH1(n)≤n/∑iViE[Si]\displaystyle \mathrm{TH}_1(n)\leq n/\sum_iV_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]TH1​(n)≤n/i∑​Vi​E[Si​], with equality at n=1n=1n=1, and TH1(n)≤1/max⁡iViE[Si]\mathrm{TH}_1(n)\leq1/\max_iV_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]TH1​(n)≤1/maxi​Vi​E[Si​], the capacity of the bottleneck.
  • Cycle time. E[J(n)]=n/TH1(n)\mathbb{E}[J(n)]=n/\mathrm{TH}_1(n)E[J(n)]=n/TH1​(n), which for a CONWIP line with nnn cards is the mean time a job spends in the line.
  • Cost. NNN populations take N⋅mN\cdot mN⋅m evaluations of the arrival-theorem formula; with the cache, a request for a population already computed returns immediately.

3. In Python. A compact version of Algorithm 7.5.1, with the cache kept in a closure:

def make_mva(ES, V):
    m = len(ES)
    cache = {0: (0, [0] * m)}  # population 0: every station is empty
    def mva(n):
        if n in cache:
            return cache[n]
        _, L_old = mva(n - 1)
        J = [(1 + L_old[i]) * ES[i] for i in range(m)]  # arrival theorem
        TH = n / sum(V[i] * J[i] for i in range(m))      # Little, network
        cache[n] = (TH, [V[i] * TH * J[i] for i in range(m)])
        return cache[n]
    return mva

mva = make_mva([2, 1], [1, 1])
print(mva(3))  # (0.4666..., [2.2666..., 0.7333...])

The call mva(3) fills the cache for n=1,2,3n=1,2,3n=1,2,3; a later mva(2) only looks it up.
