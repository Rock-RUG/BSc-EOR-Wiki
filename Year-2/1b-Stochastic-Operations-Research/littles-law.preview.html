Little's Law¶

Theorem¶

Consider an input-output system that jobs enter at times AkA_kAk​ and leave at times DkD_kDk​, with sojourn times Jk=Dk−AkJ_k=D_k-A_kJk​=Dk​−Ak​ and number in system L(t)L(t)L(t). If the system is rate-stable with δ=λ∈(0,∞)\delta=\lambda\in(0,\infty)δ=λ∈(0,∞), and the arrival-average sojourn time

E[J]=lim⁡n→∞1n∑k=1nJk\mathbb{E}[J]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_kE[J]=n→∞lim​n1​k=1∑n​Jk​

exists and is finite, then the time-average number in the system E[L]=lim⁡t→∞t−1∫0tL(s) ds\mathbb{E}[L]=\lim_{t\to\infty}t^{-1}\int_0^tL(s)\,\mathrm{d}sE[L]=limt→∞​t−1∫0t​L(s)ds exists and

E[L]=λ E[J].\mathbb{E}[L]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[J].E[L]=λE[J].

Interpretation¶

The average number of things in a system equals the rate at which they enter times the average time each one stays. On a 100100100 km highway where one car enters per minute and every car drives at 505050 km/h, each car spends 120120120 minutes on the road, so on average 1⋅120=1201\cdot120=1201⋅120=120 cars are on it.

The figure shows the five jobs of Example 2. The blue step function A(t)A(t)A(t) counts arrivals and the red one D(t)D(t)D(t) counts departures; their vertical distance is L(t)L(t)L(t). Job kkk fills a horizontal strip of height one from AkA_kAk​ to DkD_kDk​, so the strip has length JkJ_kJk​. The area between the curves can be added up vertically, ∫L(s) ds\int L(s)\,\mathrm{d}s∫L(s)ds, or horizontally, ∑kJk\displaystyle \sum_kJ_kk∑​Jk​; both equal 101010 job-minutes.

The proof charges each job for its time in the system, which gives ∑k≤D(t)Jk≤∫0tL(s) ds≤∑k≤A(t)Jk\displaystyle \sum_{k\le D(t)}J_k\le\int_0^tL(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\le\sum_{k\le A(t)}J_kk≤D(t)∑​Jk​≤∫0t​L(s)ds≤k≤A(t)∑​Jk​. Dividing by ttt, the two bounds tend to δ E[J]\delta\,\mathbb{E}[J]δE[J] and λ E[J]\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[J]λE[J], which coincide by rate stability. Both assumptions matter: without δ=λ\delta=\lambdaδ=λ the bounds differ, and rate stability alone does not make E[J]\mathbb{E}[J]E[J] finite.

The law links a time average (E[L]\mathbb{E}[L]E[L]) to an arrival average (E[J]\mathbb{E}[J]E[J]), and it applies to any part of the system with its own entry rate and residence time: the queue gives E[Q]=λ E[W]\mathbb{E}[Q]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[W]E[Q]=λE[W], the servers give E[Ls]=λ E[S]\mathbb{E}[L_s]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]E[Ls​]=λE[S]. Units are a quick check: jobs equal jobs per minute times minutes, so E[J]=λ E[L]\mathbb{E}[J]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[L]E[J]=λE[L] cannot be right. The law concerns long-run averages; it does not say how long it takes to clear a given queue.

Key Results¶

  • E[L]=λ E[J]\mathbb{E}[L]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[J]E[L]=λE[J] under rate stability and a finite average sojourn time (Theorem 4.4.1); the time average E[L]\mathbb{E}[L]E[L] exists as a consequence.
  • Applied to the waiting room: E[Q]=λ E[W]\mathbb{E}[Q]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[W]E[Q]=λE[W], with WWW the waiting time.
  • Applied to the servers of a stable G/G/cG/G/cG/G/c queue: the time-average number of busy servers is E[Ls]=λ E[S]\mathbb{E}[L_s]=\lambda\,\mathbb{E}[S]E[Ls​]=λE[S] (Ex 4.4.6); for one server this is the busy fraction ρ\rhoρ (TF 4.4.3), in line with server utilization.
  • Rate stability is not enough on its own: with Ak=kA_k=kAk​=k and Sk=2S_k=2Sk​=2 when kkk is a square and Sk=1S_k=1Sk​=1 otherwise, λ=δ=1\lambda=\delta=1λ=δ=1, but L(s)≈sL(s)\approx\sqrt{s}L(s)≈s​ and the time average is infinite.
  • The identity ∫0tL(s) ds=∑k≤A(t)Jk\displaystyle \int_0^tL(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\sum_{k\le A(t)}J_k∫0t​L(s)ds=k≤A(t)∑​Jk​ holds only at times ttt when the system is empty (TF 4.4.2).
  • Combined with PASTA and the memoryless service times, which give E[W]=E[L] E[S]\mathbb{E}[W]=\mathbb{E}[L]\,\mathbb{E}[S]E[W]=E[L]E[S] ((4.5.4c)), the law gives the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 results E[J]=E[S]/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[J]=\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)E[J]=E[S]/(1−ρ) and E[L]=ρ/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[L]=\rho/(1-\rho)E[L]=ρ/(1−ρ).

Hence E[L]=1⋅120=120\mathbb{E}[L]=1\cdot120=120E[L]=1⋅120=120 cars. If each car drives at 100100100 km/h, the number on the road halves to 606060.

2. Five jobs. 3. Busy servers. Patients arrive at a clinic at λ=12\lambda=12λ=12 per hour and each consultation takes on average E[S]=15\mathbb{E}[S]=15E[S]=15 minutes =0.25=0.25=0.25 hours. 4. A system that is not in balance. Suppose a country has 161616 million inhabitants with expected lifetime 808080 years. Little's law gives an entry rate of 16/80=0.216/80=0.216/80=0.2 million births per year, about 400040004000 per week if a year has 505050 weeks, and in a population of constant size the deaths would match this.
