Waiting Time¶

Definition¶

Interpretation¶

The waiting time is the delay before anything is done for a job: the time in the queue, not at the server. It is the quantity that patients waiting for a psychiatrist or customers in a shop experience as lost time, and it is what admission and capacity decisions try to keep small.

The figure shows the five-job path of Example 1 lane by lane. A blue bar in the queue lane runs from a job's arrival to its start of service, so its length is WkW_kWk​: W2=2W_2=2W2​=2, W3=1W_3=1W3​=1 and W5=1W_5=1W5​=1. Jobs 1 and 4 have no bar in the queue lane because they find the server idle and go straight into service.

The formula Wk=[Dk−1−Ak]+W_k=[D_{k-1}-A_k]^+Wk​=[Dk−1​−Ak​]+ is a statement about one FIFO server: job kkk waits exactly as long as its predecessor is still in the system. Equivalently, WkW_kWk​ is the amount of work found on arrival, the workload W(Ak−)W(A_k-)W(Ak​−), which is why the workload is also called the virtual waiting time. With several servers, or another service order, the predecessor no longer determines the wait.

The averages E[W]\mathbb{E}[W]E[W] and P(W≤x)\mathbb{P}(W\leq x)P(W≤x) count jobs, not time: they describe what arriving customers experience, which can differ from what an observer of the server sees over time (Example 2).

A common slip is to read the number of jobs found on arrival as a measure of the wait. In Example 1, jobs 2 and 3 both find exactly one job present, but they wait 2 and 1 minutes, because what matters is the remaining work of that job.
