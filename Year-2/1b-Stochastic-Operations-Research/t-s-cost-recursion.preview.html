(T, S) Cost Recursion¶

Method¶

Consider a single-item inventory system under periodic review, controlled by a (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) policy: once every TTT periods an order is placed that raises the inventory position PPP to SSS. Period demands D1,D2,…D_1,D_2,\dotsD1​,D2​,… are i.i.d. copies of DDD with pmf fk=P(D=k)f_k=\mathbb{P}(D=k)fk​=P(D=k), k=0,1,2,…k=0,1,2,\dotsk=0,1,2,…, the lead time is lll periods, and XXX is the lead-time demand, the demand over lll periods. Interpretation¶

The recursion follows one replenishment cycle period by period. In a period with position xxx we pay the expected inventory cost L(x)L(x)L(x), a demand DDD arrives, and we continue from position x−Dx-Dx−D with one period fewer to go. When the clock reaches n=0n=0n=0 we pay the order cost KKK and a new cycle starts at SSS.

Demand 000, 111 or 222, with probabilities 14\tfrac{1}{4}41​, 12\tfrac{1}{2}21​, 14\tfrac{1}{4}41​, leads from x=2x=2x=2 to x=2x=2x=2, 111 or 000 with one period to go. In the notes' terms LLL is the running cost, KKK the hitting cost and {n ⁣:n=0}\{n\colon n=0\}{n:n=0} the hitting set. Because the right-hand side always has one period fewer to go, the recursion terminates and can be evaluated backwards from n=0n=0n=0, just as the dynamic programming recursions of OR Modelling run stage by stage. Nothing is minimized inside the recursion, however: it evaluates one fixed policy, and TTT and SSS are chosen afterwards. The division by TTT is the renewal reward theorem: the cycles are i.i.d. because each starts at SSS and demands are i.i.d.

A common slip is to use c(x)c(x)c(x) instead of L(x)L(x)L(x). With a positive lead time the inventory level is not the position, and c(x)c(x)c(x) ignores the demand still to come before an order arrives.

Key Results¶

  • Recursion. V(n,x)=L(x)+∑k≥0fkV(n−1,x−k)\displaystyle V(n,x)=L(x)+\sum_{k\geq0}f_kV(n-1,x-k)V(n,x)=L(x)+k≥0∑​fk​V(n−1,x−k) for n>0n>0n>0 and V(0,x)=KV(0,x)=KV(0,x)=K, with running cost L(x)=E[c(x−X)]L(x)=\mathbb{E}[c(x-X)]L(x)=E[c(x−X)] from I=P−XI=P-XI=P−X (base-stock inventory distribution).
  • Average cost. Expected cycle cost V(T,S)V(T,S)V(T,S), cycle length TTT, long-run average cost V(T,S)/TV(T,S)/TV(T,S)/T.
  • Unrolled form. Expanding the recursion gives V(T,S)=K+∑j=0T−1E[L(S−D1−⋯−Dj)]\displaystyle V(T,S)=K+\sum_{j=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}[L(S-D_1-\dots-D_j)]V(T,S)=K+j=0∑T−1​E[L(S−D1​−⋯−Dj​)], where the term j=0j=0j=0 is L(S)L(S)L(S).
  • Termination. Each step lowers nnn by one, so the recursion has depth TTT; caching the values V(n,x)V(n,x)V(n,x) (memoization) avoids computing a state twice.
  • Relation to other policies. The (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) policy is the (T,s,S)(T,s,S)(T,s,S) policy with s=−∞s=-\inftys=−∞. The (s,S)(s,S)(s,S) recursion is not of this form: there the state does not move forward in time, which leads to a division by 1−f01-f_01−f0​ (TF 5.9.1).
  • Grid search. The average cost has no closed-form minimizer in general; one evaluates V(T,S)/TV(T,S)/TV(T,S)/T on a grid of integer pairs (T,S)(T,S)(T,S) and keeps the cheapest.
