Queue Sample-Path Simulation¶

Method¶

A queue sample-path simulation turns one list of inter-arrival times x1,…,xnx_1,\dots,x_nx1​,…,xn​ and service times s1,…,sns_1,\dots,s_ns1​,…,sn​ into the arrival, departure, waiting and sojourn times of the nnn jobs of a single-server queue that serves jobs in order of arrival (FIFO) and starts empty. Start with A0=D0=0A_0=D_0=0A0​=D0​=0; these two values only initialize the recursion and belong to no job. For k=1,…,nk=1,\dots,nk=1,…,n, apply the arrival-time recursion and the single-server service recursion:

Ak=Ak−1+xk,Dk=max⁡{Ak,Dk−1}+sk,Jk=Dk−Ak,Wk=Jk−sk.A_k=A_{k-1}+x_k,\qquad D_k=\max\{A_k,D_{k-1}\}+s_k,\qquad J_k=D_k-A_k,\qquad W_k=J_k-s_k.Ak​=Ak−1​+xk​,Dk​=max{Ak​,Dk−1​}+sk​,Jk​=Dk​−Ak​,Wk​=Jk​−sk​.

Here AkA_kAk​ is the arrival time, DkD_kDk​ the departure time, JkJ_kJk​ the sojourn time and WkW_kWk​ the waiting time in queue of job kkk. Along the computed path, the average waiting and sojourn times are

W‾=1n∑k=1nWk,J‾=1n∑k=1nJk.\overline{W}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}W_k,\qquad \overline{J}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_k.W=n1​k=1∑n​Wk​,J=n1​k=1∑n​Jk​.

The time-average number of jobs in the system over [0,Dn][0,D_n][0,Dn​] and the fraction of jobs that wait at least a threshold TTT are

Lˉ=1Dn∑k=1nJk,qT=1n∑k=1n1{Wk≥T}.\bar{L}=\frac{1}{D_n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_k,\qquad q_T=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{W_k\geq T\}.Lˉ=Dn​1​k=1∑n​Jk​,qT​=n1​k=1∑n​1{Wk​≥T}.

To simulate a model rather than replay data, the inputs are generated as independent draws xkx_kxk​ of the inter-arrival time XXX and sks_ksk​ of the service time SSS, for instance by inverse-transform sampling.

Interpretation¶

The recursion is careful bookkeeping. A job can start only when it has arrived and the server has finished the previous job; it leaves one service time later. Fed with a long list of random inputs, the recursion produces one possible history of the queue, and averages along that history estimate long-run performance without any formula for a stationary distribution.

The figure is Example 1. Each row is a job. Job 2 arrives at time 2 while job 1 is in service until D1=4D_1=4D1​=4, so it waits W2=2W_2=2W2​=2 (dashed) before its red service bar starts. Job 5 arrives at time 10 after the server has emptied at D4=9D_4=9D4​=9, so its service starts at once.

The recursion for DkD_kDk​ uses only Dk−1D_{k-1}Dk−1​ because one server serves the jobs in arrival order. With ccc servers, job kkk need not wait for job k−1k-1k−1, and the simulation must track the remaining work w(j)w(j)w(j) of each server jjj instead (Algorithm 3.3.6). Because the queue starts empty, early jobs wait less than typical jobs, so a path must be long before its averages mean much.

The input step is the inverse-transform sampling of Probability Theory, and the discrete-time queue recursion of Week 2 is the same bookkeeping per period. Probabilistic arithmetic (Section 1.5) can push whole pmfs through the Lindley recursion, because Wk−1W_{k-1}Wk−1​ is independent of Sk−1S_{k-1}Sk−1​ and XkX_kXk​.

A common slip is to treat AkA_kAk​, WkW_kWk​ and SkS_kSk​ as independent when computing the distribution of Dk=Ak+Wk+SkD_k=A_k+W_k+S_kDk​=Ak​+Wk​+Sk​. They are not: a long inter-arrival time makes AkA_kAk​ large and WkW_kWk​ small, so the two are negatively correlated (Example 3).

2. A simulation in Python. The notes' M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 example with λ=1\lambda=1λ=1 and μ=1.1\mu=1.1μ=1.1:

import numpy as np

rng = np.random.default_rng(3)
n, lam, mu = 10_000, 1.0, 1.1
X = rng.exponential(1 / lam, n)  # inter-arrival times x_k
S = rng.exponential(1 / mu, n)   # service times s_k

A, D = np.zeros(n + 1), np.zeros(n + 1)  # A[0] = D[0] = 0
for k in range(1, n + 1):
    A[k] = A[k - 1] + X[k - 1]
    D[k] = max(A[k], D[k - 1]) + S[k - 1]
J = D[1:] - A[1:]  # skip the initial values A[0], D[0]
W = J - S
print(W.mean(), J.mean(), J.sum() / D[n], (W >= 5).mean())
