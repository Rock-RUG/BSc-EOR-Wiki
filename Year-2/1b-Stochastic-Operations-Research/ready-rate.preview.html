Ready Rate¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system with end-of-period inventory level Ik′=Ik−DkI_k'=I_k-D_kIk′​=Ik​−Dk​, the ready rate over periods 1,…,n1,\dots,n1,…,n is the fraction of periods that end with a non-negative inventory level,

α=1n∑k=1n1{Ik′≥0},\alpha=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbf{1}\{I_k'\ge0\},α=n1​k=1∑n​1{Ik′​≥0},

and the long-run ready rate is its limit as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞, when this limit exists. Since Ik′≥0I_k'\ge0Ik′​≥0 exactly when Ik≥DkI_k\ge D_kIk​≥Dk​, a period counts as ready when the stock at its start covers all of its demand and no backlog is left at its end.

Interpretation¶

The ready rate answers a yes-or-no question for each period: did the period end without any customer waiting? It is the share of periods with the answer yes, so it measures how often the shelf is "ready", not how much demand was served.

The bars are the end-of-period levels Ik′=6,3,2,−2,4,1,−1,1,0,5I_k'=6,3,2,-2,4,1,-1,1,0,5Ik′​=6,3,2,−2,4,1,−1,1,0,5 of the (2,8)(2,8)(2,8) policy with lead time 111 (Example 1). Periods 444 and 777 end with a backlog and are not ready; the other eight, including period 999 with I9′=0I_9'=0I9′​=0, are ready, so α=8/10\alpha=8/10α=8/10.

Each part of the definition matters. The level is taken at the end of the period, after the demand, because a shortage shows only then. The test is ≥0\ge0≥0, so a period that empties the shelf exactly still counts as ready. A period with a backlog carried over is not ready even if no new demand arrives. And every failure counts the same: a period short by one unit and a period short by ten units each lower α\alphaα by 1/n1/n1/n, which is why the fill rate is a separate measure. The ready rate is an average of indicators, the inventory analogue of the time-average state probability of a queue. A high ready rate is not always the goal: when holding a unit costs much more than backlogging it, the best service levels are close to zero (Exercise 2.5.7). In Week 5 the long-run ready rate of a base-stock policy becomes the probability P(X+D≤S)\mathbb{P}(X+D\le S)P(X+D≤S).
