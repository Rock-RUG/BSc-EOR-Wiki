Batch Formation Time¶

Proposition¶

Jobs of family iii arrive with inter-arrival times of mean 1/λi1/\lambda_i1/λi​ and are collected into batches of fixed size B≥1B\geq1B≥1; a batch is complete when its BBBth job arrives. The time from a job's arrival until its batch is complete, averaged over the jobs, has expectation

B−12λi.\frac{B-1}{2\lambda_i}.2λi​B−1​.

The kkkth job of a batch waits for the B−kB-kB−k later arrivals of its batch, with expected duration (B−k)/λi(B-k)/\lambda_i(B−k)/λi​; averaging over k=1,…,Bk=1,\dots,Bk=1,…,B gives the result. The batch itself needs on average (B−1)/λi(B-1)/\lambda_i(B−1)/λi​ from its first to its last arrival.

Interpretation¶

An arbitrary job sits somewhere in its batch: the first one waits for all B−1B-1B−1 others, the last one for none. On average it waits for half of the other B−1B-1B−1 arrivals, and each of them takes 1/λi1/\lambda_i1/λi​ on average.

The figure takes B=5B=5B=5. Job 111 waits 444 inter-arrival times, job 222 waits 333, and so on down to job 555, which completes the batch and waits 000. The mean of 4,3,2,1,04,3,2,1,04,3,2,1,0 is 2=(B−1)/22=(B-1)/22=(B−1)/2, so the mean formation time is 2/λi2/\lambda_i2/λi​. The arrivals are drawn evenly spaced only for readability: by linearity of expectation the result uses nothing but the mean inter-arrival time.

Two features of the formula drive the setup model. It grows linearly in BBB, so for large batches formation dominates the sojourn time. And it uses the arrival rate λi\lambda_iλi​ of the job's own family, not the total rate λ\lambdaλ, since only jobs of the same colour fill a batch; a rare family waits much longer. The time a job spends at the server has the same structure: on average B−12\frac{B-1}{2}2B−1​ jobs of its batch are served before it, which gives the term B+12E[S0]\frac{B+1}{2}\mathbb{E}[S_0]2B+1​E[S0​] after the setup.

A common slip is to write B/(2λi)B/(2\lambda_i)B/(2λi​): that would make even a batch of size B=1B=1B=1 wait, whereas a single job completes its own batch at once.

Key Results¶

  • Mean formation time (6.2.1). B−12λi\frac{B-1}{2\lambda_i}2λi​B−1​ for a job of family iii; it is zero for B=1B=1B=1 and linear in BBB (TF 6.2.1, Exercise 6.2.7).
  • Whole batch. From first to last arrival a batch takes (B−1)/λi(B-1)/\lambda_i(B−1)/λi​ on average, twice the mean wait of an arbitrary job.
  • Only means matter. The expectation depends on the inter-arrival distribution only through 1/λi1/\lambda_i1/λi​.
  • Position in the batch. The same averaging gives the expected time at the server E[R]+B+12E[S0]\mathbb{E}[R]+\frac{B+1}{2}\mathbb{E}[S_0]E[R]+2B+1​E[S0​] (6.2.5, Exercise 6.2.10).
  • Trade-off. Formation time is the part of the sojourn time that rises with BBB, against the batch queue time from Sakasegawa's formula, which falls with BBB (server setup).
