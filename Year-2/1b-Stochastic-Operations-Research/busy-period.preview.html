Busy Period¶

Definition¶

A busy period, or busy time, UUU of a single server is the time from an arrival that finds the system empty until the server next becomes idle. An idle period III is the time from the end of a busy period until the next arrival. A work-conserving server therefore alternates idle and busy periods.

For the M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1 queue with arrival rate λ\lambdaλ, mean service time E[S]\mathbb{E}[S]E[S] and load ρ=λE[S]<1\rho=\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]<1ρ=λE[S]<1,

E[I]=1λ,E[U]=E[S]1−ρ.\mathbb{E}[I]=\frac{1}{\lambda},\qquad \mathbb{E}[U]=\frac{\mathbb{E}[S]}{1-\rho}.E[I]=λ1​,E[U]=1−ρE[S]​.

Interpretation¶

A busy period is one uninterrupted stretch of work. It starts with a job that finds the server free and lasts until all work has been cleared, including every job that arrived in the meantime; its length is the sum of the service times of the jobs served in it.

Both formulas rest on Poisson arrivals. During the first service an expected λE[S]\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]λE[S] new jobs arrive, by Adam's law with E[arrivals∣S]=λS\mathbb{E}[\text{arrivals}\mid S]=\lambda SE[arrivals∣S]=λS. Each of them starts a busy period of its own, independent of the others and with the same mean, which gives E[U]=E[S]+λE[S] E[U]\mathbb{E}[U]=\mathbb{E}[S]+\lambda\mathbb{E}[S]\,\mathbb{E}[U]E[U]=E[S]+λE[S]E[U]. Likewise, by the memoryless property, the time from the end of a busy period to the next arrival is again exponential with mean 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ. Without Poisson arrivals both steps fail (Example 4). The condition ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 is needed too: for ρ≥1\rho\geq1ρ≥1 the equation has no finite positive solution.

Since 1/(1−ρ)1/(1-\rho)1/(1−ρ) explodes as ρ↑1\rho\uparrow1ρ↑1, a heavily loaded server rarely gets a rest. In Week 4 the alternation of busy and idle periods gives ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I])\rho=\mathbb{E}[U]/(\mathbb{E}[U]+\mathbb{E}[I])ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I]).

A common slip is to confuse the busy period with a single service time or with one job's sojourn time; a busy period usually serves several jobs.

Key Results¶

  • For a work-conserving single server, the length of a busy period equals the total service time of the jobs served in it.
  • M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1 with ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1 (Section 4.1, (4.1.3)): E[I]=1/λ\mathbb{E}[I]=1/\lambdaE[I]=1/λ and E[U]=E[S]+ρ E[U]\mathbb{E}[U]=\mathbb{E}[S]+\rho\,\mathbb{E}[U]E[U]=E[S]+ρE[U], so E[U]=E[S]/(1−ρ)\mathbb{E}[U]=\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)E[U]=E[S]/(1−ρ).
  • For ρ≥1\rho\geq1ρ≥1 the busy period has no finite mean, and the formula must not be used.
  • Week 4 (Ex 4.3.7, by the renewal reward theorem): for a stable G/G/1G/G/1G/G/1 queue, ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I])\rho=\mathbb{E}[U]/(\mathbb{E}[U]+\mathbb{E}[I])ρ=E[U]/(E[U]+E[I]).
  • Week 5: in the M/G/1M/G/1M/G/1 queue it takes on average q E[U]=q E[S]/(1−ρ)q\,\mathbb{E}[U]=q\,\mathbb{E}[S]/(1-\rho)qE[U]=qE[S]/(1−ρ) to clear qqq jobs, a building block of the N-policy analysis.
