Arrival and Departure Rates¶

Definition¶

Let A(t)=max⁡{k ⁣:Ak≤t}A(t)=\max\{k\colon A_k\leq t\}A(t)=max{k:Ak​≤t} be the number of arrivals and D(t)=∑k≥11{Dk≤t}\displaystyle D(t)=\sum_{k\geq1}\mathbf{1}\{D_k\leq t\}D(t)=k≥1∑​1{Dk​≤t} the number of departures in [0,t][0,t][0,t] along one sample path of a queueing system (the counting processes of Section 3.1). The arrival rate λ\lambdaλ and the departure rate δ\deltaδ are the long-run average numbers of arrivals and departures per unit time,

λ=lim⁡t→∞A(t)t,δ=lim⁡t→∞D(t)t.\lambda=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{A(t)}{t},\qquad \delta=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{D(t)}{t}.λ=t→∞lim​tA(t)​,δ=t→∞lim​tD(t)​.

The notes assume that both limits exist and are finite, with 0<δ≤λ<∞0<\delta\leq\lambda<\infty0<δ≤λ<∞.

Interpretation¶

A rate is a count divided by elapsed time, taken over a very long horizon. It describes one sample path, so it makes sense for any arrival process whose long-run average exists, not only for a Poisson process.

The figure is Example 1, with inter-arrival times alternating 1,3,1,3,…1,3,1,3,\dots1,3,1,3,…. The ratio A(t)/tA(t)/tA(t)/t jumps up at each arrival (dots, value k/Akk/A_kk/Ak​) and decays like k/tk/tk/t until the next one. Its peaks 1,12,35,12,59,…1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{5},\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{9},\dots1,21​,53​,21​,95​,… and its troughs both close in on the green level λ=12\lambda=\frac{1}{2}λ=21​, the reciprocal of the mean inter-arrival time 222.

Theorem 4.1.1 makes this precise: counting arrivals per unit time and averaging the arrival times per job give reciprocal limits. The proof squeezes ttt between the last arrival before ttt and the next one, AA(t)≤t<AA(t)+1A_{A(t)}\leq t<A_{A(t)+1}AA(t)​≤t<AA(t)+1​. For iid inter-arrival times the strong law of large numbers gives An/n→E[X]A_n/n\to\mathbb{E}[X]An​/n→E[X], so λ=1/E[X]\lambda=1/\mathbb{E}[X]λ=1/E[X], which matches the arrival rate of the Poisson process in Probability Theory. The limit need not exist: an arrival stream that switches on and off in ever longer blocks has no long-run rate (Example 3).

Departures cannot outrun arrivals, so δ≤λ\delta\leq\lambdaδ≤λ. The departure rate is smaller when jobs are blocked, or when the queue grows without bound and the server is the bottleneck.

A common slip is to equate lim⁡A(t)/t\lim A(t)/tlimA(t)/t with lim⁡An/n\lim A_n/nlimAn​/n: the first is λ\lambdaλ, the second 1/λ1/\lambda1/λ (TF 4.1.2). Another is to read A(t)→∞A(t)\to\inftyA(t)→∞ as a sign of instability; every queue with ongoing arrivals has it (TF 4.1.1).
