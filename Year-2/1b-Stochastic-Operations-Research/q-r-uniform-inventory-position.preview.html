Interpretation¶

A (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) system behaves like QQQ base-stock systems taking turns. In a period that starts with position iii, the next lll periods of demand decide the level, exactly as in a base-stock system with order-up-to level iii; in the notes, a bear that wakes only at position iii sees just that system. The theorem says that the QQQ bears are awake equally often.

Orders come in multiples of 333, so only the demand modulo 333 matters: a demand of one or four units moves the position one level down cyclically (from 111 to 333). The assumption on the limits is not empty. If D≡2D\equiv2D≡2 and Q=2Q=2Q=2, the position never leaves r+2r+2r+2 (Example 4); a demand of one unit with positive probability is enough to let the position reach every level. Independence of PPP and XXX holds because the level in period kkk is the position lll periods earlier minus the demand since then, which has not influenced that position. The result turns the (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) system into a mixture of base-stock systems, so every base-stock formula is simply averaged over iii. A common slip is to think that the position is always rrr when an order is placed (TF 5.8.2): it can be anywhere in the window after ordering.
