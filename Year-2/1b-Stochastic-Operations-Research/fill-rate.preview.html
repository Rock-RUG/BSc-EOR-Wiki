Fill Rate¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system, the demand DkD_kDk​ of period kkk can be served from stock as far as the on-hand inventory Ik+=max⁡{Ik,0}I_k^+=\max\{I_k,0\}Ik+​=max{Ik​,0} at the start of the period allows. The fill rate over periods 1,…,n1,\dots,n1,…,n is the fraction of demand served from on-hand stock,

β=∑k=1nmin⁡{Dk,Ik+}∑k=1nDk,\beta=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\min\{D_k,I_k^+\}}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}D_k},β=∑k=1n​Dk​∑k=1n​min{Dk​,Ik+​}​,

defined when ∑k=1nDk>0\displaystyle \sum_{k=1}^{n}D_k>0k=1∑n​Dk​>0; the long-run fill rate is its limit as n→∞n\to\inftyn→∞, when this limit exists.

Interpretation¶

The fill rate counts units, not periods: of all the items customers asked for, which share could be handed over at once? A period that serves nine of ten requested units earns nine units of credit, where the ready rate gives it none.

The minimum says that no more than the on-hand stock can be handed out, the positive part that a backlog serves no one. The level is taken at the start of the period, after the replenishment and before the demand. As a ratio of totals, β\betaβ weighs a busy period more than a quiet one. In a single period with stock yyy, the served amount min⁡{D,y}\min\{D,y\}min{D,y} is exactly the sales of the newsvendor model, so E[min⁡{D,y}]/E[D]\mathbb{E}[\min\{D,y\}]/\mathbb{E}[D]E[min{D,y}]/E[D] is its fill rate. A common slip is to expect the fill rate to be at least the ready rate: if the shortages fall in periods of heavy demand, β\betaβ is the smaller of the two (Example 3).
