Cycle Service Level¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system with lead time lll, a cycle is the time between the arrivals of two consecutive replenishments. An order Qk−l>0Q_{k-l}>0Qk−l​>0 placed at the start of period k−lk-lk−l arrives just before period kkk starts, so a cycle ends in period k−1k-1k−1, and it is served when the end-of-period inventory level Ik−1′I_{k-1}'Ik−1′​ is non-negative. The cycle service level over periods 1,…,n1,\dots,n1,…,n is the fraction of cycles that end without backlog,

defined when at least one replenishment arrives in periods l+1,…,nl+1,\dots,nl+1,…,n.

Interpretation¶

The cycle service level asks, for every delivery: was there still something on the shelf when it arrived? It judges whole replenishment cycles, which usually span many periods, and is popular because it is so easy to check.

Just before them the level is I4′=−2I_4'=-2I4′​=−2, I7′=−1I_7'=-1I7′​=−1 and I9′=0I_9'=0I9′​=0: two cycles end with customers waiting, the third with an empty but not negative shelf. Hence αc=1/3\alpha_c=1/3αc​=1/3, far below the ready rate 0.80.80.8 and the fill rate 20/2320/2320/23 of the same path.

Checking only the last period of a cycle is enough, because between two deliveries the level can only fall: if it is non-negative just before a delivery, it was non-negative throughout the cycle. The index shift by lll is needed because a cycle ends when an order arrives, not when it is placed. Without a lead time an order arrives as soon as it is placed, so a cycle fails only if the level is already negative at the moment the order is triggered (Example 3).

As the notes stress, the measure is a poor guide to service: it ignores how many units were short and for how long. The cycles themselves are the random counterpart of the ordering cycle of OR Modelling, where deterministic demand makes every cycle the same.

Key Results¶

  • 0≤αc≤10\le\alpha_c\le10≤αc​≤1; the numerator counts the cycles that end with Ik−1′≥0I_{k-1}'\ge0Ik−1′​≥0, the denominator all cycles that end in periods l,…,n−1l,\dots,n-1l,…,n−1.
  • Within a cycle no replenishment arrives, so the level is nonincreasing and Ik−1′≥0I_{k-1}'\ge0Ik−1′​≥0 holds exactly when no backlog occurred anywhere in the cycle.
  • The cycle service level differs in general from the ready rate, a fraction of periods, and from the fill rate, a fraction of units; all three can be read off one simulated sample path.
  • Unlike the ready rate and the fill rate, the notes give no closed expression for the cycle service level under a base-stock policy (Section 5.7); it is estimated by simulation.
  • If holding stock is much more expensive than backlog (h≫h≫bh\gg bb), the preferred cycle service level, like the other two service levels, is close to zero (Exercise 2.5.7).
