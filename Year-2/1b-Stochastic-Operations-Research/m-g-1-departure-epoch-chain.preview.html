Interpretation¶

With general service times, the number of jobs at an arbitrary moment does not determine the future: one also needs the remaining service time of the job in service. Just after a departure that remaining time is zero, so at departure epochs the number of jobs alone suffices and the queue becomes a one-dimensional recursion.

In the figure (Example 1) jobs arrive at 000, 0.60.60.6, 1.41.41.4, 3.83.83.8, 6.06.06.0 and 6.56.56.5, and services take 2.02.02.0, 1.01.01.0, 1.51.51.5, 0.80.80.8 and 1.21.21.2; the green dots are the values LkL_kLk​. From one green dot to the next, LLL gains the YkY_kYk​ jobs that arrive during the next service and loses the job that completes it, except after the fourth departure: the system is empty, the server waits for job 5, and the next value is Y5Y_5Y5​, not Y5−1Y_5-1Y5​−1.

The positive part [Lk−1−1]+[L_{k-1}-1]^{+}[Lk−1​−1]+ handles this empty case. Poisson arrivals are essential: by the independent increments of the Poisson process, Yk∣Sk=s∼Pois⁡(λs)Y_k\mid S_k=s\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda s)Yk​∣Sk​=s∼Pois(λs) regardless of the past, so conditioning on SSS gives f(j)f(j)f(j) and YkY_kYk​ is independent of Lk−1L_{k-1}Lk−1​.

(5.5.1) is a level-crossing equation at departure epochs: level nnn is down-crossed only from Lk−1=n+1L_{k-1}=n+1Lk−1​=n+1 with no arrival during SkS_kSk​, probability f(0)f(0)f(0), and up-crossed from m≥1m\ge1m≥1 when Yk>n+1−mY_k>n+1-mYk​>n+1−m and from 000 when Yk>nY_k>nYk​>n. The recursion resembles the discrete-time queue recursion of Week 2, with a service as the period. A common off-by-one slip: for m≥1m\ge1m≥1 the survivor function is evaluated at n+1−mn+1-mn+1−m, not n−mn-mn−m, because the departing job leaves first.
