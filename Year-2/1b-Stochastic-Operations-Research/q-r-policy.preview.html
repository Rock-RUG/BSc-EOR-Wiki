(Q, r) Policy¶

Definition¶

In a periodic-review single-item inventory system, a (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) policy with lot size Q∈{1,2,… }Q\in\{1,2,\dots\}Q∈{1,2,…} and reorder level r∈Zr\in\mathbb{Z}r∈Z places at the start of period kkk the order

Qk=Q⌈r+1−Pk−1′Q⌉1{Pk−1′≤r},Q_k=Q\left\lceil\frac{r+1-P_{k-1}'}{Q}\right\rceil\mathbf{1}\{P_{k-1}'\le r\},Qk​=Q⌈Qr+1−Pk−1′​​⌉1{Pk−1′​≤r},

where Pk−1′P_{k-1}'Pk−1′​ is the end-of-period inventory position of period k−1k-1k−1 and ⌈⋅⌉\lceil\cdot\rceil⌈⋅⌉ is the ceiling. When the position has fallen to rrr or below, the policy orders the smallest number of lots of size QQQ that brings it back into {r+1,…,r+Q}\{r+1,\dots,r+Q\}{r+1,…,r+Q}. The name (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) rather than (Q,s)(Q,s)(Q,s) is conventional.

Interpretation¶

A (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) policy orders in fixed lots. Factories ship pallets and distribution centres ship batches, so a supermarket cannot order "exactly enough"; it only chooses how many lots. The reorder level decides when, the lot size fixes the unit of "how much".

The ceiling in the formula counts these lots: ⌈(r+1−P′)/Q⌉\lceil(r+1-P')/Q\rceil⌈(r+1−P′)/Q⌉ is the smallest mmm with P′+mQ≥r+1P'+mQ\ge r+1P′+mQ≥r+1, and that mmm also keeps P′+mQ≤r+QP'+mQ\le r+QP′+mQ≤r+Q. Unlike the (s,S)(s,S)(s,S) policy with s=rs=rs=r and S=r+QS=r+QS=r+Q, which returns to exactly SSS, the (Q,r)(Q,r)(Q,r) policy lands anywhere in the band. With Q=1Q=1Q=1 the band is the single level r+1r+1r+1: the base-stock policy with S=r+1S=r+1S=r+1.

The EOQ of OR Modelling, which balances setup and holding cost, is a natural first choice for QQQ (Example 4). A common slip is to think that the position sits at rrr after each order (TF 5.8.2): rrr is only the trigger. In Week 5 the position turns out to be uniformly distributed over the band, which gives exact formulas for cost and service.
