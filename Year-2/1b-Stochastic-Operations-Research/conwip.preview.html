CONWIP¶

Definition¶

CONWIP (constant work in process) controls a production line with a fixed number nnn of cards. Every job on the shop floor holds a card, and a new job may enter the line only with a free card. When a job leaves the line, its card is released and permits another job to enter. If new work is always waiting and each released card is reused immediately, the line contains exactly nnn jobs at all times: it is a closed queueing network with population nnn, in which a departing job is replaced at once by a new one at the first station.

For single-server first-come-first-served stations with exponential service times, mean value analysis gives the throughput TH1(n)\mathrm{TH}_1(n)TH1​(n) and, by Little's law, the mean cycle time

E[J(n)]=nTH1(n).\mathbb{E}[J(n)]=\frac{n}{\mathrm{TH}_1(n)}.E[J(n)]=TH1​(n)n​.

Interpretation¶

The card count is a dial for the amount of work in process. Few cards keep the line lean and jobs move through quickly, but servers often wait for work; many cards keep the bottleneck busy, but jobs queue in front of it. CONWIP makes this trade-off explicit by fixing nnn and computing what follows from it.

The closed-network model needs two conditions from the definition: work must always be waiting, otherwise fewer than nnn jobs are in the line, and cards must return immediately. The cycle time E[J(n)]\mathbb{E}[J(n)]E[J(n)] is the time on the shop floor only; time spent waiting for a card is not included.
