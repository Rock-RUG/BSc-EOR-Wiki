Interpretation¶

The state-dependent arrival rate separates two questions that the overall arrival rate mixes up: how often the system is in state nnn, and how intensely jobs arrive while it is there. A shop that customers avoid when it looks crowded has a high λ(0)\lambda(0)λ(0) and a low λ(5)\lambda(5)λ(5), even if it is rarely empty.

All arrivals, marked by the green dots, occur in state 000: one arrival per 111 minute spent there gives λ(0)=1\lambda(0)=1λ(0)=1, while no arrival ever sees state 111, so λ(1)=0\lambda(1)=0λ(1)=0. Over all time there is one arrival every 222 minutes, so λ=1/2\lambda=1/2λ=1/2.

Three rates are easily confused. The overall rate λ\lambdaλ counts arrivals per unit of total time, the fraction π(n)\pi(n)π(n) counts arrivals in state nnn per arrival, and λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) counts them per unit of time in nnn. Counting A(n,t)/tA(n,t)/tA(n,t)/t in two ways links them: λ π(n)=λ(n) p(n)\lambda\,\pi(n)=\lambda(n)\,p(n)λπ(n)=λ(n)p(n). The definition only makes sense for states that the system occupies a positive fraction of the time. Note also that λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n) is not the rate at which the system enters state nnn; that is λ(n−1)p(n−1)\lambda(n-1)p(n-1)λ(n−1)p(n−1) plus the rate of departures that leave nnn behind.

The rate generalizes the arrival rate of a Poisson process, for which λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ in every state; this is the content of PASTA. Choosing λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n), or the entry rate λ+(n)\lambda^{+}(n)λ+(n) with blocking, is how the level-crossing equation turns a verbal description of a queue into its time-average distribution.

Key Results¶

  • λ π(n)=λ(n) p(n)\lambda\,\pi(n)=\lambda(n)\,p(n)λπ(n)=λ(n)p(n) for every state nnn, whenever the limits exist (Theorem 4.5.1).
  • Since ∑nπ(n)=1\displaystyle \sum_n\pi(n)=1n∑​π(n)=1, the overall arrival rate is the time-weighted average λ=∑nλ(n) p(n)\displaystyle \lambda=\sum_n\lambda(n)\,p(n)λ=n∑​λ(n)p(n) (4.5.2).
  • If λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ, then π(n)=p(n)\pi(n)=p(n)π(n)=p(n); Poisson arrivals have constant λ(n)\lambda(n)λ(n), which is PASTA.
  • With blocking, an external Poisson stream still has λ(n)=λ\lambda(n)=\lambdaλ(n)=λ, but the entry rate λ+(n)\lambda^{+}(n)λ+(n) is zero in a full system; in an M/M/1/KM/M/1/KM/M/1/K queue, λ+(n)=λ\lambda^{+}(n)=\lambdaλ+(n)=λ for n<Kn<Kn<K and λ+(K)=0\lambda^{+}(K)=0λ+(K)=0.
  • In a finite calling population of NNN machines that each fail at rate θ\thetaθ while working, λ(n)=(N−n)θ\lambda(n)=(N-n)\thetaλ(n)=(N−n)θ when nnn machines are down; with balking the entry rate decreases in nnn.
