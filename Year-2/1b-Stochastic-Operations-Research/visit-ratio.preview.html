Visit Ratio¶

Definition¶

The visit ratio of a station is the mean number of visits a job pays to it per unit of reference. The notes use two versions.

In an open network, per job entering the network, station iii is visited on average λi/∣γ∣\lambda_i/|\boldsymbol{\gamma}|λi​/∣γ∣ times, where λi\lambda_iλi​ solves the traffic equations and ∣γ∣=∑jγj\displaystyle |\boldsymbol{\gamma}|=\sum_j\gamma_j∣γ∣=j∑​γj​ is the total external arrival rate.

In a closed network, with station 1 as reference station, ViV_iVi​ is the mean number of visits to station iii per visit to station 1, so V1=1V_1=1V1​=1. Under Markov routing with routing matrix P\boldsymbol{P}P, flow balance at each station gives

Vj=∑i=1mViPij,j=1,…,m,V1=1,V_j=\sum_{i=1}^mV_iP_{ij},\qquad j=1,\ldots,m,\qquad V_1=1,Vj​=i=1∑m​Vi​Pij​,j=1,…,m,V1​=1,

that is, V=VP\boldsymbol{V}=\boldsymbol{V}\boldsymbol{P}V=VP for the row vector V=(V1,…,Vm)\boldsymbol{V}=(V_1,\ldots,V_m)V=(V1​,…,Vm​), normalized by V1=1V_1=1V1​=1.

Interpretation¶

A visit ratio converts per-visit quantities into per-job or per-cycle quantities. A job that visits station iii on average ViV_iVi​ times per cycle spends ViE[Ji]V_i\mathbb{E}[J_i]Vi​E[Ji​] there per cycle and brings ViE[Si]V_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]Vi​E[Si​] of work to its server.

Every cycle passes station 1 once, and only one of the two branches, so V2=0.6V_2=0.6V2​=0.6 and V3=0.4V_3=0.4V3​=0.4; indeed V1=0.6⋅1+0.4⋅1V_1=0.6\cdot1+0.4\cdot1V1​=0.6⋅1+0.4⋅1 balances. Multiplying by E[S]=(1,1.5,2)\mathbb{E}[S]=(1,1.5,2)E[S]=(1,1.5,2) gives the work per cycle (1,0.9,0.8)(1,0.9,0.8)(1,0.9,0.8). Station 1, the fastest per visit, is the bottleneck, because it is visited most.

A visit ratio is a mean number of visits, not a probability: with rework or feedback it can exceed one. In a closed network every row of P\boldsymbol{P}P sums to one, so V=VP\boldsymbol{V}=\boldsymbol{V}\boldsymbol{P}V=VP has no unique solution; when every station can be reached from every other, it fixes V\boldsymbol{V}V up to a constant factor, and the choice V1=1V_1=1V1​=1 removes it. Changing the reference station rescales all ViV_iVi​, and with them the reference throughput and the cycle time, but not the station throughputs THi(n)=ViTH1(n)\mathrm{TH}_i(n)=V_i\mathrm{TH}_1(n)THi​(n)=Vi​TH1​(n). In an open network the reference is the total external rate ∣γ∣|\boldsymbol{\gamma}|∣γ∣, not one γi\gamma_iγi​.

When a job returns to the same station with probability α\alphaα after each visit and otherwise moves on, its number of visits there has the first success distribution with parameter 1−α1-\alpha1−α, hence mean 1/(1−α)1/(1-\alpha)1/(1−α). A common slip is to name the station with the longest service time the bottleneck; the right measure is ViE[Si]V_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]Vi​E[Si​].
