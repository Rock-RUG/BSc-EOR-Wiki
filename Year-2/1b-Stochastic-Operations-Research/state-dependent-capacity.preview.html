State-Dependent Capacity¶

Definition¶

A state-dependent capacity rule sets the capacity of period kkk as a function of the state observed at the end of the previous period, typically the queue length,

ck=g(Lk−1),c_k=g(L_{k-1}),ck​=g(Lk−1​),

after which the queue evolves by the discrete-time queue recursion with this ckc_kck​. In the psychiatrists case of the notes the weekly intake capacity follows rule (2.1.7) with control size e≥0e\ge0e≥0:

ck={12+e,Lk−1≥48,12,24<Lk−1<48,12−e,Lk−1≤24,c_k=\begin{cases}12+e,&L_{k-1}\ge48,\\12,&24<L_{k-1}<48,\\12-e,&L_{k-1}\le24,\end{cases}ck​=⎩⎨⎧​12+e,12,12−e,​Lk−1​≥48,24<Lk−1​<48,Lk−1​≤24,​

and Algorithm 2.2.2 then sets dk=min⁡{Lk−1+ak,ck}d_k=\min\{L_{k-1}+a_k,c_k\}dk​=min{Lk−1​+ak​,ck​} and Lk=Lk−1+ak−dkL_k=L_{k-1}+a_k-d_kLk​=Lk−1​+ak​−dk​, so jobs can be served in the week they arrive. The arrivals {ak}\{a_k\}{ak​} are not affected by the rule.

Interpretation¶

This is what a supermarket does when it opens an extra till as the line grows: capacity follows the backlog. A long queue gets more capacity and drains, a short queue gets less and refills, so the queue is held near a band instead of wandering off.
