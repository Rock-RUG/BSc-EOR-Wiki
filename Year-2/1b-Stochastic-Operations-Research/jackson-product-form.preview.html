Jackson Product Form¶

Theorem¶

Consider an open network of MMM stations in which external jobs arrive at station iii as a Poisson process with rate γi\gamma_iγi​, routing is Markov with routing matrix P\boldsymbol{P}P, and station iii has cic_ici​ servers with exponential service times of mean E[Si]\mathbb{E}[S_i]E[Si​], all independent. Let λ\boldsymbol{\lambda}λ solve the traffic equations λ=γ+λP\boldsymbol{\lambda}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}+\boldsymbol{\lambda}\boldsymbol{P}λ=γ+λP and suppose every station is stable, λiE[Si]/ci<1\lambda_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]/c_i<1λi​E[Si​]/ci​<1. Then the stationary distribution of the numbers of jobs at the stations is the product of the station distributions (Jackson's theorem):

p(n1,…,nM)=P(L1=n1,…,LM=nM)=∏i=1Mpi(ni),p(n_1,\ldots,n_M)=\mathbb{P}(L_1=n_1,\ldots,L_M=n_M)=\prod_{i=1}^Mp_i(n_i),p(n1​,…,nM​)=P(L1​=n1​,…,LM​=nM​)=i=1∏M​pi​(ni​),

where pip_ipi​ is the stationary distribution of an isolated M/M/ciM/M/c_iM/M/ci​ queue with arrival rate λi\lambda_iλi​. For single-server stations, with ρi=λiE[Si]\rho_i=\lambda_i\mathbb{E}[S_i]ρi​=λi​E[Si​], this is pi(ni)=(1−ρi)ρinip_i(n_i)=(1-\rho_i)\rho_i^{n_i}pi​(ni​)=(1−ρi​)ρini​​.

Interpretation¶

At any fixed moment, a stationary Jackson network looks as if each station were a separate M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 queue fed at its traffic-equation rate λi\lambda_iλi​, and the station contents are independent.

The figure reproduces the tandem transitions of Fig. 7.3.1. An arrival moves the state right (blue, λ\lambdaλ), a service completion at station 1 moves one job from station 1 to station 2 (red, μ1\mu_1μ1​), and one at station 2 lets a job leave (red dashed, μ2\mu_2μ2​). Three arrows leave (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) and three enter it; with p(i,j)∝ρ1iρ2jp(i,j)\propto\rho_1^i\rho_2^jp(i,j)∝ρ1i​ρ2j​ the three outflows (λ+μ1+μ2)ρ1iρ2j(\lambda+\mu_1+\mu_2)\rho_1^i\rho_2^j(λ+μ1​+μ2​)ρ1i​ρ2j​ equal the three inflows; together with the boundary states this proves the theorem for the tandem (Example 2).

The assumptions matter. Poisson arrivals and exponential service make the departures of a stationary M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 station a Poisson process again (Exercise 7.3.2), which is how the notes decouple the stations of a tandem line; with general service times the notes only give approximations for tandem lines. Every load must be below one, since otherwise a station has no stationary distribution. Independence holds at one moment, not over time: a departure from station 1 is an arrival at station 2.

The notes motivate the result by following Poisson streams through the network. In a network with feedback the stream into a station is in general no longer Poisson, yet the product form still holds; the theorem is about the stationary joint distribution, not about arrival processes. A common slip is to use the external rate γi\gamma_iγi​ instead of λi\lambda_iλi​ in ρi\rho_iρi​.

Key Results¶

  • Marginals and tails. Each station alone has the M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 law, so P(Li>n)=ρin+1\mathbb{P}(L_i>n)=\rho_i^{n+1}P(Li​>n)=ρin+1​ and E[Li]=ρi/(1−ρi)\mathbb{E}[L_i]=\rho_i/(1-\rho_i)E[Li​]=ρi​/(1−ρi​), without summing over the other stations.
  • Whole network. P(network empty)=∏i(1−ρi)\mathbb{P}(\text{network empty})=\prod_i(1-\rho_i)P(network empty)=∏i​(1−ρi​), E[L]=∑iρi/(1−ρi)\displaystyle \mathbb{E}[L]=\sum_i\rho_i/(1-\rho_i)E[L]=i∑​ρi​/(1−ρi​) and, by Little's law, E[J]=E[L]/∣γ∣\mathbb{E}[J]=\mathbb{E}[L]/|\boldsymbol{\gamma}|E[J]=E[L]/∣γ∣.
  • Departures of M/M/1M/M/1M/M/1 are Poisson (Exercises 7.3.1–7.3.2). A departure leaving the system empty is followed by an idle period and a service, with density fX+S(t)=λμμ−λ(e−λt−e−μt)f_{X+S}(t)=\frac{\lambda\mu}{\mu-\lambda}(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}-\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t})fX+S​(t)=μ−λλμ​(e−λt−e−μt); conditioning on busy or idle gives fD(t)=(1−ρ)fX+S(t)+ρμe−μt=λe−λtf_D(t)=(1-\rho)f_{X+S}(t)+\rho\mu\mathrm{e}^{-\mu t}=\lambda\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda t}fD​(t)=(1−ρ)fX+S​(t)+ρμe−μt=λe−λt.
  • Proof for the tandem (equation (7.3.2)). p(i,j)=(1−ρ1)(1−ρ2)ρ1iρ2jp(i,j)=(1-\rho_1)(1-\rho_2)\rho_1^i\rho_2^jp(i,j)=(1−ρ1​)(1−ρ2​)ρ1i​ρ2j​ satisfies the balance equations (λ+μ1+μ2)p(i,j)=λp(i−1,j)+μ1p(i+1,j−1)+μ2p(i,j+1)(\lambda+\mu_1+\mu_2)p(i,j)=\lambda p(i-1,j)+\mu_1p(i+1,j-1)+\mu_2p(i,j+1)(λ+μ1​+μ2​)p(i,j)=λp(i−1,j)+μ1​p(i+1,j−1)+μ2​p(i,j+1) for i,j≥1i,j\geq1i,j≥1; Exercises 7.3.4–7.3.6 check the boundary states (0,0)(0,0)(0,0), (i,0)(i,0)(i,0) and (0,j)(0,j)(0,j).
  • Multi-server stations. The theorem holds for M/M/cM/M/cM/M/c stations as well, with the M/M/cM/M/cM/M/c distribution as factor.
