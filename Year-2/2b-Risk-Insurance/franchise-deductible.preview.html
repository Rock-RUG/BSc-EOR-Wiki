Franchise Deductible¶

Definition¶

With a franchise deductible d≥0d\ge0d≥0, the insurer pays the entire loss once it exceeds ddd: YL=X1{X>d}Y^L=X\mathbf1_{\{X>d\}}YL=X1{X>d}​. If X≤dX\le dX≤d, payment is zero. Conditional on a payment, the paid amount has the law of X∣X>dX\mid X>dX∣X>d.

Interpretation¶

The threshold decides whether the insurer pays, but is not deducted from a qualifying loss. This creates a jump in the payment rule at the threshold.

Key Results¶

  • E[YL]=E[(X−d)+]+dP(X>d)\mathbb E[Y^L]=\mathbb E[(X-d)_+]+d\mathbb P(X>d)E[YL]=E[(X−d)+​]+dP(X>d) when finite.
  • Payment probability is the same as for an ordinary deductible, but qualifying payments are larger by ddd.
  • For d>0d>0d>0, there are no payment amounts strictly between zero and ddd.
  • Whether equality qualifies is a contract convention; here the threshold rule is strict.

Examples¶

At deductible 100, losses 80 and 150 produce franchise payments 0 and 150. The ordinary deductible would produce 0 and 50.
