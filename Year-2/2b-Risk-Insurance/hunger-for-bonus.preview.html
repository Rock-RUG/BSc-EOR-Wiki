Hunger for Bonus¶

Definition¶

Hunger for bonus is a policyholder's incentive not to report a small covered loss when reporting would increase expected future premiums by more than the current reimbursement. It is a behavioural response to an experience-rating rule.

Interpretation¶

The reported claim record can understate accident experience. The course treats this response as a form of moral hazard; it changes the observation process on which rating relies.

Key Results¶

  • A simple decision compares the current payment with the present value of extra future premiums caused by reporting.
  • The relevant future difference depends on current level, transition rules, time horizon and discounting.
  • Observed claim frequency can differ from accident frequency.
  • A reporting threshold need not equal a contractual deductible.

Examples¶

If reimbursement would be 80 but reporting increases expected discounted future premiums by 120, a policyholder minimising expected monetary cost prefers not to report. For reimbursement 200, the same comparison favours reporting.
