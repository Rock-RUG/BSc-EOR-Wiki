Expected Utility and Risk Aversion¶

Definition¶

The expected utility model compares uncertain wealth WWW through E[u(W)]\mathbb E[u(W)]E[u(W)]. An increasing concave utility function uuu represents risk aversion: for integrable wealth with well-defined utilities, E[u(W)]≤u(E[W])\mathbb E[u(W)]\le u(\mathbb E[W])E[u(W)]≤u(E[W]). A linear increasing utility represents risk neutrality.

Interpretation¶

A risk-averse policyholder can prefer a fixed payment to an uncertain loss with the same mean. Utility is applied to final wealth, so larger losses usually reduce utility.

  • Concavity gives the Jensen comparison; strict preference requires suitable strict concavity and non-degenerate wealth.
  • Fractional power utility requires positive wealth. Quadratic utility is increasing only on a restricted domain.
  • The expected value of a payoff and expected utility are different criteria.
