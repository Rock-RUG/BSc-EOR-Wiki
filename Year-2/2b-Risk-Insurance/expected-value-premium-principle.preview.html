Expected Value Premium Principle¶

Definition¶

For integrable loss X≥0X\ge0X≥0, the expected value principle sets Π(X)=(1+θ)E[X]\Pi(X)=(1+\theta)\mathbb E[X]Π(X)=(1+θ)E[X], where θ≥0\theta\ge0θ≥0 is a fixed loading coefficient. At θ=0\theta=0θ=0, it is the net premium principle, Π(X)=E[X]\Pi(X)=\mathbb E[X]Π(X)=E[X].

Interpretation¶

The loading is a constant proportion of expected loss. Different risks with the same mean receive the same price, regardless of their dispersion.

Key Results¶

  • The principle is proportional and additive for integrable losses, even without independence.
  • It has non-negative loading and is monotone under stochastic or stop-loss order.
  • For θ>0\theta>0θ>0, translation consistency fails: adding certain cost ccc adds (1+θ)c(1+\theta)c(1+θ)c to the premium.
  • Iterativity follows from total expectation only in the net case; repeating a positive loading generally applies it twice.

Examples¶

For expected loss 100 and θ=0.2\theta=0.2θ=0.2, premium is 120. Adding a certain 10 raises the premium to 132, rather than 130.
