Stop-Loss Order¶

Definition¶

For integrable non-negative losses, stop-loss order X≤slYX\le_{\mathrm{sl}}YX≤sl​Y means E[(X−d)+]≤E[(Y−d)+]\mathbb E[(X-d)_+]\le\mathbb E[(Y-d)_+]E[(X−d)+​]≤E[(Y−d)+​] for every d≥0d\ge0d≥0. The course states it for d>0d>0d>0; continuity as d↓0d\downarrow0d↓0 supplies the mean comparison at zero.

Interpretation¶

Every excess-loss layer has no larger expected payment for XXX. This order combines size and dispersion, so it does not require equal means.

Key Results¶

  • It is equivalent to increasing convex order: E[h(X)]≤E[h(Y)]\mathbb E[h(X)]\le\mathbb E[h(Y)]E[h(X)]≤E[h(Y)] for increasing convex hhh whenever the expectations are defined.
  • It implies E[X]≤E[Y]\mathbb E[X]\le\mathbb E[Y]E[X]≤E[Y] and is transitive.
  • Stochastic order and the equal-mean thicker-tailed comparison each imply stop-loss order.
  • Without equal means, stop-loss order need not imply a variance comparison.
  • Increasing convex transformations preserve the order when the transformed quantities meet the needed integrability conditions.
