Risk Orders Under Aggregation¶

Theorem¶

For independent nonnegative summands, stochastic and stop-loss orders are preserved when adding correspondingly ordered risks. For random sums, suppose i.i.d. nonnegative severities XiX_iXi​ and YiY_iYi​ are independent of their respective counts M,NM,NM,N. If severities and counts are ordered in the same stochastic or stop-loss order, the corresponding aggregate losses inherit that order, subject to required integrability.

Interpretation¶

One can compare portfolios through frequency and severity without computing their full aggregate laws. Independence from the count and nonnegative severity are essential to the basic compounding statements.

In the figure, the left column builds the aggregate loss ∑i=1MXi\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^MX_ii=1∑M​Xi​ from the severities XiX_iXi​ and the count MMM, and the right column builds ∑i=1NYi\displaystyle \sum_{i=1}^NY_ii=1∑N​Yi​ from YiY_iYi​ and NNN. The two assumed comparisons, severity against severity and count against count, are in the same order (≤st\le_{\mathrm{st}}≤st​, ≤sl\le_{\mathrm{sl}}≤sl​ or ≤e\le_e≤e​); compounding under the independence and nonnegativity assumptions then passes that order on to the aggregate losses, with finite aggregate means needed in the stop-loss case.

Stochastic order compares all increasing tests, including indicators of threshold exceedance. The compound risk distribution supplies the random-sum structure: comparing both count and severity laws under its independence assumptions leads to the aggregate comparisons below.

Examples¶

An individual policy with claim probability qqq and severity XXX is stop-loss dominated by a compound Poisson model with rate qqq and the same severity. Their means agree, but the Poisson model allows multiple claims.
