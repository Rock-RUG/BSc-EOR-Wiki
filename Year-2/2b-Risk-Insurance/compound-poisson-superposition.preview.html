Interpretation¶

Pooling portfolios (see Poisson merging) adds their claim-arrival rates. A randomly chosen pooled claim belongs to a portfolio with probability proportional to that portfolio's arrival rate.

The figure uses the Example's portfolios with illustrative arrival times; each bar's height is the claim amount. Pooling keeps every claim, so the pooled stream has rate 2+3=52+3=52+3=5. A pooled claim comes from portfolio 1 and equals 10 with probability 2/52/52/5, and from portfolio 2 and equals 20 with probability 3/53/53/5 (in the stretch drawn, 4 of the 10 claims are 10s), so the pooled severity is the mixture F=25F1+35F2F=\tfrac{2}{5}F_1+\tfrac{3}{5}F_2F=52​F1​+53​F2​. The mean total loss is 5(25⋅10+35⋅20)=805(\tfrac{2}{5}\cdot10+\tfrac{3}{5}\cdot20)=805(52​⋅10+53​⋅20)=80.

The pooled severity law is a mixture distribution: first choose the originating portfolio with probability λj/λ\lambda_j/\lambdaλj​/λ, then draw a claim from that portfolio's severity law. Those probabilities are proportional to claim rates, not to portfolio counts.

Key Results¶

  • Pooled severity weights are λj/λ\lambda_j/\lambdaλj​/λ, not equal portfolio weights unless rates are equal.
  • Independent losses have additive means and variances.
  • Multiplying the component moment generating functions (MGFs) yields exp⁡[λ(MX(t)−1)]\exp[\lambda(M_X(t)-1)]exp[λ(MX​(t)−1)] when the MGFs exist.
  • The result also holds without a finite positive MGF, by viewing the pooled claims as one Poisson arrival stream in which each arrival independently carries a portfolio label jjj (probability λj/λ\lambda_j/\lambdaλj​/λ) and a severity from FjF_jFj​; compare Poisson splitting.

Examples¶

Two portfolios have rates 2 and 3 and fixed claim amounts 10 and 20. Their pooled rate is 5 and severities are 10 with probability 2/52/52/5 and 20 with probability 3/53/53/5. Mean total loss is 80.
