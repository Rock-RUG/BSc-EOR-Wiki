Exponential Family¶

Definition¶

An exponential family is a family of probability laws whose mass function or density, relative to a fixed reference measure (counting for a probability mass function, so probabilities are sums; length for a probability density function, so probabilities are integrals), can be written as

f(y;θ)=h(y)exp⁡ ⁣{θ⊤T(y)−A(θ)}.f(y;\boldsymbol{\theta})=h(y)\exp\!\left\{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{\top}\boldsymbol{T}(y)-A(\boldsymbol{\theta})\right\}.f(y;θ)=h(y)exp{θ⊤T(y)−A(θ)}.

Here θ\boldsymbol{\theta}θ is a vector of natural parameters, T(y)\boldsymbol{T}(y)T(y) a vector of statistics, (⋅)⊤(\cdot)^{\top}(⋅)⊤ the transpose (written (⋅)⊤(\cdot)^{\top}(⋅)⊤ rather than the (⋅)⊤(\cdot)^{\top}(⋅)⊤ used on the other pages, to avoid a clash with the statistic T\boldsymbol{T}T), h(y)h(y)h(y) a nonnegative base factor, and AAA the log-normalizing function. The parameter domain contains values for which the normalizing sum or integral is finite. The support is fixed across the family in the regular setting used below.

For the one-parameter response families used in generalized linear models, the usual form is

f(y;θ,ϕ)=exp⁡ ⁣{yθ−b(θ)a(ϕ)+c(y,ϕ)}.f(y;\theta,\phi)=\exp\!\left\{\frac{y\theta-b(\theta)}{a(\phi)}+c(y,\phi)\right\}.f(y;θ,ϕ)=exp{a(ϕ)yθ−b(θ)​+c(y,ϕ)}.

Here θ\thetaθ is the scalar natural parameter, ϕ\phiϕ a dispersion parameter, a(ϕ)>0a(\phi)>0a(ϕ)>0 a scale factor, bbb the normalizing function, and ccc the remaining term. This GLM form is a specialized representation; it does not describe every exponential family with an arbitrary statistic T(y)\boldsymbol{T}(y)T(y).

Interpretation¶

The mass function sums to one for a discrete response, whereas the density integrates to one for a continuous response. Their logarithms have the same parameter structure, allowing a common modelling framework for claim counts and continuous responses. An exponential family is a collection of distributions, not just the exponential waiting-time distribution.

Key Results¶

When the support is parameter-independent and differentiation can pass through the normalizing sum or integral, the GLM form gives

μ=E[Y]=b′(θ),Var⁡(Y)=a(ϕ)b′′(θ).\mu=\mathbb{E}[Y]=b'(\theta),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(Y)=a(\phi)b''(\theta).μ=E[Y]=b′(θ),Var(Y)=a(ϕ)b′′(θ).

For example, differentiating log⁡f\log flogf with respect to θ\thetaθ gives (y−b′(θ))/a(ϕ)(y-b'(\theta))/a(\phi)(y−b′(θ))/a(ϕ). The derivative of the normalization condition has expectation zero, yielding the mean identity; differentiating again yields the variance identity. These calculations require the stated regularity, rather than just a visual resemblance to the formula.

The canonical link of a GLM is the link that makes the linear predictor the natural parameter, g(μ)=θg(\mu)=\thetag(μ)=θ, that is g=(b′)−1g=(b')^{-1}g=(b′)−1 for the chosen representation. The response family and the link are separate choices; a GLM need not use its canonical link.
