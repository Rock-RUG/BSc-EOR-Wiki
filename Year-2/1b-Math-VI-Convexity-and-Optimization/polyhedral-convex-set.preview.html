Polyhedral Convex Set¶

Definition¶

Let A∈Rm×n\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}A∈Rm×n and b∈Rm\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^mb∈Rm. The set

V={x∈Rn ⁣:Ax≤b}V=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n\colon \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}\}V={x∈Rn:Ax≤b}

is called a polyhedral convex set, or polyhedral set; the inequality is componentwise. Writing ai⊤\boldsymbol{a}_i^{\top}ai⊤​ for the iii-th row of the matrix A\boldsymbol{A}A,

V=⋂i=1m{x∈Rn ⁣:ai⊤x≤bi},V=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n\colon \boldsymbol{a}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq b_i\},V=i=1⋂m​{x∈Rn:ai⊤​x≤bi​},

an intersection of finitely many sets, each of which is a closed halfspace if ai≠0\boldsymbol{a}_i\neq\boldsymbol{0}ai​=0, and Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn (if bi≥0b_i\geq0bi​≥0) or ∅\varnothing∅ (if bi<0b_i<0bi​<0) if ai=0\boldsymbol{a}_i=\boldsymbol{0}ai​=0. As an intersection of closed convex sets, VVV is always closed and convex.

Interpretation¶

A polyhedral set is the solution set of finitely many weak linear inequalities: start with Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn and cut it with finitely many flat cuts. The feasible region of a linear program is the typical example.

The figure draws Example 1. The shaded region VVV is what remains after each of the three rows cuts away the side its normal vector ai\boldsymbol{a}_iai​ points to (the arrows show directions only, not lengths); it is unbounded downwards. The boundary lines meet pairwise in three points: (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) and (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) are corners of VVV, while (1.2,1.4)(1.2,1.4)(1.2,1.4), where the first two lines cross, is cut off by x2≤1x_2\leq1x2​≤1.

Each row of Ax≤b\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}Ax≤b is one constraint, with normal vector ai\boldsymbol{a}_iai​ pointing out of its halfspace. Other weak linear constraints fit the same form: a ≥\geq≥ row becomes ≤\leq≤ after multiplying by −1-1−1, an equation a⊤x=b\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}=ba⊤x=b is the pair a⊤x≤b\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq ba⊤x≤b and −a⊤x≤−b-\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq-b−a⊤x≤−b, and xj≥0x_j\geq0xj​≥0 is the row −xj≤0-x_j\leq0−xj​≤0. Strict inequalities are not allowed, since they would destroy closedness.

Closedness and convexity come from the intersection: each piece is closed and convex, intersections of closed sets are closed, and intersections of convex sets are convex. The word "finitely" matters. The closed unit disc is the intersection of the infinitely many halfspaces {x ⁣:u⊤x≤1}\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{u}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq1\}{x:u⊤x≤1} with ∥u∥=1\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\rVert=1∥u∥=1, so it is closed and convex, but it is not polyhedral: finitely many cuts leave a boundary made of finitely many flat pieces, never a round one.

In OR Modelling two nonparallel boundary lines in the plane meet in a corner point, and not every such corner is feasible (Example 1 below). A common misconception is that a polyhedral set is a bounded polygon or polytope; it may be unbounded, empty or even all of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn. Polyhedral sets return throughout the course, above all as the feasible regions of linear programs.

Key Results¶

  • For every A∈Rm×n\boldsymbol{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}A∈Rm×n and b∈Rm\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^mb∈Rm, the set {x∈Rn ⁣:Ax≤b}\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n\colon \boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}\}{x∈Rn:Ax≤b} is closed and convex.
  • {x ⁣:Ax≤b}\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}\}{x:Ax≤b} is the intersection of the mmm sets {x ⁣:ai⊤x≤bi}\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{a}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq b_i\}{x:ai⊤​x≤bi​}; a zero row gives Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn or ∅\varnothing∅.
  • Every closed halfspace and every hyperplane is polyhedral, and so is the solution set of any finite system of weak linear inequalities and linear equations.
  • The feasible region {x ⁣:Ax≤b, x≥0}\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b},\ \boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{0}\}{x:Ax≤b, x≥0} of a linear program is polyhedral, with matrix (A−In)\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{A}\\-\boldsymbol{I}_{n}\end{pmatrix}(A−In​​) and right-hand side (b0)\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{b}\\\boldsymbol{0}\end{pmatrix}(b0​).
  • A polyhedral set may be empty, unbounded or all of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn (Example 1 below is unbounded).
