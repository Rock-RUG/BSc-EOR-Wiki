Weak Duality¶

Theorem¶

Theorem (weak duality). Let (P) be the linear program max⁡{c⊤x ⁣:Ax≤b, x≥0}\max\{\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\colon\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b},\ \boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{0}\}max{c⊤x:Ax≤b, x≥0} and (D) its dual min⁡{b⊤y ⁣:A⊤y≥c, y≥0}\min\{\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\colon\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{c},\ \boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{0}\}min{b⊤y:A⊤y≥c, y≥0}. If x\boldsymbol{x}x is a feasible solution of (P) and y\boldsymbol{y}y is a feasible solution of (D), then

c⊤x≤b⊤y.\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}.c⊤x≤b⊤y.

Proof. Since A⊤y≥c\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{c}A⊤y≥c and x≥0\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{0}x≥0, c⊤x≤x⊤A⊤y\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}c⊤x≤x⊤A⊤y. Since x⊤A⊤=(Ax)⊤≤b⊤\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}=(\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x})^{\top}\leq\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}x⊤A⊤=(Ax)⊤≤b⊤ and y≥0\boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{0}y≥0, x⊤A⊤y≤b⊤y\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\leq\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}x⊤A⊤y≤b⊤y. Hence c⊤x≤x⊤A⊤y≤b⊤y\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\leq\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}c⊤x≤x⊤A⊤y≤b⊤y.

Interpretation¶

Every feasible solution of the dual is a ceiling for the primal, and every feasible solution of the primal is a floor for the dual. The primal values all lie below the dual values, so the optimal values, when they exist, satisfy Z∗≤W∗Z^{\ast}\leq W^{\ast}Z∗≤W∗.

The proof uses all four parts of feasibility, each exactly once: A⊤y≥c\boldsymbol{A}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{c}A⊤y≥c together with x≥0\boldsymbol{x}\geq\boldsymbol{0}x≥0, and Ax≤b\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}Ax≤b together with y≥0\boldsymbol{y}\geq\boldsymbol{0}y≥0. A componentwise inequality may only be multiplied by a nonnegative vector, so dropping a sign condition breaks the chain (Example 4 below). For a linear program in another form the dual changes accordingly (see the transformation scheme), and the inequality holds for that pair.

Weak duality gives a cheap test of optimality. If a feasible x∗\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}x∗ and a feasible y∗\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}y∗ have c⊤x∗=b⊤y∗\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}=\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}c⊤x∗=b⊤y∗, then no primal value can exceed b⊤y∗\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}b⊤y∗, so x∗\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}x∗ is optimal, and likewise y∗\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}y∗. It also explains the link between unboundedness and infeasibility: an unbounded primal has no upper bound, while every dual feasible y\boldsymbol{y}y would give one, so the dual has no feasible solution.

Economically, read bib_ibi​ as a company's capacity of resource iii, cjc_jcj​ as the profit per unit of product jjj, and yiy_iyi​ as the price per unit of resource iii offered by a buyer. The company only has an incentive to sell its resources if the buyer's offer b⊤y\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}b⊤y is at least the profit c⊤x\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}c⊤x of production. A common misreading is that weak duality makes the two optimal values equal; it gives only an inequality, and the equality is the content of strong duality.

Key Results¶

  • c⊤x≤b⊤y\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\leq\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}c⊤x≤b⊤y for every primal feasible x\boldsymbol{x}x and dual feasible y\boldsymbol{y}y.
  • Any feasible x\boldsymbol{x}x gives a lower bound on the optimal value of (D), and any feasible y\boldsymbol{y}y an upper bound on the optimal value of (P).
  • If (P) is unbounded, then (D) is infeasible; if (D) is unbounded, then (P) is infeasible.
  • If a primal feasible x∗\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}x∗ and a dual feasible y∗\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}y∗ satisfy c⊤x∗=b⊤y∗\boldsymbol{c}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}=\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}c⊤x∗=b⊤y∗, then x∗\boldsymbol{x}^{\ast}x∗ is optimal for (P) and y∗\boldsymbol{y}^{\ast}y∗ is optimal for (D).
  • Weak duality, together with the fact that every linear program is infeasible, unbounded or has an optimal solution, gives: if (P) has an optimal solution, then (D) has an optimal solution or is infeasible, and vice versa; strong duality rules out the second option.
