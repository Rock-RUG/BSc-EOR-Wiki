Row Space¶

Definition¶

Let AAA be a real m×nm\times nm×n matrix with rows r1⊤,…,rm⊤\boldsymbol{r}_1^{\top},\ldots,\boldsymbol{r}_m^{\top}r1⊤​,…,rm⊤​, so that each ri∈Rn\boldsymbol{r}_i\in\mathbb{R}^nri​∈Rn. The row space of AAA is the span of its rows,

Row⁡(A)=span⁡{r1,…,rm}={A⊤y ⁣:y∈Rm}⊆Rn.\operatorname{Row}(A)=\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{r}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{r}_m\} =\{A^{\top}\boldsymbol{y}\colon\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^m\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n.Row(A)=span{r1​,…,rm​}={A⊤y:y∈Rm}⊆Rn.

It is the column space (range) of the transpose A⊤A^{\top}A⊤: Row⁡(A)=Col⁡(A⊤)\operatorname{Row}(A)=\operatorname{Col}(A^{\top})Row(A)=Col(A⊤). Math V writes Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A), Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A) and Null⁡(A)\operatorname{Null}(A)Null(A) for the column space, row space and null space.

Interpretation¶

Each row of AAA is the coefficient vector of one equation of Ax=bA\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{b}Ax=b. The row space therefore contains the coefficient vectors of all equations that follow from the system by taking linear combinations of its equations.

The figure uses the example below. Row operations replace rows by combinations of rows, so AAA and RRR have the same row space, and the two nonzero rows of RRR (blue) are a basis of Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A); row 3 of AAA, which is row 1 plus row 2, becomes a zero row. The pivots of RRR (circled) sit in columns 1 and 3, and it is these columns of the original AAA (red), not of RRR, that form a basis of the column space.

The row space lives in the domain Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, alongside the null space; the column space lives in the codomain Rm\mathbb{R}^mRm. Only the component of x\boldsymbol{x}x in the row space affects AxA\boldsymbol{x}Ax.

Row operations and column bases behave differently. Elementary row operations replace rows by combinations of rows, so they keep the row space but usually change the column space. Hence a basis of the row space is read off from the nonzero rows of the echelon form, while a basis of the column space consists of the pivot columns of the original matrix.

Key Results¶

  • Row-equivalent matrices have the same row space.
  • The nonzero rows of any row echelon form of AAA, in particular of its reduced row echelon form, form a basis of the row space.
  • dim⁡Row⁡(A)=rank⁡A\dim\operatorname{Row}(A)=\operatorname{rank}AdimRow(A)=rankA: row rank equals column rank.
  • Row⁡(A)=Null⁡(A)⊥\operatorname{Row}(A)=\operatorname{Null}(A)^\perpRow(A)=Null(A)⊥, the orthogonal complement of the null space (kernel), so dim⁡Row⁡(A)+dim⁡Null⁡(A)=n\dim\operatorname{Row}(A)+\dim\operatorname{Null}(A)=ndimRow(A)+dimNull(A)=n (Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra).
  • AAA restricted to Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A) is a bijection onto the column space Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A).
