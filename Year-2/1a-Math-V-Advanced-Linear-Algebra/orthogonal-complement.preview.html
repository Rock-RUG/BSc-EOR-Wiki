Orthogonal Complement¶

Definition¶

Let VVV be a real vector space with an inner product, written x⋅y\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}x⋅y (in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn: the dot product), and let W⊆VW\subseteq VW⊆V. The orthogonal complement of WWW is

W⊥={v∈V ⁣:v⋅w=0for all w∈W},W^\perp=\{\boldsymbol{v}\in V\colon{}\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=0\quad \text{for all }\boldsymbol{w}\in W\},W⊥={v∈V:v⋅w=0for all w∈W},

the set of vectors orthogonal to every vector of WWW. It is read "WWW perp". Usually WWW is a subspace.

Interpretation¶

W⊥W^\perpW⊥ collects all directions perpendicular to WWW. In R3\mathbb{R}^3R3 the complement of a plane through the origin is its normal line, and the complement of a line through the origin is the plane perpendicular to it.

The figure draws the first example below: W=span⁡{(1,1,1)⊤}W=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^{\top}\}W=span{(1,1,1)⊤} is a line, and W⊥W^\perpW⊥ is the plane x1+x2+x3=0x_1+x_2+x_3=0x1​+x2​+x3​=0 with basis (−1,1,0)⊤,(−1,0,1)⊤(-1,1,0)^{\top},(-1,0,1)^{\top}(−1,1,0)⊤,(−1,0,1)⊤. Every vector of the plane is orthogonal to (1,1,1)⊤(1,1,1)^{\top}(1,1,1)⊤, the two meet only in 0\boldsymbol{0}0, and the dimensions add up to 1+2=31+2=31+2=3.

"Complement" does not mean set complement: V∖WV\setminus WV∖W is never a subspace, as it misses 0\boldsymbol{0}0. Nor is W⊥W^\perpW⊥ just some subspace UUU with V=W⊕UV=W\oplus UV=W⊕U: such complements are many, but the orthogonal one is unique. It depends on the inner product; with a different inner product on Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn the orthogonal complement can change.

To compute it, it suffices to test a spanning set: if W=span⁡{w1,…,wk}W=\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{w}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{w}_k\}W=span{w1​,…,wk​}, then v∈W⊥\boldsymbol{v}\in W^\perpv∈W⊥ if and only if v⋅wi=0\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}_i=0v⋅wi​=0 for i=1,…,ki=1,\ldots,ki=1,…,k. In Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn this is the homogeneous system M⊤v=0M^{\top}\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{0}M⊤v=0 with M=(w1⋯wk)M=\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{w}_1&\cdots&\boldsymbol{w}_k\end{pmatrix}M=(w1​​⋯​wk​​), so W⊥W^\perpW⊥ is the null space (kernel) of M⊤M^{\top}M⊤.

Key Results¶

  • W⊥W^\perpW⊥ is a subspace for every subset WWW, and W⊥=(span⁡W)⊥W^\perp=(\operatorname{span}W)^\perpW⊥=(spanW)⊥.
  • W∩W⊥={0}W\cap W^\perp=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}W∩W⊥={0} for a subspace WWW, since w⋅w=0\boldsymbol{w}\cdot\boldsymbol{w}=0w⋅w=0 forces w=0\boldsymbol{w}=\boldsymbol{0}w=0.
  • If VVV is finite-dimensional and WWW a subspace: V=W⊕W⊥V=W\oplus W^\perpV=W⊕W⊥ (a direct sum), dim⁡W+dim⁡W⊥=dim⁡V\dim W+\dim W^\perp=\dim VdimW+dimW⊥=dimV, and (W⊥)⊥=W(W^\perp)^\perp=W(W⊥)⊥=W.
  • Inclusions reverse: W1⊆W2W_1\subseteq W_2W1​⊆W2​ implies W2⊥⊆W1⊥W_2^\perp\subseteq W_1^\perpW2⊥​⊆W1⊥​. Also (W1+W2)⊥=W1⊥∩W2⊥(W_1+W_2)^\perp=W_1^\perp\cap W_2^\perp(W1​+W2​)⊥=W1⊥​∩W2⊥​.
  • {0}⊥=V\{\boldsymbol{0}\}^\perp=V{0}⊥=V and V⊥={0}V^\perp=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}V⊥={0}.
  • For a real matrix AAA, Null⁡(A)=Row⁡(A)⊥\operatorname{Null}(A)=\operatorname{Row}(A)^\perpNull(A)=Row(A)⊥ and Null⁡(A⊤)=Col⁡(A)⊥\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top})=\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perpNull(A⊤)=Col(A)⊥ (Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra).

Examples¶

A line in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3. For W=span⁡{(1,1,1)⊤}W=\operatorname{span}\{(1,1,1)^{\top}\}W=span{(1,1,1)⊤}, W⊥={x ⁣:x1+x2+x3=0}W^\perp=\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon x_1+x_2+x_3=0\}W⊥={x:x1​+x2​+x3​=0}, with basis (−1,1,0)⊤,(−1,0,1)⊤(-1,1,0)^{\top},(-1,0,1)^{\top}(−1,1,0)⊤,(−1,0,1)⊤. Dimensions: 1+2=31+2=31+2=3.

A plane in R3\mathbb{R}^3R3. For W={x ⁣:x1−2x2+x3=0}W=\{\boldsymbol{x}\colon x_1-2x_2+x_3=0\}W={x:x1​−2x2​+x3​=0}, W⊥=span⁡{(1,−2,1)⊤}W^\perp=\operatorname{span}\{(1,-2,1)^{\top}\}W⊥=span{(1,−2,1)⊤}: the coefficient vector is the normal vector of the plane.

W⊥=span⁡{(10−10),(010−1)},W^\perp=\operatorname{span}\left\{\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\-1\\0\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\\-1\end{pmatrix}\right\},W⊥=span⎩⎨⎧​​10−10​​,​010−1​​⎭⎬⎫​,

and 2+2=42+2=42+2=4. Note that (1,0,0,0)⊤(1,0,0,0)^{\top}(1,0,0,0)⊤ is not in WWW, yet it is not in W⊥W^\perpW⊥ either: most vectors lie in neither.
