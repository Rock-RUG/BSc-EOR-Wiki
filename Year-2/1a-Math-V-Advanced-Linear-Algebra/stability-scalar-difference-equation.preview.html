The definition quantifies over all initial values. An unstable equation can still have particular solutions that converge, namely those whose coefficients on the large roots happen to be zero.

In Math V, asymptotic stability (all solutions converge) requires all roots strictly inside the unit circle. The equation is called stable when all solutions stay bounded: all roots satisfy ∣r∣≤1\lvert r\rvert\le1∣r∣≤1 and those with ∣r∣=1\lvert r\rvert=1∣r∣=1 are simple. Such roots give solutions that neither grow nor die out, so the equation is stable but not asymptotically stable. Compare the continuous-time criterion for the stability of an equilibrium of an ordinary differential equation, where decay requires f′(x∗)<0f'(x^*)<0f′(x∗)<0 (negative real parts for linear systems) rather than a modulus below 111.
