Integrating Factor¶

Method¶

Consider the linear first-order differential equation

x′(t)+p(t) x(t)=q(t),t∈I,x'(t)+p(t)\,x(t)=q(t),\qquad t\in I,x′(t)+p(t)x(t)=q(t),t∈I,

with p,qp,qp,q continuous on an interval III and x′(t)=dxdt(t)x'(t)=\dfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{\mathrm{d}t}(t)x′(t)=dtdx​(t).

  1. Choose an antiderivative PPP of ppp and set μ(t)=eP(t)\mu(t)=\mathrm{e}^{P(t)}μ(t)=eP(t), the integrating factor.
  2. Multiply the equation by μ(t)\mu(t)μ(t). Since μ′(t)=p(t)μ(t)\mu'(t)=p(t)\mu(t)μ′(t)=p(t)μ(t), the left-hand side becomes an exact derivative: μx′+μpx=(μx)′\mu x'+\mu p x=(\mu x)'μx′+μpx=(μx)′.
  3. Integrate (μx)′=μq(\mu x)'=\mu q(μx)′=μq:  μ(t)x(t)=∫μ(t)q(t) dt+C\ \mu(t)x(t)=\int\mu(t)q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+C μ(t)x(t)=∫μ(t)q(t)dt+C.
  4. Divide by μ(t)>0\mu(t)>0μ(t)>0:  x(t)=e−P(t)(∫eP(t)q(t) dt+C)\ x(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-P(t)}\Big(\int \mathrm{e}^{P(t)}q(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+C\Big) x(t)=e−P(t)(∫eP(t)q(t)dt+C), and fix CCC from an initial condition.

Interpretation¶

The left-hand side x′+pxx'+pxx′+px is not a derivative of anything simple; multiplying by μ\muμ turns it into the derivative of the product μx\mu xμx. Step 2 uses the product rule from differentiation rules and the chain rule for ddteP(t)=P′(t)eP(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{e}^{P(t)}=P'(t)\mathrm{e}^{P(t)}dtd​eP(t)=P′(t)eP(t).

The two terms of the closed form have a meaning: e−P(t)x0\mathrm{e}^{-P(t)}x_0e−P(t)x0​ solves the homogeneous equation x′+px=0x'+px=0x′+px=0, and the integral is a particular solution that starts at 000, so the solution is homogeneous part plus particular part, as in the general solution of a linear ODE. For constant p=−ap=-ap=−a the formula reads x(t)=ea(t−t0)x0+∫t0tea(t−s)q(s) dsx(t)=\mathrm{e}^{a(t-t_0)}x_0+\int_{t_0}^t \mathrm{e}^{a(t-s)}q(s)\,\mathrm{d}sx(t)=ea(t−t0​)x0​+∫t0​t​ea(t−s)q(s)ds, the scalar form of the variation-of-constants formula that returns in Week 4 with the matrix exponential.

Common slips: forgetting to write the equation with coefficient 111 in front of x′x'x′ before reading off ppp; using e−P\mathrm{e}^{-P}e−P instead of eP\mathrm{e}^{P}eP; adding the constant CCC after dividing by μ\muμ instead of before.

Key Results¶

  • μ(t)=eP(t)\mu(t)=\mathrm{e}^{P(t)}μ(t)=eP(t) is never zero, so multiplying by it neither loses nor creates solutions.
  • Any antiderivative of ppp can be used: replacing PPP by P+kP+kP+k multiplies μ\muμ by ek\mathrm{e}^{k}ek, which cancels.
  • If p,qp,qp,q are continuous on III, the method yields the unique solution of each initial value problem on all of III.
  • For constant p≠0p\neq0p=0 and constant qqq the solution tends to q/pq/pq/p when p>0p>0p>0; this limit is the equilibrium.
