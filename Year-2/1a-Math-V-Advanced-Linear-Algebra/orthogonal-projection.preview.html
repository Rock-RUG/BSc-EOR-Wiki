Orthogonal Projection¶

Definition¶

Let WWW be a subspace of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn (or of a finite-dimensional inner product space VVV). Every b\boldsymbol{b}b can be written uniquely as

b=p+e,p∈W,e∈W⊥,\boldsymbol{b}=\boldsymbol{p}+\boldsymbol{e},\qquad \boldsymbol{p}\in W,\quad \boldsymbol{e}\in W^\perp,b=p+e,p∈W,e∈W⊥,

where W⊥W^\perpW⊥ is the orthogonal complement of WWW. The vector p=proj⁡Wb\boldsymbol{p}=\operatorname{proj}_W\boldsymbol{b}p=projW​b is the orthogonal projection of b\boldsymbol{b}b onto WWW, and e=b−p\boldsymbol{e}=\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{p}e=b−p is the error. The map b↦proj⁡Wb\boldsymbol{b}\mapsto\operatorname{proj}_W\boldsymbol{b}b↦projW​b is a linear map; its matrix PPP is the orthogonal projection matrix onto WWW.

Interpretation¶

Drop a perpendicular from b\boldsymbol{b}b to WWW: its foot is p\boldsymbol{p}p. The error is orthogonal to every vector of WWW, not just to p\boldsymbol{p}p; checking e⊥p\boldsymbol{e}\perp\boldsymbol{p}e⊥p alone is not enough.

In the figure, e\boldsymbol{e}e stands perpendicular to the whole plane WWW, not only to p\boldsymbol{p}p. For any other w∈W\boldsymbol{w}\in Ww∈W the points b\boldsymbol{b}b, p\boldsymbol{p}p, w\boldsymbol{w}w form a right triangle with the right angle at p\boldsymbol{p}p, so the hypotenuse ∥b−w∥\|\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{w}\|∥b−w∥ is longer than ∥e∥\|\boldsymbol{e}\|∥e∥. Pythagoras makes this exact: ∥b−w∥2=∥e∥2+∥p−w∥2\|\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{w}\|^2=\|\boldsymbol{e}\|^2+\|\boldsymbol{p}-\boldsymbol{w}\|^2∥b−w∥2=∥e∥2+∥p−w∥2. So orthogonality is what makes p\boldsymbol{p}p the closest point of WWW to b\boldsymbol{b}b in the Euclidean norm; this best-approximation property is the basis of least squares.

Key Results¶

  • Best approximation: ∥b−proj⁡Wb∥<∥b−w∥\|\boldsymbol{b}-\operatorname{proj}_W\boldsymbol{b}\|<\|\boldsymbol{b}-\boldsymbol{w}\|∥b−projW​b∥<∥b−w∥ for every w∈W\boldsymbol{w}\in Ww∈W with w≠proj⁡Wb\boldsymbol{w}\neq\operatorname{proj}_W\boldsymbol{b}w=projW​b.
  • Onto a line spanned by a≠0\boldsymbol{a}\neq\boldsymbol{0}a=0: p=a⊤ba⊤a a\boldsymbol{p}=\dfrac{\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}}{\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{a}}\,\boldsymbol{a}p=a⊤aa⊤b​a and P=aa⊤a⊤aP=\dfrac{\boldsymbol{a}\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}}{\boldsymbol{a}^{\top}\boldsymbol{a}}P=a⊤aaa⊤​.
  • With an orthonormal basis q1,…,qk\boldsymbol{q}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{q}_kq1​,…,qk​ of WWW as columns of QQQ: p=∑i(qi⊤b)qi\displaystyle \boldsymbol{p}=\sum_i(\boldsymbol{q}_i^{\top}\boldsymbol{b})\boldsymbol{q}_ip=i∑​(qi⊤​b)qi​ and P=QQ⊤P=QQ^{\top}P=QQ⊤.
  • If W=Col⁡(A)W=\operatorname{Col}(A)W=Col(A), the column space of an n×kn\times kn×k matrix AAA of full column rank: P=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤P=A(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}P=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤.
  • A square matrix PPP is an orthogonal projection matrix if and only if P2=PP^2=PP2=P and P⊤=PP^{\top}=PP⊤=P; it then projects onto its column space.
  • Every orthogonal projection matrix is idempotent, but oblique projections are idempotent without being symmetric, so not every idempotent matrix is an orthogonal projection.
  • I−PI-PI−P is the orthogonal projection onto W⊥W^\perpW⊥, and ∥Pb∥≤∥b∥\|P\boldsymbol{b}\|\le\|\boldsymbol{b}\|∥Pb∥≤∥b∥.
  • The eigenvalues of PPP lie in {0,1}\{0,1\}{0,1}, and rank⁡P=tr⁡P=dim⁡W\operatorname{rank}P=\operatorname{tr}P=\dim WrankP=trP=dimW (trace).
