Least Squares¶

Method¶

Let AAA be a real m×nm\times nm×n matrix and b∈Rm\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^mb∈Rm. A least-squares solution of Ax=bA\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{b}Ax=b is a vector x^∈Rn\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^nx^∈Rn such that, for all x∈Rn\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^nx∈Rn,

∥b−Ax^∥≤∥b−Ax∥,\|\boldsymbol{b}-A\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\|\le\|\boldsymbol{b}-A\boldsymbol{x}\|,∥b−Ax^∥≤∥b−Ax∥,

where ∥⋅∥\|\cdot\|∥⋅∥ is the Euclidean norm. To find it, solve the normal equations

A⊤Ax^=A⊤b.A^{\top}A\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}=A^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}.A⊤Ax^=A⊤b.

The vector b−Ax^\boldsymbol{b}-A\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}b−Ax^ is the residual.

Interpretation¶

When b\boldsymbol{b}b is not in the column space Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) (the range of AAA), the system has no solution, typically because there are more equations than unknowns. Least squares replaces b\boldsymbol{b}b by the closest attainable vector, the orthogonal projection p\boldsymbol{p}p of b\boldsymbol{b}b onto Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A), and solves Ax^=pA\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}=\boldsymbol{p}Ax^=p.

The normal equations say that the residual is orthogonal to every column, since the rows of the transpose are the columns of AAA: A⊤(b−Ax^)=0A^{\top}(\boldsymbol{b}-A\hat{\boldsymbol{x}})=\boldsymbol{0}A⊤(b−Ax^)=0, that is, the residual lies in Null⁡(A⊤)=Col⁡(A)⊥\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top})=\operatorname{Col}(A)^\perpNull(A⊤)=Col(A)⊥ given by the Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra. The same equations follow from setting the gradient of ∥b−Ax∥2\|\boldsymbol{b}-A\boldsymbol{x}\|^2∥b−Ax∥2, namely 2A⊤Ax−2A⊤b2A^{\top}A\boldsymbol{x}-2A^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}2A⊤Ax−2A⊤b, to zero.

Misunderstandings to avoid: the fitted vector Ax^A\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}Ax^ is always unique, but x^\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}x^ need not be; and the formula (A⊤A)−1A⊤b(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}(A⊤A)−1A⊤b is valid only when the columns of AAA are linearly independent.

Key Results¶

  • The normal equations are always consistent, and their solution set equals the set of least-squares solutions.
  • Since Null⁡(A⊤A)=Null⁡(A)\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top}A)=\operatorname{Null}(A)Null(A⊤A)=Null(A), the least-squares solutions form the affine subspace {x^}+Null⁡(A)\{\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}\}+\operatorname{Null}(A){x^}+Null(A).
  • The solution is unique if and only if AAA has full column rank nnn; then x^=(A⊤A)−1A⊤b\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}=(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}x^=(A⊤A)−1A⊤b and p=Hb\boldsymbol{p}=H\boldsymbol{b}p=Hb with the symmetric idempotent matrix H=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤H=A(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}H=A(A⊤A)−1A⊤.
  • In general, x^+=A+b\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}^+=A^+\boldsymbol{b}x^+=A+b, with A+A^+A+ the Moore-Penrose inverse, is the unique least-squares solution of minimum norm; it is the one lying in the row space Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A).
