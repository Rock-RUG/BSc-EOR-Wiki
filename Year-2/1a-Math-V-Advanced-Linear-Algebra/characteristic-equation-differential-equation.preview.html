Interpretation¶

The substitution turns a differential equation into an algebraic one: each root of the polynomial ppp gives an exponential "mode" ert\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert of the exponential function. Writing the equation as a first-order system with a companion matrix, ppp is exactly the characteristic polynomial of that matrix, so the roots are its eigenvalues.

The figure follows the complex-roots example x′′+2x′+5x=0x''+2x'+5x=0x′′+2x′+5x=0. Its characteristic equation r2+2r+5=0r^2+2r+5=0r2+2r+5=0 has the roots r=−1±2ir=-1\pm2\mathrm{i}r=−1±2i, marked on the left in the complex plane. Through ert\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert and Euler's formula they give the real mode e−tcos⁡2t\mathrm{e}^{-t}\cos2te−tcos2t on the right: the real part −1-1−1 fixes the shrinking envelope ±e−t\pm \mathrm{e}^{-t}±e−t, and the imaginary part 222 fixes how fast the solution oscillates inside it.

The discrete-time analogue is the characteristic polynomial for a difference equation, where the trial solution is λt\lambda^tλt instead of ert\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert. The two are linked by λ=er\lambda=\mathrm{e}^{r}λ=er: sampling ert\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert at integer times gives (er)t(\mathrm{e}^{r})^t(er)t.

Common errors: forgetting that a repeated root needs the extra factor ttt; writing e(α±iβ)t\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha\pm\mathrm{i}\beta)t}e(α±iβ)t as the final real answer instead of converting it with Euler's formula; and applying the method to equations with non-constant coefficients, where ert\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert is generally not a solution.

  • By the fundamental theorem of algebra, ppp has nnn complex roots counted with multiplicity.
  • A real root rrr of multiplicity mmm gives the mmm solutions ert, tert, …, tm−1ert\mathrm{e}^{rt},\,t\mathrm{e}^{rt},\,\dots,\,t^{m-1}\mathrm{e}^{rt}ert,tert,…,tm−1ert.
  • Since the coefficients are real, nonreal roots come in pairs α±iβ\alpha\pm\mathrm{i}\betaα±iβ (β≠0\beta\neq0β=0) by the complex conjugate root theorem. Each pair gives the real solutions eαtcos⁡βt\mathrm{e}^{\alpha t}\cos\beta teαtcosβt and eαtsin⁡βt\mathrm{e}^{\alpha t}\sin\beta teαtsinβt, because e(α+iβ)t=eαt(cos⁡βt+isin⁡βt)\mathrm{e}^{(\alpha+\mathrm{i}\beta)t}=\mathrm{e}^{\alpha t}(\cos\beta t+\mathrm{i}\sin\beta t)e(α+iβ)t=eαt(cosβt+isinβt); a pair of multiplicity mmm gives these two functions multiplied by 1,t,…,tm−11,t,\dots,t^{m-1}1,t,…,tm−1.
