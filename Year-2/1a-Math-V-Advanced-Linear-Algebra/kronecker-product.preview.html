Kronecker Product¶

Definition¶

Let A=(aij)A=(a_{ij})A=(aij​) be an m×nm\times nm×n matrix and BBB a p×qp\times qp×q matrix. The Kronecker product A⊗BA\otimes BA⊗B is the mp×nqmp\times nqmp×nq block matrix

A⊗B=(a11B⋯a1nB⋮⋱⋮am1B⋯amnB),A\otimes B=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}B&\cdots&a_{1n}B\\\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\a_{m1}B&\cdots&a_{mn}B\end{pmatrix},A⊗B=​a11​B⋮am1​B​⋯⋱⋯​a1n​B⋮amn​B​​,

whose (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) block is the scalar multiple aijBa_{ij}Baij​B. No compatibility of sizes is required.

Interpretation¶

Unlike the ordinary matrix product, the Kronecker product is defined for matrices of any sizes, and the dimensions multiply: every entry of AAA is replaced by a whole copy of BBB. Entry by entry, (A⊗B)(i−1)p+k, (j−1)q+l=aijbkl(A\otimes B)_{(i-1)p+k,\,(j-1)q+l}=a_{ij}b_{kl}(A⊗B)(i−1)p+k,(j−1)q+l​=aij​bkl​.

The figure uses A=(1234)A=\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{pmatrix}A=(13​24​) and B=(0110)B=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}B=(01​10​) from the examples. In A⊗BA\otimes BA⊗B each entry aija_{ij}aij​ becomes the block aijBa_{ij}Baij​B; in B⊗AB\otimes AB⊗A each entry bklb_{kl}bkl​ becomes bklAb_{kl}Abkl​A. Both are 4×44\times44×4 with the same entries, but arranged differently, so the product is not commutative.

It is the natural tool for linear maps acting on matrices or on two indices at once.

The product is not commutative: A⊗BA\otimes BA⊗B and B⊗AB\otimes AB⊗A have the same size and the same entries, but in a different arrangement. They differ by a reordering of rows and columns (they are similar via a permutation matrix when both factors are square).

Key Results¶

  • Bilinear and associative: (A+A′)⊗B=A⊗B+A′⊗B(A+A')\otimes B=A\otimes B+A'\otimes B(A+A′)⊗B=A⊗B+A′⊗B, (cA)⊗B=c(A⊗B)(cA)\otimes B=c(A\otimes B)(cA)⊗B=c(A⊗B), (A⊗B)⊗C=A⊗(B⊗C)(A\otimes B)\otimes C=A\otimes(B\otimes C)(A⊗B)⊗C=A⊗(B⊗C).
  • Mixed product: (A⊗B)(C⊗D)=(AC)⊗(BD)(A\otimes B)(C\otimes D)=(AC)\otimes(BD)(A⊗B)(C⊗D)=(AC)⊗(BD) whenever ACACAC and BDBDBD are defined.

Beyond the course

Econometrics texts often pair the Kronecker product with the vec operator, which stacks the columns of a matrix into one long vector. The vec operator is not part of Math V.
