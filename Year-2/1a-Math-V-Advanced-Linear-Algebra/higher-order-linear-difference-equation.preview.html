Higher-Order Linear Difference Equation¶

Definition¶

A linear difference equation of order kkk with constant coefficients is

xt+k+ak−1xt+k−1+⋯+a1xt+1+a0xt=gt,x_{t+k}+a_{k-1}x_{t+k-1}+\cdots +a_1x_{t+1}+a_0x_t=g_t,xt+k​+ak−1​xt+k−1​+⋯+a1​xt+1​+a0​xt​=gt​,

Interpretation¶

The second-order equation generalizes directly: the next value uses the kkk most recent values, so kkk initial values x0,…,xk−1x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1}x0​,…,xk−1​ are needed. The homogeneous equation is solved by the roots of the characteristic polynomial p(r)=rk+ak−1rk−1+⋯+a0p(r)=r^k+a_{k-1}r^{k-1}+\cdots+a_0p(r)=rk+ak−1​rk−1+⋯+a0​, now of degree kkk, so for k≥3k\ge3k≥3 the roots may be harder to find and a factor is often guessed first (with integer coefficients, every integer root divides a0a_0a0​).

The companion form shows why difference equations belong to linear algebra. The first k−1k-1k−1 rows of CCC only shift the window forward one period; the last row is the equation itself. Iterating is repeated matrix multiplication, yt=Cty0\boldsymbol{y}_t=C^t\boldsymbol{y}_0yt​=Cty0​ in the homogeneous case, and det⁡(λI−C)=p(λ)\det(\lambda I-C)=p(\lambda)det(λI−C)=p(λ), so the characteristic roots are the eigenvalues of CCC.

A common slip is to write the last row of CCC with +ai+a_i+ai​ instead of −ai-a_i−ai​: the signs flip because xt+kx_{t+k}xt+k​ is isolated on the left.

Key Results¶

  • Every choice of x0,…,xk−1x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1}x0​,…,xk−1​ gives exactly one solution; the homogeneous solutions form a kkk-dimensional space with a basis of solutions built from the characteristic roots.
  • A root rrr of multiplicity mmm contributes rt,trt,…,tm−1rtr^t,tr^t,\ldots,t^{m-1}r^trt,trt,…,tm−1rt.
  • General solution = particular solution + general homogeneous solution; for gt=bg_t=bgt​=b and p(1)≠0p(1)\ne0p(1)=0 the equilibrium x∗=b/p(1)x^*=b/p(1)x∗=b/p(1) is a particular solution.
  • det⁡C=(−1)ka0≠0\det C=(-1)^k a_0\ne0detC=(−1)ka0​=0, so CCC is invertible.
  • All solutions converge to x∗x^*x∗ if and only if every characteristic root has modulus less than 111 (stability).
