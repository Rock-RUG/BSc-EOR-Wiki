Singular Value Decomposition¶

Theorem¶

Let AAA be a real m×nm\times nm×n matrix of rank rrr and p=min⁡(m,n)p=\min(m,n)p=min(m,n). Then

A=UΣV⊤,A=U\Sigma V^{\top},A=UΣV⊤,

where UUU (m×m×mm\times mm) and VVV (n×n×nn\times nn) are orthogonal, U⊤U=ImU^{\top}U=I_mU⊤U=Im​, V⊤V=InV^{\top}V=I_nV⊤V=In​, and Σ\SigmaΣ is m×nm\times nm×n with diagonal entries σ1≥⋯≥σr>0=σr+1=⋯=σp\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_r>0=\sigma_{r+1}=\cdots=\sigma_pσ1​≥⋯≥σr​>0=σr+1​=⋯=σp​ and zeros off the diagonal. The σi\sigma_iσi​ are the singular values of AAA; the columns ui\boldsymbol{u}_iui​ of UUU and vi\boldsymbol{v}_ivi​ of VVV are the left and right singular vectors. The factorization is the singular value decomposition (SVD) of AAA.

Interpretation¶

Every linear map x↦Ax\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto A\boldsymbol{x}x↦Ax is a rotation or reflection (V⊤V^{\top}V⊤), then a scaling of the coordinate axes by σi\sigma_iσi​, possibly with a change of dimension (Σ\SigmaΣ), then another rotation or reflection (UUU). The unit sphere is mapped onto an ellipsoid, possibly flattened, with semi-axes σiui\sigma_i\boldsymbol{u}_iσi​ui​.

The figure follows the 2×22\times22×2 example below, A=(3045)A=\begin{pmatrix}3&0\\4&5\end{pmatrix}A=(34​05​), with all four panels drawn to the same scale. V⊤V^{\top}V⊤ sends the right singular vectors v1,v2\boldsymbol{v}_1,\boldsymbol{v}_2v1​,v2​ to the standard basis vectors e1,e2\boldsymbol{e}_1,\boldsymbol{e}_2e1​,e2​; Σ\SigmaΣ stretches them to lengths σ1=35\sigma_1=3\sqrt{5}σ1​=35​ and σ2=5\sigma_2=\sqrt{5}σ2​=5​; UUU moves the axes onto u1,u2\boldsymbol{u}_1,\boldsymbol{u}_2u1​,u2​. The result is the image of the unit circle under AAA, with semi-axes σ1u1=Av1\sigma_1\boldsymbol{u}_1=A\boldsymbol{v}_1σ1​u1​=Av1​ and σ2u2=Av2\sigma_2\boldsymbol{u}_2=A\boldsymbol{v}_2σ2​u2​=Av2​. In this example UUU and VVV both have determinant −1-1−1, so both outer steps are reflections.

The proof is constructive. A⊤AA^{\top}AA⊤A is symmetric and positive semidefinite, so its orthogonal diagonalization supplies orthonormal eigenvectors vi\boldsymbol{v}_ivi​ with eigenvalues σi2\sigma_i^2σi2​. Then ui=Avi/σi\boldsymbol{u}_i=A\boldsymbol{v}_i/\sigma_iui​=Avi​/σi​ for i≤ri\le ri≤r are orthonormal, and the Gram-Schmidt algorithm completes them to an orthonormal basis of Rm\mathbb{R}^mRm.

Unlike diagonalization P−1APP^{-1}APP−1AP, the SVD exists for every matrix, square or not, and uses two different orthonormal bases. It coincides with the spectral decomposition only for symmetric positive semidefinite matrices; for a symmetric matrix in general σi=∣λi∣\sigma_i=|\lambda_i|σi​=∣λi​∣.

Key Results¶

  • A⊤A=VΣ⊤ΣV⊤A^{\top}A=V\Sigma^{\top}\Sigma V^{\top}A⊤A=VΣ⊤ΣV⊤ and AA⊤=UΣΣ⊤U⊤AA^{\top}=U\Sigma\Sigma^{\top}U^{\top}AA⊤=UΣΣ⊤U⊤; both have nonzero eigenvalues σ12,…,σr2\sigma_1^2,\ldots,\sigma_r^2σ12​,…,σr2​, and all their other eigenvalues are 000.
  • Avi=σiuiA\boldsymbol{v}_i=\sigma_i\boldsymbol{u}_iAvi​=σi​ui​ and A⊤ui=σiviA^{\top}\boldsymbol{u}_i=\sigma_i\boldsymbol{v}_iA⊤ui​=σi​vi​ for i≤pi\le pi≤p.
  • Rank-one expansion and reduced SVD: A=∑i=1rσiuivi⊤=UrΣrVr⊤\displaystyle A=\sum_{i=1}^r\sigma_i\boldsymbol{u}_i\boldsymbol{v}_i^{\top}=U_r\Sigma_rV_r^{\top}A=i=1∑r​σi​ui​vi⊤​=Ur​Σr​Vr⊤​, using only the first rrr columns.
  • Orthonormal bases of the four subspaces (Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra): u1,…,ur\boldsymbol{u}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_ru1​,…,ur​ for Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A), ur+1,…,um\boldsymbol{u}_{r+1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{u}_mur+1​,…,um​ for Null⁡(A⊤)\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top})Null(A⊤), v1,…,vr\boldsymbol{v}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_rv1​,…,vr​ for Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A), vr+1,…,vn\boldsymbol{v}_{r+1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_nvr+1​,…,vn​ for Null⁡(A)\operatorname{Null}(A)Null(A).
  • The singular values are unique; UUU and VVV are not (signs can be flipped in pairs ui,vi\boldsymbol{u}_i,\boldsymbol{v}_iui​,vi​, and repeated or zero singular values allow more freedom).
  • The Moore-Penrose inverse is A+=VΣ+U⊤A^+=V\Sigma^+U^{\top}A+=VΣ+U⊤.

Beyond the course

Many textbooks add the Eckart-Young theorem: keeping only the first k<rk<rk<r terms of the rank-one expansion gives the best approximation of AAA by a matrix of rank at most kkk. Math V does not cover it.
