Complex Conjugate Pair of Eigenvalues¶

Proposition¶

Let AAA be a real n×nn\times nn×n matrix and let λ=a+bi\lambda=a+b\mathrm{i}λ=a+bi with a,b∈Ra,b\in\mathbb{R}a,b∈R, b≠0b\ne0b=0, be an eigenvalue of AAA with eigenvector p∈Cn\boldsymbol{p}\in\mathbb{C}^np∈Cn. Then the complex conjugate λˉ=a−bi\bar\lambda=a-b\mathrm{i}λˉ=a−bi is also an eigenvalue of AAA, with eigenvector pˉ\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}pˉ​ (entrywise conjugate), and λ\lambdaλ and λˉ\bar\lambdaλˉ have the same algebraic multiplicity.

Proof. Conjugating Ap=λpA\boldsymbol{p}=\lambda\boldsymbol{p}Ap=λp and using Aˉ=A\bar{A}=AAˉ=A gives Apˉ=λˉpˉA\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}=\bar\lambda\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}Apˉ​=λˉpˉ​, and pˉ≠0\bar{\boldsymbol{p}}\ne\boldsymbol{0}pˉ​=0. The multiplicity statement holds because the characteristic polynomial has real coefficients, so its factorization is invariant under conjugation.

Interpretation¶

The characteristic polynomial det⁡(A−λI)\det(A-\lambda I)det(A−λI) of a real matrix has real coefficients, so this is the complex conjugate root theorem from Math II applied to eigenvalues. The hypothesis that AAA is real is essential: the 1×11\times11×1 complex matrix (i)(\mathrm{i})(i) has eigenvalue i\mathrm{i}i but not −i-\mathrm{i}−i.

The figure uses the numerical matrix from the examples, A=(1−213)A=\begin{pmatrix}1&-2\\1&3\end{pmatrix}A=(11​−23​). On the left its eigenvalues λ=2+i\lambda=2+\mathrm{i}λ=2+i and λˉ=2−i\bar\lambda=2-\mathrm{i}λˉ=2−i are reflections of each other in the real axis, with the same modulus ρ=5\rho=\sqrt{5}ρ=5​ and opposite angles ±θ\pm\theta±θ. On the right are the real and imaginary parts u=(−1,1)⊤\boldsymbol{u}=(-1,1)^{\top}u=(−1,1)⊤ and w=(1,0)⊤\boldsymbol{w}=(1,0)^{\top}w=(1,0)⊤ of the eigenvector p\boldsymbol{p}p: the image Au=(−3,2)⊤=2u−wA\boldsymbol{u}=(-3,2)^{\top}=2\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{w}Au=(−3,2)⊤=2u−w stays in the plane of u\boldsymbol{u}u and w\boldsymbol{w}w rather than on the line through u\boldsymbol{u}u.

A nonreal eigenvalue has no real eigenvector, so it does not describe a stretched line. Instead the pair acts on a real plane: writing p=u+iw\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{u}+\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{w}p=u+iw with real u,w\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}u,w, the plane span⁡{u,w}\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}\}span{u,w} is mapped into itself, and in the coordinates given by u,w\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}u,w the coordinate block is (ab−ba)=ρR−θ\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}=\rho R_{-\theta}(a−b​ba​)=ρR−θ​, with λ=ρeiθ\lambda=\rho \mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}λ=ρeiθ in polar form. Thus the coordinate vector rotates by −θ-\theta−θ and scales by ρ\rhoρ each step. The basis u,w\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}u,w need not be orthonormal, so this is a similarity description: lengths and angles in the original Euclidean plane need not change by those same rules.

The pair matters for linear difference equations: for xt+1=Axt\boldsymbol{x}_{t+1}=A\boldsymbol{x}_txt+1​=Axt​ it produces oscillating terms ρtcos⁡(θt)\rho^t\cos(\theta t)ρtcos(θt) and ρtsin⁡(θt)\rho^t\sin(\theta t)ρtsin(θt), which decay exactly when ρ=∣λ∣<1\rho=\lvert\lambda\rvert<1ρ=∣λ∣<1.

Key Results¶

  • Nonreal eigenvalues of a real matrix come in pairs, so their number (with multiplicity) is even; a real n×nn\times nn×n matrix with nnn odd has at least one real eigenvalue.
  • If p=u+iw\boldsymbol{p}=\boldsymbol{u}+\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{w}p=u+iw is an eigenvector for a+bia+b\mathrm{i}a+bi, b≠0b\neq0b=0, then u,w\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{w}u,w are linearly independent and Au=au−bwA\boldsymbol{u}=a\boldsymbol{u}-b\boldsymbol{w}Au=au−bw, Aw=bu+awA\boldsymbol{w}=b\boldsymbol{u}+a\boldsymbol{w}Aw=bu+aw, i.e. A [u w]=[u w](ab−ba)A\,[\boldsymbol{u}\ \boldsymbol{w}]=[\boldsymbol{u}\ \boldsymbol{w}]\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\-b&a\end{pmatrix}A[u w]=[u w](a−b​ba​).
  • The pair contributes λλˉ=a2+b2=∣λ∣2>0\lambda\bar\lambda=a^2+b^2=\lvert\lambda\rvert^2>0λλˉ=a2+b2=∣λ∣2>0 to det⁡A\det AdetA and 2a2a2a to tr⁡A\operatorname{tr}AtrA.
  • A 2×22\times22×2 real matrix has a nonreal pair if and only if (tr⁡A)2−4det⁡A<0(\operatorname{tr}A)^2-4\det A<0(trA)2−4detA<0.
  • By De Moivre's formula, λt=ρt(cos⁡(θt)+isin⁡(θt))\lambda^t=\rho^t\big(\cos(\theta t)+\mathrm{i}\sin(\theta t)\big)λt=ρt(cos(θt)+isin(θt)), so λt→0\lambda^t\to0λt→0 if and only if ρ<1\rho<1ρ<1.
