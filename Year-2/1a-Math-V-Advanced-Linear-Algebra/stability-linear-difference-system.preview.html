Stability of a System of Linear Difference Equations¶

Proposition¶

Let AAA be a real n×nn\times nn×n matrix and consider the system xt+1=Axt+b\boldsymbol{x}_{t+1}=A\boldsymbol{x}_t+\boldsymbol{b}xt+1​=Axt​+b with constant b∈Rn\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^nb∈Rn. The following are equivalent:

  1. every eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ of AAA, real or complex, satisfies ∣λ∣<1\lvert\lambda\rvert<1∣λ∣<1;
  2. At→OA^t\to OAt→O as t→∞t\to\inftyt→∞;
  3. for every x0\boldsymbol{x}_0x0​ the solution of the homogeneous system satisfies xt=Atx0→0\boldsymbol{x}_t=A^t\boldsymbol{x}_0\to\boldsymbol{0}xt​=Atx0​→0.

In that case 111 is not an eigenvalue, the equilibrium x∗=(I−A)−1b\boldsymbol{x}^*=(I-A)^{-1}\boldsymbol{b}x∗=(I−A)−1b is unique, and every solution converges to it: the equilibrium is (globally) asymptotically stable.

Interpretation¶

The deviation from equilibrium evolves as xt−x∗=At(x0−x∗)\boldsymbol{x}_t-\boldsymbol{x}^*=A^t(\boldsymbol{x}_0-\boldsymbol{x}^*)xt​−x∗=At(x0​−x∗). For a diagonalizable matrix it is a sum of terms ciλitpic_i\lambda_i^t\boldsymbol{p}_ici​λit​pi​, and each dies out exactly when the modulus ∣λi∣\lvert\lambda_i\rvert∣λi​∣ is below 111. In general the Jordan normal form adds factors tjt^jtj, which do not change the conclusion. This is the matrix version of the scalar criterion ∣a∣<1\lvert a\rvert<1∣a∣<1.

The figure plots the eigenvalues of the three examples below. The first matrix has the single eigenvalue 0.50.50.5 (green), inside the unit circle, so the system is asymptotically stable. The second has −0.5-0.5−0.5 inside but 1.51.51.5 outside (red), and one eigenvalue outside is enough for instability. The third has 0.70.70.7 inside and 111 on the circle (orange): solutions stay bounded and converge, but not to a single equilibrium.

Complex eigenvalues must be included: a conjugate pair ρe±iθ\rho \mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\theta}ρe±iθ produces rotations whose size is multiplied by ρ\rhoρ each period. A common mistake is to inspect the entries of AAA instead of its eigenvalues; small diagonal entries do not imply stability unless AAA is triangular.

In Math V, asymptotic stability means that all solutions converge, which needs all eigenvalues strictly inside the unit circle. The weaker notion, stable, means that all solutions stay bounded; it holds if and only if all ∣λ∣≤1\lvert\lambda\rvert\le1∣λ∣≤1 and every eigenvalue with ∣λ∣=1\lvert\lambda\rvert=1∣λ∣=1 has equal algebraic and geometric multiplicity.

Key Results¶

  • If some eigenvalue has ∣λ∣>1\lvert\lambda\rvert>1∣λ∣>1, solutions starting on its eigenvector (or its real invariant plane, for a complex pair) grow without bound; solutions starting in the span of the generalized eigenvectors with ∣λ∣<1\lvert\lambda\rvert<1∣λ∣<1 still converge.
  • Eigenvalues with ∣λ∣=1\lvert\lambda\rvert=1∣λ∣=1 exclude asymptotic stability: λ=1\lambda=1λ=1 gives constant modes, λ=−1\lambda=-1λ=−1 a two-cycle, a nonreal pair a rotation of constant size.
  • For n=2n=2n=2: both eigenvalues lie inside the unit circle if and only if ∣det⁡A∣<1\lvert\det A\rvert<1∣detA∣<1 and ∣tr⁡A∣<1+det⁡A\lvert\operatorname{tr}A\rvert<1+\det A∣trA∣<1+detA (the determinant and trace are the product and sum of the eigenvalues).
  • Necessary condition for any nnn: ∣det⁡A∣<1\lvert\det A\rvert<1∣detA∣<1, since ∣det⁡A∣=∏i∣λi∣\lvert\det A\rvert=\prod_i\lvert\lambda_i\rvert∣detA∣=∏i​∣λi​∣.
  • A scalar equation of order kkk is (asymptotically) stable if and only if its companion matrix is, since the eigenvalues are the characteristic roots (higher-order equation).
