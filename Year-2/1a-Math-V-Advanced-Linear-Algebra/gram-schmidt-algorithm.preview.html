Gram-Schmidt Algorithm¶

Method¶

Input: linearly independent vectors a1,…,ak\boldsymbol{a}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_ka1​,…,ak​ in a real inner product space VVV (in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn: the dot product).

Steps: for j=1,…,kj=1,\ldots,kj=1,…,k,

vj=aj−∑i=1j−1aj⋅vivi⋅vi vi,qj=vj∥vj∥.\boldsymbol{v}_j=\boldsymbol{a}_j-\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\frac{\boldsymbol{a}_j\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_i}{\boldsymbol{v}_i\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_i}\,\boldsymbol{v}_i,\qquad \boldsymbol{q}_j=\frac{\boldsymbol{v}_j}{\|\boldsymbol{v}_j\|}.vj​=aj​−i=1∑j−1​vi​⋅vi​aj​⋅vi​​vi​,qj​=∥vj​∥vj​​.

Output: orthogonal vectors v1,…,vk\boldsymbol{v}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{v}_kv1​,…,vk​ and orthonormal vectors q1,…,qk\boldsymbol{q}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{q}_kq1​,…,qk​ such that, for every jjj,

span⁡{q1,…,qj}=span⁡{a1,…,aj}.\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{q}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{q}_j\}=\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{a}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_j\}.span{q1​,…,qj​}=span{a1​,…,aj​}.

Interpretation¶

Each step removes from aj\boldsymbol{a}_jaj​ its orthogonal projection onto the span of the earlier vectors; what is left, vj\boldsymbol{v}_jvj​, is the component of aj\boldsymbol{a}_jaj​ in the orthogonal complement of that span. Dividing by the norm ∥vj∥=vj⋅vj\|\boldsymbol{v}_j\|=\sqrt{\boldsymbol{v}_j\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_j}∥vj​∥=vj​⋅vj​​ (the Euclidean norm in Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn) then gives a unit vector. Since the qi\boldsymbol{q}_iqi​ are already orthonormal, the update can equally be written vj=aj−∑i<j(aj⋅qi)qi\displaystyle \boldsymbol{v}_j=\boldsymbol{a}_j-\sum_{i<j}(\boldsymbol{a}_j\cdot\boldsymbol{q}_i)\boldsymbol{q}_ivj​=aj​−i<j∑​(aj​⋅qi​)qi​.

The projection of a2\boldsymbol{a}_2a2​ onto v1=a1\boldsymbol{v}_1=\boldsymbol{a}_1v1​=a1​ is 12v1\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{v}_121​v1​, since (a2⋅v1)/(v1⋅v1)=12(\boldsymbol{a}_2\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_1)/(\boldsymbol{v}_1\cdot\boldsymbol{v}_1)=\tfrac{1}{2}(a2​⋅v1​)/(v1​⋅v1​)=21​. Removing it leaves v2=a2−12v1=(12,−12,1)⊤\boldsymbol{v}_2=\boldsymbol{a}_2-\tfrac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{v}_1=(\tfrac{1}{2},-\tfrac{1}{2},1)^{\top}v2​=a2​−21​v1​=(21​,−21​,1)⊤, the dashed perpendicular moved to the origin, which is orthogonal to v1\boldsymbol{v}_1v1​.

Linear independence is what guarantees vj≠0\boldsymbol{v}_j\neq\boldsymbol{0}vj​=0, so that the division is allowed. The output depends on the order of the input: reordering the aj\boldsymbol{a}_jaj​ generally gives a different orthonormal basis of the same space.

A frequent slip is to project onto the original ai\boldsymbol{a}_iai​ instead of the new vi\boldsymbol{v}_ivi​; the ai\boldsymbol{a}_iai​ are not orthogonal, so their projections overlap and the result is wrong.

Key Results¶

  • vj=0\boldsymbol{v}_j=\boldsymbol{0}vj​=0 occurs exactly when aj∈span⁡{a1,…,aj−1}\boldsymbol{a}_j\in\operatorname{span}\{\boldsymbol{a}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{a}_{j-1}\}aj​∈span{a1​,…,aj−1​}; run on any list, the algorithm detects dependence, and discarding such aj\boldsymbol{a}_jaj​ yields an orthonormal basis of the span of the list.
  • Every finite-dimensional inner product space has an orthonormal basis, and every orthonormal list extends to one.
  • In floating-point arithmetic the classical version loses orthogonality; the modified version, which subtracts projections one at a time from the updated vector, is the numerically preferred form.

Beyond the course

Many textbooks collect the Gram-Schmidt output in a factorization A=QRA=QRA=QR of a matrix with independent columns, with QQQ having orthonormal columns and RRR upper triangular. This QR decomposition is not part of Math V.
