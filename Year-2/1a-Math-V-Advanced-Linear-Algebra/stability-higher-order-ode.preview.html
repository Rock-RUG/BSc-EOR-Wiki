Proposition¶

In Math V, stable means that all solutions stay bounded, and asymptotically stable that they all converge (case 1). A simple root on the imaginary axis therefore gives a stable equation that is not asymptotically stable.

The criterion is the continuous-time counterpart of stability of an equilibrium: writing the equation as a first-order system, the roots are the eigenvalues of the companion matrix, and "negative real part" plays the role of f′(x∗)<0f'(x^*)<0f′(x∗)<0. Typical mistakes: requiring only real roots to be negative and ignoring complex ones; treating purely imaginary roots as stable in the asymptotic sense; and concluding stability from positive coefficients alone when n≥3n\geq3n≥3.

Beyond the course

Many textbooks state the Routh-Hurwitz criterion, which decides from the coefficients alone whether all roots have negative real part. It is not part of Math V.
