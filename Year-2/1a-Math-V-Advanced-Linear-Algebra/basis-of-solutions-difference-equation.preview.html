Basis of Solutions of a Difference Equation¶

Definition¶

Consider the homogeneous linear difference equation of order kkk with constant coefficients and a0≠0a_0\ne0a0​=0,

xt+k+ak−1xt+k−1+⋯+a0xt=0.x_{t+k}+a_{k-1}x_{t+k-1}+\cdots+a_0x_t=0.xt+k​+ak−1​xt+k−1​+⋯+a0​xt​=0.

Its solutions form a subspace SSS of the vector space of real sequences. A basis of solutions (fundamental set) is a list of solutions y(1),…,y(k)y^{(1)},\ldots,y^{(k)}y(1),…,y(k) that is a basis of SSS: the sequences are linearly independent, and every solution can be written uniquely as

xt=c1yt(1)+⋯+ckyt(k).x_t=c_1y^{(1)}_t+\cdots+c_ky^{(k)}_t.xt​=c1​yt(1)​+⋯+ck​yt(k)​.

This expression with arbitrary constants c1,…,ckc_1,\ldots,c_kc1​,…,ck​ is the general solution.

Interpretation¶

Linearity makes sums and scalar multiples of solutions again solutions, so the solution set is a vector space. Its dimension is kkk because a solution is fixed by its kkk initial values: the map x↦(x0,…,xk−1)x\mapsto(x_0,\ldots,x_{k-1})x↦(x0​,…,xk−1​) is a linear bijection from SSS onto Rk\mathbb{R}^kRk. A basis therefore gives coordinates (c1,…,ck)(c_1,\ldots,c_k)(c1​,…,ck​) for every possible trajectory, and fitting initial values means solving a k×kk\times kk×k linear system.

The figure uses the first example, xt+2−3xt+1+2xt=0x_{t+2}-3x_{t+1}+2x_t=0xt+2​−3xt+1​+2xt​=0. Its basis solutions are the constant sequence yt(1)=1y^{(1)}_t=1yt(1)​=1 and yt(2)=2ty^{(2)}_t=2^tyt(2)​=2t. The initial values x0=5x_0=5x0​=5, x1=7x_1=7x1​=7 fix the coordinates c1=3c_1=3c1​=3, c2=2c_2=2c2​=2, and the green trajectory xt=3yt(1)+2yt(2)=3+2t+1x_t=3y^{(1)}_t+2y^{(2)}_t=3+2^{t+1}xt​=3yt(1)​+2yt(2)​=3+2t+1 follows: 5,7,11,195,7,11,195,7,11,19. Other initial values give other coordinates for the same two basis sequences.

The standard basis comes from the characteristic polynomial. A common error is to take fewer than kkk sequences, for example only rtr^trt for a double root rrr; the list then spans a proper subspace and cannot match all initial values. Another is to take kkk sequences that are multiples of each other, such as 2t2^t2t and 3⋅2t3\cdot2^t3⋅2t, which are dependent.

The inhomogeneous equation has no basis of solutions: its solution set is the affine subspace "particular solution + S+\,S+S".

Key Results¶

  • dim⁡S=k\dim S=kdimS=k; any kkk linearly independent solutions form a basis.
  • kkk solutions form a basis if and only if their vectors of initial values (y0(j),…,yk−1(j))\big(y^{(j)}_0,\ldots,y^{(j)}_{k-1}\big)(y0(j)​,…,yk−1(j)​), j=1,…,kj=1,\ldots,kj=1,…,k, are linearly independent, that is, the k×kk\times kk×k matrix with these vectors as columns has nonzero determinant.
  • If the roots r1,…,rkr_1,\ldots,r_kr1​,…,rk​ are distinct, r1t,…,rktr_1^t,\ldots,r_k^tr1t​,…,rkt​ is a basis (the matrix of initial values is a Vandermonde matrix).
  • A root rrr of multiplicity mmm contributes rt,trt,…,tm−1rtr^t,tr^t,\ldots,t^{m-1}r^trt,trt,…,tm−1rt; a nonreal pair ρe±iθ\rho \mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\theta}ρe±iθ may be replaced by ρtcos⁡(θt), ρtsin⁡(θt)\rho^t\cos(\theta t),\ \rho^t\sin(\theta t)ρtcos(θt), ρtsin(θt).
