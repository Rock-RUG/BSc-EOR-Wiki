Interpretation¶

A differential equation describes continuous-time change: it states how the rate of change of a quantity depends on time and on the current state. It is the continuous-time counterpart of a difference equation, where the state jumps from xtx_txt​ to xt+1x_{t+1}xt+1​.

The unknown is a whole function, not a number. An equation without initial conditions therefore has a family of solutions, usually indexed by nnn free constants; the nnn initial conditions select one member of that family.

A common misunderstanding is to treat "solution" as a formula valid for all ttt. A solution lives on an interval, and for nonlinear equations it can cease to exist after finite time (see the last example).

Key Results¶

  • The order is the highest derivative that occurs; x′′+(x′)3=0x''+(x')^3=0x′′+(x′)3=0 has order 2, not 3.
  • The simplest case x′(t)=g(t)x'(t)=g(t)x′(t)=g(t) with ggg continuous is solved by any antiderivative: x(t)=x(t0)+∫t0tg(s) dsx(t)=x(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^t g(s)\,\mathrm{d}sx(t)=x(t0​)+∫t0​t​g(s)ds.
  • For a linear equation with continuous coefficients ak(t)a_k(t)ak​(t) and g(t)g(t)g(t) on III, every initial value problem has exactly one solution, defined on all of III.
  • For a homogeneous linear equation, sums and scalar multiples of solutions are solutions, so the solutions form a vector space.
  • A nonlinear equation can have solutions that exist only on a bounded interval.
