Orthogonal Matrix¶

Definition¶

A real n×nn\times nn×n matrix QQQ is orthogonal if

Q⊤Q=I,Q^{\top}Q=I,Q⊤Q=I,

where III is the n×nn\times nn×n identity matrix. Equivalently, QQQ is invertible with Q−1=Q⊤Q^{-1}=Q^{\top}Q−1=Q⊤, so also QQ⊤=IQQ^{\top}=IQQ⊤=I.

Interpretation¶

The (i,j)(i,j)(i,j) entry of Q⊤QQ^{\top}QQ⊤Q is the inner product qi⋅qj\boldsymbol{q}_i\cdot\boldsymbol{q}_jqi​⋅qj​ of columns iii and jjj of QQQ. So Q⊤Q=IQ^{\top}Q=IQ⊤Q=I says exactly that the columns form an orthonormal basis of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, and QQ⊤=IQQ^{\top}=IQQ⊤=I says the same of the rows. The name is traditional: the columns must be orthonormal, not merely orthogonal. The diagonal matrix diag⁡(2,1)\operatorname{diag}(2,1)diag(2,1) has orthogonal columns but is not an orthogonal matrix.

Geometrically, x↦Qx\boldsymbol{x}\mapsto Q\boldsymbol{x}x↦Qx is a rigid motion that fixes the origin: it preserves lengths, angles and distances. In R2\mathbb{R}^2R2 every orthogonal matrix is a rotation or a reflection in a line through the origin.

In both cases the columns stay on the unit circle and at a right angle, so lengths and angles are preserved; only the orientation of the square differs, and that is what the sign of det⁡Q\det QdetQ records.

Orthogonal matrices make changes of basis cheap: if the new basis is orthonormal, its transformation matrix QQQ is orthogonal and the inverse needed for new coordinates is just the transpose, y=Q⊤x\boldsymbol{y}=Q^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}y=Q⊤x. This is why they appear in central factorizations. A symmetric matrix can be diagonalized by an orthogonal matrix, A=PDP⊤A=PDP^{\top}A=PDP⊤ with orthonormal eigenvectors as the columns of PPP. In Week 7, the singular value decomposition A=UΣV⊤A=U\Sigma V^{\top}A=UΣV⊤ of an arbitrary matrix also uses orthogonal UUU and VVV.

A rectangular n×kn\times kn×k matrix QQQ with k<nk<nk<n and orthonormal columns satisfies Q⊤Q=IkQ^{\top}Q=I_kQ⊤Q=Ik​ but not QQ⊤=InQQ^{\top}=I_nQQ⊤=In​; QQ⊤QQ^{\top}QQ⊤ is then the orthogonal projection onto its column space. The word "orthogonal matrix" is reserved for the square case.

Key Results¶

  • QQQ preserves inner products and lengths: (Qx)⋅(Qy)=x⊤Q⊤Qy=x⋅y(Q\boldsymbol{x})\cdot(Q\boldsymbol{y})=\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}Q^{\top}Q\boldsymbol{y}=\boldsymbol{x}\cdot\boldsymbol{y}(Qx)⋅(Qy)=x⊤Q⊤Qy=x⋅y, hence ∥Qx∥=∥x∥\|Q\boldsymbol{x}\|=\|\boldsymbol{x}\|∥Qx∥=∥x∥. Conversely, a square matrix with ∥Qx∥=∥x∥\|Q\boldsymbol{x}\|=\|\boldsymbol{x}\|∥Qx∥=∥x∥ for all x\boldsymbol{x}x is orthogonal.
  • det⁡Q=±1\det Q=\pm1detQ=±1, since (det⁡Q)2=det⁡(Q⊤Q)=det⁡I=1(\det Q)^2=\det(Q^{\top}Q)=\det I=1(detQ)2=det(Q⊤Q)=detI=1 by the product rule for the determinant. In R2\mathbb{R}^2R2 and R3\mathbb{R}^3R3, det⁡Q=1\det Q=1detQ=1 gives a rotation and det⁡Q=−1\det Q=-1detQ=−1 includes a reflection. The converse fails: (1101)\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix}(10​11​) has determinant 111 but is not orthogonal, since its second column has length 2\sqrt{2}2​.
  • Every (possibly complex) eigenvalue λ\lambdaλ of QQQ has ∣λ∣=1|\lambda|=1∣λ∣=1; the only possible real eigenvalues are 111 and −1-1−1.
  • Products, inverses and transposes of orthogonal matrices are orthogonal: (Q1Q2)⊤Q1Q2=Q2⊤Q2=I(Q_1Q_2)^{\top}Q_1Q_2=Q_2^{\top}Q_2=I(Q1​Q2​)⊤Q1​Q2​=Q2⊤​Q2​=I. Sums generally are not: I+I=2II+I=2II+I=2I is not orthogonal.
  • A linear system Qx=bQ\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{b}Qx=b with QQQ orthogonal is solved by x=Q⊤b\boldsymbol{x}=Q^{\top}\boldsymbol{b}x=Q⊤b, without elimination; equivalently, xi=qi⋅bx_i=\boldsymbol{q}_i\cdot\boldsymbol{b}xi​=qi​⋅b.
  • Every permutation matrix is orthogonal.
  • Applying the Gram-Schmidt algorithm to any basis of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn and placing the resulting vectors as columns produces an orthogonal matrix.

Examples¶

Rotations and reflections in R2\mathbb{R}^2R2. For an angle θ\thetaθ,

Rθ=(cos⁡θ−sin⁡θsin⁡θcos⁡θ),Sθ=(cos⁡θsin⁡θsin⁡θ−cos⁡θ).\begin{aligned} R_\theta&=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix},\\ S_\theta&=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&-\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned}Rθ​Sθ​​=(cosθsinθ​−sinθcosθ​),=(cosθsinθ​sinθ−cosθ​).​

Both have orthonormal columns because cos⁡2θ+sin⁡2θ=1\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1cos2θ+sin2θ=1. RθR_\thetaRθ​ rotates by θ\thetaθ and has det⁡Rθ=1\det R_\theta=1detRθ​=1; SθS_\thetaSθ​ reflects in the line at angle θ/2\theta/2θ/2 and has det⁡Sθ=−1\det S_\theta=-1detSθ​=−1.

For example, Rπ/2=(0−110)R_{\pi/2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}Rπ/2​=(01​−10​) maps (3,4)⊤(3,4)^{\top}(3,4)⊤ to (−4,3)⊤(-4,3)^{\top}(−4,3)⊤; both vectors have length 555, and their dot product −12+12=0-12+12=0−12+12=0 confirms the right angle.
