Moore-Penrose Inverse¶

Definition¶

Let AAA be a real m×nm\times nm×n matrix. The Moore-Penrose inverse of AAA is the n×mn\times mn×m matrix A+A^+A+ satisfying the four Penrose conditions

(1) AA+A=A,(2) A+AA+=A+,(3) (AA+)⊤=AA+,(4) (A+A)⊤=A+A.\text{(1) }AA^+A=A,\quad \text{(2) }A^+AA^+=A^+,\quad \text{(3) }(AA^+)^{\top}=AA^+,\quad \text{(4) }(A^+A)^{\top}=A^+A.(1) AA+A=A,(2) A+AA+=A+,(3) (AA+)⊤=AA+,(4) (A+A)⊤=A+A.
(1) AA+A=A,(2) A+AA+=A+,(3) (AA+)⊤=AA+,(4) (A+A)⊤=A+A.\begin{aligned} &\text{(1) }AA^+A=A,\\ &\text{(2) }A^+AA^+=A^+,\\ &\text{(3) }(AA^+)^{\top}=AA^+,\\ &\text{(4) }(A^+A)^{\top}=A^+A. \end{aligned}​(1) AA+A=A,(2) A+AA+=A+,(3) (AA+)⊤=AA+,(4) (A+A)⊤=A+A.​

For every AAA exactly one such matrix exists.

Interpretation¶

Condition (1) alone defines a generalized inverse, of which there are usually many. Condition (2) makes AAA in turn a generalized inverse of A+A^+A+, and conditions (3) and (4) require the two products to be symmetric. Together they force AA+AA^+AA+ and A+AA^+AA+A to be orthogonal projections, which pins down a single matrix.

In the figure, AAA (blue) carries the row space Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A) onto the column space Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) and sends Null⁡(A)\operatorname{Null}(A)Null(A) to 0\boldsymbol{0}0. The Moore-Penrose inverse A+A^+A+ (red) runs the other way: it undoes AAA from Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) back onto the row space and sends Null⁡(A⊤)\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top})Null(A⊤) to 0\boldsymbol{0}0. This is why AA+AA^+AA+ projects onto Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) and A+AA^+AA+A onto Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A).

Geometrically, A+A^+A+ inverts AAA where it can and sends the rest to zero: it maps Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) back onto the row space by undoing AAA, and maps Null⁡(A⊤)\operatorname{Null}(A^{\top})Null(A⊤) to 0\boldsymbol{0}0.

Two cautions. In general (AB)+≠B+A+(AB)^+\neq B^+A^+(AB)+=B+A+. And A+A^+A+ does not depend continuously on AAA: for ε≠0\varepsilon\neq0ε=0, diag⁡(1,ε)+=diag⁡(1,1/ε)\operatorname{diag}(1,\varepsilon)^+=\operatorname{diag}(1,1/\varepsilon)diag(1,ε)+=diag(1,1/ε), which explodes as ε→0\varepsilon\to0ε→0, while diag⁡(1,0)+=diag⁡(1,0)\operatorname{diag}(1,0)^+=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)diag(1,0)+=diag(1,0).

  • If AAA is square and invertible, A+=A−1A^+=A^{-1}A+=A−1. For a scalar, a+=1/aa^+=1/aa+=1/a if a≠0a\neq0a=0 and 0+=00^+=00+=0.
  • Full column rank: A+=(A⊤A)−1A⊤A^+=(A^{\top}A)^{-1}A^{\top}A+=(A⊤A)−1A⊤, a left inverse. Full row rank: A+=A⊤(AA⊤)−1A^+=A^{\top}(AA^{\top})^{-1}A+=A⊤(AA⊤)−1, a right inverse.
  • AA+AA^+AA+ is the orthogonal projection onto Col⁡(A)\operatorname{Col}(A)Col(A) and A+AA^+AA+A the orthogonal projection onto the row space Row⁡(A)\operatorname{Row}(A)Row(A).
  • x=A+b\boldsymbol{x}=A^+\boldsymbol{b}x=A+b is the least-squares solution of Ax=bA\boldsymbol{x}=\boldsymbol{b}Ax=b of minimum norm; if the system is consistent, it is its minimum-norm solution.
