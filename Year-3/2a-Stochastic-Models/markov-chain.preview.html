Markov Chain¶

Definition¶

A discrete-time Markov chain is a stochastic process (Xn)n≥0(X_n)_{n\ge0}(Xn​)n≥0​ on a finite or countable state space SSS whose next-state probabilities depend on the observed past only through the present state. For every history with positive probability,

P(Xn+1=j∣X0=i0,…,Xn=i)=P(Xn+1=j∣Xn=i).\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}=j\mid X_0=i_0,\ldots,X_n=i) =\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}=j\mid X_n=i).P(Xn+1​=j∣X0​=i0​,…,Xn​=i)=P(Xn+1​=j∣Xn​=i).

The chain is time-homogeneous when these conditional probabilities do not depend on nnn. Its transition matrix is P=(pij)\boldsymbol{P}=(p_{ij})P=(pij​), where pij=P(Xn+1=j∣Xn=i)p_{ij}=\mathbb{P}(X_{n+1}=j\mid X_n=i)pij​=P(Xn+1​=j∣Xn​=i), pij≥0p_{ij}\ge0pij​≥0, and ∑j∈Spij=1\displaystyle\sum_{j\in S}p_{ij}=1j∈S∑​pij​=1.

Interpretation¶

The state must retain all information from the past needed to predict the next transition. A rating class alone may be insufficient if next year's premium also depends on how many consecutive claim-free years have elapsed; include that count in the state. The property does not say that successive states are independent.

Key Results¶

For a finite homogeneous chain, let αn\boldsymbol{\alpha}_nαn​ be the row vector of state probabilities. The matrix product sums over possible intermediate states, giving

αn+1=αnP,αn=α0Pn.\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{n+1}=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_n\boldsymbol{P},\qquad \boldsymbol{\alpha}_n=\boldsymbol{\alpha}_0\boldsymbol{P}^n.αn+1​=αn​P,αn​=α0​Pn.

The entry (Pn)ij(\boldsymbol{P}^n)_{ij}(Pn)ij​ is the probability of reaching jjj in nnn steps when starting at iii. These powers require homogeneity; a changing transition rule instead uses the ordered product of the successive matrices.
